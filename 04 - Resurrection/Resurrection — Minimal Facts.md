## What the argument is

Take only the facts most scholars grant, including hostile ones. Then argue that the resurrection explains that set better than the alternatives.

The appeal is obvious. You are not asking anyone to accept the Bible first.

## What I found when I tested it

I ran the case as Layer 2 of the interlock project. The rule that carried the study was that the apologetics site was the object under test and never the source of the objections. Objections came from named critics only.

Two things failed, and they failed against me.

**The consensus premise is cost 4.** The claim that most scholars accept the empty tomb traces back to a bibliography Habermas himself described in 2012 as mostly unpublished. That makes the warrant unauditable. It is the same discount I applied to my own weak fit ratios in run 1, so I have to apply it here.

**Enemy attestation is cost 3.** Matthew reporting his opponents' explanation is not opponents attesting anything. Matthew 28:12-13 is single source guards material, and Justin and Tertullian both had Matthew in front of them.

## Drop these

**The percentage.** Any version of "seventy five percent of scholars." Carrier's sampling point stands on its own merits without trusting Carrier. It is 75% of people who published on the empty tomb, which is a self selected group.

**Enemy attestation.** See above.

**The martyrdoms, mostly.** The traditions about how the apostles died are late, resting on the Acts of Peter, the Acts of Paul and later hagiography. The argument that nobody dies for a known lie is decent as a psychological point and thin as history. The answer to it is that they could be sincerely mistaken, and sincerity is all the martyrdom argument ever establishes. That answer is standard and much older than any current debater, so attribute it to nobody in particular rather than to whoever you last heard say it.

What survives the cut is in Paul's own hand, and it is better evidence than what it replaces. See below.

**The low status of women, in the form that leans on rabbinic sources.** Those citations are later than the events. The point survives without them, stated plainly.

## Keep these

**1 Corinthians 15:3-8.** The strongest thing in the whole case.

> "For what I received I passed on to you as of first importance: that Christ
> died for our sins according to the Scriptures, that he was buried, that he was
> raised on the third day according to the Scriptures, and that he appeared to
> Cephas, and then to the Twelve."
> — 1 Corinthians 15:3-5 (NIV)

Received and passed on are technical transmission language. Paul is quoting something older than the letter, and the letter is AD 53 to 55. Gerd Lüdemann, an atheist, dates the tradition to within two years of the crucifixion (tier K, page unverified). Paul names witnesses and says most of them are still alive as he writes.

**What the early date buys, and what it does not.** It rules out slow legendary accretion as the origin of the claim, because there is no interval for that to happen in. It does not settle what the claim was about.

Lüdemann supplies the dating and then concludes the appearances were visions. So the scholar cited for the date is himself a standing demonstration that the date alone does not decide the content. Anyone who runs from the two year window straight to a bodily resurrection has skipped the step the opponent is actually standing on, and will be told so.

**Paul's own hand, which is the part that survives after the martyrdoms go.** He reports persecuting the movement in three separate letters, Galatians 1:13, 1 Corinthians 15:9, Philippians 3:6. A shameful self report, written to people positioned to contradict it. And 2 Corinthians 11:23 to 27 is his own catalogue of the cost, imprisonments, thirty nine lashes five times, three beatings with rods, a stoning. First person and contemporary, which is exactly what the apostolic martyrdom traditions are not.

State the claim narrowly and it holds. The earliest named proclaimers accepted serious physical cost, and one of them documents it himself. That buys sincerity, and Galatians 1:16 says the revelation was "in me," which is where Lüdemann and Goulder live. What sincerity does and does not buy is worked out properly in [[Sincerity Is Not Truth]], including which of the martyrdoms are actually attested. Do not re-derive it here.

**James.** Better as a composite than as any single item. Family scepticism during the ministry (Mark 3:21, 3:31-35, John 7:5), an appearance to him in the earliest formula (1 Corinthians 15:7), leadership at Jerusalem attested by Paul who had no motive to elevate a rival (Galatians 1:19, 2:9), and his execution attested by a non Christian in Josephus, *Antiquities* 20.200, around AD 62. Sceptical brother becomes leader and dies. Josephus attests the death and not the motive, so do not stretch it further than that.

**The offence itself.** Deuteronomy 21:22-23 makes a hanged man cursed. A crucified messiah is not a story a first century Jew invents in order to win an argument. That is Paul's own point at 1 Corinthians 1:23.

**The women.** The tomb is found by women in a setting where their testimony carried less weight. Nobody building a persuasive account puts them there. Say it that way and you do not need a single rabbinic citation.

**The Jerusalem proclamation.** The claim was made first in the city where the tomb was, within the lifetime of everyone involved. The wrong city to start that in.

**Dale Allison, by name.** He grants the facts and rejects the methodology built on them. Naming your best critic yourself is the strongest move available here.

## Axis 2, unscored, and the steelman written out

Layer 2's round 2 has not been run, so nothing below is scored. But the eliminative structure of the whole argument depends on Axis 2, and a cost 4 there invalidates it regardless of how well Axis 1 goes. Which means the alternatives have to be written out in their strongest published form and not in the form an apologetics site summarises them in.

The named proponents are **Gerd Lüdemann** and **Michael Goulder** on visions, **Bart Ehrman** on legendary development, and **Dale Allison** on apparition experiences, which is the most serious version and the hardest to answer.

**Dead on arrival, so do not spend time here.** Conspiracy and fabrication both fail on Paul and James, and nearly nobody serious holds them. Swoon fails on the medicine. Wrong tomb, stolen body and twin all fail on Paul, whose experience has nothing to do with a tomb at all. If a defence of the case spends most of its effort on these while under treating the vision hypothesis, **that pattern is itself the finding**, and it is the same pattern Layer 1 caught, where the strongest opposing thesis got answered in its weakest form.

**The steelman, in one paragraph, written so I am not shadowboxing.** A charismatic apocalyptic prophet is executed in Jerusalem around AD 30. His inner circle, devastated and primed by Daniel and Isaiah 53 and a live expectation of imminent vindication, has one or more visionary experiences of the kind bereavement reliably produces. Peter first, then others, then group experiences amplified inside a small high cohesion sect. The grave is unmarked, or a common one, or simply unlocated within a generation. "Raised" begins as a claim about exaltation and vindication and hardens into physical detail as the tradition moves into a Greek setting where bare survival sounded like a ghost story. Paul, an outsider with his own pressures, has an independent visionary experience and reads it through categories the movement has already built. Forty years later, people who were not there write narratives.

That is coherent. It is not stupid and it is not bad faith. It pays at four joints, and those four joints are the entire case: the tomb, the group appearances, the enemy and the sceptic who were not grieving followers, and the speed of the corporeal claim given the Aramaic stratum. Make the other person show where their version pays, rather than asserting that it must.

## The ceiling, fixed in advance

Layer 2 can test whether the claimed facts are supported, whether the consensus claims are checkable, whether alternatives are fairly eliminated, and whether the case is circular.

It cannot test whether a miracle happened.

Once the facts are fixed, the inference is dominated by the prior on miracles, and no amount of source checking touches a prior. See [[Naturalism as a Presupposition — Not a Conclusion]].

The best possible outcome of this study is "the case is honestly built." Never "it happened."

Say that out loud before the other person works it out. It does more for you than the version that claims more.
