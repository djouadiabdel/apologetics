# WHO DECIDED THE BIBLE IS GOD'S WORD?
## A Full Dismantling of the "Men Chose the Canon" Argument

---

## THE ARGUMENT BEING MADE

The skeptic's position can be summarized in four claims:

1. **Paul invented divine inspiration** — he coined "God-breathed" in a personal letter and was talking only about the Old Testament
2. **Church councils voted on the canon** — meaning men decided what was God's word, not God
3. **The Bible is full of contradictions** — proving it cannot be a unified divine text
4. **Divine inspiration is a late, man-made doctrine** — only formalized in 1978

Each of these claims sounds devastating on the surface. Each of them collapses under scrutiny. This document dismantles all four — and then turns the argument back on the skeptic's own position.

---

# PART 1 — "PAUL INVENTED THE CONCEPT OF INSPIRATION"

## The Claim

> "Paul coined 'God-breathed' in a personal letter to Timothy. Timothy was Paul's disciple. Luke was Paul's disciple. 50% of the New Testament is Luke and Paul. None of them met Jesus. Paul was writing about the Old Testament, not the New Testament."

## The Dismantling

### WALL 1 — The Claim Proves Too Much

If Paul's use of "God-breathed" is invalid because it appears in a personal letter, then the skeptic has just destroyed their own ability to use any ancient text as evidence for anything.

Every ancient document we possess — including the ones the skeptic trusts for historical claims — were written by individuals with personal perspectives and agendas. Caesar's Gallic Wars is a personal military report. Josephus's Antiquities is a personal historical account. The Dead Sea Scrolls were copied by a religious community with theological commitments.

The skeptic's argument is: personal authorship = unreliable claim to authority.

Apply that consistently:
- The Quran was dictated through Muhammad's personal experience of Jibril — personal.
- The Bhagavad Gita was spoken by Krishna to Arjuna in a personal conversation — personal.
- Every wisdom text the skeptic respects (Deepak Chopra, Wayne Dyer) is a personal letter of belief.

If personal origin disqualifies a text from divine authority, everything the skeptic respects is equally disqualified. The skeptic is not applying a principle. They are applying a selective filter that only targets the Bible.

### WALL 2 — "God-Breathed" Describes Nature, Not Origin

2 Timothy 3:16 says:

> _"All Scripture is **God-breathed (θεόπνευστος — theopneustos)** and is useful for teaching, rebuking, correcting and training in righteousness."_

The skeptic says: Paul coined this term for the Old Testament only.

But the argument misfires in two directions:

**First:** The Old Testament being God-breathed is exactly what Jesus himself affirmed:

- Matthew 5:18: _"Until heaven and earth disappear, not the smallest letter, not the least stroke of a pen, will by any means disappear from the Law."_
- John 10:35: _"Scripture cannot be broken."_
- Luke 24:44: _"Everything must be fulfilled that is written about me in the Law of Moses, the Prophets and the Psalms."_

Jesus — who the skeptic claims to respect as a teacher of spiritual truth — treated the Old Testament as unbreakable, permanent, and authoritative down to its smallest letter. If Paul "coined" a concept that Jesus himself held before Paul wrote a single letter, Paul is not the inventor — he is the reporter.

**Second:** The New Testament authors recognized each other's writings as Scripture in real time:

- 2 Peter 3:15-16: Peter calls Paul's letters "scripture" alongside "the other Scriptures"
- 1 Timothy 5:18 quotes Luke 10:7 and calls it "scripture"

Peter and Paul were contemporaries. Peter — who actually walked with Jesus — read Paul's letters and called them scripture. This happened before any council. Before any vote. Before any institution decided anything. The recognition was organic and immediate.

### WALL 3 — Paul Never Said "These Are Just My Opinions"

The skeptic says Paul was "writing letters to local churches about their problems" — implying informal, personal, non-authoritative correspondence.

But Paul explicitly claims the opposite, repeatedly:

- Galatians 1:11-12: _"The gospel I preached is not of human origin. I did not receive it from any man, nor was I taught it; rather, I received it **by revelation from Jesus Christ.**"_
- 1 Thessalonians 2:13: _"You received the word of God which you heard from us, you accepted it not as a human word, but as it actually is, **the word of God.**"_
- 1 Corinthians 14:37: _"If anyone thinks they are a prophet or otherwise gifted by the Spirit, let them acknowledge that **what I am writing to you is the Lord's command.**"_

Paul explicitly and repeatedly claims divine authority for his writings. The skeptic is free to reject that claim — but they cannot accurately say Paul "never said this is divine revelation." He said it constantly.

# PART 2 — "CHURCH COUNCILS VOTED ON THE CANON"

## The Claim

> "A group of bishops voted on what was God's word. Not God. Not angels. People. They decided what was divinely inspired. If the Bible was divinely dictated, why did they need to vote on it?"

## This Is the Most Common Misunderstanding of How Canonization Works

### What Actually Happened at the Councils

The skeptic describes the Council of Carthage (397 AD) and the Nicene Council (325 AD) as if they were men sitting in a room deciding which books to make holy.

This is precisely backwards.

The councils did not **create** the canon. They **recognized and confirmed** what the churches had already been using for centuries.

**The distinction is everything:**
- A council that **creates** authority is inventing something new
- A council that **recognizes** existing authority is formalizing what already existed

When the Council of Carthage listed the 27 New Testament books in 397 AD, those 27 books had already been:
- Circulating in churches across the known world for 250-300 years
- Being read in Sunday worship since the late 1st century
- Quoted by church fathers in every century since the apostles
- Cross-referenced by every major Christian community from Rome to Alexandria to Antioch to Syria

The council did not give the books authority. The books had demonstrated their authority for centuries. The council **wrote down what was already universally recognized.**

### The Criteria Used — Not Arbitrary

The early church applied specific, rigorous tests before accepting any book:

**1. Apostolic origin:** Was the book written by an apostle or a close associate of an apostle?
**2. Universal reception:** Was it accepted and used by churches everywhere, not just one region?
**3. Doctrinal consistency:** Did it agree with the established teaching that went back to Jesus through the apostles?
**4. Transformative power:** Did it demonstrate the quality expected of divine text — life-changing, spiritually authoritative?

These are not subjective "feelings." These are historical and textual criteria. The books that failed these tests — the Gnostic gospels, the Gospel of Thomas, the Shepherd of Hermas — failed because:
- They were written in the 2nd-3rd century, long after the apostles were dead
- They were only used by specific regional or sectarian groups, not universally
- They contradicted the core apostolic teaching in verifiable ways

### The Gnostic Texts Were Not "Suppressed Alternatives"

The skeptic implies the councils suppressed equally valid Gnostic texts. This is historically false.

The Gospel of Thomas was written c. 140-180 AD — 100+ years after Jesus.
The Gospel of Philip was written c. 150-250 AD.
The Gospel of Judas was written c. 130-180 AD.

The four canonical Gospels:
- Matthew: c. 50-70 AD
- Mark: c. 45-65 AD
- Luke: c. 60-80 AD
- John: c. 85-95 AD

The canonical Gospels are 50-150 years closer to the events than the Gnostic texts. They are not competing with equals. They are being compared to documents written by people who never met any eyewitness to Jesus, composing theological fiction 150 years after the crucifixion.

The councils did not suppress the Gnostic gospels. They noted they were too late, too regional, and too inconsistent with everything attested closer to the source. This is basic historical reasoning — not power politics.

### Why Did They Need to Vote If It Was Already Clear?

The skeptic asks: "If the Bible was divinely dictated, why did they need to vote?"

Because the vote was not about divinity. It was about ending regional disagreements about edge cases.

The core 20 New Testament books — the four Gospels, Acts, Paul's major letters, 1 Peter, 1 John — were never seriously disputed. Their acceptance was universal from the beginning.

The debate was about 7 peripheral books: Hebrews, James, 2 Peter, 2-3 John, Jude, Revelation. Churches in different regions had different levels of familiarity with these. The council's role was to resolve these edge cases based on the established criteria — not to decide whether Matthew or John were scripture. Nobody disputed those.

**Analogy:** A medical association publishes an official list of approved medications. They didn't invent the drugs. The drugs had been tested and used for years. The list formalizes what practitioners already knew. The council didn't make the Gospels holy. The Gospels had been transforming lives for three centuries before the council picked up a pen.

# PART 3 — "THE BIBLE IS FULL OF CONTRADICTIONS"

## The Claim

> "There are two versions of how Judas died. Three versions of the walking on water story. 190+ contradictions in the New Testament. God is love and God is jealous. The texts cannot be trusted."

## The Dismantling

### WALL 1 — Differences in Accounts Are Not Contradictions

The two accounts of Judas' death:

**Matthew 27:5:** _"So Judas threw the money into the temple and left. Then he went away and hanged himself."_
**Acts 1:18:** _"He bought a field; there he fell headlong, his body burst open and all his intestines spilled out."_

The skeptic says: two different stories, contradiction, the Bible cannot be trusted.

But notice what is actually happening:

Matthew describes the cause of death: **hanging.**
Acts describes the result: **his body fell and burst open.**

These are not contradictions. They are two different details of the same event — exactly what you get from two independent witnesses describing different aspects of the same occurrence.

If someone hangs themselves and the rope breaks or the body is later cut down and falls, the body bursting open is the physical consequence. Matthew reports the act. Acts reports the aftermath. A detective examining two witness statements that describe different aspects of the same event does not conclude "contradiction." They conclude "complementary testimony."

The skeptic has read 190 "contradictions" using this same method: finding any two accounts that don't describe exactly the same details and calling them contradictions. But this is not how textual analysis works. This is how someone who has decided in advance to find contradictions reads a text.

### WALL 2 — "God is Love" and "God is Jealous" Are Not Contradictions

A loving parent who disciplines their child is not contradicting themselves. A judge who loves justice and is angry at injustice is not contradicting themselves. Love without standards is not love — it is indifference.

The God of the Bible is:
- Love (1 John 4:8) — his fundamental nature
- Jealous for the allegiance of those he loves (Exodus 20:5) — because he is in a covenant relationship, not indifferent
- Vengeful toward injustice (Romans 12:19) — because love without justice allows evil to destroy the loved

These are not contradictions in any coherent philosophical framework. They are the attributes of a being who is completely love precisely because he takes evil, injustice, and betrayal seriously. A God who loves everyone and is utterly indifferent to genocide, abuse, and cruelty would not be loving — he would be callous.

### WALL 3 — Multiple Versions of One Event Prove Authenticity

The skeptic says "three versions of the walking on water story" prove the Bible is unreliable.

The opposite is true.

If all four Gospels described every event in exactly identical language, word for word, the conclusion would be: they copied from one source. This would suggest a single author controlling the narrative — not four independent witnesses.

The fact that the Gospels describe the same events with different details, different emphases, and from different perspectives is exactly what historians expect from **independent eyewitness testimony.** Police investigators are suspicious when witnesses give perfectly identical accounts — it suggests coordination. Independent accounts with complementary variations suggest genuine independent observation.

The four Gospels are:
- Written by different authors
- For different audiences (Matthew for Jews, Mark for Romans, Luke for Greeks, John for the broader church)
- From different perspectives (two apostles, one physician-historian, one who knew Peter's account)
- Agreeing on all core facts: Jesus' ministry, crucifixion, empty tomb, resurrection appearances

No ancient historian looks at four independent accounts agreeing on all essential facts and concludes "unreliable." They conclude: well-attested.

# PART 4 — "BIBLICAL INERRANCY IS A MODERN INVENTION FROM 1978"

## The Claim

> "The Chicago Statement on Biblical Inerrancy (1978) is where inerrancy was formalized. It didn't exist in the early church. It's a modern creation."

## The Dismantling

### WALL 1 — The Early Church Fathers Stated Inerrancy Explicitly

The skeptic cites Augustine saying "the most disastrous consequences must follow upon our believing that anything false is found in the sacred books" — and calls it "just a letter."

But the position is not Augustine's personal opinion. It is the position of virtually every major church father:

- **Origen (185-254 AD):** _"For my part, I believe that not one jot or tittle of the divine scriptures is without meaning."_
- **Clement of Alexandria (150-215 AD):** _"The Scriptures are the utterances of the Holy Spirit."_
- **Jerome (347-420 AD):** _"I believe that the gospel is the body of Christ... whatever is asserted without the support of scripture is not to be believed."_
- **Chrysostom (347-407 AD):** _"Not a syllable of what is contained in the sacred scriptures can be passed by."_
- **Augustine (354-430 AD) — in his letter to Jerome:** _"Only to those books which are called canonical have I learned to defer this honor so that I believe most firmly that no author of these books made any error."_

These are not isolated opinions. This is the **consensus position of the entire patristic tradition.** Augustine's "letter to Jerome" is the clearest statement of the patristic consensus — not a personal quirk.

The Chicago Statement of 1978 did not invent inerrancy. It codified a doctrine that had been held from the beginning against a specific modern challenge: the rise of higher criticism and liberal theology in the 19th-20th centuries. Codifying something under pressure is not the same as inventing it.

**Analogy:** The Council of Nicaea in 325 AD formally codified the doctrine of the Trinity. The Trinity was not invented in 325 AD. It had been taught from the apostles. The formal statement was a response to Arian heresy — not a new creation.

If the criteria that disqualify the Bible from divine authority also disqualify every other text the skeptic respects, then the skeptic has no external authority at all — only their own feelings.

This is not a philosophical argument. It is a circular preference.

# PART 7 — THE TRANSMISSION ARGUMENT: THE BIBLE IS THE MOST VERIFIED TEXT IN HISTORY

The skeptic implies the Bible's transmission is unreliable. The opposite is true by any historical standard.

| Text | Number of Manuscripts | Earliest Manuscript | Time Gap from Original |
|---|---|---|---|
| Homer's Iliad | ~1,800 | 400 BC | ~400 years |
| Caesar's Gallic Wars | ~250 | 900 AD | ~1,000 years |
| Plato's writings | ~210 | 895 AD | ~1,200 years |
| **New Testament** | **5,800+ Greek** / **24,000+ total** | **c. 125 AD** | **25-70 years** |

The New Testament has **24,000+ manuscript copies** — more than any other ancient text by a factor of ten. The earliest fragments (Papyrus P52, John's Gospel) date to within 25-30 years of the original writing.

No secular historian doubts Julius Caesar existed based on 250 manuscripts with a 1,000-year gap. No scholar doubts Homer based on 1,800 manuscripts. The New Testament's transmission record is not a weakness. It is the strongest manuscript tradition of any ancient document in existence.

**Textual scholars confirm:** The variations between manuscripts are overwhelmingly minor (spelling differences, word order). No core Christian doctrine rests on a disputed textual variant. The text we have is what was originally written to a degree of confidence unmatched by any other ancient text.
