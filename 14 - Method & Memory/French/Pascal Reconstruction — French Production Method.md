# 🇫🇷 Pascal Reconstruction — French Production Method

> [!abstract] The design, in one line
> Read a Pascal fragment, close the book, **rebuild it in French from memory of the meaning**, then let the original mark me.
> Every place I said the same thing with clumsier machinery is a delta.
> **Steal the joints, not the content.**

---

## 0. The goal, stated honestly

Not "improve my French." The goal is:

> **Make my own arguments, in French, out loud, unrehearsed.**

Underneath that:

- Say what I actually think about God, desire, money and the divided will, in French, to a person, without preparing it.
- Read hard French theology without hitting a wall.
- The material has to land as **confession, not display.** "Pascal says man's misery comes from X" is a man performing his reading, and it makes the other person an audience. "I chase things I don't actually want, because I can't stand being alone in a room" is the same content and does the opposite. Learn the fragments in French for the French. Let them come out of my mouth as something true about me.

---

## 1. The diagnosis

**Medical French lies about where I am.** It is easy for three reasons that all disappear at once in a real book:

1. Vocabulary is Latinate and transparent. *Dyspnée, épanchement, insuffisance* cost nothing.
2. Syntax is flat. Present tense, declarative, no aspect games, no register shifts.
3. Enormous domain schema, so I am **predicting content, not parsing it.** Recognition, not reading.

I recognize three to five times more French than I can produce, and **reading only grows the recognition side.** Writing is the only thing that moves a word across that line.

So the bottleneck is not vocabulary, and not the passé simple (I have that already, see [[Mensonge Romantique Words]]). It is **production of the connective machinery.** The joints. Same conclusion the psalm delta log reached: nearly every error is at a joint.

### Why Pascal and not a novel

| | Novel (Plath) | Pascal |
|---|---|---|
| Vocabulary | furniture, clothes, food, gestures — the exact hole medicine left | abstract, Latinate — **my strong zone transfers** |
| Tense | passé simple + imparfait, aspect everywhere | **almost entirely present**, gnomic |
| Length | chapters | **fragments**, holdable in working memory |
| Schema | none | full, I already think about this |
| Register | irony, free indirect discourse | argument, which is what I want to produce |

Pascal is pre-cut for this method. A treatise would be useless.

> [!danger] Skip the Wager
> It is the one fragment everybody knows and the weakest thing in the apologetic tradition. Many-gods problem, you cannot choose to believe by deciding to, and the faith it produces is mercenary. Lead with it and lose.
>
> **Arguments get dismissed. Diagnoses don't.** Divertissement is not an argument for God, it is an observation about why men hunt and gamble and chase promotions, and it works whether or not the listener believes anything. Same class of weapon as Girard, which is why Girard works on me.

## 2. The rules

> [!warning] The source text is the answer key. Claude is not.
> Being handed the correct sentence is not learning. I struggle, I produce, **the original French marks me**, and only then does anyone explain anything.

- **Order, not absence.** Producing a wrong answer and then seeing the right one beats studying the right one, and it also beats struggling with no feedback at all. Struggle with no feedback just fossilizes the error and I get fluent at saying it wrong. So the feedback comes, it just comes second, and it comes from the book.
- **Claude triages, never writes the sentence.** After I compare I will have fifteen differences and no idea which matter. Sorting noise from curriculum is a real job that doesn't spoil the struggle. Writing the sentence for me does.
- **The verb list is not given to me.** It gets built out of my own failures, in the order my mouth actually needs them.
- **Five passes is a ceiling, not a quota.** Producing it cleanly by pass three means move on. Grinding a fragment I already own is theatre.

## 3. The loop — one fragment, five passes

Each pass is a **different exercise**, not the same one five times. Retrieval distance grows.

| # | Pass | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Cold baseline.** Read twice, look things up, close the book, write it in French. | Fails badly. That failure is the measurement. |
| 2 | **Next day, cold again.** | Now I am fighting *my own version from yesterday*, which has already hardened. This is where my defaults become visible, because I reach for the same clumsy joint twice and watch myself do it. |
| 3 | **Transfer.** No reconstruction. Take one harvested move and write my own paragraph on my own subject. | The step that makes it mine. Without it the phrase stays Pascal's property forever. |
| 4 | **Longer distance.** Reconstruct from a one-line summary only, not from memory of the text. | More retrieval, less recognition. |
| 5 | **Out loud, cold, before writing.** Say it, then write it. | Speaking exposes what writing lets me stall on. If it comes out of my mouth clean, it is mine. |

### The two piles

After every comparison, sort the differences:

- **Pile A — gold.** Places where I said the same thing with clumsier machinery. This is the entire curriculum.
- **Pile B — comprehension.** Places where I got the meaning wrong. Matters much less. Fix and move on.

## 4. The harvest filter

> [!tip] Not "I don't know this word." **"I know this word and I would never have written it."**

A French book hands over 200+ unknown verbs and most are literary junk (*se rembrunir, chevroter, s'esclaffer*). Writing with those makes me sound like a translated 19th-century narrator.

The words that matter are the mid-frequency workhorses I understand instantly and never reach for: *aborder, entamer, relever de, tenir à, s'agir de, revenir à, découler, susciter, rendre compte de, porter sur, aboutir à*. That set is small. Maybe 150 verbs in the whole language.

Ten per session, maximum. Ten a day beats a hundred I never touch.

## 5. The month — four fragments, one argument

They are not four separate things. They are **one argument in four moves**, so by week four I can reconstruct the whole chain.

Find them by opening words. Every edition renumbers. My copy is **GF Flammarion** (French original, modernized spelling, notes).

Irrelevant here since I am mining fragments, not reconstructing his plan. But **the liasse titles do not exist in my copy.** Numbers and opening words only.

### Week 1 — Divertissement, the diagnosis
> *"Quand je m'y suis mis quelquefois à considérer les diverses agitations des hommes, et les périls et les peines où ils s'exposent..."*

**Brunschvicg 139.** Contains the short one too: *"Tout le malheur des hommes vient d'une seule chose, qui est de ne savoir pas demeurer en repos dans une chambre."*

**Machinery:** stacking subordinate clauses under one observation, and `d'où vient que`.
**Why it's mine:** this is the rich-but-not-rich problem, diagnosed four hundred years early.

### Week 2 — The hare
> *"Ils ne savent pas que ce n'est que la chasse, et non pas la prise, qu'ils recherchent."*

**Inside Br. 139 itself**, further down (check 140-143 if my edition breaks it out). The sharpest single move he makes, and the answer to the puzzle week 1 sets up. The hunter does not want the hare. Offer him the hare and he refuses it.

**Machinery:** `ce n'est que X, et non pas Y` and the whole family of French negation-restriction structures. I produce none of these now.

### Week 3 — Amour-propre
> *"La nature de l'amour-propre et de ce moi humain est de n'aimer que soi et de ne considérer que soi."*

**Brunschvicg 100**, Section II, same neighbourhood as 139. Runs on into preferring to be *thought* good over being good, and hating whoever tells us the truth about ourselves.

**Machinery:** definition followed by consequence, `de là vient que`, heavy reflexives.
**Why it's mine:** this is the Girard bridge. Proto-mimetic, and he knew it.

### Week 4 — Ni ange ni bête
> *"L'homme n'est ni ange ni bête, et le malheur veut que qui veut faire l'ange fait la bête."*

**Brunschvicg 358**, Section VI *Les philosophes*. Short. Pair with *le roseau pensant* (**Br. 347**, same section) if the week feels thin.

**Machinery:** paradox and chiasmus, and `qui veut X fait Y`, a relative pronoun standing as bare subject. **Exactly the joint class the delta log says I break.**
**Why it's mine:** the sexual struggle without a single word of moralizing, which is the only way that subject is survivable.

### The arc

> Why I can't sit still → what I'm actually chasing → why I only love myself → what kind of creature that makes me.

Money, Girard, sex. All four weeks in the present tense.

### Capstone

End of month, one sitting: **write the whole argument in French, in my own words, about my own life.** Not a reconstruction. My version of the chain, using his machinery.

That is the thing I would actually be able to say out loud to someone.

## 7. Correction to the psalm drill

Reconstruction from nothing is impossible, not brave.

The drill only works when I **hold the sense and have lost the form.** I should be able to say what the psalm says, freely, in English or Arabic, *before* writing a word of French. Then I rebuild the French around meaning I already own. What is missing is the wording, never the content.

Order: **cold reconstruction first**, nothing in front of me. English only as a rescue when genuinely stuck, never as the starting input, or it becomes translation. The French original comes out last, and only to mark me.

**Only run it on psalms I already have.** If I don't know what it says, that is a first reading, which is a different day's work.

One a day for known psalms. Maintenance and delta harvest in the same twenty minutes.
