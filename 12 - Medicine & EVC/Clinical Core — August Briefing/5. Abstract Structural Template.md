_French humanities-track psychiatric abstracts follow a tight rhetorical structure. This section gives you the structure, the rhetorical job each section performs, the verb families and stock phrases to draw from, and the common failure modes. In August, pour content into this skeleton rather than inventing structure from scratch. Before drafting, re-read 3-5 of the model abstracts saved from Encéphale 2024-2026 to calibrate register._

---

**Total target: 400 words.** Some venues allow 350 or 500 — verify the exact limit on the portal before drafting and adjust proportionally. The Résultats section absorbs or releases words first; Introduction and Conclusion stay roughly fixed.

---

#### Introduction / Contexte (~80 words)

**Rhetorical job:** Establish a conceptual tension or lacuna in current psychiatric thinking that the paper will address. Not a history lesson, not a definition dump — a _problem_. The reader should finish this section thinking "yes, there is a real question here." If the reader finishes thinking "this is interesting background," the section has failed.

**Structure (3 moves):**

1. _The accepted position_ — one sentence stating what contemporary psychiatry takes for granted about the phenomenon in question.
2. _The tension_ — one or two sentences identifying where this accepted position runs into difficulty, or where a phenomenon escapes the standard framing.
3. _The hinge_ — one sentence announcing that a Girardian relecture offers a way into this tension. Do _not_ explain Girard here. Just name him and the concept.

**Stock opening phrases** (adapt, don't copy):

- « La psychiatrie contemporaine tend à concevoir [X] comme... »
- « Le modèle clinique dominant de [X] repose sur l'hypothèse d'un sujet... »
- « Malgré les progrès des approches [cognitivo-comportementales / neurobiologiques / systémiques] dans la compréhension de [X], certaines configurations cliniques résistent à... »

**Stock hinge phrases:**

- « Nous proposons d'interroger cette configuration à la lumière de la théorie mimétique de René Girard. »
- « Le présent travail met en regard [phénomène clinique] et le concept girardien de [X]. »
- « Une relecture girardienne de [X] permet, nous semble-t-il, de déplacer les termes du problème. »

**Failure modes to avoid:**

- Defining Girard's concepts in the introduction. Save that for Méthode or Résultats.
- Historical throat-clearing ("Depuis Freud, la question de..."). French reviewers tolerate this less than anglophone ones think.
- Announcing conclusions. The introduction poses the question; it doesn't answer it.

---

#### Objectif (~40 words)

**Rhetorical job:** State in one or two sentences exactly what the paper does. This is the section where the reviewer decides whether your scope matches the track. Too ambitious and it reads as undergraduate overreach; too narrow and it reads as trivial.

**Verb family (pick one, be exact):**

- _Proposer_ — the most common, signals a conceptual contribution. « Cet article propose de... »
- _Relire_ — signals a re-reading of existing material through a new lens. « Nous proposons une relecture... »
- _Interroger_ — signals critical examination without committing to a positive thesis. Safer. « Ce travail interroge... »
- _Mettre en regard_ — signals a comparative/analogical move. « Nous mettons en regard X et Y afin de... »
- _Éclairer_ — signals that the paper uses one framework to illuminate another. « La théorie mimétique permet d'éclairer... »

**Stock structures:**

- « Cet article propose de [verbe] [phénomène clinique] à la lumière du concept girardien de [X]. »
- « Nous proposons une relecture de [X] à partir de la notion de [concept girardien], afin de [effet recherché]. »
- « Ce travail met en regard [entité clinique francophone] et [mécanisme girardien] pour interroger [question précise]. »

**Failure modes:**

- Using English-flavored verbs like _explorer_ (too vague in French academic register) or _analyser_ (too generic — say what kind of analysis).
- Claiming the paper will "prouver" or "démontrer" something. Humanities-track abstracts propose and interrogate; they don't prove.
- Adding a second sentence that restates the first. One sentence is often enough here.

#### Méthode (~80 words)

**Rhetorical job:** Signal that you know this is a research object, not a personal essay. This is the section non-French applicants most often skip, and it's the tell that distinguishes an abstract that gets read from one that gets desk-rejected. Even a purely conceptual paper has a method — name it.

**Method types available to you:**

- _Analyse conceptuelle_ — for papers that work at the level of concepts and their articulation. Most Girardian work lives here.
- _Relecture critique_ — for papers that re-read a clinical tradition or a corpus through a new framework.
- _Mise en regard_ — for explicit comparative work between two frameworks.
- _Étude de cas tirée de la littérature_ — for papers anchored in published clinical case reports (name the sources).
- _Analyse historique_ / _approche épistémologique_ — for papers in the "Histoire et épistémologie" track specifically.
- _Herméneutique croisée_ — higher register, signals a sophisticated conceptual move between two interpretive traditions.

**Required elements in this section:**

1. Name the method explicitly (one of the above or a defensible variant).
2. Name the corpus you're working from — Girard's texts (specify which: _Mensonge romantique et vérité romanesque_, _La Violence et le sacré_, _Je vois Satan tomber comme l'éclair_) and the psychiatric texts or cases you're reading them against.
3. State the comparative or analytical procedure in one sentence.

**Stock phrases:**

- « Notre démarche relève de l'analyse conceptuelle. Nous confrontons [corpus girardien] à [corpus clinique], en privilégiant une lecture centrée sur [axe précis]. »
- « Ce travail s'inscrit dans une approche épistémologique. À partir d'une relecture de [texte de Girard], nous interrogeons les présupposés anthropologiques de [cadre clinique]. »
- « La méthode retenue est celle de la mise en regard critique. Nous confrontons le concept de [X] tel que développé par Girard dans [œuvre] à [entité clinique] telle que formalisée dans la tradition [francophone / CIM-11]. »

**Failure modes:**

- Skipping this section entirely. Fatal.
- Being vague ("une approche pluridisciplinaire," "une réflexion critique"). Name the method.
- Listing too many corpora. Two sources on each side is plenty for 400 words.

#### Résultats (~150 words)

**Rhetorical job:** Deliver the actual claims. This is where the Clinical Core lives. The reader should finish this section with a clear sense of what the paper _establishes_ — not what it "explores" or "discusses." Humanities-track Résultats sections in psychiatric abstracts are closer to a philosophy paper's argument sketch than to a scientific paper's data report, but they retain the expectation of discrete, enumerable claims.

**Structure (3 claims, sequenced):**

1. _The structural claim_ — what homology, analogy, or critique you establish. One or two sentences. This is where you name the Type (a/b/c/d) of correspondence from the Clinical Core extraction, though you don't use that terminology in the abstract itself.
2. _The mechanism claim_ — how the two mechanisms actually recoup each other. Two or three sentences. This is the intellectual heart of the abstract. Use the nominal academic register from the Clinical Core "Mécanisme" field.
3. _The clinical consequence claim_ — what this changes for how the phenomenon is understood or approached. One or two sentences. Modest is better than sweeping.

**Sequencing cues (use these to make the structure visible to the reviewer):**

- « En premier lieu... / En second lieu... / Enfin... »
- « Nous établissons d'abord que... Nous montrons ensuite... Il en découle... »
- « Trois résultats se dégagent : ... ; ... ; ... »

**Stock phrases for claims:**

- « Nous établissons que le mécanisme de [X girardien] présente une homologie structurale avec [mécanisme clinique], dans la mesure où... »
- « Cette mise en regard révèle que [entité clinique] peut être relue comme... »
- « Il en découle une conception élargie de [phénomène clinique], qui... »
- « Cette relecture suggère que [cadre clinique standard] laisse dans l'ombre [dimension que Girard éclaire]. »

**Failure modes:**

- Writing flowing prose instead of discrete claims. Reviewers skim; they need the claims to be structurally visible.
- Hedging every claim ("il semblerait que," "on pourrait envisager"). One or two hedges are fine; six is a tell.
- Introducing new concepts here that weren't named in Méthode. If a concept appears in Résultats, it should have been set up earlier.
- Running past the word budget. 150 words is tight. Every sentence has to earn its place.

#### Conclusion / Discussion (~50 words)

**Rhetorical job:** State what the paper changes for clinical thinking and gesture modestly toward further work. This is the section where young authors most often overreach. Reviewers are allergic to grand claims in conclusions — the safer the closing, the more seriously the reviewer takes what came before.

**Structure (2 moves):**

1. _The implication_ — one or two sentences on what this relecture offers to clinical thinking. Modest register.
2. _The opening_ — one sentence gesturing toward further work, a clinical question to be taken up, or a limitation to be explored.

**Stock phrases:**

- « Cette relecture girardienne ne prétend pas se substituer aux cadres cliniques existants, mais suggère que... »
- « L'apport de cette mise en regard réside moins dans une reformulation diagnostique que dans un déplacement du regard clinique vers... »
- « Des recherches ultérieures pourraient explorer les implications de cette homologie pour [question clinique précise]. »
- « Cette approche invite à interroger [présupposé du cadre standard], question qui mériterait un développement clinique plus approfondi. »

**Failure modes:**

- Overclaiming. "Cette approche révolutionne la compréhension de..." is desk-reject material.
- Repeating the Résultats in different words. The conclusion adds something; it doesn't recap.
- Vague gestures toward "further research" without naming a question. Name the question.

---

#### The sentence that ties the whole abstract together

One rhetorical move that distinguishes a strong humanities-track abstract from a competent one: somewhere in the Résultats or the transition into Conclusion, there is usually a single sentence that names, crisply, _what the paper changes_. Not what it proposes, not what it interrogates — what it _changes_. A reviewer scanning fifty abstracts pauses on the ones where that sentence is visible.

Examples of the form:

- « Ce déplacement conceptuel permet de reconnaître, dans [phénomène clinique], une dimension [mimétique / rivalitaire / sacrificielle] que le cadre standard laisse impensée. »
- « L'intérêt de cette relecture tient à ce qu'elle restitue à [phénomène clinique] sa dimension intersubjective, que l'individualisme méthodologique dominant occulte. »

Identify this sentence before drafting. If you can't articulate in one sentence what the paper changes, the abstract isn't ready to write — go back to the Clinical Core extraction and pick a different Core.

#### Pre-submission checklist for the drafted abstract

Before the polish pass, verify:

- [ ]  Introduction poses a real tension, not background.
- [ ]  Objectif uses one of the five verb families and names Girard explicitly.
- [ ]  Méthode section is present and names both method and corpus.
- [ ]  Résultats contains three structurally visible claims.
- [ ]  The "what the paper changes" sentence is identifiable.
- [ ]  Conclusion is modest and names a specific further question.
- [ ]  Word count is within the venue's limit.
- [ ]  Register is nominal, impersonal, academic — no « je pense », no conversational connectors.
- [ ]  No English-flavored verbs or calques.
- [ ]  Entités cliniques nommées selon l'usage francophone, non selon la traduction littérale de l'anglais.
- [ ]  Track selected on the portal matches the paper's actual character (humanities/épistémologie, not clinical).
