**Step 1 — "Both bear the title Messiah."** Establish the bare fact first, before any inference: in Islam, Jesus is _al-Masīḥ_ and the Antichrist is _al-Masīḥ ad-Dajjāl_. Don't argue yet — just plant that the deceiver is named after the office. If he won't grant the shared title, the whole argument stalls, so secure it here. (Reinforce with the root د-ج-ل = gilding/coating a fake to mimic something precious — but only if he resists; otherwise hold it for later.)

**Step 2 — "A counterfeit is parasitic on a real original."** Establish the principle in the neutral case (the forged bill) _before_ applying it to the Dajjal, so he agrees to it on its own terms and can't later reject it as special pleading. What must stick: a forgery borrows the silhouette of the genuine article; the fake's power to deceive _depends on_ the real one existing. Get explicit agreement to this as a general truth.

**Step 3 — "So ask: what is the Dajjal counterfeiting?"** This is the pivot question, not a claim. Establish only that the question is legitimate — that if the Dajjal is a counterfeit Messiah, it's fair to ask what genuine thing his fake is cut from. Make _him_ feel the question needs an answer.

**Step 4 — "His signature crime is claiming divinity."** Establish from the hadith that the Dajjal's defining deception is claiming to be the lord and demanding worship (false signs, counterfeit paradise/fire). What must stick: the central lie is _divinity_, not generic wickedness, false prophecy, or false kingship. This is the fact that makes the fork in Step 5 bite. Have the textual anchor ready.

**Step 5 — "Distinguish two kinds of lie: usurpation vs. category."** This is the hinge; if it doesn't land, nothing downstream works. Establish both definitions cleanly:

- _Usurpation lie_ — "the real thing exists, and **I** am it" (concedes the category, steals the identity).
- _Category lie_ — "the real thing doesn't exist at all." Then establish the key consequence: **a counterfeit can only ever tell the usurpation lie.** A forger needs real bills to exist — that's what gives his fake its shape. No counterfeit tells the category lie. So the Dajjal's lie is necessarily "a divine Messiah is real and I am he." Lock this before proceeding.

**Step 6 — "Force the fork: the thing usurped is either real or not real."** Establish that these are the only two options once usurpation is granted, and walk both:

- _Real_ → the true Messiah genuinely bears divine messiahship → the Christian claim.
- _Not real_ → the central deception (divinity) and the shared title are counterfeits of _nothing_ → both go unexplained and arbitrary. What must stick under the "not real" branch: name the failure precisely — it's not a gap to be filled later, it's an _arbitrary coincidence the doctrine is forced to swallow_ (why _that_ title, why divinity specifically?).

**Step 7 — "Guard the third door."** Anticipate the escape: _"He usurps a real thing, but the real thing is just the category of false divine-claimants."_ Establish the close: a counterfeit mimics something _genuine and valuable_, never other fakes — forging a forgery is pointless, it has no value to borrow. So "what he usurps" must have genuine divine-messianic status worth stealing, and the only candidate is a _true_ divine Messiah. The third door empties back into "real."

**Step 8 — "Expect the flip, and name what it costs."** The main counter-move: _"The divine-claiming messiah just IS the Antichrist — so your Christ is the Dajjal."_ Don't refute it head-on; establish that to make it work he must convert the Dajjal from a _person at the end of time_ into a _timeless principle_. That conversion is what generates the costs in Step 9. Make him commit to the flip in his own words first.

**Step 9 — "Lay the three costs down one at a time."** Each is independently established; don't dump them together. Make him answer each before adding the next:

- _Cost 1 — contradicts his own definition._ Islam's Dajjal is a specific end-time person; the flip makes him a timeless principle. Definition abandoned.
- _Cost 2 — breaks the timeline._ The same move needs "comes at the end of time" and "has always been true everywhere" to both hold of one thing.
- _Cost 3 — makes Allah the deceiver._ Qur'an 4:157 (_shubbiha lahum_) says the crucifixion was a divinely staged _appearance_. If the crucified divine Messiah is the great lie and Allah staged its appearance, Allah authored the deception. Incoherent for a Muslim.

**Step 10 — "Independently, the outcome doesn't fit a deception."** This stands on its own even if he survives everything above, so keep it in reserve as a separate strike. Establish: deceptions flatter — they produce thrones, winners, vindication. A _crucified_ messiah is a _disproven_ messiah (Deut. 21:23, "cursed is everyone who hangs on a tree"). "Worship the executed cursed one" is the worst possible result for a messianic claim, the opposite of what any successful deception would produce. That it happened anyway is evidence _against_ the deception reading.

**Step 11 — "Return to the title as the fossil."** The closing move you keep coming back to whenever he wriggles. Establish that of everything, the _shared name_ is the least dissolvable fact. He can reinterpret the divinity claim; he can reframe the timeline. He cannot easily explain why tradition _kept calling the deceiver "Messiah"_ rather than false-_nabī_ or false-_malik_. The name is a fossil of the original structure — the vocabulary itself carries the trace of a divine Messiah the doctrine elsewhere denies. A gilded Messiah implies a golden one.

---

**Governing rule over all steps:** establish the _general principle_ (Steps 2, 5) on neutral ground before applying it to Islam, so he agrees to the machinery before he sees where it points. Lead with questions, let him choose the flip himself, and never lay multiple costs simultaneously — the pile-up is devastating when he builds it under your questioning and just noise when you announce it.
