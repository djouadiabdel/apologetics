## The Allah Praying Argument — Salah

The Christian raised this and the Muslim gave a technical linguistic response without fully engaging it.

Here is the full argument:

Surah 33:56:

**إن الله وملائكته يصلون على النبي**

"Indeed Allah and His angels **perform salah** upon the Prophet."

The word **يصلون** — they perform salah — is the same root as **صلاة** — prayer. The same word used for human prayer to God.

Muslim translators translate it as "send blessings" for Allah and "pray" for humans to avoid the obvious problem — if salah means prayer when humans do it, why does Allah perform it?

The Muslim in the debate said the preposition after the verb changes the meaning. This is partially true — the preposition **على** — upon — indicates something directed toward someone rather than upward to God.

But here's what this reveals:

**Allah performs a communicative act directed at a human being — Muhammad — that uses the same root as human worship.**

Islamic theology says Allah is utterly transcendent — completely other — no act of His resembles human acts. Yet here Allah is described using the same vocabulary as human worship directed toward a prophet.

This is an internal tension in Islamic theology that the Muslim in the debate didn't resolve — he just explained the linguistics without addressing the theological problem of a transcendent God performing worship-adjacent acts toward a human.

Compare this to Christianity — where the Trinity involves internal relations between Father, Son, and Spirit — which makes the divine communicative acts in the Trinity internally coherent. A God who is relational in His own being can perform relational acts. A God who is absolutely singular and utterly transcendent — cannot pray to himself or toward a prophet without creating a category problem.
