### 1. “Muhammad was illiterate, so he couldn’t have colluded or copied from the Bible/Torah”

**Argument**: An illiterate man in 7th-century Arabia couldn’t have produced something as sophisticated as the Quran without divine help. He must have received it from God.

**Why it’s weak**:

- Being illiterate doesn’t prevent someone from **memorizing** stories or having literate people help him. The transcript itself admits he had **scribes** who wrote things down.
- The Quran contains many biblical stories (Joseph, Moses, Abraham, etc.) but with **clear errors** (e.g., Mary being part of the Trinity in Surah 5:116, or confusing Miriam with Mary). This is exactly what you’d expect if someone was **hearing** these stories second-hand rather than receiving them from God.
- Joseph Smith was also claimed to be poorly educated, yet produced the Book of Mormon. Muslims reject that as proof of divine origin. So this standard is applied inconsistently.

---

### 2. “His life was very public — collusion would have been noticed”

**Argument**: Everything Muhammad did was public. If he was colluding with someone, people would have seen letters, visitors, drafts, etc. There are no such witnesses.

**Why it’s weak**:

- This completely misses the **real** accusation. The strongest parallel with Joseph Smith is **not** about collusion or copying text. It’s about the **circular prophecy claim**:
    
    - Muhammad claims Jesus foretold him (Surah 61:6).
    - But there is **zero independent evidence** of this prophecy anywhere before the Quran.
    - The only source is Muhammad himself.
    
    This is the same problem Joseph Smith had with Genesis 50:33. Public life doesn’t solve this.
    
- The “no letters, no drafts” point is also weak because the Quran **was** written down by scribes during his lifetime (as the transcript admits). The standardization happened later under Caliph Uthman, who burned variant copies — which actually shows there **were** differences.
    

---

### 3. “No editing, no drafts, perfect consistency for 23 years”

**Argument**: The Quran was revealed perfectly with no revisions, even during times of war and stress. This proves it’s divine.

**Why it’s weak**:

- This is simply **not true**. There were variant readings of the Quran. Uthman standardized one version and ordered others to be burned. Early Islamic sources admit this.
- Many parts of the Quran were revealed in response to **specific personal situations** (e.g., Surah 33:53 about house guests and not marrying Muhammad’s widows). This looks exactly like a man making up rules as he goes, not eternal divine guidance.
- Joseph Smith also claimed his revelations were perfect and from God, yet he revised many of them. Muslims reject that. Again, inconsistent standard.

---

### 4. “Physical effects of revelation prove it was real” (sweating, body becoming heavy, animals buckling)

**Argument**: Companions witnessed Muhammad having intense physical reactions when receiving revelation. This couldn’t be faked.

**Why it’s weak**:

- These stories come from **later hadith collections**, written 150–200+ years after Muhammad’s death. They’re not contemporary eyewitness accounts.
- People can have genuine **psychological or epileptic-like episodes** and still be wrong about the source. Many cult leaders throughout history have had dramatic “revelations” with physical effects.
- This doesn’t address the **content** problem — the circular prophecy claim and personal, self-serving revelations.

---

### 5. Defense of Surah 33:53 (the verse about house guests and not marrying Muhammad’s widows)

**Argument**:

- The first part is general etiquette advice.
- The second part (don’t marry his widows) is to prevent favoritism and because his wives are special.
- Prophets have special rules (e.g., Muhammad could have more than 4 wives).

**Why it’s weak** (This is actually one of the **strongest** points _against_ the Quran):

- This verse is **extremely personal and time-specific**. It literally tells people how to behave in **Muhammad’s house** and bans anyone from marrying **his specific widows**.
- A timeless, eternal guide for all humanity should not contain rules about one man’s living room etiquette or his personal marital restrictions.
- The excuse that “prophets have special rules” is circular. It’s the same defense used for Joseph Smith’s polygamy revelations in Doctrine and Covenants 132.
- This verse looks exactly like what a powerful man would add to protect his own interests and legacy — not what an all-knowing God would include in a universal scripture.

---

### Summary: The Core Problem

The Muslim’s arguments mostly focus on:

- Muhammad’s **character and public life**
- The **style** of revelation (no drafts, physical effects)
- Special pleading for prophets

But they completely **fail to address** the real issues:

- The **circular prophecy claim** (the strongest parallel with Joseph Smith)
- **Self-serving revelations** that benefit Muhammad personally (like Surah 33:53 and many others)
- The lack of **independent verification** for his most important claims

Even if everything he said about Muhammad’s public life and “perfect” revelation was true, it still wouldn’t solve the fundamental logical problem that also sinks Joseph Smith.
