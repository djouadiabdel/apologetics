### 1. How the Scholars Interpreted “Musaddiq”

Here is what the earliest trusted sources actually said (from the tafsirs we examined earlier):

**Ibn ʿAbbās** (_Tanwīr al-Miqbās_):

> “(confirming) corroborating Allah’s divine Oneness (that which was revealed before it) of scriptures…”

**Al-Ṭabarī** (and those he quotes — Mujāhid, Qatādah, etc.):

> The Quran “testifies to the truth of the previous Books, and the previous Books testify to the truth of the Quran” — especially the glad tidings of Muhammad. It confirms the original revelation sent to the prophets.

**Ibn Kathīr** (summarizing the classical view):

> “_Muṣaddiqan limā bayna yadayhi_ means… from the previous divinely revealed Books… These Books testify to the truth of the Qur’an, and the Qur’an also testifies to the truth these Books contained…”

**The consistent scholarly move**: They interpret _musaddiq_ as the Quran **bearing witness to the original divine message** that was sent down to Moses and Jesus — while allowing that the **transmitted physical books** later suffered _tahrif_ (alteration or distortion) in some parts.

This is the standard classical position. It is **not** a modern invention.

> First, **musaddiq** (from the root _ṣadaqa_). It means **bearing witness to the truth and integrity** of something — to verify it, to attest that it is true and authentic. It does **not** mean ‘confirming only parts of’ or ‘generally confirming while editing the rest.’ It means bearing full witness that the thing is true.

> Second, in Surah 5:48 the Quran calls itself **muhaymin** over the previous scriptures. _Muhaymin_ means **guardian, protector, keeper**. It is the one who guards and protects the truth of what came before.

### 2. What Is Wrong With This Interpretation (The Logical Problems)

Using the trilemma and the document you gave me, here are the clear problems:

**Problem A — The Quran Never Says This Distinction**

The Quran simply says it is _muṣaddiq_ of “what was before it”, “what is with you”, “the Torah and the Gospel”. It does **not** add the qualifier: “the original uncorrupted version, not the current manuscripts”.

The scholars are **adding words** to Allah’s words to solve a problem the plain text creates. This is exactly the charge the Quran levels at the Jews (_yuharrifūn al-kalima ʿan mawādiʿihī_ — twisting words from their places).

**Problem B — It Is Circular (The Core Weakness)**

The reasoning works like this:

1. The Quran says it is _muṣaddiq_ of the previous scriptures.
2. But the previous scriptures (as they exist) contradict the Quran on many points.
3. Therefore, to protect the Quran, scholars say: “It only confirms the _original_ message, not the corrupted version.”
4. How do we know what the “original message” was? Because it agrees with the Quran.

This is a closed circle. The conclusion (Quran is true and cannot be wrong) is used to define the meaning of the word that is supposed to be evidence for that conclusion.

**Problem C — It Destroys the Factive Language**

The Quran uses strong factive verbs:

- “They **find him written** with them” (7:157)
- “They **recognize** him as they recognize their own sons” (2:146)
- “They **conceal** clear proofs” (2:159)

If the text in their hands was already corrupted enough that _musaddiq_ only refers to some lost “original”, then:

- You cannot “find him written” in a corrupted text.
- You cannot “recognize with certainty” what has been obscured.
- You cannot “conceal” proofs you cannot access.

The scholarly interpretation makes the Quran’s own language impossible.

**Problem D — It Turns “Musaddiq” Into Something Weak**

If _musaddiq_ only means “confirms the original that we decide is authentic”, then it is no longer a strong confirmation. It becomes a selective, circular endorsement. This weakens the word far below what the root _ṣadaqa_ (to bear truthful witness) actually means.

----

- The root **ṣadaqa** means to be truthful, to verify, to bear witness to integrity.
- In Quran 5:48 the Book is also called _muhaymin_ — guardian, protector, 

So _musaddiq_ + _muhaymin_ together do carry the sense of **protecting / keeping / guarding the truth** — like a winged animal protecting its egg.


If the Quran is acting as a **guardian/protector** of the previous revelation, then how can it:

- Confirm scriptures that are **totally reliable** (Option A) → then why claim _tahrif_?
- Confirm scriptures that are **totally corrupted** (Option B) → a guardian that protects forgeries is not a guardian.
- Confirm scriptures that **no longer exist** (Option C) → a guardian that protects something no one can access is meaningless.

**The image breaks**: A winged animal that claims to protect the egg but allows the egg to be smashed, replaced with a fake egg, or lost — and then says “I confirm the original egg” — is not protecting anything. It is making an empty claim.

This is why the trilemma is so powerful. _Musaddiq_ as “guardian of truth” cannot coherently apply to any of the three options without contradiction or circularity.
