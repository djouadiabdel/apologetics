### 1. Predicted by an earlier prophet ✅ **Bahá'u'lláh wins, decisively**

**Muhammad:** Muslims point to Deuteronomy 18:18, John 14:16, Isaiah 42, Song of Songs 5:16. Problem: _no Jew or Christian read those verses as referring to a future Arabian prophet before Muhammad existed._ The "prophecy" appears only after Muhammad claims it. There is no document from 500 AD that says "an Arab prophet is coming named Muhammad." Zero.

**Bahá'u'lláh:** The Báb, in his own handwritten books between 1844 and 1850, wrote specifically about "Him Whom God Shall Make Manifest" coming in _the year nine_ and _the year nineteen_. These writings circulated across Persia _while the Báb was still alive_. Thousands of people had copies before the Báb's execution in 1850. Bahá'u'lláh was exiled in 1853 (year 9) and declared himself in 1863 (year 19). The dates were written down before he fulfilled them.

**This is the real deal — a written, dated, pre-existing prophecy from a publicly executed predecessor.** Muhammad has nothing comparable.

### 2. Fulfilled his predictions ✅ **Bahá'u'lláh wins**

**Muhammad:** The famous one is Surah 30:2-4 — the Romans (Byzantines) will defeat the Persians "in a few years" (bidʿi siníin). They did, at the Battle of Nineveh in 627. This is the best one Muslims have, and it's solid. But it's also one prediction, and "a few years" is vague.

**Bahá'u'lláh:**

- Predicted the fall of Napoleon III in a tablet written _before_ it happened. Napoleon ignored his letter, threw it away contemptuously. He fell from power in 1870, two years after receiving the tablet.
- Predicted the loss of the Papal States to Pope Pius IX in a tablet sent before it happened. It happened in 1870.
- Predicted to Kaiser Wilhelm I that "the banks of the Rhine will be covered with gore" and Berlin's "outward splendor will plunge thee into grief." Written in 1870. Fulfilled in WWI and WWII.
- Predicted the collapse of the Ottoman Sultanate to the very Sultan who imprisoned him. The Ottoman Empire dissolved in 1922.
- Predicted the fall of the Qajar dynasty in Persia. Fell in 1925.

These are written, dated tablets, addressed to specific named rulers, sent through diplomatic channels, with specific predictions. **Every one was fulfilled.** Muhammad has one good prediction; Bahá'u'lláh has half a dozen specific, dated, addressed-to-named-rulers predictions, all fulfilled.

**Muhammad:** The famous miracles — splitting the moon, the night journey to Jerusalem, multiplying water — are attested only in **hadith compiled 200+ years after his death**, by his own community, with no contemporary outside witnesses. Surah 17:59 and Surah 13:7 actually say Muhammad was _not_ given miracles like previous prophets; the Quran is the miracle. The miracle stories in hadith contradict this Quranic self-understanding.

**Bahá'u'lláh:** The Báb's execution in 1850 — the first volley of 750 soldiers failed, ropes severed, the Báb returned to his cell unharmed mid-conversation. A second regiment had to be brought in. This is in:

- Bahá'í primary sources
- British diplomatic dispatches by Justin Sheil to London
- Russian diplomatic correspondence
- French orientalist A.L.M. Nicolas's interviews with survivors
- Qajar court records
- Lord Curzon's _Persia and the Persian Question_ (1892)

Whether you believe the miracle or not, _the documentation is better than anything in Muhammad's life_. Hostile witnesses, foreign diplomats, multiple independent sources, all within the lifetime of people involved.
