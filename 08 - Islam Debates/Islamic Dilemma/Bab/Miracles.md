### The execution of the Báb — what happened

**Date:** 9 July 1850 (or noon on 8 July by some sources) **Place:** The barracks square of Tabriz, northwestern Iran **Crowd:** Approximately 10,000 spectators packed the rooftops, walls, and square

The Báb (Sayyid Ali Muhammad Shirazi), 30 years old, had been preaching for 6 years. The Shia clergy considered him a heretic — he claimed to be the Mahdi _and_ a new messenger from God _and_ that his book superseded the Quran. The Prime Minister, Mirza Taqi Khan Amir Kabir, ordered his execution to crush the movement.

**The execution itself:**

1. The Báb was brought out of his prison cell. Before they took him, he was dictating final instructions to his scribe, Mirza Sayyid Husayn. As the guards interrupted, he said: _"Not until I have said to him all those things that I wish to say, can any earthly power silence me. Though all the world be armed against me, yet shall it be powerless to deter me from fulfilling, to the last word, my intention."_ The guards forcibly removed him mid-sentence.
2. A young follower, **Anis** (Mirza Muhammad Ali Zunuzi), aged about 27, had insisted on dying with the Báb. He was tied with the Báb against a stone wall in the public square. Two ropes around their chests, suspended so they were standing.
3. The firing squad: **750 Armenian Christian soldiers** under the command of **Colonel Sam Khan**. They were chosen _specifically because_ they were Christian — the Muslim authorities feared Muslim soldiers might refuse to shoot a man claiming to be the Mahdi.
4. Before the firing, Sam Khan approached the Báb privately, troubled. He said: _"I profess the Christian Faith and entertain no ill will against you. If your Cause be the Cause of truth, enable me to free myself from the obligation to shed your blood."_ The Báb reportedly answered: _"Follow your instructions, and if your intention be sincere, the Almighty is surely able to relieve you from your perplexity."_
5. The 750 soldiers were formed into **three rows of 250**, firing in succession. They fired all three volleys.
6. **When the smoke cleared, the Báb was gone.** Anis stood unharmed against the wall. The ropes that had bound them were severed by the bullets. The Báb had vanished from sight.
7. Panic in the crowd. Soldiers searched. They found the Báb back in his cell, **finishing the dictation to his scribe that had been interrupted.** He looked up calmly and said to the guards: _"I have finished my conversation with Sayyid Husayn. Now you may proceed to fulfil your intention."_
8. Colonel Sam Khan refused to fire again. He marched his entire regiment out of the square and swore he would never raise a hand against the Báb or his followers again. He kept his word.
9. The authorities scrambled to find another regiment. A **Muslim** regiment under **Aqa Jan Khan-i-Khamsih** was brought in. The Báb and Anis were tied up again in the same place.
10. The second regiment fired. This time the Báb was killed. Witnesses reported that at the moment of the second volley, **a strong wind swept across the city, and a dust storm of darkness blackened the sky from noon until evening over Tabriz.**
11. The bodies of the Báb and Anis were thrown outside the city walls to be eaten by dogs. Bahá'ís recovered them at night, hid them, and the remains were eventually moved — over 50 years and many countries — to a shrine on Mount Carmel in Haifa, Israel, where they rest today.

### Why this is "miraculous" — three possible interpretations

You don't have to believe a supernatural explanation to use this story. The Bahá'í claim is that God protected the Báb until his work was done, then permitted his death. But even if you take a _purely naturalistic_ view:

2. **The fact that Sam Khan, an unbeliever, refused to fire a second time and marched out** — historically attested — is itself extraordinary. 3. **The dust storm at midday** — recorded in multiple independent sources — eclipsing the sky from the moment of the second volley. Even if you call it a coincidence, it's the kind of coincidence that Muslims accept as miraculous when it happens around Muhammad.

The miracle isn't that physics was violated.
