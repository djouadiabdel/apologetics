### 1. Simon Magus (1st Century)

**Verification notes**: Primarily from the Book of Acts (eyewitness-level apostolic account) + early Church Fathers (Justin Martyr, Irenaeus — writing within 1–2 generations). Multiple independent sources agree on his reputation for wonders.

1. **Mass amazement through sorcery/miracles in Samaria** — Reported by many Samaritans that he performed great signs and wonders, leading the entire city to call him “the Great Power of God” (Acts 8:9-11). Multiple people in Samaria testified to his powers before Philip arrived.
2. **Public demonstrations of supernatural power** — Early sources describe him astonishing crowds with magical feats that appeared genuine and drew large followings.
3. **Claimed ability to perform ongoing signs after conversion attempt** — Later Gnostic traditions (transmitted through multiple early heresiologists) record him continuing to produce wonders to maintain authority.

**Miracle**: He performed great signs and wonders that amazed the Samaritans.

**Hadith-style Chain (Modeled)**:

> It was narrated to us by Luke (the author of Acts) from the eyewitnesses in Samaria, who said: We saw Simon performing great signs and wonders, and the whole city was amazed, saying, “This man is the Great Power of God.” (Also transmitted through Justin Martyr → Irenaeus → later Church historians.)

**Limitation**: Short chain. Only one primary early source (Acts) + later Church Fathers. No _mutawatir_ (mass) transmission outside the Christian community.


### 2. Montanus, Priscilla & Maximilla (Montanism, 2nd Century)

**Verification notes**: Recorded by multiple early Church writers (Eusebius, the Anonymous anti-Montanist writer, Epiphanius) within decades of the events. Several independent chains of testimony from Asia Minor churches.

1. **Ecstatic prophetic utterances with physical manifestations** — Multiple eyewitnesses reported the prophets falling into trance states, speaking in first-person as God (“I am the Father… I am the Paraclete”), with visible signs of spiritual power that convinced many followers.
2. **Strict moral and ascetic “signs” confirming their message** — Widespread reports of followers adopting extreme fasting and purity as visible proof of the “New Prophecy.”
3. **Prophetic words that appeared to come true in the short term** — Early adherents reported that some immediate prophecies about local events or persecutions were fulfilled, strengthening belief (recorded in Eusebius’ _Church History_).

**Miracle**: Ecstatic prophecy with visible spiritual manifestations.

**Hadith-style Chain (Modeled)**:

> It was narrated to us by the Anonymous writer against Montanism (late 2nd century) from multiple bishops in Asia Minor, who said: We witnessed Montanus, Priscilla, and Maximilla fall into ecstasy and speak in the first person as God, saying “I am the Paraclete,” and many were convinced by the power they displayed. (Also transmitted through Eusebius of Caesarea → Epiphanius.)

**Limitation**: Chains come from opponents of the movement. Very few independent pro-Montanist chains survive.


### 3. Joseph Smith (Latter-day Saints, 1820s–1840s)

**Verification notes**: Official LDS histories (Joseph Smith—History, multiple eyewitness affidavits from the Three Witnesses and Eight Witnesses, early church records). Multiple independent chains from family members, scribes, and early converts.

1. **First Vision (1820)** — Joseph reported seeing God the Father and Jesus Christ in a pillar of light; accepted by early followers as a foundational miracle.
2. **Visits from Angel Moroni (1823 onward)** — Multiple appearances in a room filled with light brighter than day; Moroni showed him the golden plates. Testified by Joseph and later confirmed in the testimony of the Three Witnesses who saw the plates and the angel.
3. **Translation of the Book of Mormon by divine power** — Eyewitnesses (scribes and family) reported supernatural assistance during translation; the completed book itself presented as a miracle.
4. **Restoration of the Aaronic and Melchizedek Priesthoods** — Joseph and Oliver Cowdery reported angelic ordinations (John the Baptist and Peter, James, John) — multiple early accounts.
5. **Healing miracles in the early church** — Numerous reports (recorded in _History of the Church_) of the sick being healed through laying on of hands by Joseph and other elders.
6. **Vision of the Celestial Kingdom and degrees of glory** (D&C 76) — Received with Sidney Rigdon as witness; described in detail and accepted by early Saints.

**Miracle 1**: Visits from Angel Moroni in bright light.

**Hadith-style Chain (Modeled)**:

> It was narrated to us by Joseph Smith from the angel Moroni, who said: “I am Moroni, a messenger sent from God…” This was also witnessed and narrated by the **Three Witnesses** (Oliver Cowdery, David Whitmer, Martin Harris) who said: We saw the angel Moroni and the golden plates in a bright light.

**Miracle 2**: Translation of the Book of Mormon by divine power.

**Hadith-style Chain (Modeled)**:

> Narrated by Emma Smith (wife) and multiple scribes (Oliver Cowdery, Martin Harris): We sat with Joseph while he dictated the translation; he had no manuscript, yet the words flowed continuously for hours.

**Miracle 3**: Healing miracles in the early church.

**Hadith-style Chain (Modeled)**:

> Narrated by multiple early Saints (recorded in _History of the Church_, vol. 2 & 4): We saw the sick healed instantly when Joseph Smith or other elders laid hands on them in the name of Jesus.

**Limitation**: All chains originate from Joseph Smith or his immediate circle. No independent non-LDS sources from the 1820s–1830s confirm the angelic visits.


### 4. Ellen G. White (Seventh-day Adventists, 1840s–1915)

**Verification notes**: Her own writings + official White Estate records + numerous eyewitness accounts from Adventist pioneers (James White, Uriah Smith, etc.). Hundreds of visions documented with multiple corroborating testimonies.

1. **First Vision (December 1844)** — She was taken in vision, saw the path to the New Jerusalem, the “midnight cry” light, and Jesus leading His people. Multiple people present when the vision began.
2. **Hundreds of subsequent visions** — Over 2,000 documented; many witnessed by groups who observed her physical state (no breathing, eyes open, supernatural strength when holding heavy Bibles).
3. **Health reform vision (1863)** — Detailed instructions on diet, dress, and lifestyle that early Adventists adopted; many later claimed improved health as confirmation.
4. **Vision of the Great Controversy** — Comprehensive view of the cosmic conflict; written as _The Great Controversy_ and accepted as inspired.
5. **Specific personal guidance visions** — Numerous accounts of her receiving exact instructions for church leaders or individuals that later proved accurate (recorded in her letters and testimonies).


**Miracle**: Hundreds of visions with supernatural physical phenomena.

**Hadith-style Chain (Modeled)**:

> It was narrated to us by James White (her husband) and multiple Adventist pioneers (Uriah Smith, J.N. Andrews, etc.): We observed Ellen White in vision — she stopped breathing for long periods, held a heavy Bible above her head for extended time with supernatural strength, and her eyes remained open while she described scenes of heaven and future events.

**Another Chain**:

> Narrated by the 1850s–1860s Adventist community in multiple testimonies: We heard her give exact health reform instructions in vision (1863), and many later reported improved health after following them.

**Limitation**: Chains are almost entirely internal to the Seventh-day Adventist movement. No independent non-Adventist eyewitness chains from the 1840s onward.

**Miracle**: Massive conviction and revival during lectures.

**Hadith-style Chain (Modeled)**:

> Narrated by thousands of Millerite converts (recorded in Millerite newspapers _Signs of the Times_ and _Midnight Cry_): We attended William Miller’s lectures and felt overwhelming conviction of sin; many were converted on the spot, and the movement spread rapidly across America in 1843–1844.

**Limitation**: Very few named individual chains. Mostly collective testimony through movement publications.
