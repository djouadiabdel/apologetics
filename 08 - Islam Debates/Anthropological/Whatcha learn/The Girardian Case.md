# The Girardian Case — Internalization Reference

So when Christianity proclaims, from day one, _we killed the Righteous One, the Holy One, the Author of life, and he was God in the flesh and we were wrong_ — this is not a possible output of the satanic mechanism. It is what the mechanism looks like _when it breaks._ Peter says it explicitly in Acts 3:14–15: _"ye denied the Holy One and the Just... and killed the Prince of life."_ Read that as an apologetic exhibit. No founding mythology in human history ever talks that way about its own founding event. Every other founding story protects the killers. This one indicts them — including the very people preaching it. _"Ye killed."_ That sentence is structurally impossible inside Satan's machinery.

----
**First lock — structural.** The satanic mechanism, by its own logic, _cannot produce an innocent-and-divine victim_. The mechanism survives only by concealment. Concealment requires the victim to be remembered as **guilty in some real sense** — polluting Thebes, threatening the city, deserving expulsion. If you remove the victim's guilt, you remove the community's reason for unanimity, and the mechanism fails to crystallize a peace. Mythology _needs_ the victim to be at least ambiguous. Romulus killed Remus because Remus _did_ leap the wall; Oedipus _did_ pollute the city; the pharmakos _was_ somehow odd, lame, foreign, suspect. The guilt is the load-bearing element of the lie. A mechanism that openly declared "we killed a wholly innocent and divine being and were wrong to do so" would be a mechanism that **just exposed itself** — which is precisely what mechanisms cannot do and continue functioning.

----
**Second lock — historical.** A divinized scapegoat in the pagan world is divinized **gradually, ambiguously, and to manage the community's discomfort about the killing.** Romulus apotheosis is centuries of folklore consolidating. Hero cults are slow accretions. The divinity claim _follows_ the killing by long periods and serves the function of _retrospectively justifying_ the community. The Christian high Christology runs the opposite way on every metric. It is **early** — Philippians 2:6–11 is a hymn Paul is quoting as already established, within twenty years of the crucifixion. It is **proclaimed by the killers' own community**, not by distant admirers. It **explicitly condemns the killers** rather than absolving them. And it identifies the divine status with _the very moment of greatest shame_ — the cross itself — rather than spiritualizing the death away. _"We preach Christ crucified, unto the Jews a stumbling block, and unto the Greeks foolishness"_ (1 Corinthians 1:23). Paul _knows_ this is structurally absurd by every ancient religious standard. He names the absurdity and says: _this is the power and wisdom of God._ You cannot get this output from the satanic mechanism by any path. It is the wrong shape.

---
**Third lock — and this is the one to use with your atheist friend especially.** Ask the question backwards. _If_ Christianity were a successful satanic scapegoating that got mythologized, what would we expect to find? We would expect: a victim remembered as at least partly guilty; a community that absolved itself; a slow accretion of divinity claims; founding texts that protect the founders; a peace built on a forgotten killing. What we actually find: a victim proclaimed wholly innocent; a community that indicts itself (_"ye killed"_); explosive immediate high Christology; founding texts that **preserve and emphasize** the founders' shame (Peter's denial, the disciples' flight, Thomas' doubt — all _kept in_, not edited out); and a peace built on a killing that the entire liturgy _re-presents and refuses to let be forgotten._ Every diagnostic of a successful satanic mechanism comes back negative. Every diagnostic of a _broken_ satanic mechanism comes back positive. **This is the fingerprint evidence.** It does not prove Christianity is true by itself — but it locks the door against the "Christianity is just another myth" thesis with structural certainty.

---
> Lazarus is raised. The Sanhedrin sees Jesus as the obstacle to their authority and the Roman settlement (scandal). They accuse him of being the cause of the nation's coming destruction (accuse — and notice the lie: Rome came anyway). The previously divided council unifies in a single resolution (converge). Caiaphas articulates the formula: _expedient that one die for the people_ — the murder rebranded as ritual salvation, the lynching dressed in the language of sacrifice (expel-and-conceal). The whole mechanism is here, named by the high priest himself. And then John shatters the concealment in the next verse: _he prophesied without knowing it._ The Spirit speaks the divine meaning of the same words Caiaphas used in their satanic meaning. The cross is going to run Caiaphas' formula in truth — _one for all_ as self-gift, not as lynching — and that flip is what makes Christianity the exposure of the mechanism rather than another instance of it.

## I. THE CORE MOVE (memorize this first)

The whole argument rests on one inversion of strategy:

**Do not minimize the similarities between the Gospel and myth. Concede all of them, then turn them.**

Girard, _I See Satan_: the similarities "provide the sole basis upon which [Christian uniqueness] can be made obvious." Crisis, collective violence, a victim who dies and rises, cosmic signs, a venerating community — myth has them, Karbala has them, the Gospel has them. The difference is **never in the ingredients. It is in the point of view.**

The one sentence that carries everything: _Every culture is built on a victim; the only question is whether your holy book hides him, commands his removal, or stands him up and calls him innocent._

---

## II. THE THREE-WAY FRAME (the spine of the case)

Three things a tradition can do with its own violence:

1. **Hide it (myth).** Victim guilty, crowd innocent, mechanism invisible. The killing works and stays buried. The community never knows it scapegoated.
2. **Expose and end it (Gospel).** Victim innocent, crowd implicated, mechanism named. The cycle breaks.
3. **Sacralize it (sacred law).** Keep doing it, openly, and declare God commands it. The removal of the transgressor becomes permanent divine order.

Plainest form: **Myth hides the scapegoating. The Gospel exposes it. Sacred law legalizes it.**

This frame is the load-bearing wall. Everything else hangs off it.

---

## III. THE DIAGNOSTIC (what to actually run on a text)

Forget "does it feel sacred" and "does it have miracles" — true stories and myths both have those. The real test, in order of force:

**Strongest — broken unanimity from inside:** _Name one story where the people who did the killing are the ones who tell you the victim was innocent._ A crowd does not invent that about itself. (Acts 2:23, 3:15 — the killers told the victim was innocent.) This is the kill-shot because it can't be dodged with "grief made the hero glow."

**Second — whose side the text takes:** crowd or victim. Myth sides with the crowd (victim guilty). Gospel sides with the victim (innocent, unjustly killed).

**Third — reveal vs. conceal the mechanism:** Does the text expose the scapegoat machine (Caiaphas: "better one man die," John 11:50 — the operator confessing the mechanism) or run it silently?

All three are true; lead with the first because it's the one a sharp interlocutor can't argue around.

---

## IV. THE TWO LEGS (and which one bears weight)

**Load-bearing leg — anthropological/revelatory.** Reveal vs. conceal; victim's side vs. crowd's side; dissent vs. unanimity. This is a claim about the _stance of the text_, not its date. It survives every dating objection: even if the skeptic says "all composed in 90 AD," the text that got composed still takes the victim's side and exposes the lynching — and no myth does that. **This is the leg to fight on.**

**Supporting leg — historical-critical.** Early dating, criterion of embarrassment, pre-Pauline creed (1 Cor 15:3–5, dated within ~1–5 years; Ehrman/Lüdemann concede this), Philippians 2:6–11 hymn, _maranatha_ (1 Cor 16:22, Aramaic), 1 Cor 8:6 (Shema split around Jesus). **Use as corroboration, second chair — never the foundation.**

The mistake to avoid: leading with the historical leg. It's contestable on dating, partially shared by Islam (see VII), and it walks into the "structural similarity" trap Girard names.

---

## V. WHY KARBALA IS NOT A COUNTEREXAMPLE (the wall you kept hitting)

Karbala _shares the ingredients_ — real death, grief-accretion, cosmic signs, glorification, a venerating community. On the structural level the analogy never fully breaks. That's why minimizing-similarities failed.

The clean separation is point-of-view, not detail:

- Karbala's dissent is _"the villains were evil"_ (Yazīd, Shimr, ʿUmar ibn Saʿd — named, cursed in liturgy). That is **still the crowd defending itself** — us-good, them-bad. The mechanism (we bind our identity around this victim) stays concealed.
- The Gospel's dissent is _"we killed an innocent man"_ — the in-group accuses **itself**.

One protects the group. The other accuses it. Only the second is something a group never does to itself. **Karbala conceals. The Gospel reveals.**

---

## VI. MYTH vs. THE PASSION — the four features (use carefully)

A myth, by default human pattern, does four things:

1. Cleans up the victim over time (humiliations sanded off; accretion runs _upward_ toward glory).
2. Names villains and tells you to hate them (community needs the violence to be someone's fault).
3. Makes the death make sense cosmically (heavens weep, soil holy, bloodline supernatural — scandal absorbed).
4. Hides the mechanism (never "we needed a victim and picked him"; the crowd is unanimous and invisible to itself).

**CAUTION — do not overclaim this.** The Gospel partially shares all four (temple veil tears, "the Jews" in John, John's high-Christology prologue, liturgical cursing in later tradition). If you claim clean structural inversion at every point, a sharp friend catches it. The four-feature grid is _illustrative_, not the locked argument. The locked argument is the point-of-view diagnostic (III) and the three-way frame (II).

## VII. THE EMBARRASSMENT CRITERION (real but narrow)

The honest version: communities domesticate their embarrassments eventually (Christians now read the cry of dereliction as Psalm 22 fulfillment; Muslims read ʿAbasa as proof of revelation's independence). So the test is **not** "does the community feel embarrassed now." It's: **at the moment the material entered the tradition, did the surrounding theology have the resolution built in, or did it have to be constructed afterward against the grain?**

- ʿAbasa (Q 80:1–10), al-Taḥrīm (66:1), al-Anfāl (8:67–68): minor rebukes _resolved in the text itself_ — the correction proves the revelation isn't Muhammad's invention. Embarrassment **pre-resolved**, metabolized into strength.
- Passion failures (cry of dereliction, Peter's denial, disciples' flight, burial by an outsider, women as legally-inadmissible first witnesses): **unresolved at the textual level.** Paul is still calling the cross a scandal in the 50s (1 Cor 1:23). The resolution had to be built afterward, under pressure, against inherited categories (Deut 21:23 — the hanged man cursed).

The defensible claim: not "Islam has no embarrassment criterion" (false — concede ʿAbasa openly). But "**the embarrassment in Islam is the kind a community can use; the embarrassment in the Gospels is the kind it cannot use and kept anyway.**"

## VIII. THE LAW vs. THE FULFILLMENT

What the Law is _for_ (Girard — and it is **protective, not evil**):

- Fences off objects/rivalries that trigger mimetic crisis (prohibition).
- Re-enacts the founding murder in controlled substitute form (ritual, sacrifice).
- Discharges disorder onto a designated transgressor in regulated form (law, penalty).
- It _manages_ the violence — scar tissue around the wound. Keeps the lid on.

What the Fulfillment _does_:

- Exposes the victim the system was built to hide; names him innocent.
- Ends the sacrificial machinery (Hebrews — one final victim).
- Drains the thing under the lid instead of containing it; the cycle breaks.

Hinge sentence: **The Law keeps the violence asleep; the Gospel wakes it up, names it, and lets it die.**

The biblical arc: Law → prophetic critique of Law (Hosea 6:6, Isa 1, Ps 51) → fulfillment/transcendence (Hebrews, Galatians, Romans). The text is at war with its own ritual system. That tension is the engine of revelation.

## IX. THE QURAN / SHARIA STRUCTURALLY (handle with discipline)

**Whose side:** The Quran is NOT archaic crowd-siding myth. It sides with the victim in real places (Q 81:8–9, the buried infant girl asked for what sin she was killed; the whole persecuted-prophet pattern — Noah, Hud, Salih, Lot, Shuayb, Moses). Concede this first or get dismantled.

**The structural difference is in HOW the victim is vindicated:**

- Gospel: victim absorbs violence and refuses to return it ("Father, forgive them"); the cycle is _broken_; violence stops being sacred.
- Quran: victim vindicated _through_ violence — persecutors annihilated (Flood, Lot's city, Pharaoh drowned); Muhammad's arc runs persecuted-victim → sovereign → conqueror. The victim is vindicated by _becoming the victor_. Violence remains holy; it changes hands.

Girard's "two transcendences" (_Things Hidden_): the Gospel demythologizes the transcendence of violence and glorifies the transcendence of love. The Quranic resolution **relocates** violence (mob → God-the-avenger + victorious community) rather than dissolving it. **Re-sacralization.**

**The Law angle (your sharpest instinct):** the biblical arc opens the Law toward exposure of the victim; the Sharia _seals_ it — installs the protective apparatus (hudud, qisas, ritual repetition) as permanent, total, divine. **The Quran does not expose the mechanism — it perfects it.** "Perfects" = completes and installs permanently, NOT "makes better."

**CRITICAL CORRECTION you reached:** the Islamic frame is _desert / repayment_, NOT communal purification. In qisas the right belongs to the **victim's family**, and the penalty is framed as the wrongdoer being **repaid** for a guilty act (Q 5:38, 24:2, 2:178–179 — "life for you, O people of understanding"). A scapegoat is _innocent_; the hudud subject is _guilty by the system's own lights_. **Do NOT argue "Allah is the mob/executioner scapegoating the sinner" — it collapses instantly under "a scapegoat is innocent; a thief is not; you've relabeled justice as lynching."** This is an open escape route. Don't build there.

The defensible claim: not "Islam is cruel" and not "Allah is a murderer." Only the structural one — **the Sharia installs and sanctifies the removal of the transgressor as permanent divine order; the Gospel exposes and ends the community's right to purify itself through the transgressor's removal.** Stay on structure and trajectory.

## X. SIN AND PURITY — the precise distinction (the one you almost missed)

The Gospel does NOT abolish sin, transgression, or the existence of sinners. It ends **the use of the sinner as the instrument of the community's purity.**

- Scapegoat systems (mob / myth / sacred law): _purity flows from expelling the bad one._ The community is clean _because_ it removed the transgressor. The victim is the drain.
- Gospel: _purity flows from the victim absorbing violence without passing it on._ Sin stays real; the sinner remains; but he is no longer the sacrificial drain.

The scene: **John 8:1–11** (woman caught in adultery). Jesus does NOT say "she didn't sin." He says "let him without sin throw the first stone" (dissolves the mob by turning accusation back on each accuser) then "neither do I condemn you; go and sin no more" (sin named — it's real). The machine is disarmed; nobody gets clean by killing her.

Corrected formulation: **The Gospel ends the community's RIGHT to purify itself by destroying the transgressor. Sin stays. The sacrificial use of the sinner ends.**

And the root beneath this: Gospel purity is **not** "stop desiring" (that's Buddhism; Girard explicitly rejects it). It's **changing what you imitate** — from rivalrous models (who block you, create scarcity, pull you toward a victim) to a non-rivalrous self-giving model (Christ), which drains the rivalry out of desire and so dissolves both the crisis and the scapegoating that resolves it. Two layers: **desire healed (root), scapegoating refused (fruit).**

## XI. THE INQUISITION OBJECTION (turn it, don't concede the field)

When the friend says "Christendom re-sacralized too — burned heretics, ran the Inquisition" — this is **evidence FOR the thesis, not against it.**

The move: _where did the Inquisition's critics get the words "innocent victim," "scapegoat," "persecution"?_ From the Gospel's own revelation. Christendom betrayed its text, and the text is what lets us name the betrayal. Christianity contains a self-detonating charge: any time the Christian community scapegoats, its own scripture convicts it (Las Casas, abolitionists — fighting Christendom with the Bible in hand).

The question to put back: **does your founding text contain the resource that exposes and condemns its own sacred violence — or does it command that violence as permanent divine order?** Christendom's violence _contradicts_ its text and is judged by it. The Sharia's penalties _are_ the text, enacted. One betrays its source; the other obeys it.

The conclusion (and the guardrail): the difference between traditions is **not "who behaved better"** — it's **"whose founding text turns on its own people's violence."** Trajectory and self-critique, not body count. The second you say "Islam is violent, Christianity is loving," you've left Girard, started a fight you can't win, and wounded your mother for nothing.

## XII. THE EIGHT AXIOMS (the defensible skeleton)

1. Human communities have always restored unity by turning on a victim, then telling themselves the victim deserved it. _(Anthropological floor — not specifically Christian.)_
2. A text's relation to violence shows in its point of view — crowd's side or victim's side — not its ingredients. _(Literary, checkable.)_
3. Myth narrates from the crowd's side: victim guilty, community innocent, mechanism hidden.
4. The biblical trajectory inverts this — victim's side, victim innocent, mechanism progressively exposed — even against its own community, which is why no group would produce it as self-flattery.
5. Every religious-legal system exists to manage the violence the founding murder once resolved; the Law is protective, not evil.
6. Traditions relate to their violence three ways — hide (myth), expose-and-end (Gospel), sacralize (sacred law); the decisive question is which the founding text does.
7. A tradition's historical violence does not itself indict its text; what matters is whether the text condemns that violence or commands it. _(Absorbs the Inquisition objection.)_
8. Therefore the difference is "whose founding text turns on its own people's violence" — trajectory and self-critique, not body count.

These never say Islam is cruel, never say Christians were innocent, never rank Gods' niceness. All stay on structure. That's the only locked ground.

## XV. METHOD DISCIPLINE (the standard that governs all of it)

- An argument is only as strong as its weakest escape route. **Locked, not suggestive.**
- Concede the strongest version of the opponent's case _first_, then make the narrower claim that survives the concession. (ʿAbasa, the persecuted-prophet pattern, Christendom's atrocities — name them before he does.)
- Distinguish what the text _directly states_ from what is _inferred_.
- One argument at a time; let each land.
- Burden-shift: the goal is often not to prove a conclusion but to force the interlocutor to make increasingly costly claims.
- Stay on **structure and trajectory**, never sentiment. "My God is nicer / your religion is violent" is a sentiment that loses — and costs you your mother and your friend.
- The loud argument is not the strong argument. Don't trade the locked case for the loud one.
