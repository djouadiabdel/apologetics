**1. The Law and the Fulfillment, as two lists.**

What the Law is _for_ (Girard's reading):

- It fences off the objects and rivalries that pull a community back into mimetic crisis (prohibition).
- It re-enacts the founding murder in controlled, substitute form so the real violence doesn't return (ritual and sacrifice).
- It discharges disorder onto a designated transgressor in a regulated way, so the mob doesn't do it chaotically (law and penalty).
- In short: the Law _manages_ the violence. It's the scar tissue around the wound. It keeps the lid on.

What the Fulfillment _does_:

- It exposes the victim the whole system was built to hide — names him innocent.
- It ends the sacrificial machinery instead of maintaining it — one final victim, no more needed (Hebrews).
- It removes the need for the lid by draining the thing under it — the cycle is broken, not contained.
- In short: the Fulfillment doesn't manage the violence. It _exposes and exhausts_ it.

The one-sentence hinge between the two lists: _The Law keeps the violence asleep; the Gospel wakes it up, names it, and lets it die._
