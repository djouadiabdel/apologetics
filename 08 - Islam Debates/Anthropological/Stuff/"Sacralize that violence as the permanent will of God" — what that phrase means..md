To "sacralize" violence = to make it holy, to declare that _God commands or approves_ this act of removing the victim. Not "we did a regrettable necessary thing," but "this killing is righteous, divinely mandated, an act of worship."

Three ways a tradition can relate to its own violence:

- _Hide_ it (myth): pretend the victim was guilty and the crowd innocent; never admit a mechanism is running.
- _Expose_ it (Gospel): admit "we killed an innocent man," and let that admission break the cycle.
- _Sacralize_ it (the third path): keep doing it, openly, and declare that God wills it — make the violence a permanent feature of the holy order.

"Permanent will of God" is the part that locks it. A one-time punishment you might repent of. But when the removal of the transgressor is written into eternal divine law — when the penalty for apostasy or theft or adultery is not a historical contingency but _the standing command of God for all time_ — the violence is no longer something the community might one day grow out of by reading its own text more deeply. It's sealed in as sacred and final. That's the difference between violence a tradition can _outgrow using its own scripture_ and violence a tradition would have to _overrule its own scripture_ to abandon.

The whole thing in one line for your friend: _"Every religion has violence in its history. The question is whether your holy book is the thing that finally condemns that violence — or the thing that commands it."_

And one honest guardrail, because you'll be tempted to swing this like a hammer: a serious Muslim will answer that _qisas_ and the hudud are _justice_, not scapegoating — that a victim's family receiving redress is the opposite of an innocent being lynched, and that calling divine law "sacralized violence" smuggles in a Christian value judgment as if it were neutral anthropology. He's not wrong that the move is contestable. So keep it where it's defensible: 

you're not claiming Islamic law is unjust by its own lights — you're making the _structural_ observation that the system installs and sanctifies the removal of the transgressor as permanent divine order, where the Gospel exposes and ends it. Stay on structure and trajectory. The second you say "and therefore Islam is just cruelty," you've left Girard and you've started a fight you can't win and that costs you your mother.
