Sunni Islam must simultaneously hold that (1) the Quran is perfectly preserved, (2) Companions recited verses no longer in the mushaf, and (3) the doctrine reconciling these is established by sources weaker than what it adjudicates. No two can be defended without surrendering the third.

### The Anchor (before anything else)

Get them to commit to Q 15:9 as preservation of _the revealed recitation itself_, not just the final compilation.

> "When Allah promises in 15:9 to preserve the dhikr, is He promising to preserve everything He revealed as Quran, or only what survives in the final mushaf?"

If they say the former — every trap below lands harder. If they say the latter — preservation has already become a tautology and you've won before starting.

### Layer 1 — The Data

> "Did Companions, after the Prophet's death, recite as Quran material that is not in the Uthmanic mushaf, believing it to be Quran?"

Sources: Umar (stoning verse) and Aisha (suckling verse), in Bukhari and Muslim.

**The Umar sub-question (this closes the "abrogated before death" escape):**

> "Why did Umar want to write the stoning verse into the mushaf? He said he would have done so if he weren't afraid people would accuse him of adding to the Quran. If the Prophet had abrogated its recitation, this is the second caliph trying to insert abrogated material. Either he didn't know it was abrogated — which destroys the chain of transmission — or he didn't believe it was abrogated, in which case naskh al-tilawa is being applied retroactively to explain his behavior. Which is it?"

This is a fork they cannot escape.

### Layer 2 — The Three Possible Explanations

Only three answers exist in the conceptual space:

(a) Companions were wrong about what was Quran → destroys hadith epistemology and Sunni reliance on Companion testimony.

(b) Quran was lost → contradicts Q 15:9.

(c) Naskh al-tilawa — Allah deliberately removed the recitation while preserving the ruling. The only survivable answer.

Don't refute (a) or (b) yourself. Ask one question:

- For (a): _"So Umar — the second caliph — was wrong about what is the speech of Allah?"_
- For (b): _"So Q 15:9's promise of preservation has exceptions?"_

They will retreat to (c).

### The Lock-In Question (between Layer 2 and Layer 3)

> "So your position is that Allah revealed verses, commanded their recitation as Quran, then commanded their removal from recitation while preserving their rulings — and this entire process is part of the perfect preservation promised in 15:9. Is that correct?"

Wait for explicit confirmation. This prevents retreat later.

### Layer 3 — The Five Coherence Traps (Q-T-C-S-M)

Deploy in order, one at a time, slowly. Wait for each answer.

**Q — Quran**

> "Q 2:106 defines abrogation as substitution — Allah brings something better or similar in its place. Where in the Quran is naskh al-tilawa — deletion without replacement — described?"

**T — Tawatur**

> "So a hadith with a single chain is being used to determine what counts as Quran transmitted by hundreds. Walk me through how that works."

**C — Companions (Uthman-anchored)**

> "Uthman burned the codices of Ibn Mas'ud and others because they contained material that disagreed with Zayd's compilation — variant readings, different sura ordering, and additional verses Ibn Mas'ud believed were Quran. On what basis was that material rejected? If naskh al-tilawa is the mechanism, who recorded the abrogation? If it wasn't naskh, then verses considered Quran by senior Companions were destroyed by political authority. Which was it?"

**S — Sunnah**

> "If the ruling of stoning is preserved through Sunnah after the Quranic verse was lifted, then Sunnah is doing the preservation work. What, specifically, does Q 15:9 promise to preserve?"

**M — Manuscripts (always deploy, never optional)**

> "Naskh al-tilawa explains verses that left the mushaf. What explains verses that appear in different forms in early manuscripts of the mushaf itself — like the Sana'a palimpsest, where the lower text aligns with non-Uthmanic codices? You now need a second doctrine. Why does Allah's preservation require this many auxiliary explanations?"

### The Escape Loop (the property that makes the argument inescapable)

Every escape activates another trap:

- Escape Q ("naskh al-tilawa is implicit in 2:106") → admits the doctrine has no direct Quranic basis.
- Escape T ("ahad can establish former Quranic status") → opens the door to Shia claims about missing verses transmitted by single chains.
- Escape C ("Uthman had authority to standardize") → political authority is now determining Quranic boundaries, which inverts the hierarchy again.
- Escape S ("Q 15:9 only refers to the final form") → preservation becomes a tautology: Allah preserved what was preserved.
- Escape M ("manuscript variants are minor") → you have just admitted variants exist, which is what the doctrine was constructed to deny.
