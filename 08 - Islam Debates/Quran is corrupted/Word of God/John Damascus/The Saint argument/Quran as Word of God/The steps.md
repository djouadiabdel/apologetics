**Beat 1 — their belief.** The Quran is the uncreated word of God. _(You don't argue this. They hand it to you.)_

**Beat 2 — the variants are all the word of God.** Multiple canonical readings (مَلِك / مَالِك). They say all are equally the word of God — none is a human choice. _(They hand you this too, proudly.)_

**Beat 3 — so the full word of God = all of them together.** If every reading is the word of God, then the _complete_ word of God contains _every_ reading at once. مَلِك **and** مَالِك, at the same spot. The whole thing is the bundle of all variants.

**Beat 4 — but a mouth can only say one.** You physically cannot recite both words in one slot. So any recitation is _one thread_ pulled out of the bundle.

**Beat 5 — therefore no one ever recites the full Quran.** Every reciter, every mushaf, every hafiz holds _one mode_ — a slice. The complete uncreated word exists only as the total bundle in God's mind. **No human has ever held or recited the whole thing.**

That's the whole argument. Five beats. The _shape_ to remember: **all are the word → the whole is all of them → a mouth says one → so everyone has a slice, no one has the whole.**

**The one-sentence version (this is what you actually say to him):**

> "If every reading is the word of God, then the full word of God is all of them at once — but you can only recite one at a time. So you've never recited the whole Quran. You have a mode. The complete thing is something no human can speak."

**The image that makes it stick** (you like mechanisms with parts): The full Quran is a **chord** — all the notes sounded together. A recitation is you **humming one note** of it. Every Muslim is humming a single note and calling it the chord. The chord itself — every reading simultaneously — can't be sung by a human voice at all. It only exists whole in the One who holds all notes at once.

**The two escapes, and the one-line answer to each** (so you're not caught):

- _"One reading is enough / sufficient."_ → "Enough _for you to recite_ isn't _the whole word of God_. You're telling me what satisfies your obligation. I'm asking where the complete thing is. 'Enough' is about you. I'm asking about _it_."
- _"The readings all unite in the rasm (the consonants)."_ → "The bare consonants don't tell you the word — مَلِك and مَالِك have the same consonants and different meanings. So the one thing that's preserved-as-written is _silent_; it doesn't give you the word of God by itself. The thing that's 'one' is mute, and the word of God is the part that _isn't_ on the page."

So they say: **Hafs alone is the whole Quran. Warsh alone is the whole Quran.** One reading, complete.

Here's the move. If Hafs is the _whole_ and Warsh is the _whole_, then ask the one question that breaks it:

> "Then is مَلِك the whole word of God at that spot, or is مَالِك? Because they're different words. If Hafs is complete _and_ Warsh is complete, you've got _two different complete words of God_ at the same position. Which one did God eternally say there — or did He say both?"

Now watch the squeeze, because "whole" forces a worse problem than "part" did:

- If **Hafs is the whole** and it has مَلِك, then مَالِك is **not** part of the whole word of God — it's _surplus_, it's not needed, it's not in the complete thing. But Warsh-readers recite مَالِك _as the word of God._ So you've just told a Warsh-reciter he's adding a word that isn't in the complete Quran. **One reading being "the whole" makes every other reading an unnecessary extra — which contradicts "all readings are the word of God."**
- And it runs both ways: if Warsh is also the whole, then مَلِك is the surplus. So _each_ reading, by being "whole," declares the _others'_ unique words to be **not part of the complete word of God.** They cancel each other.

So "whole" isn't a safe answer — it's _worse_. The "part" branch only cost them "no one recites all of it." The "whole" branch costs them their own doctrine: **they said every reading is the word of God, and now "each reading is complete" turns every other reading's distinctive words into non-Quran.** They can't hold "all readings are the word of God" _and_ "each reading is the whole word of God" at the same time. Pick.

Here's the intuition you were missing, in plain form:

**"Whole" and "different" can't both be true of two things at the same spot.** If two things are each "the whole" of something, they have to be _the same._ The moment they _differ_ — and مَلِك ≠ مَالِك, different word, different meaning — they can't _both_ be the whole. At least one of them has something the "whole" doesn't, or is missing something the "whole" has. So "each reading is the whole and they're different" is just a contradiction wearing a calm face.

The one sentence to say on the "whole" branch:

> "If Hafs is the whole word of God and Warsh is the whole word of God, and they're _different_ — then you've got two different 'wholes.' Two different complete words of God can't both be complete unless they're identical, and they're not. So either one of them isn't really the word of God — or 'whole' is the wrong word, and each is only a part. You don't get 'each is the complete Quran' _and_ 'they genuinely differ.'"

So now the _whole fork collapses into one outcome_, which is why it's so clean:

- They say **"part"** → no one recites the full Quran (your easy branch).
- They say **"whole"** → contradiction: two different things can't each be the whole, so they're forced back to "part" anyway — _and_ they've momentarily called every other reading's words non-Quran.

Both roads end at **"part."** "Whole" just self-destructs and dumps them back onto the "part" road. That's why you only ever needed to really understand one branch — the other isn't a real escape, it's a longer way to the same place.

The thing to retain, dead simple:

**Two different things can't both be "the whole." Different ⇒ at most one is whole ⇒ so they're parts ⇒ no one has the whole.**

Say _that_ back to me — "different things can't both be whole" — in your own words. That sentence is the entire "whole" branch. Once it's yours, the fork has no exit and you only have to carry one idea.



If God eternally says **both** مَلِك and مَالِك at that spot, then **the complete word of God at that spot is two words, not one.** So:

> "If God eternally says both, then the whole word of God _contains both_. Then Hafs — which has only مَلِك — is missing مَالِك. So Hafs is **not** the whole. You just told me each reading is complete; now you're telling me the complete thing has both words and each reading has only one. Those can't both be true. Hafs is a part."

That's the kill. "God says both" is the very thing that proves no single reading is whole — because the moment the complete word has _two_ words at that spot, _any reading with one_ is _short by one._ They reached for "both" to honor all the readings, and "both" is exactly what demotes every reading to a fragment.

So the chain closes on itself, and it's tight enough to hold in your head:

1. Each reading is the whole? → two different "wholes" can't both be whole → contradiction.
2. So which word does God eternally say? → "both."
3. "Both" means the complete word has _both words_ at that spot.
4. But every reading has only _one_ word there.
5. Therefore every reading is **missing one** → every reading is a **part** → no one recites the whole.

"Both" was supposed to be the pious escape. It's actually the lock. The one line to say:

> "If He eternally says both, the whole has both — and your reading has one. So your reading isn't the whole. It's a part. We're back to: nobody recites the full Quran."

Now — be ready, because "both" opens _one_ last door and you should see it coming. They may say:

> "God says both, and reciting _either_ fully discharges it — both are accepted, so each recitation _counts as_ the whole even though it contains one."

Answer: that's switching from _what the Quran is_ to _what God accepts from you._ "Either one is accepted" is a statement about **your obligation** — that God is pleased with either recitation. It is not a statement that **the word of God is one word.** You can fully discharge a duty with a part. "Reciting one satisfies God" and "one is the whole word of God" are different sentences — the first is about _you_, the second is about _the text._ Hold them to the second:

> "I'm not asking what God accepts from you. I'm asking what the word of God _is_ at that spot. You said both. Then the thing itself is two words, and you only ever say one. Whether He's pleased with that is a separate question — and a 'yes' there doesn't turn one word into two."

That's the whole machine now, sealed. The thing to carry:

**"Both" means the whole has two; your mouth says one; so you have a part.** Same destination as every other branch. There is genuinely no exit left — every honest answer ("part," "whole," "both") lands on _the complete Quran is something no human recites._

Say the chain back in your own words — just beats 2→3→4 ("both → whole has both → my reading has one → so it's a part"). If you can say those three arrows, you own the entire argument and never have to memorize a sentence again.
