

# Where the argument can be philosophically upgraded

I'll lay out the additions in order of devastating-ness. Each one is a tool John didn't have or didn't deploy.

---

## Upgrade 1: The modal collapse of "eternal attribute"

This is the most powerful new tool, and it comes from modal logic developed in the 20th century (Kripke, Plantinga, contemporary analytic theology).

**The Sunni position:** the Quran is God's eternal, uncreated _kalām_. It is an eternal attribute of God, real, distinct from God's essence but not separate from him.

**The new pressure:** what is the _modal status_ of the content of the Quran?

The Quran contains specific statements: "Muhammad is the seal of the prophets" (Q 33:40), "the People of the Trench were destroyed" (Q 85), "Pharaoh drowned" (Q 10:90-92), "Abu Lahab will perish" (Q 111). These are _contingent_ statements — they refer to particular events in particular times that did not have to be that way. Pharaoh might not have existed. Muhammad might not have existed. Abu Lahab might not have insulted Muhammad.

But if the Quran is eternal and uncreated — if it exists in God _necessarily_ and from eternity — then **every contingent statement in it must be eternally necessary in God's being**. The proposition "Abu Lahab will perish" must have existed in God's mind from eternity, before Abu Lahab was created, as part of God's necessary, uncreated essence.

Now the question: is the existence of Abu Lahab himself _necessary_ or _contingent_?

If contingent (which every Sunni must say — only God is necessary), then God's eternal, uncreated essence contains _contingent propositions_ — which is a contradiction. The eternal cannot contain the contingent. The necessary cannot contain the non-necessary as a constituent of its essence.

If necessary (the only way out), then Abu Lahab's existence and rebellion are _as necessary as God himself_. Which makes God the necessitator of evil and unbelief, destroys human freedom completely, and turns the entire Quranic ethical apparatus (commands, prohibitions, judgments) into theater. Allah commanded what could not have been otherwise and condemned people who could not have done otherwise.

**The Christian Logos doctrine does not have this problem.** The Logos is a Person, not a set of propositions. The eternal Word does not contain "Abu Lahab will perish" as a constituent of his eternal being. The Logos can speak contingent words in time without those words being eternally necessary, because the Logos is the _speaker_, not the _speech-content_. Propositions about contingent events arise _in time_ when the eternal Person acts in time. The Person is eternal; specific propositions are temporal acts of the Person.

This is a _new_ argument. John didn't have modal logic. The Muslim has no clean answer. Either:

- The Quran is necessary in its content → fatalism and the destruction of the Quranic ethical framework
- The Quran is contingent in its content → the Quran is not eternal → Muʿtazilite position rejected by Sunni Islam

Force the choice.

## Upgrade 2: The ontological status of _kalām nafsī_

The Ashʿarī tradition tried to escape an earlier version of this problem by distinguishing **inner speech** (_kalām nafsī_) from **outer expression** (_kalām lafẓī_, the Arabic words). The inner speech is uncreated and eternal; the Arabic letters and sounds are created.

This is a brilliant move and it bought them centuries of peace. But it has a hidden cost that contemporary philosophy of mind makes visible.

**The new pressure:** what _is_ an "inner speech" that contains no language, no symbols, no propositional structure, no temporal sequence?

If the _kalām nafsī_ is _not_ propositional, it is not "speech" in any recognizable sense — it is some kind of unitive divine self-awareness, which is exactly what Christian Trinitarianism calls the eternal generation of the Son. The Father knows himself, and this self-knowledge is the eternal Logos. Welcome to Aquinas's account of the Trinity, where the Son is the Father's eternal act of self-understanding.

If the _kalām nafsī_ _is_ propositional — if it contains the _meanings_ of "Muhammad is the seal" and "Abu Lahab will perish" without their Arabic dress — then the same modal collapse from Upgrade 1 applies, just one level up. The _meanings_ are still contingent referring to contingent events.

If the _kalām nafsī_ is the _capacity_ to speak, not actual speech, then "God's eternal Word" reduces to "God's eternal ability to speak" — which is just God's omnipotence and adds nothing. The whole edifice collapses into trivial omnipotence.

The Muslim has three options, none coherent:

- Inner speech = non-propositional self-awareness → that's the Logos / Trinitarian Son
- Inner speech = propositional content → modal collapse
- Inner speech = mere capacity → eternal Word doctrine is empty

John could not run this argument because he didn't have a philosophy of mind. We do.

## Objection 1: "God speaking to himself doesn't require an eternal Other"

You're right that this is a weak point as I had it. Let me strengthen it properly.

The Muslim move is: God speaks _in himself_ without speaking _to_ anyone. Like a person thinking, not like a person addressing. No addressee required. "Inner speech" can just be inner _thought_ — God's eternal self-awareness, with no real distinction within God.

Here's the problem with this move when you press it.

**Sub-question:** what kind of thing is "inner thought without an addressee"?

If it's _contentless_ — just God's pure self-presence to himself, undifferentiated awareness — then it has no propositional structure, no "speech" in any meaningful sense. The Sunni cannot accept this, because the Quran _has content_. The Quran says specific things ("Muhammad is the messenger," "do not eat pork," "the Trinity is wrong"). If God's eternal _kalām_ is the Quran, then God's eternal speech has _propositions_ in it. Propositions are _about_ something. They have subject-predicate structure. They make truth-claims.

A proposition without an addressee is incoherent. The proposition "Abu Lahab will perish" is about Abu Lahab, addressed _to_ someone who is told this. If God has been eternally holding this proposition with no addressee, he has been eternally holding an unspoken truth-claim about a contingent being. To whom is this proposition addressed eternally? To no one?

But "speech to no one" is not speech. It is at most _information storage_. The Muslim has reduced God's eternal Word from "the divine self-communication" to "a kind of eternal database of contingent facts about a creation that doesn't yet exist."

This is worse than the position it tried to escape. The Sunni now has God eternally _containing_ propositional content about contingent creatures, with no act of communication occurring eternally. That makes the propositional content _modally_ problematic (as we discussed) _and_ phenomenologically empty (it's not speech, just stored information).

**The press:** _if God's "inner speech" is not addressed to anyone, it's not speech — it's a static eternal proposition-store about contingent things that didn't have to exist. That's worse than denying eternal Word entirely. You haven't escaped the problem; you've made it sharper._

The Christian alternative: the Father eternally generates the Son as his perfect Word. This is not "God talking to a separate person" in some crude sense — it is the Father's eternal self-knowledge, which is so complete that it _is_ a Person, eternally. This is Aquinas's analysis: the Father knows himself perfectly; his self-knowledge is the Logos; because God's knowledge is identical with God's being, the Logos is fully God; because the Father's knowing is a real act, the Logos is a real subsistent term of that act, not just an attribute. The "Other" within God is not foreign to God — it is God's own act of self-understanding subsisting as Person.

**The key insight to deploy:** _the only way to have eternal speech without an eternal addressee is to deny it's really speech. The only way to have it be really speech is to have an eternal addressee. The Christian doctrine just names what the Muslim is implicitly committed to but refuses to formalize._

## Objection 2: "Inner speech = mere capacity is not really a problem"

You're right again. Let me reconsider this.

The Muslim might say: "Fine, God's eternal Word is just God's eternal _capacity_ to speak. So what? God has many capacities — knowledge, power, life. The capacity exists eternally; the exercise of the capacity is temporal."

This is actually a reasonable position philosophically. The problem is **this is the Muʿtazilite position**, which Sunni Islam already rejected.

Why? Because if the _capacity_ is eternal but the _speech itself_ is not, then:

- The Quran (as actual speech with actual content) is not eternal
- The Quran is created (the actualization of the eternal capacity)
- This contradicts the Hanbalite victory in the _Miḥna_
- This contradicts the standard Sunni _aqīda_

So the Muslim cannot retreat to this position without throwing away 1,200 years of Sunni orthodoxy. They are bound by their tradition's settled position.

**The press:** _if you say God's eternal Word is mere capacity, you've joined the Muʿtazilites. Sunni Islam fought a civil war to reject that position. Either you reject the settled creed of your tradition, or you cannot retreat to this option. Which is it?_

This is a _historical_ fork, not just a philosophical one. The Muslim has to choose between:

- (a) The Quran is created (Muʿtazilite, condemned by Sunni tradition)
- (b) The Quran is eternal speech with eternal propositional content (modal collapse, phenomenological problems)
- (c) The Quran's content is somehow eternally in God without being propositions or speech (incoherence — what is it then?)

There's no (d) that doesn't slide back into one of these.

## Upgrade 3: The act/potency problem

This is Aquinas's tool, sharpened by Étienne Gilson and the 20th-century Thomist revival.

**Background:** Aquinas, building on Aristotle, distinguished _act_ (actuality) from _potency_ (potentiality). God is _actus purus_ — pure act, no potency, no unrealized capacity. Everything God is, God is fully and necessarily.

**The pressure on Islam:** if God's speech is an act of God, is it always actualized?

If yes (the Sunni position): then God is _eternally speaking_ the Quran. He is eternally saying "Muhammad is the seal" before Muhammad exists. He is eternally saying "Abu Lahab will perish" before Abu Lahab is born. The temporal ordering implied by these statements (the prophets _before_ Muhammad, Abu Lahab existing _before_ perishing) collapses into an eternal "now" in which all these statements are simultaneously and necessarily true. This either makes time an illusion (which Islam doesn't hold) or makes the Quran's statements about temporal events incoherent as eternal truths.

If no (some statements are eternal, some temporal): then God has _potency_ — he was capable of saying "Muhammad is the seal" before he actualized that saying. But pure act has no potency. Therefore God is not pure act. Therefore God is a being among beings, composite, with unrealized capacities. This breaks classical theism, which Sunni Islam claims to hold.

**The Christian solution:** the Logos is eternally actualized as the Father's perfect self-expression. Specific propositional content in time (the words of Christ in his earthly life) are _acts of the incarnate Logos in time_, not eternal propositions in the divine essence. The Logos _speaks in time_ without being _constituted by_ the temporal speech. God acts in time without changing in himself.

This is the _exact_ problem the medieval Christian Trinitarian tradition solved with the _processions_ doctrine — the eternal generation of the Son and the eternal procession of the Spirit are eternal acts within the Godhead, while the _missions_ (Incarnation, Pentecost) are these eternal acts extending into time. The Muslim tradition does not have this distinction and cannot construct it without becoming Trinitarian.

John didn't have Aquinas's act/potency analysis ready to hand. We do.

## Upgrade 4: The problem of the speaker

This is a phenomenological argument, drawing on contemporary philosophy of language (Searle, Grice, Wittgenstein) and the recovery of speech-act theory.

**The pressure:** speech is the act of a _speaker_. There is no speech without someone speaking. Speech requires intentionality, addresser, addressee, intention to communicate.

If the Quran is God's eternal speech — who is God speaking _to_ eternally? Before creation, there was no creature to address. So either:

- God speaks eternally to himself → which means God has an eternal Other within himself to address → which is the Trinitarian doctrine
- God's "speech" before creation is not speech in any normal sense → which empties the doctrine of meaning
- God's speech is contingent on the existence of a creaturely audience → then it is not eternal, only as-old-as-creation, which the Muʿtazila held and the Sunni rejected

The Christian Trinitarian doctrine _requires_ an eternal Other within God for precisely this reason: the Father eternally addresses the Son, and the Son is the Father's eternal Word, and the Spirit is the eternal love between them. The communicative structure of God is _built into_ God's very being.

The Muslim has no eternal addressee for God's eternal speech. This is a phenomenological dead-end.

John didn't have speech-act theory. We do.

## Upgrade 5: The problem of divine simplicity (the strongest)

This is the argument that, in my view, philosophically _closes_ the Muslim position, and it's the one that should be in every notebook for the rest of your life. It uses tools developed by Aquinas but only fully sharpened in the analytic theology of the last 40 years (Stump, Pruss, Feser).

**Divine simplicity:** God is _not composed_ of parts. He has no separable attributes. He _is_ his attributes. God doesn't _have_ goodness — he _is_ goodness. God doesn't _have_ knowledge — he _is_ knowledge. God doesn't _have_ a Word — he _is_ his Word. Anything else makes God composite, and a composite being depends on its parts, and what depends on parts is not the ultimate metaphysical reality. Simplicity is _required_ by classical theism, which Sunni Islam claims.

**The pressure:** the Sunni position holds that God has _real eternal attributes_ — knowledge, power, life, speech — that are _distinct_ from his essence but _not separate_ from him. This is the Ashʿarī finesse, but it violates divine simplicity. If God's speech is _really distinct_ from God's essence, then God has parts. If it is _not distinct_, then God's speech _is_ God himself — which is exactly the Christian position about the Logos.

The Muslim has three options:

- The Quran's eternal Word is _really distinct_ from God → divine composition → breaks classical theism
- The Quran's eternal Word is _identical_ with God's essence → then the Quran _is_ God → strict identity that even Sunni theology won't go to
- The Quran's eternal Word is _neither identical nor separable_ → distinction-in-unity → which is exactly the Christian doctrine of the Persons of the Trinity

This last option is what the Greek Fathers called _homoousios_ — same essence, distinct hypostasis. It is the precise philosophical move Christianity made to handle the relation of Father, Son, and Spirit while preserving divine simplicity. The Muslim, when pushed, must either adopt this move (and become Trinitarian) or break simplicity (and lose classical theism).

**This is the argument's final form.** Divine simplicity is the metaphysical ground; the Sunni Quran doctrine cannot stand on it; the Christian Trinity _is_ what divine simplicity requires when applied to the data of revelation.

John could not see this in its full form because the doctrine of divine simplicity was not fully articulated until the high scholastics. The analytic theologians of the last 40 years have brought it back into sharp focus. You can deploy it.

## Objection 3 — your real question: "how do we make it a problem that God's speech is God himself, since they don't want to concede this at all?"

This is the deep one. The Muslim resists the Christian conclusion _strenuously_, even when the logic pushes them there. So how do we make the _resistance itself_ expose the inconsistency?

Here's the move. Let me lay it out carefully.

The Muslim wants to say:

- God's speech is _not_ God himself (or it would be a person, which is Christianity)
- God's speech is _not_ a separate being (or it would be polytheism)
- God's speech is _not_ created (or it would be Muʿtazilite)
- God's speech is _not_ nothing (or revelation is empty)

This is a four-way "not." What is left?

The classical Sunni answer is: God's speech is an _eternal attribute_ (_ṣifa_) — neither identical with God's essence nor separate from him, "_lā huwa wa lā ghayruhu_" (neither he nor other than he).

**This is the formula.** This is what they will say. And this is where you press.

The formula "neither identical nor separate" is **either incoherent or it is the Trinity**. There is no third option. Let me show why.

In logic, two things stand in some relation. The possible relations are:

1. **Identical** (A = B)
2. **Separate** (A and B are wholly distinct, no shared being)
3. **Related-but-distinct** (A and B share something while being distinct in some respect)

The Muslim says God's Word is _not_ (1) and _not_ (2). So they must mean (3) — related-but-distinct. The Word shares God's essence (it's not separate) but is distinct in some respect (it's not identical _simpliciter_).

But _that is exactly the Christian doctrine of the Trinity_. The Son shares the Father's essence (_homoousios_ — one _ousia_) but is distinct as Person (_hypostasis_). The Greek Fathers worked this out in the 4th century: _mia ousia, treis hypostaseis_ — one essence, three persons. The relation is real distinction within shared essence.

**So when the Muslim says God's eternal Word is "neither God nor other than God," they have either:**

- (a) Affirmed nothing coherent (just contradicted themselves), or
- (b) Affirmed the Christian distinction of essence and Person without using the Christian vocabulary

**The press:** _The phrase "neither he nor other than he" has a name in philosophy. It is the relation of essence to person, or substance to subsistence. The Greek Fathers in the 4th century gave this relation the name "homoousios" — one essence, distinct hypostasis. You have the same metaphysical structure. You just refuse the vocabulary because you don't want the conclusion._

## The constraints any solution must satisfy

Here's where we get rigorous. For a solution to the Word problem to be coherent, it must satisfy these constraints (and both Christians and Muslims affirm each one independently):

**C1: Divine simplicity.** God is not composed of separable parts. Whatever God is, he is essentially. (Affirmed by Aquinas, Maimonides, al-Ghazālī, Ashʿarī, Maturidi.)

**C2: Eternal completeness.** God is never deprived of his perfections. God is never without his knowledge, his power, his wisdom, his speech. (Affirmed by all classical theists.)

**C3: Genuine contact.** God's Word actually reaches creation — it is not merely a divine name we invent for something that stays sealed inside God. Revelation actually happens. (Affirmed by anyone who believes in scripture.)

**C4: Preservation of monotheism.** Whatever the Word is, it does not amount to two Gods, two ultimates, two competing centers of divinity. (Affirmed by _tawḥīd_ and the Shema both.)

**C5: Personal communicability.** The Word is the kind of thing that _says_ something — that has propositional, intentional, communicative content. A non-speaking "Word" is not a Word. (Affirmed implicitly by both — the Quran says things; Christ said things.)

These five constraints are not Christian impositions. Every classical Sunni theologian accepts each one. So does every patristic and medieval Christian theologian. They are _shared constraints_.

Now: how many positions satisfy all five?

## The four candidate solutions

There are only four logically possible positions on the Word problem. Let me lay them out.

### Position 1: The Word is a created thing

The Word is something God made. Like the world, like angels, like prophets. It comes into being when God creates it.

**Examples:** Muʿtazilite Islam, Jehovah's Witnesses, Arianism, Mormon theology.

**Constraint check:**

- C1 (simplicity): ✓
- C2 (eternal completeness): ✗ — God was without his Word before he created it. God acquired the capacity to speak. This means God changes, and God is incomplete prior to the creation. Violation.
- C3 (genuine contact): ✓
- C4 (monotheism): ✓
- C5 (personal communicability): ✓

**Verdict:** fails C2. This is why Sunni Islam rejected the Muʿtazilites. This is why the Council of Nicaea rejected Arius. The position cannot satisfy eternal completeness.

### Position 2: The Word is an eternal attribute distinct from God's essence

The Word is eternally with God but not identical to God's essence. It is a real, eternal, distinct attribute, "subsistent in" God but not God's essence simpliciter.

**Examples:** Sunni Ashʿarī-Maturidi orthodoxy, the mainstream Sunni position on the uncreated Quran.

**Constraint check:**

- C1 (simplicity): ✗ — if the Word is _really distinct_ from God's essence and yet _eternally exists_, God has eternal parts. This is composition. Violation of simplicity.
- C2 (eternal completeness): ✓
- C3 (genuine contact): ✓
- C4 (monotheism): contested
- C5 (personal communicability): ✓

**Verdict:** fails C1. This is the entire reason the Muʿtazilites attacked the position — they saw the simplicity violation. The Ashʿarīs developed sophisticated finesses to avoid it (the Word is "neither God nor other than God"), but these finesses are formally identical to a contradiction or formally identical to position 4 (the Christian position). They cannot satisfy simplicity without collapsing into Trinitarianism.

### Position 3: The Word is identical with God's essence simpliciter

The Word _is_ God, full stop. No distinction at all. God's Word and God's essence are the same thing without remainder.

**Examples:** strict modalism, some forms of Jewish monotheism, occasionalist accounts.

**Constraint check:**

- C1 (simplicity): ✓
- C2 (eternal completeness): ✓
- C3 (genuine contact): ✗ — if the Word _is_ God's essence with no distinction, then for the Word to enter creation, God's essence must enter creation, which means God becomes a creature, which is pantheism or violation of transcendence. Alternatively, the Word cannot enter creation at all, in which case there is no revelation, no contact. Violation.
- C4 (monotheism): ✓
- C5 (personal communicability): ✗ — speech requires distinction between speaker and what is spoken. Pure identity has nothing to communicate. Violation.

**Verdict:** fails C3 and C5. No major theology actually holds this position because it collapses revelation entirely.

### Position 4: The Word is distinct in person but identical in essence (homoousios)

The Word is _of the same essence_ as God (so divine simplicity preserved — no composition, one essence) but _distinct as Person_ from the Father (so genuine address, genuine communication possible). The Word is eternally generated by the Father, eternally with God, eternally God, but not the Father.

**Examples:** Christian Trinitarianism (Nicene-Constantinopolitan creed, defined 325 and 381).

**Constraint check:**

- C1 (simplicity): ✓ — one essence, not composite. The distinction between Persons is _relational_, not material. (Aquinas's account: the Persons are subsistent relations within the one divine essence.)
- C2 (eternal completeness): ✓ — God is never without his Word.
- C3 (genuine contact): ✓ — the Word can enter creation (incarnation) without God ceasing to be God, because the Person of the Word assumes a human nature without the divine essence being modified.
- C4 (monotheism): ✓ — one essence, one God, three Persons in eternal communion. Not three gods.
- C5 (personal communicability): ✓ — the Father eternally addresses the Son; the Son eternally is the Father's Word; communication is built into the divine being itself.

**Verdict:** satisfies all five constraints.

## The positive conclusion

Look at what just happened. We did not assume Christianity. We did not assume the Bible. We did not assume any Christian premise. We took **five constraints that every classical theist accepts**, surveyed **all four logically possible positions** on the Word problem, and found:

- Position 1 (created Word) fails C2.
- Position 2 (eternal attribute, the Sunni position) fails C1.
- Position 3 (strict identity) fails C3 and C5.
- Position 4 (Trinitarian _homoousios_) satisfies all five.

**Therefore, by elimination of alternatives, the Christian doctrine of the Logos is the only coherent solution to the Word problem.**

This is positive. This is not "Islam is incoherent, therefore Christianity by default." This is: _here is a real problem, here are the constraints any solution must satisfy, here are all the possible solutions, only one works._

The argument moves from negative to positive **without smuggling in any new premise** because the constraints are accepted by the Muslim from the start. The Muslim affirms simplicity, eternal completeness, genuine revelation, monotheism, and personal communicability. They simply have not realized that these affirmations _commit_ them to position 4 once positions 1, 2, and 3 are eliminated.

## Why this is the form you wanted

You said: _"I want to make it that we need something to make us structure things that if it is not coherent then X or Y happens."_

You were reaching for what philosophers call a **transcendental argument** or an **argument from indispensability**. The structure is:

> _X must be true (everyone agrees). For X to be coherent, Y must also be true. Therefore Y._

In our case:

> _Revelation must occur (we both agree). For revelation to be coherent under the constraints we both accept, the Trinitarian Logos doctrine must be true. Therefore the Trinitarian Logos doctrine is true._

This is the same logical form Kant used in his transcendental arguments, the same form Anselm used in the ontological argument, the same form contemporary analytic theologians like Plantinga and Swinburne use in their cumulative-case arguments for Christianity. It is a respected, well-understood form of positive philosophical argument.

And critically: **it bypasses the historical argument entirely.** Your friend cannot escape into "but maybe the Bible is corrupted" or "but Muhammad clarified things." The argument operates at the level of _what any coherent theology must hold_, regardless of which specific revelation one accepts. Once the Muslim accepts the five constraints (which their own theological tradition obligates them to accept), the conclusion follows.

## The argument's name and the form for the notebook

Let me give you the canonical structure to memorize. Call it **the abductive argument from the Word problem** or **the constraint-based argument for the Logos**.

**Step 1: The shared problem.** _God communicates with creatures. His communication is his Word. How is this possible?_

**Step 2: The shared constraints.** _Any coherent solution must preserve: divine simplicity (C1), eternal completeness (C2), genuine contact with creation (C3), monotheism (C4), and personal communicability (C5)._

**Step 3: The exhaustive set of solutions.** _Logically, only four positions are possible: created Word (1), eternal-attribute Word (2), strict-identity Word (3), or distinct-Person-same-essence Word (4)._

**Step 4: The elimination.** _Position 1 fails C2 (eternal completeness). Position 2 fails C1 (simplicity). Position 3 fails C3 and C5 (contact and communicability). Position 4 satisfies all five._

**Step 5: The conclusion.** _Therefore the only coherent account of the Word is the Christian Trinitarian doctrine: the Logos is of the same essence as the Father, eternally distinct as Person, who in time became flesh in Jesus Christ._

Here's the move that closes it, because it doesn't ask them to _agree_ with Christianity — it asks them to _describe_ their own position.

Ask: _what kind of distinction is the distinction between God's essence and God's eternal Word?_

Possible answers:

- **No distinction** → the Word _is_ God's essence simpliciter. But then when the Quran says God _has_ a Word and _speaks_ it, this is meaningless — God's essence doesn't "have" itself or "speak" itself; it just is itself. The eternal Word doctrine collapses into trivial divine self-identity.
- **Real distinction with separation** → two beings. Polytheism. Rejected.
- **Real distinction without separation** → _this is the Trinitarian formula_. One essence, distinct subsistents. The Muslim has now described the Trinity without naming it.
- **Conceptual distinction only** → the Word is just a way _we_ think about God's essence, not anything real in God. But then the Word isn't eternally real in God; it's a feature of our mental representation. This makes the Quran's eternality dependent on human cognition, which is absurd.
- **"It's a mystery, we don't ask"** → fine, but then the Muslim has admitted the same kind of mystery the Christian asserts. They cannot accuse Christianity of incoherence on the Trinity if they refuse to specify the structure of their own analogous position.

There is no escape. Every possible answer either collapses the eternal Word doctrine, lands in polytheism, lands in the Trinity, or admits mystery on equal footing with the Christian.
