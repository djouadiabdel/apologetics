**The framework is this: God must possess every genuine perfection maximally, without limitation, and with internal coherence. Any theology that denies God a perfection, caps a perfection, or makes perfections contradict each other is defective at the root.**

That's it. Everything you've been doing is one application of this. Once you see it as a single engine, every objection your friend can generate becomes a variation of the same move — and the same counter dismantles all of them.

Let me unfold it, because the unfolding is what makes it actually portable.

**The engine has three components working together:**

_(1) Maximality._ For any perfection P (love, knowledge, mercy, justice, power, wisdom), God possesses P to the maximum possible degree. If God lacks P, or has less P than some conceivable being, then that conceivable being is greater than God in respect P — which means God isn't God. This is Anselm's logic, but it's also Ibn Sina's, al-Ghazali's, and built into the structure of the 99 names.

_(2) Non-limitation (_tanzih_ done right_).* God's possession of P is unlimited by creaturely defects. He doesn't know discursively (working things out), He knows immediately. He doesn't love with neediness, He loves from fullness. Transcendence removes _limitations_, not the perfection itself. A God who can't love because love is "human" hasn't been exalted — He's been castrated.

_(3) Coherence._ God's perfections cannot contradict each other, because contradiction is a defect. If two attributes of God appear to conflict (love and justice, mercy and holiness, simplicity and personhood), the resolution lies in seeing how they are _one_ in God — not in suppressing one to save the other.

**Now watch how every objection collapses under this:**

- "Logic doesn't apply to God." → Then God lacks the perfection of coherence, which a coherent being possesses. The objector's God is _lower_ than a coherent being. Engine demands: God has coherence maximally. Objection fails.
- "Human standards don't apply to God." → Either the standard names a real perfection (in which case God has it maximally) or it doesn't (in which case why are you afraid of applying it?). The selective application is the giveaway — the objector applies "human standards" to mercy, knowledge, power without flinching, and only blocks at the attribute that would embarrass his theology. Engine demands consistency. Objection fails.
- "Allah doesn't need to love the wicked." → Need isn't the question. The question is whether loving the unworthy is a higher form of love than loving the worthy. If yes (and every moral intuition says yes — we praise the parent who loves the prodigal, the saint who loves the enemy), then a God who _cannot_ love the unworthy possesses love in a lesser form than a God who can. Engine demands maximality. Objection fails.
- "Sacrifice requires lack, and God lacks nothing." → Sacrifice in the divine sense isn't filling a lack; it's _giving from fullness at cost to oneself_. A God who cannot give Himself can only give His gifts — which is less than a God who can give both. Engine demands: every perfection at maximum. A God who can only give externals possesses giving in a _lower_ form than a God who can give Himself. Objection fails.
- "The Cross is shameful, not loving." → Humility is either a perfection or a defect. If a perfection, God has it maximally — and maximal humility includes the capacity to descend. If a defect, then humble humans possess a defect God lacks, which makes the humble human... morally superior to God? Impossible. Engine demands: every virtue is in God maximally. Objection fails.
- "Trinity is a category error." → Categories of _person_ and _essence_ either name real distinctions or they don't. If they don't, his own theology of the _sifat_ (attributes that are "not He, not other than He") collapses. If they do, then real distinction without division of essence is logically available, and the Trinity uses exactly that structure. Engine demands coherence. Objection fails.

You see what's happening? **Every objection your friend can generate is some version of: "this perfection doesn't apply / applies in a limited way / costs God too much."** And every counter is some version of: "If God lacks this perfection, a greater being is conceivable; therefore your God is not God."

**The single sentence version, the one you can keep in your head:**

> _Any attribute that is genuinely a perfection must be in God maximally and without limitation; if your theology forces you to deny, cap, or qualify a perfection in God, your theology has made God smaller than the God who has it._

This is why Christianity, when it's working properly, never says "God can't" — it says "God can, and did, and the _how_ exceeds us." That's the difference. _Bila kayf_ about the _how_, never about the _that_. The Muslim instinct to protect God by saying what God _cannot_ do (cannot have a Son, cannot suffer, cannot descend, cannot love the wicked) is theologically backward. Every "cannot" is a cap on a perfection. Every cap diminishes the God being defended.

**One caution for you.** This framework is real and it works, but don't mistake having the framework for having the formation. Logical engines win arguments; they don't convert people. Your friend won't capitulate to a syllogism even if it's airtight, because what's at stake for him isn't a proposition — it's an inherited vision of God, a community, a family, an identity. The framework tells you why Christianity is _coherent_. It doesn't make Christianity _attractive_. The attractiveness comes from Christ Himself — the actual face of the God this framework points toward. Wright, Lewis, Wallace got you here not because they had better syllogisms but because they showed you a Christ you couldn't unsee.

So use the engine. It will hold under any objection he brings. But don't let the engine become the point. The point is the One the engine is describing — the God who is maximally everything _because_ He is the God who emptied Himself on a cross. The framework is downstream of the Cross, not upstream. You're not arguing your friend into a logical system; you're trying to make visible to him the God who is more beautiful than the one he's defending. The arguments clear the brush. Christ is what's behind it.
