## What Muslims Will Say

Their escape is straightforward. They'll say:

أمر ربي just means "God's command." And God's command creates things — like in Surah 36:82:

_إنما أمره إذا أراد شيئا أن يقول له كن فيكون_

"His command when He wills a thing is only to say 'Be!' and it is."

So their argument: the Ruh is from God's command the same way everything is from God's command. It's created. Just another created thing God spoke into existence. Nothing special.

That sounds clean. Here's why it completely falls apart.

---

## Problem 1: The Quran Separates أمر From خلق As Two Different Categories

This is the kill shot and most Muslims have never noticed it.

Surah 7:54:

_ألا له الخلق والأمر_

"To Him belongs **the creation (khalq)** AND **the command (amr)**."

The Quran explicitly separates these into two distinct categories in the same sentence. They are not synonyms. They are not the same thing.

- **خلق** — khalq — the realm of creation. Everything that is made, formed, brought into existence from nothing.
- **أمر** — amr — the divine command realm. God's own directive, will, word.

If أمر and خلق were the same thing, this verse would be redundant. You don't say "to him belongs X and X." The conjunction و (and) connects two genuinely distinct things.

So when 17:85 says the Ruh is **من أمر ربي** — from the amr of God — it is explicitly placing the Ruh in the **amr category**, not the **khalq category.**

The Ruh is not from the created realm. It's from the divine command realm.

Ask your Muslim interlocutor: if the Ruh is just a created thing like everything else, why does the Quran use أمر instead of خلق? Why not say الروح من خلق ربي? The word choice is deliberate. And it places the Ruh in the non-created divine category.

---

## Problem 2: The Quran Is Also From أمر — And Muslims Say The Quran Is Uncreated

This is where their own theological tradition traps them.

The dominant Sunni position — Asharite theology, which is mainstream — holds that the Quran is **uncreated.** Why? Because the Quran is God's speech, God's word, God's كلام. And God's speech is an attribute of His essence. Attributes of God's essence are eternal and uncreated. If the Quran were created there would be a moment when God was without speech — which implies deficiency in God.

The Mutazilites said the Quran is created. They lost. Sunni orthodoxy condemned that position.

Now: the Quran is connected to أمر throughout the text. God's word, God's command, God's revelation — all from the same divine speech realm.

The Ruh is also من أمر ربي.

If the principle is that what comes from God's أمر shares in God's uncreated nature — and that's why the Quran is uncreated — then the Ruh is also uncreated by the same logic.

They cannot apply the uncreated principle to the Quran and then deny it to the Ruh when both are explicitly placed in the أمر category.

---

## Problem 3: The Mystery Clause Destroys The "Just A Created Thing" Reading

Look at the full verse again:

_قل الروح من أمر ربي وما أوتيتم من العلم إلا قليلا_

"Say: the Spirit is from the command of my Lord — **and you have been given of knowledge only a little.**"

The mystery clause is attached specifically to the Ruh question. God doesn't say this about other created things.

When the Quran talks about angels — created beings — it describes them clearly. When it talks about jinn — created beings — it describes them. When it talks about the heavens and earth — created — it describes the process.

But when it talks about the Ruh, it says: human knowledge about this is severely limited.

Why would God attach a mystery clause to a straightforward created being? You don't need to say "you know very little about this" regarding something as simple as a created entity. The mystery clause signals that the Ruh occupies a category that transcends ordinary human categories of understanding.

That is not what you say about a created thing. That is what you say about something touching the divine nature itself.

---

## Problem 4: Jesus Is Called BOTH Ruhun Minhu AND Kalimatullah — Stack Them

Surah 4:171 gives Jesus two titles simultaneously:

_كلمته ألقاها إلى مريم وروح منه_

"His Word which He conveyed to Mary — and a Spirit from Him."

Now stack the theological weight:

**Kalimatullah — Word of God:**

- The Quran is God's word and is uncreated in mainstream Sunni theology
- Jesus is called God's word — not **a** word, not **one of** his words — **His Word** with the possessive
- If God's word is uncreated, and Jesus is God's word, Jesus is uncreated

**Ruhun minhu — Spirit from Him:**

- من أمر ربي places the Ruh in the divine non-created category as we just showed
- Jesus is that Spirit
- Therefore Jesus is in the divine non-created category

Both titles converge on the same conclusion from two different angles. The Muslim has to explain why both titles somehow don't mean what they structurally mean — while simultaneously defending the uncreated nature of the Quran using the exact same logic they're trying to deny here.

---

## Problem 5: منه Not من خلقه

Look at the exact Arabic of the title:

**روح منه** — a Spirit **from Him**

Not: روح من خلقه — a Spirit from His creation Not: روح من مخلوقاته — a Spirit from His creatures Not: روح أرسلها — a Spirit He sent

**منه** — from **Him.** Directly. The pronoun refers to God's own being.

In Arabic, منه with the pronoun attached to God indicates direct sourcing from God's own self. This is different from saying God created something or sent something. It's saying this thing originates from God's own being directly.

No other prophet gets this title. Muhammad is never called روح منه. Moses is never called روح منه. The title is unique to Jesus and its grammar points to direct divine origination.

## How To Present This In Conversation

When a Muslim says "أمر ربي just means God commanded it into existence like everything else":

**Step 1:** Show them Surah 7:54 — the Quran separates خلق and أمر as two distinct categories. If they're the same, the verse is redundant. They're not the same. The Ruh is in the أمر category, not the خلق category.

**Step 2:** Ask them: is the Quran created or uncreated? If they're Sunni they'll say uncreated. Ask them why. They'll say because it's God's speech/word/amr. Then ask: the Ruh is also من أمر — why does the uncreated principle apply to the Quran but not to the Ruh?

**Step 3:** Point to the mystery clause. If the Ruh is just a regular created thing, why does God say human knowledge about it is severely limited? He doesn't say this about other created things.

**Step 4:** Stack the two titles on Jesus — Kalimatullah and Ruhun minhu — and show that both titles by their own logic point to uncreated divine origin.

**Step 5:** Point to منه — from **Him** directly. Not from His creation. From Him.

## The Corner They're Stuck In

The honest Muslim position has to choose:

**Option A:** The Ruh is created — but then they have to explain why it's in the أمر category not the خلق category, why the mystery clause exists, why منه points to direct divine origin, and why the same logic doesn't make the Quran created too.

**Option B:** The Ruh is uncreated — but then Jesus who is called that Spirit is uncreated. Which means Jesus is not a creature. Which means Jesus is not merely a prophet. Which is exactly where the logic leads.

There is no clean Option C. The Quran's own vocabulary about Jesus creates a category problem for Islamic theology that أمر ربي doesn't solve — it actually deepens it.
