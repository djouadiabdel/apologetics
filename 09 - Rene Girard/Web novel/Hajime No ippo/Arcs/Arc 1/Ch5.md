Ch5

High on his thoughts, Ippo marched to the date spot. His steps were meticulous, precise — part of training, and a part of rest is to walk a certain way. But Ippo chose to walk as if it was another morning run, each step carrying weight, a weight he used to feel and shift into the strength behind every punch, every dash, every evasion.

In the city, people who see him do not recognise him instantly. He is walking like a boxer with intent, but that intent is shadowed by the air around him. A featherweight champion still looks like a normal person walking by. He is holding weapons as hands, but he does look normal, does look like a human. Everyone knows this. Everyone internalises it.

Ippo is nervous thinking about the date. He is concerned with his coach's words — to rest, to move less, to box less, to heal, to be back again and fight for a stage bigger than Japan. A stage that will probably make his hands tremble, make them itch in delight but fear.

He reaches the café, looks at Kumi, and smiles.

---

Kumi.

I don't know why I came this early. The café is almost empty. The noise we experience in the rush hours, the noise that can lighten the headache in my head — and I don't think this is a real headache. Medication did not help to relieve it and I still feel pressure, like water inside my head, like two hands I saw in a dream on my head trying to squeeze it as hard as possible. Even though they are gentle, not trying to harm me. It feels like the café's emptiness is making me think of this more and more, and the stress is still hunting me regardless of what I do. And here I am drinking milk and oh... it's Ippo.

He walks in a shy way, reaching inside the shop. He instantly spotted me even though I was hidden, technically — I thought he would at least have to put in some effort to find me, but he did it instantly, as if he has more eyes than any normal human being.

He sits and greets me with a smile. I look at his face — rough and soft at the same time. The face of a boxer and the face of a nice person. To me boxing can't be done by a person like Ippo, and till now, he is a Japan champion, and I can't look at him and guess that he is a boxer unless I look at his hands. At the bruise next to his eyes, how blue it got, swollen and full of liquid. All the damage he is taking, all the hard work he is putting in, is present on his face like a sign telling everyone to look at me — I am struggling and I smile and I suffer yet I never give up. This is Ippo. He is acting like there is something on his face just because I looked at him. Ippo is just Ippo.

And his hands — rough, harsh, yet non-violent. The callous in his palms, the hardening, just like my brother's. Ippo's hands are not small, they are big. It feels like Ippo is trying to lift something heavy with them. The hands look like they do not know what they are doing, as if they are programmed, as if they are robots that fulfil a prophecy. This is why they are special to Ippo in my eyes. But I am not sure that Ippo sees it that way. Ippo just loves boxing, even if I don't understand it.

---

Ippo.

Why is Kumi looking at me like that? Is there something wrong with my face? The bruises are from Sawamura. I took care of myself before I came, but the fight left what it left.

---

Kumi: So what are you doing these days?

Ippo replied that he is taking care of his health, that he wants to get back again to boxing as fast as possible. The coach ordered him to rest and he is obeying, because the coach is the one who guides him and knows everything there is to know. He is eating well, doing less work on the boat, not even training. What about you?

Kumi sighed. She felt like her question did not include boxing. She wanted to know what Ippo thinks — his future, everything. But she lets it go.

Kumi: I am doing well too. I have been really busy with work at the hospital, studying more, because there is no stopping when it comes to human health. And frankly it is amazing. I chose this profession specifically because I wanted to be able to help my brother, Ryo.

She said that to Ippo and kept the rest to herself. Now I am taking full accountability for even Ippo, because they are both always bruised, hurt and in pain. I regularly see the physical toll the sport takes on athletes and I fear for the long-term health of those I love. But I don't know exactly what this is, and still Ryo and Ippo are both healthy now.

Ippo smiled and got excited for Kumi because she is doing something important to herself. As she mentioned the purpose of her goal, Ippo remembered his desire — to be strong, to know what those hands are made of, to struggle, to suffer, to be himself. He had to ask her: what does that word, purpose, mean to her? What is her desire in life?

Kumi: I want something else other than this life of action. I want a safe, quiet, happy life, free from the dangers of life. Frankly I have seen enough. I don't want this life to be full of blood. The life of the people in the hospital always makes me sad, but the fact that I can help even a little bit is a relief.

Ippo finds this strange. How could one want something like that? How could one just want something so simple? He envies her and at the same time wonders about the meaning of such words. About the duality of two opposites, his world and her world. Is it possible for two worlds to co-exist and for both to be correct? Or does one have to take control of the other, just like in boxing — there is a winner and a loser, a champion and the others, a hierarchy where only one wins. Life feels like it should be like that. But maybe life isn't boxing. Maybe both can co-exist.

Kumi: So what about you, Ippo? Why do you do what you do?

Ippo listens to this question. He knows he wants more. He wants to take over, to take the road of his father — the struggle, the pain, the strength. He wants to find his tool, just like his father had the road, the ship, the sea. His father is now son of the sea. Ippo said to himself: I am the son of the son of the sea. But I am not the sea. I am not a fisherman. My father had a tool and I do not know what these hands will hold, what they can collect, what they can amount to.

I know. I want more. But I can't tell her that I want more than her. This is not fair to her. Both worlds can co-exist because life is not boxing.

Ippo: I want to be strong. I want strength.

Kumi: Strength? What do you mean by strength? What is strength?

Ippo looks at her. His smile, his confidence — all of it left his face, left the table, left the atmosphere of the café. He is now in need of an answer to a question he thought he knew.

What is strength. Ippo is scared because he doesn't know.

What is strength. Ippo is scared because he doesn't know.

And Kumi does not answer. She does not fill it. She holds the question in the air between them the way you hold something breakable, carefully, without squeezing.

Ippo waited for her to say something. He did not know he was waiting. He thought he was just sitting. But his hands were on the table and his hands were very still and somewhere in the back of his chest something was pulling tight, like the rope in the corner of the ring before the bell, like the moment before a punch lands when you already know it is coming and there is nothing left to do but receive it.

Kumi looked at him. Just looked. No answer. No question. Just her eyes on his face, and in them something he could not read, something that was not boxing, something he had no training for.

The silence sat between them.

And that was when Ippo understood, without words, without reason, the way the body understands a thing before the mind catches up — that something was wrong. Not with the fight. Not with the hand. Not with the training. Something else. Something that had no name in any gym he had ever walked into. He did not know what it was. He only knew it was big. He only knew it was his. And he only knew, sitting across from Kumi in an almost empty café with his hands on the table, that he had never prepared for it.

**The three movements of Chapter 3:**

The date has to move like this — Ippo arrives already somewhere else. He is physically present and mentally in the gym. That's not a flaw to fix, that's the wound on display. The reader sees it before Kumi names it.

Then the memory layer arrives not because Ippo summons it but because something small forces it. The way Kumi holds her cup. The shape of her hands. Something that has nothing to do with boxing pulls him suddenly to the boat, the knot, his father's hands moving without looking. That image compounds into the rod. The rod that felt wrong. Too light. Like pretending.

Then Kumi asks the question and the chapter stops moving forward and goes deep instead.

**The specific problem you need to solve:**

Kumi's question cannot arrive too clean. "What is strong?" lands differently depending on what precedes it. She needs to get there through something — a small observation she makes about him, something she notices across the table that she almost doesn't say. Her asking has to cost her something too. She is not a therapist. She is a woman sitting across from someone she loves who keeps answering questions she hasn't asked.

**The silence after the question is the chapter's real scene.**

Don't let Ippo answer. Don't let him not-answer in a way that resolves it. Let him start three different answers internally — the boxing answer, the father answer, the nothing answer — and find that none of them fit what she's actually asking. The silence gets long. Kumi doesn't fill it. That's what makes her different from everyone else in his life. Everyone else fills the silence with boxing. She just waits.

He will eventually say something that sounds like an answer but is actually a deflection. She will know. The reader will know. He will almost know.

**The closing image:**

Not resolution. His hands on the table on the walk home or the ride back. He looks at them the way he looks at them in the dark at night. But now the question is different. Before it was _what are these hands for._ After Kumi it is something closer to — _what are these hands for that isn't this._

He can't complete it yet. That incompleteness is what you need.
