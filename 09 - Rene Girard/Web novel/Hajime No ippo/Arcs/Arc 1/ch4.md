I was in the ring.

Not fighting. Just standing in the center of it, in the dark, with the ropes around me and nowhere to go. And someone was on the floor. I could not see who. I could only see the hand — palm up, fingers slightly open, the way hands go when they stop deciding anything.

I woke up before I could look at the face.

I lay there for a moment with the ceiling above me and the morning light coming in soft through the curtain and I made myself breathe. In. Out. The dream dissolving the way they do, leaving only the feeling behind. The hand on the canvas. Palm up.

My stomach hurt.

Not sick exactly. The other kind. The kind that lives just below the ribs and does not respond to anything you do — not eating, not not eating, not lying still. Just present. Like a stone someone placed there while I slept.

The last thing I did was stare at the roof of my room, there is something about observing it non stop, there is something about it that I can not comprehend the architecture, the lamp or is it nothing? And the first thing I did today was that too but this time the painful feeling I had in my dreams was there again and it is scary because it feels like my brother is there, and I am worried about him just like I would do on those big nights of his in the ring, but today there is nothing like that, today I am meeting ippo and I can't know why my stomach is hurting me as if I am going to a ring.

I turned onto my side and looked at the window.

There is a mark on the sill. Small, dark, the shape of nothing in particular. I have looked at it a thousand mornings. But today it caught the light differently — or I looked at it differently — and for one second it looked like a stain, the brownish kind, and I thought: blood.

And then I was back in the dream.

Not all the way. Just enough to feel it — the ring, the dark, the hand on the canvas. I did not know whose hand it was and that was the worst part. It could be Ryo's. It could be Ippo's. In the dream it did not matter which, and somehow that was the thing I could not shake. That my sleeping mind had stopped distinguishing between them.

I got up. Stood at the window properly this time, both feet on the floor, arms crossed against the morning cold.

The street below was ordinary. A woman walking a dog. A man on a bicycle. A kid with a backpack too large for his body. Everyone going somewhere with no particular weight to it, just the ordinary movement of a morning, and I stood there watching them and tried to locate the stone under my ribs and understand what it was made of.

Ippo today.

We said we would meet at eleven. It is not a fight night. There is no reason for my stomach to behave this way.

But here is what I know about myself: my body does not understand the difference between the nights and the ordinary days. It keeps its own calendar. Fight nights, hospital visits, the morning Ryo told me his next opponent — my stomach has never learned to distinguish. Someone I care about is going to a place where they can be hurt, and the mechanism in me that responds to that does not wait for confirmation. It starts early. It starts in dreams.

I thought about Ippo's hands.

I have watched those hands through a lot of fights now. I watched them against Sendo — quick, low, driving forward, always forward, the way Ippo does everything. I remember being scared before that fight and then watching him and thinking: he is not scared at all. He was almost peaceful in the ring that night. Like the ring was the one place where everything in him made sense.

And then Date.

I don't watch replays. I watched it once, the whole thing, and that was enough. What I remember is not the technical part — I do not understand the technical part the way the gym men do. What I remember is a specific round, late, when Ippo took a punch and his legs did something wrong underneath him and he did not go down but the way he looked in that half second — before his body caught itself, before the training took over — there was something in his face that I had not seen before.

Not pain. Something underneath pain.

Like a question.

I have thought about that look many times since. I think it was the first time I understood something about Ippo's style that he has never said in words. He absorbs things. On purpose. That is not a flaw in his technique — it is the technique. He takes the hit to land the hit. He steps into damage the way other people step back from it. And I do not know if he understands what that means across ten years, twenty years, a whole career of choosing that.

Ryo fights differently. Ryo is dangerous to be near — everyone knows this, I know this — but Ryo keeps distance. Ryo punishes. He does not offer himself to be hit. I used to think that was coldness. Now I think it might just be a different calculation.

Ippo's calculation scares me.

Not because I think he will lose. Because I think he will keep choosing it even when the cost goes up. Because I have seen his face after bad nights — not defeated, something stranger than defeated — and the next morning he is running, and the morning after that he is in the gym, and the thing that happened in the ring has already been folded into the reason to continue. He does not seem to accumulate damage the way I think he should. He accumulates something else. Determination maybe. Appetite. I don't have a word for it.

I got dressed slowly.

The stone under my ribs was still there. I looked at myself in the mirror for a moment — straightened my jacket, looked at my face, tried to find something there that looked like someone who was just going to meet a friend on an ordinary morning.

I looked like someone going to a fight.

I turned away from the mirror.

Outside the mark on the window sill was just a mark again. The light had shifted. The street was emptier now — the woman with the dog gone, the kid with the backpack gone, just the ordinary quiet of mid-morning.

I picked up my bag.

The dream was already fading the way dreams do. The ring, the dark, the hand on the canvas — the edges going soft. By tonight I would not remember the specific shape of it. But the stone would still be there. It always is.

I have been in the center of boxing my whole life without choosing it. My brother first, then the gym, then Ippo. I have never been in the ring. But I know what it feels like to absorb something you did not choose to absorb. To take the hit because someone you love threw it and there was nowhere to step back to.

Maybe that is not so different.

I opened the door and went out into the morning.
