**BLOCK 1 — The gym before Ippo arrives**

Beat 1: Dawn light hitting the gym exterior. The building exists before anyone fills it. One detail — the sign, or the condensation on the windows, or the sound of the street before the door opens.

- _Reminds of:_ the darkness Coach walked into at the end of Ch.17
- _Opens:_ morning answers darkness, but does it actually change anything?


The cold morning sun hits the back of Kamogawa as he walks toward the gym door, his legs moving steadily. Unlike his usual self, the sun this time touches his back and makes him remember what he experienced last night as he was closing the gym.

The morning keeps giving him hints, yet he walks mindlessly, his hands slowly reaching into his pocket for the keys. His mouth clenches. He looks to his right, left, and behind him — the body's reactions slower than his old self, but he still has to stay sharp.

The key rises slowly to the lock. The sound of it turning brings him a small joy. He looks to his right. No one walking by. But the wind hits him and reminds him that again he is the first person home. To his left, the sun echoes a new song for a new morning, the same way it did in the old days when he was a young man who had the world to conquer. He feels a bitterness even now, even with the belt — the belt of the world. It rubs against him because this is exactly the darkness Coach walked into last night and ran from.

The door is unlocked. His heart beats faster at the sight of the ring with no one inside it, but then the disappointment kicks in. All that carefulness he exhibits, and still he wishes someone had tried to sneak in. He remembers the day someone tried to break into the gym, which ended as a comedic thing, and since then no one approaches the place.

His first step in and he feels like he is swallowed up — by the gloves, urging him to wear them, by the mouth guards, and finally by the headgear. His body's temperature rises even though seconds earlier he was freezing from the cold. He walks, and with each step his heart beats twice, and the closer he is to the gloves, the more his heart is singing.

Carrying one, holding it, he feels the leather and the thousands of micro tears in the surface — each one a sign of the thousands of hours of painful training his students endured. And still it doesn't feel as good as the old days, when he was the one who held it like his beloved and favorite tool.

The gym doesn't know that today is different. And Coach doesn't either. To them it is another day with another rhythm, full of sweat and tears. As the sun climbs and reaches into the darkest corners — corners it said goodbye to once, and yet again it came — athletes arrive one by one, in pairs, to punch the bag. The sound of the punches, for Coach, is a measure of skill.

He listens to it like a symphony, a sign of his hard work with his students. It reminds him that for years upon years he has heard this sound from the world of boxing, daily, nonstop, even when he was sick. The only thing missing was Ippo's.

The gym doesn't know today is different. Only Ippo knows.

---

Ippo at the door. Breathing heavier, full of energy, shadow boxing his way inside. No pain in his back or his knees. A smirk on his face as he wonders if everything has finally healed. The right hook, the left hook, the side rotations of his head evading punches — the only thing missing is a glove and a punching bag. To him, seeing that is the world. Takamura and Aoki and Kimura are waiting for him.

As he gets closer he notices the ring — the one where he fought Miyata and knocked him down for the first time. That was his first victory. All he can remember is his heart racing faster, the ropes silent, and everyone's eyes on the broken and the fallen.

Only a few seconds of stillness and Ippo is excited for more. Every time he walks through the door of this gym it feels the same, and every time he looks at the ring he sees a vision of himself — as THE BOXER.

Itagaki spots Ippo but he is in the middle of training. He can't ditch his coach to talk to him, so he shrugs it off.

The last time Ippo was here, he was broken. Now, like every other time before that, he feels restless to train. Because now, in his own eyes, he is fixed.

The smell of sweat. The air that can't exist without Ippo's smile. The bags being punched — not for the sake of anger or punishment but for one thing and only one: music. A symphony for the ears. Then his friends. Kimura's footwork drills. Aoki's basics shining once again. Takamura's heavy punches that would kill a wild animal. Some guys nod, some don't look up. Ippo wants it to feel like home again.

Kamogawa spots Ippo with his dreamy face and energetic movements. He grins at the kid, then screams at him to not be lazy and waste another day. Ippo is surprised, but home isn't home without seriousness.

---

Kamogawa gets closer and touches Ippo's quads, his hamstrings, searching for the tendon's density and smoothness. He moves to the calves and Achilles tendon — they are the base. Then up, raising to the lower back, the V-taper, and finally the traps and shoulders.

Coach hums as he looks at the ground. He remembers Ippo's body at the weigh-in ceremony against Sendo, the Japan title fight. Ippo was huge then. He looks him in the face now. Ippo feels like a punch is coming, yet he doesn't react, his eyes shifting left and right, until Coach says: "Kid, we have a lot to do."

Every fighter Coach ever had, he built from zero. Every time he did it. This time will be no different. But Coach catches himself in his own thoughts, and touches Ippo's shoulder — as if to apologize. Across the gym, Itagaki gets hit by his trainer because he was lost in the moment watching Ippo. He earns a break, and uses it to observe.

But Ippo is scared. Coach is acting conflicted. He gets ordered to go for a run — the usual run Coach always asks him to do. The test of superiority or mediocrity. But this time, Ippo is wearing a heavy vest.

The restlessness is going to be satisfied, Ippo thinks, as he puts on the vest and starts jumping in place, ready for the signal to press the gas and go. But Itagaki feels like this is his moment. He begs Coach to let him join the run, even though it is overkill after the hours he's already spent training.

Kamogawa tells them not to be soft. As fast as possible. If they fail, he will punish them. Itagaki now understands that this is trouble, and that he should not have pushed it this hard — especially after the last hours of training.

---

Takamura saw that look on Coach's face last night. So he is monitoring them all — Ippo, Kamogawa, Yagi's movements — all of it. After all, Takamura is the face of the gym. And ironically, with a smirk, nodding over and over to himself as if someone is praising him, he feels like it's his duty.

He will keep time. Takamura knows the route. He knows Ippo's numbers and expects a decline because of the lost fitness. The question is how much. Coach watches Ippo and Itagaki run and make a heavy noise with their feet striking the ground on their way out. And it is again in his face — the kid's back. He grabs his crutch and throws it at Aoki.

Aoki screams in shock — "Whaaaat happened?!" — which provokes Kamogawa even more. He gets on a chase behind him, with Kimura and Takamura dying in the corner at Aoki's misfortune.

---

Ippo's usual route is six miles, tracing paths near the Arakawa River. The standard for Ippo has always been perfection. He started energetic but behind Itagaki.

The distance between them was so large that Itagaki felt like it was just a casual day for a winner. But at a certain point he stopped smiling. A noise behind him was catching up. His heart beat faster but it was no use. He felt it getting closer, so close he felt a punch coming his way. He closed his eyes and braced — but nothing.

Ippo surpassed him. The distance grew larger. Itagaki could not recover.

Forty-five minutes, Coach and Takamura counted.

Takamura, arms crossed, serious, looked at Coach from a distance. Coach looked troubled. Takamura said the fitness is gone.

He had to say it because for him it was the truth. Ippo knew it too. But this looks like another chapter Ippo has already lived — just like his first days of training, when he couldn't even finish a round.

Coach started raising his voice gradually, making Ippo understand that with this effort he wouldn't last thirty seconds against a world-level threat. Itagaki didn't go unnoticed either — now he is stuck with Ippo on another round of grueling core and legwork, ending with intense shadow boxing.

Coach screams. Breathing betrays Ippo as he starts struggling.

Takamura walks slowly to Ippo, looks down at him, and tells him to rest. Then he throws the boxing gloves and the headguard to him.

Coach lets him do as he pleases this time. Takamura says: "I am the world champion. I am the standard. And right now you are just a worm. But I bestow upon you the gift of fighting me. You got five minutes. If you can survive a punch, you are not a dead case."

---

Takamura saw that look on Coach's face last night. He wants to confirm it. He takes a glance at Coach and finds him just watching from afar, with a look Takamura has never seen before. Just observing.

Aoki starts praying for Ippo's soul. Kimura stays busy to not anger Takamura. This time, Takamura is serious.

Takamura starts drifting right and left, peek-a-boo style, mimicking Ippo. Itagaki wants to look away, yet he doesn't. The closer Takamura gets, the more Ippo plants himself like a rock, ready to take the punch. A straight comes his way — obvious, but behind it, a devilish sensation.

He avoids it. But feels like his head would have been ripped apart if he took it. Takamura removes his gloves and applauds Ippo, surprised that he could avoid it. But Ippo is disappointed — he thought the whole exam was to accept the punch.

---

Itagaki was there. He watched what Ippo did. The reaction speed — it increased, even after a long period of rest. Ippo watched Takamura's body the way Coach watched Ippo's. The student measured Takamura, measured himself against the thing he hasn't reached, and was able to survive it.

Takamura said: "You are not useless after all."

Coach, carried away but this time relieved, went to Ippo, gave him a structure to follow, then went inside the office. This time he closed the door behind him.

---

Ippo is doing his work. Sweating, grinding, trying to find rhythm. Itagaki is still trying to understand — why are Coach and Takamura satisfied, even when Ippo was not? The goal was to avoid the punch. And now Ippo is doing shadow boxing, trying to evade punches left and right, mimicking what should be the Dempsey Roll but never rolling — just a casual peek-a-boo.

A foot tapping. Fingers tightening on a wrap. A jaw shifting.

Itagaki's eyes are wide open. He observes the footwork — how Ippo's feet tap softly on the ground compared to how they used to. He used to look like a snake dragging himself. Now he is jumping.

Once again Itagaki feels like he is a step behind. He can't even surpass Ippo in speed, the one thing he thought he had.

"Footwork, huh?" Itagaki says, looking puzzled. He lifts his hand to his chest. Someone punched him there. The sound of Ippo's feet on the ground, the punches everyone is making — all of it lays heavy on him. This feels like high school again. Always being topped by that one guy.

When will I stop feeling like a failure, he said.
