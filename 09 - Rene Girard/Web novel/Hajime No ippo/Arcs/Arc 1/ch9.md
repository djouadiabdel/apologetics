
_What would I even say to her?_

I tried to assemble a sentence. Something professional. Something that didn't sound like a nurse who made a promise she couldn't keep to a man she — something clinical. Start clinical.

_Dr. Yamaguchi, I've been reading about chronic stress on the musculotendinous unit and I wanted to ask —_

No. Too stiff. She'd see through it in a second.

_I have a question about cumulative joint degradation in a patient who —_

He's not a patient. He's not her patient.

_There's someone I care about who performs high-intensity rotational movements and I believe his body is —_

Someone I care about. She could already hear the pause on the other end. The careful, professional silence of a doctor recalibrating who she was really talking to.

_What do you actually want to ask her, Kumi?_

The real question. The one underneath all the rehearsed ones. The one she had been circling since she opened the book:

_Is it already too late?_

No — worse than that.

_If it's not too late, what does helping him actually look like? And is it something I can do, or is it something I just have to watch someone else do — or something no one can do at all?_

She put the book down on the floor beside her. Open. Spine up. The page she'd been reading still visible.

The phone sat on the edge of the shelf where she'd left it. Not far. Not close. The kind of distance that only matters when you're deciding whether to reach for it.

The room was still quiet. But the quiet had changed. At the start it had pressed against her like a wall — something heavy, something that kept her still. Now it was thinner. Hollow. It was the silence before a phone call she hadn't made yet.

Same room. Same floor. Same light.

She reached for the phone. Held it. Did not dial.

Not yet.
