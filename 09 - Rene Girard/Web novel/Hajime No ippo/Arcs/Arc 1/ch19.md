# Chapter 19

Walking into the gym I can feel my heart racing. The closer I get, the flashier the surroundings feel — the higher the silk-smooth contact of feet on the ground, athletes who always try to be one of two things: a snake, pure fighter planted and coiled, or a bee gently tipping the ground to dance from side to side.

The punches that strike fear into your soul. The booming effect on the punching bag — I can recognize a few of them. The heaviest one gives you the feeling that someone is trying to murder the bag. Others work the bag with their coach, the soft strikes and the rhythm they create mimicking a perfect sequence that only muscle memory can produce.

Muscle memory of someone who has given it his all.

The door gets closer and closer. I see the windows open and I get my first glimpse at the inside of the gym.

The ring sits in the middle. No one is inside of it, yet it has a few spots of blood spilled from sparring. A few athletes outside the ring, thrown down on the ground with their eyes on the floor, chests heaving. Yes, that checks out.

The intent to kill that comes from the farthest spot in the gym — I see the swiftness of the upper body movements, his lower body planted on the ground, immovable, capable of rotating all of his energy toward his punches.

Takamura is always there getting ready for his fight. For the World Title.

I get inside the gym and only now I am capable of hearing another voice. Not of punches. Not of air — no, not air exactly, but something is moving through the air, trying to be hit by it, pushing it away from its spot.

I mistake it for a whip at first, but who would even use one here? Maybe Kamogawa when he's trying to smack Aoki or Kimura — but why did I even think of that?

No, it is not a whip. It has a certain rhythm to it. I can hear the feet being planted on the ground, yet only the front part of them, the foot rotating here and there, pivoting on something I can't yet see.

I turn my head toward that sound and as I do I feel my heart beating faster. All I can do is put my hand on my chest to rest it a bit — not that it would, but at least so that I am not buried under it.

I have listened to this sound over and over again in the Korakuen Hall. I listened to it in the gym multiple times. Frankly enough, I have memorized it.

The body rotation swings — the kind a person would train to get his head work perfect. No, beyond perfect. To distribute his weight to such a level that it becomes a movement that has a name.

My eyes land on a featherweight boxer with Kamogawa, who is surprisingly not loud at all, just observing his student.

It's Ippo.

I am hesitant on going to him or starting my training, but Takamura cuts my line of thought. He is already heading toward Ippo. Not casually — deliberately, the way he walks when something has caught his attention, and Takamura's attention is not something that lands on just anyone.

My feet stop moving before I tell them to.

He mumbles something to Ippo. Ippo stops training. Coach doesn't say a single thing to either of them, just watches. My throat tightens and I swallow it down, pretending I'm adjusting my wraps. Takamura — the world champion — has walked away from his own training to say something to Ippo, and Coach is letting it happen, and I am standing here with my hands half-wrapped like I'm not even in the room.

Is Coach acting weird, or did I get hit in the head?

Anyways, whatever Takamura is instructing Ippo to do, it makes me hurry up and warm up. Maybe I can at least try to mimic Ippo's movements and compete with him. That's what I'm here for.

Takamura still has his eyes on Ippo as Aoki and Kimura get close to the scene — the field that I am not part of — but Aoki suddenly starts to laugh at Kimura. Takamura surges a punch between them that makes them both look the other direction while whistling. Yes, they are trying to look innocent.

The funny thing is that Ippo is in his corner, not worried at all about them, as they both run away to their own corner to avoid the wrath of Takamura, who left his training to observe something in Ippo.

Something I can't see yet.

I came here warm. The road was a long way here and in the middle of it I had been acting a little weird, shadow boxing here and there, so the warmup shouldn't take long. I can do my thing and follow onto Ippo's movements.

A couple of squats and push-ups, some soft shadow boxing and I should be at it. Then I have to warm up my footwork memory, get in the zone in case something will happen — after all, many things happen in this gym.

In the middle of my work I see Ippo has stopped being observed by Coach and Takamura. Each to their corner. Ippo is now shadow boxing alone.

I will do as he does.

But his movements make me unable to even take it seriously. What is he doing? All that body swinging, for what? His head dips left, dips right, his torso rotating like something loose. There is no guard in that. There is no structure. I can hit him at any time if I wanted to. This serves no purpose in boxing.

But hey — my shadow boxing will be around everything I would do if I were to face Ippo, and it is working well. I throw my jab, step to the angle, throw the one-two.

My punches are not sharp enough though.

I reset. I look at Ippo again. I take his rhythm into my head, his weird cranky movement, and I shadow box against that version. My jab goes out — and in my mind, his head isn't there anymore. The body dipped. I adjust, throw the straight — and the Ippo in my head is already somewhere else, rotating underneath it.

Wait. Wait wait wait.

I throw again. He isn't dodging — he is just moving like he always moves, that swinging, and my punches go where he was. My feet stop. My lead foot is flat on the ground. When did that happen?

I am stuck in my spot, ready to eat a punch that hasn't been thrown yet.

I try again. This time I tell myself to move, use my footwork, be the bee. But the second I mirror his rhythm in my head, my body locks up. Not because he is fast — because there is no gap. The openings I see close the second I commit to them. My weight shifts forward to throw and I am already off-balance because his body was never where my eyes said it was.

Every time I reset I am getting hit. Every time I move, I stop relying on my footwork and plant myself like a tree with holes opening up in my defense, one after the other.

Coach noticed me. He can see I am not moving the way I used to. He has told me about that before — the planting, the freezing — but still, isn't this the only way to be if I were actually facing Ippo with that movement?

Coach just doesn't get it, I guess. I am looking at Ippo and not really doing something different from my usual sequence. But in the end, no matter what angle I take, I am always the one getting hit.

I drop my hands.

Weird.

Let me just forget about it and try to do some leg work drills. Maybe this is the part that I should have started with from the beginning — and cutting my warmup short to chase behind Ippo has made me lose my touch.

I can feel it. I am getting the hang of it. The footwork is clicking again, and I think I am ready —

The door is getting opened.

That's weird. All the athletes are here and we are not yet close enough to finishing our session. Ippo is there, Takamura, Aoki and Kimura — all the people I know are here. Who is that?

The footsteps are getting closer. Not to me. I haven't turned around yet. They are getting closer to Kamogawa, who is standing next to Ippo.

The steps are not the steps of one person. It's a group of people, and it seems like everyone has caught on. They turned around, and instinctively I turned around too.

Another coach with his boxer and staff. They are here.

My hands go cold. I feel it in my fingertips first, like the blood just left them.

The boxer — he is tall, hair pointy, broad shoulders. My jaw locks before my brain catches up, and my feet, the feet that were just finding their rhythm again, are cemented to the floor.

That's the same man whose every single twitch in the ring I know by heart. I have studied him the way you study something you can't beat. Every feint, every shift in weight, every breath between combinations. I know all of it and none of it has ever been enough.

What is he doing here?

He is the one who took the Inter-High School Title from me three times. I couldn't even be on par with him once. All he has to show for is his family's wealth, and he thinks boxing is as cheap as his money — that he can use it to prove he wasn't just some rich brat. But the worst part, the part that sits in my chest like a stone, is that he did prove it. Every single time. Against me.

Why is he here?

His eyes are not on me. They pass right through me like I am part of the equipment. They are on Kamogawa. They are on Ippo.

Is he going for the Japanese Title? The Japanese Title, which is held by Ippo Makunouchi?

Before me?

My hand is on my chest again. The same hand, the same spot. But this time it isn't Ippo's rhythm making my heart race.

Imai. I can't let you do that.
