**Movement 1 — The Psalm**

Someone sees Ippo before Ippo sees himself.

This is not a story about a broken boxer. It is a story about a man who is completely certain — and everyone who loves him is quietly not. That gap is where the reader lives. Do not close it. Do not let Ippo doubt himself yet. His certainty is not stupidity. It is the most human thing about him. We are always the last to see the thing that is most visible about us.

Ippo walks into every conversation thinking the conversation is about boxing. It is never only about boxing. But he cannot see that.

Kumi calls him and asks _what are you going to do about everything_ — she means: about us, about your life, about who you are when the ring is gone. Ippo hears: what is your next technical adjustment. That is the gap. She is standing in front of a whole person. He is standing in front of a fighter.

Itagaki watches Ippo in the gym and sees a man who has nothing else. Not a man who loves boxing — a man who has no door out of it. Itagaki is young enough and far enough outside it to feel the difference. He doesn't name it. He just watches with that uneasy feeling you get when you see something that should worry you but you don't know why yet.

That is what they both see: **Ippo has no life outside the ring, and he doesn't know it, and it is starting to cost him things he doesn't realize he is losing.**

Kumi is losing him to it. Itagaki is watching him disappear into it.

Ippo thinks everything is fine. He is recovering, he is planning, he is moving forward.

That is the gap.

Kumi sees it. Itagaki sees it. The reader sees it. Ippo does not.

Write from inside that dramatic irony and do not explain it. The tenderness comes from the gap itself. The moment you name the gap, it closes.

The hands are present but unexamined. They itch. They reach. They move before he decides to move them. Do not explain what the hands mean. The hands do not know what they mean yet either. They are asking a question the rest of the story will answer.

Ippo's desire for the ring feels like his own. It has never been questioned. It arrived through Takamura, through Kamogawa, through every opponent who needed to beat him — but Ippo does not know this. To him it is native. Like hunger. Like the morning.

Do not let him question it yet. Let Kumi question it. Let Itagaki watch. Let the reader feel the weight of what nobody is saying.

Your job in these chapters is not to move the plot. Your job is to make the reader love Ippo so much that what is coming will cost them something.

Write what Ippo notices. Not what he thinks.

Find the hands. Begin.
