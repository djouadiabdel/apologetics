That pattern is not a Rubin problem. It's not a scheduling problem. It's not a pull problem. It's a completion-reward problem. Your brain treats the minimum as a finish line. The moment you cross it, the task is "done" in your nervous system, and all motivation drains. This is incredibly common and it has nothing to do with being lazy or half-assing. It's how your brain processes goals.

The fix is not "find more pull." The fix is stupidly simple and it comes from something you already know from Kapp's gamification work: don't set the goal as the chapter. Set the goal as the sitting time.

Right now your brain says: write one chapter → chapter done → shut down. Change it to: sit with the novel open for 90 minutes. Not "write two chapters." Just sit there for 90 minutes. If you finish one chapter in 50 minutes, you still have 40 minutes left. You're not forcing a second chapter. You're just... still there. With the document open. Maybe you reread what you wrote. Maybe you start sketching the next scene. Maybe you write 200 words of something that goes nowhere. Maybe you sit and think. The point is the time doesn't end when the chapter ends. The chapter ending is no longer the finish line. The clock is.

This is not anti-Rubin. This is actually what Rubin says on pages 80-82 in the Habits section. He tells the story of John Wooden teaching players how to put on their socks. He talks about committing to a set time, then extending it once the habit is established. The time container is the structure. The pull fills it or doesn't. But the container stays open either way.

For your current life on vacation, this is what it looks like in practice.

90 minutes with the novel. Not "one chapter." 90 minutes. Some days you'll write two chapters. Some days one and a half. Some days one chapter and 40 minutes of staring. All of those are success because you stayed in the container.

One anime episode in French dub. This doesn't change. It's already a fixed container (one episode = 22 minutes).

Everything else — YouTube, music, walking, scrolling — is just life. No guilt.
