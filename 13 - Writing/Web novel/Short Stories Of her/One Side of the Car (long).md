# One Side of the Car

show me a man who washes one side of his car and I will show you a man who has worked out exactly how much he is willing to finish.

me, half past four, over the bucket

show me a man who washes the whole car and I will show you a man who was written to this morning.

me, ten minutes later, same bucket, worse mood

---

Where I am from a man is allowed two faces. There is the one for the street, which is level. There is the one for a funeral, which is also level. Between those two there is nothing. Nobody has built a third. This is not cruelty, it is efficient. A man with a level face can be sat next to in a car for forty minutes and nobody has to work.

So the whole business goes into objects. You cannot say a thing. You can price paint. You can put your hand on the roof of a car. You can wash one side of it.

Everybody's car here is dusty. That is the wind. It comes off the plateau and it does not care what you did on Friday. So the dust is not information. What is information is which part a man decides to do.

There is a man on our street who does the whole thing every Friday. Both sides, the roof, the wheels. The little rubber strip along the bottom of the door that nobody sees unless they are lying in the road. He does it with a radio on. He is not thinking about anything. I would like to be him for about an hour and then I would like to be released.

And there is the other kind. The other kind does the side you sit in. Passenger side. The side a person would look at if a person got in.

I am the second kind. I want that established, because everything after it is going to argue with it.

---

I read in the morning. The Plath, the one on top of the pile. Sixty pages in French, then the same sixty in English. For her. She read it and I wanted the thing in my mouth in both directions before I said anything about it.

That is what I told myself at half past nine. It held until about page forty.

The two versions are not the same book. In English a sentence can be flat and mean and it sits in your hand like a stone. In French the same sentence puts on a small coat. Everything gets one degree more polite crossing over. One degree more arranged. By page forty you are reading a woman who is better mannered about wanting to die.

I do not know if that is the translator or the language. I have a theory that it is the language. I have no evidence and I am keeping it.

Some things survive the crossing. They are always the flattest ones. *I am. I am. I am.* You cannot do anything to that in any language. It arrives the same in both, and it is the only place in the book where the two of them are standing in the same room.

I put it face down on the arm of the chair without marking the page. I do that so it does not look like I stopped.

---

I ate standing up at the counter. Bread and the leftover thing from Tuesday, gone soft in a way that was not yet a problem. A man who sits down at a table alone is doing something. A man standing at a counter has not started his day and can still be about to do anything.

I put a gameplay on and propped the phone against the sugar. Some man in Ohio going through a game I finished four years ago. He was doing the part with the tower and he was doing it wrong. He kept going back to the same corner and dying there. He laughed each time. Eleven thousand people were watching him laugh.

I am one of the eleven thousand more nights than I am going to write down. Nobody is there for the game. The game was finished four years ago by everybody. That is the point. There is nothing left in it to find out. They are there because a man is talking in a room and the talking does not stop and it does not require you. That is the entire product. A voice that keeps going and asks you for nothing. So a man eating bread standing up at half past two is technically not eating alone.

You could sell that. Somebody has. I am subscribed.

I got to nine minutes and I thought of her and I did not finish the video.

Nothing had happened. Nobody had said anything. The bread was fine.

---

My father's friend needed to be across town at four. He is sixty-one. He telephones the night before. He says it as a question and it has never once been a question.

He talks the whole way and he does not require anything back. That is the best arrangement I have with anybody.

He started before the first roundabout. His brother's land, the boundary on the north side. The problem is not new. It is from before, from their father, and the neighbour knows it is from before, and that is why the neighbour will not sit down about it. He said the word for boundary four times. He said the neighbour is not a bad man. He said the son is worse. He said the son will have it in ten years and then it is finished, we lose it. He said that the way you say the shop closes at seven.

Then the paint. He had gone to price the paint for the room at the back. The man told him a number and he laughed at him, in the shop, out loud, and left, and did not buy it. He has not bought it since. The room at the back is the colour it was.

He told me the number. He told me what the paint was three years ago. He told me what a kilo of the good coffee was three years ago and what it is now. He did not connect these things and he did not need to, and neither did I.

At the second roundabout he said my father would have laughed at that.

Then he did not say anything for a while.

Then he started the land again from the beginning, as if it were new, and I let it be new.

He had one sentence in that car that was his. He put it down between the paint and the coffee, where it would not be picked up. Then he went back to the boundary so neither of us had to sit in the room with it. Everybody has a technique. His is better than mine because his is kind.

Forty minutes and he did not ask me anything. I have now said twice that this is the best arrangement I have with anybody. Do what you like with that.

I dropped him at the corner by the pharmacy. He put his hand flat on the roof of the car twice before he walked off. He always does that. I have never asked him what it is. It is a thank you, or it is something his own father did to a car once. I am not going to find out. If I ask, he will tell me, and then it will be a fact instead of the thing it is now.

---

I drove home the long way for no reason.

The passenger side was dusty. I filled the bucket and did that side, and the front, and half the bonnet. Then I stopped. It was never going to match. I knew that when I filled the bucket.

There is a moment where the wet part and the dry part are both in front of you and the line between them is completely straight. You understand that you put it there. That is when I dropped the sponge back in the water and stood up.

I went in and washed my hands and looked in the mirror over the sink and cried for about two minutes.

Nothing was wrong. I want that written down. It is the only part of this that would sound like something out loud, and it was the least eventful two minutes of the day. Nobody had said anything. Nothing arrived and nothing was taken.

I had read the same sixty pages twice. Eaten bread standing up. Watched a man in Ohio die in the same corner. Driven a good man across town and let him put his one true sentence down between the paint and the coffee. Washed one side of a car.

I did it in the mirror. I did not go and do it on the bed in the dark. There was nobody in the house.

Then I dried my hands and put the book back on the pile.

It goes on the top. It has always gone on the top.
