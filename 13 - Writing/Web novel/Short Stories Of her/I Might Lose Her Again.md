# The Kind of Person I Am

He stared into her eyes, his heart visible in the anxiety of his face, tears gathering but unable to fall.

He wants to be seen. He becomes ashamed of wanting to be seen. He screams that he does not need anyone to see him.

He had always screamed that he did not need it, that nothing would ever matter enough, that he was the kind of person capable of living inside a life that did not contain it.

The kind of person I am.

We were walking through the mist when she placed her hand next to mine. Not in mine. Next to it. It kept touching the side of my smallest finger and leaving, touching and leaving as our steps fell out of agreement. The wind would come between us and press the sleeve of her coat against my arm, then stop, and every time it stopped, I felt as if something had been taken away.

She was talking about the longest word in English. She had found it somewhere and spent the whole walk trying to make it leave her mouth alive.

Pneumonoultramicroscopicsilicovolcanoconiosis.

She would reach the volcano and lose everything after it, then attack the word again from the beginning as if it had insulted her. Her tongue moved behind her teeth. The lenses over her eyes collected the white of the streetlights, and whenever she looked toward me, I could not tell whether I was seeing her eyes or two small lights placed where her eyes should have been.

I told her the word was about the lungs.

She already knew that. That was why she had chosen it.

She pronounced the beginning again, slowly this time, keeping her eyes on me as if I were the word she intended to finish.

Then everything stopped breathing. The word died in her mouth as I looked into her eyes, the lenses becoming one with them.

I had noticed.

She had noticed me noticing.

The kind of person I am, the kind of person I am, the kind of person I am, I kept muttering beneath my breath as she continued walking beside me. The kind of person I am does not stop breathing because of a woman. The kind of person I am does not wait for her hand to touch him again. The kind of person I am does not the kind of person I am does not bother with these Formalities he just lets himself be. 

The words kept stuttering through me until there was no person left inside them, only someone trying to remain where he had placed himself.

Her hand touched mine again.

This time I held it.

She did not look down. She continued fighting the word, her fingers closing around mine as she reached the microscopic, entered the volcano, and died again somewhere before the lungs. I corrected her although I could not pronounce it either. She knew I could not. She allowed me to correct her because she wanted to watch me pretend.

The mist entered her hair. Small pieces of it rested there, as though the world had begun disappearing around her but had not yet reached her face.

The kind of person I am.

She looked at me.

I told her I was hers.

———

I told her I was hers.

The world stayed the same. My organs did not explode. My hand remained inside hers, and my feet continued moving one after the other. Somewhere behind us, a car passed over the wet road and threw water against the pavement. The longest word in English still meant a disease of the lungs. I was still the same human being, but I had let the words leave my mouth the same words I had sworn never to use again.

I am yours.

The tears fell as I stared into her face. The sun had already disappeared, but something luminous remained around her, the streetlights opening behind the mist and growing around her head. For a moment, I thought the light was staring back at me.

Then I understood that I was blurry.

The tears. It was the tears. Heaven had not opened behind her. I simply could not see her clearly enough.

She wiped nothing away.

She watched.

I told her I wanted to cry. I wanted always to be able to cry in front of her. I did not want to hold my breath. I wanted the sane, level-headed person inside me to keep living, and unless I could stand before her like this, wet-faced and still human, I would not be able to believe that person had ever been alive.

She tried the word again.

Pneumonoultra microscopic silicovolcano coniosis.

Perfect this time.

A disease of the lungs spoken by the woman who had watched me stop breathing.

We kept walking. Her hand had warmed inside mine, or mine had become as cold as hers. I could no longer tell which of us was giving the heat and which was receiving it. The road descended slightly, and our bodies moved faster without either of us choosing to move faster. She pulled me to the side before I stepped into a puddle.

I told her I had seen it.

She knew I had not.

She smiled without forgiving the lie.

Time, you know, I told her. Time. I am a slave to time. Everything becomes September or December, before or after, beginning or ending. I cannot allow a moment to remain a moment because it might leave before I have discovered what it promised.

I could never simply taste anything. Either I swallowed the whole of it or died thirsty beside it. I had vowed never to drink from the fruit of the vine. I had vowed never. I had even vowed to stop making vows.

She walked half a step ahead of me while I spoke, pulling me by the hand as if she knew I would wander into the road the moment I started talking about existence. The mist thickened. Her back became faint, and her hand remained the only part of her I could trust was there.

I told her again that I was hers.

She stopped.

I nearly walked into her.

She turned and looked through every question I had asked, every path, every date, every cup I had refused and every future I had put on trial.

“Look me in the eyes,” she said.

So I looked.

She kept looking at me with a smile. Shyness had gathered around her eyes, but something daring moved beneath it something that had seen what the words had done to me and wanted to touch the wound again to know whether it was real.

“Good boy,” she said.

The kind of person I am disappeared.

The wind moved the mist between us. Her hand was still in mine. The street continued behind her, wet and pale and leading somewhere I did not care to name.

Can I stay here forever?
