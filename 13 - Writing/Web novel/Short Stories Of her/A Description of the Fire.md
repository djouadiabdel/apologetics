A Description of the Fire

I finish a piece and cannot stop, so I keep going, and I print the rest at the back and label it the necessary part. It is the only work I have ever delivered on time.

There are no readers. I do not read it myself. I tear the page out of the book so that later on I can believe the thought was mine.

Here is what is on the list.

The psalms. Twenty nine of them, in French, on a calendar I built myself with dates on it. I am doing it. That is the honest part. I am not doing it at the speed the calendar says and I am scared of how slow I am, and the fear is not that I will fail, it is that I already know the failure will be repetition. I do not repeat enough. I know exactly what would fix it. I have known for weeks.

The Hail Mary. I know it. I have known it a long time. I do not say it.

Some say praying to a woman is demonic, some say she is dead and gone, some assume I worship her, and not one of them is describing anything I actually do, and I have the answer ready for all three of them. She is the mother of my Lord. To deny that she is the mother of God is to deny who Jesus is. She did not make the divine nature. She carried it. I can defend this for an hour against a man who has not asked me to.

I still do not say the prayer.

I have a rosary in the post. Ordered, paid for, shipped, in transit. I told myself I was waiting for a real one. I have ten fingers.

The Jesus prayer, which the monks said all day, every day, for their whole lives. I do not know what the fathers actually did with their hours. I have never looked it up. It would take me four minutes to find out and I have been carrying the question around for months instead, because carrying it feels more like something than knowing would.

The books. There is a stack. You know how it is with the stack.

And the messages. I can write to a hundred people who have never heard of me and I cannot write to one who has. A stranger has no opinion of me yet, so there is nothing in the sending. I have the thing to say. I have had it for days. It sits there and I look at it the way you look at a bill.

Now here is what I can do.

I can explain theosis for forty minutes without a note. I can tell you that heaven and hell are the same fire and the whole difference is whether you take it. I can take the hardest thing in the room and make it clear to somebody who has never heard of it, at two in the morning, at length, for free.

So it is not that I cannot do difficult things. I do the difficult one and skip the twenty second one. That is not laziness.

God does not want the burnt offering. He says so plainly, and David works it out only after there is nothing left of him to burn, and hands over his heart because it is the last item on the table. My sacrifice is a broken spirit. A broken and contrite heart you will not despise. I have the broken part. Broken is easy. I keep checking the other word.

But David had been emptied first. That is the part I skip when I quote it. He had lost the child, the wife was a crime, the prophet had already walked into the room and pointed at him. Nothing of mine has burned. I am sitting on a full inventory and offering God a description of the fire.

Bone fire.

I keep the window in mind. One day I open it and the Theotokos is raining down over Algeria and the whole street has to stop and see her, and nobody could argue with me again, and I would not have to do anything, because it would all be settled from outside. That is the shape of every hope I have. Something arrives and does it for me.

November is coming. The heart may heal. It may start again. It may only stop collapsing.

It is four in the morning. Outside the first mosque starts, and then the one behind the market, and then all of them, and every one of those men got up.

The Hail Mary takes about twenty seconds.

This has taken me two hours.
