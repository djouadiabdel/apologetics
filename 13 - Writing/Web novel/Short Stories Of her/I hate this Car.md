Where I am from a man is allowed two faces. There is the one for the street, which is level. There is the one for a funeral, which is also level. Between those two there is nothing. Nobody has built a third. It is efficient. A man with a level face can be sat next to in a car for forty minutes and nobody has to work.

So the whole business goes into objects. You cannot say a thing. You can price paint. You can put your hand on the roof of a car. You can wash one side of it.

Everybody's car here is dusty. That is the wind. It comes off the plateau and it does not care what you did on Friday. So the dust is not information. What is information is which part a man decides to do.

There is a man on our street who does the whole thing every Friday. Both sides, the roof, the wheels. The little rubber strip along the bottom of the door that nobody sees unless they are lying in the road. He does it with a radio on. He is not thinking about anything. I would like to be him for about an hour and then I would like to be released.

And there is the other kind. The other kind does the side you sit in. Passenger side. The side a person would look at if a person got in.

I am the second kind. I want that established, because everything after it is going to argue with it.

I woke at twenty past six and the room was already light.

The first thing is not a thought. The first thing is the arm going out to the floor by the bed, and the thought arrives about a second behind it and has to catch up with a hand that is already holding something.

There was a football score. There was a message from the operator about my balance.

I read the one about my balance twice. There is no version of that message that is different the second time.

Then I got up, because a man who stays in bed after that is a man who is waiting, and I was not.

I read in the morning. The Plath, the one on top of the pile. Sixty pages in French, then the same sixty in English. For her. She is the one who put it there.

She put it in my hands with both of hers. People do that. It means nothing and I know it means nothing.

I have been back through it enough times to have worn it out. There is less in it every time. I go back.

That is what I told myself at half past nine. It held until about page forty.

The two versions are not the same book. In English a sentence can be flat and mean and it sits in your hand like a stone. In French the same sentence puts on a small coat. Everything gets one degree more polite crossing over. One degree more arranged. By page forty you are reading a woman who is better mannered about wanting to die.

I do not know if that is the translator or the language. I have a theory that it is the language. I have no evidence and I am keeping it.

I said it out loud in the chair to check the length of it. Eleven seconds. Eleven seconds is short enough that a person does not have to find somewhere to put their eyes while you say it.

Nobody has asked me about this book.

Some things survive the crossing. They are always the flattest ones. *I am. I am. I am.* You cannot do anything to that in any language. It arrives the same in both, and it is the only place in the book where the two of them are standing in the same room.

I put it face down on the arm of the chair without marking the page. I do that so it does not look like I stopped.

I ate standing up at the counter. Bread and the leftover thing from Tuesday, gone soft in a way that was not yet a problem. A man who sits down at a table alone is doing something. A man standing at a counter has not started his day and can still be about to do anything.

I put a gameplay on and propped the phone against the sugar. Some man in Ohio going through a game I finished four years ago. He was doing the part with the tower and he was doing it wrong. He kept going back to the same corner and dying there. He laughed each time. Eleven thousand people were watching him laugh.

I am one of the eleven thousand more nights than I am going to write down. Nobody is there for the game. The game was finished four years ago by everybody. They are there because a man is talking in a room and the talking does not stop and it does not require you. A voice that keeps going and asks you for nothing. So a man eating bread standing up at half past two is technically not eating alone.

You could sell that. Somebody has. I am subscribed.

I have a preferred one. He greets everybody at once at the start, the whole room, all of us. I have watched him do that with the sound low.

I got to nine minutes and I thought of her and I did not finish the video. I turned the phone face down.

Then I turned it back over to check the time. There is a clock on the oven. It is a metre from where I was standing and I have never once looked at it.

The song came on by itself after something else finished. I did not put it on. I want that on the record, because everything after it is mine.

I sent it to her at eight minutes past three. No message with it. Just the song, which is the version of this that a normal man does.

Nothing came back. That is not a complaint about her. She works.

Nothing had happened. Nobody had said anything. The bread was fine.

My father's friend needed to be across town at four. He is forty-one. He telephones the night before. He says it as a question and it has never once been a question.

He does not ask whether I am free. Nobody asks me that any more. They worked the answer out some time ago and they were kind enough to stop making me say it. He is not the first one either. There is always one of them and it is always me who is available.

He talks the whole way and he does not require anything back. That is the best arrangement I have with anybody.

He started before the first roundabout, on his brother's land, the boundary on the north side. The problem is not new. It is from before, from their father, and the neighbour knows it is from before, and that is why the neighbour will not sit down about it. He said the word for boundary four times. He said the neighbour is not a bad man. He said the son is worse. He said the son will have it in ten years and then it is finished, we lose it. He said that the way you say the shop closes at seven.

Then the paint. He had gone to price the paint for the room at the back. The man told him a number and he laughed at him, in the shop, out loud, and left, and did not buy it. He has not bought it since. The room at the back is the colour it was.

He sleeps in the room at the back. He put that in between the paint and the price of the coffee, where it would not be picked up, and then he went straight on to what a kilo was three years ago and what it is now.

I let him go on to the coffee. That is what he wanted and I gave it to him and I would do it again.

At the second roundabout he said my father would have laughed at that.

Then he did not say anything for a while.

I told her about the warps in that quiet.

I told her it is not a hole and it is not a tunnel. Nothing goes faster inside it. You are not moving at all. The room is moving. You stand still in your one bit of space and the space itself is brought toward you at the front and let out behind you, and you arrive somewhere you had no right to arrive at, and the rule is not broken. That was the part I wanted her to have. Nothing is broken. It is only that the distance was never the real quantity.

Then I told her that if you went out to the edge you would get there and there would not be one. What we call the edge is the last place light has had time to come from. There is more behind it. There is more behind that. The number does not stop and it was never going to stop, and the size of the universe is a fact about how long we have been here and how well we can see. It is not a fact about the universe.

There was a night in March when she was tired and she said my name once instead of saying anything else. I have used that more than I have used most of the things that have actually happened to me.

Forty minutes. A man of forty-one who is not wanted in the front of his own house, going over a field his father lost and a room he is going to paint on his own, and I sat next to it working out how to word a thing about the stars.

I want to be honest here because this is the part I would not say out loud. I did the comparison. I gave it a fair go, both sides, properly. I did not come out thinking his was worse than mine.

I dropped him at the corner. He put his hand flat on the roof of the car twice before he walked off. He always does that. I have never asked him what it is. It is a thank you, or it is something his own father did to a car once. I am not going to find out. If I ask, he will tell me, and then it will be a fact instead of the thing it is now.

I read the three parts back while the engine was still on. The second one has a word missing. It says the last place light has had time come from.

There is no way to fix that. There is a way. You send a fourth one with the word in it, and then there are four of them.

It says she read it at nineteen minutes past four.

I drove home the long way for no reason.

The passenger side was dusty. I filled the bucket and did that side, and the front, and half the bonnet. I did the handle twice. Nobody has pulled that handle since March.

Then I stopped. It was never going to match. I knew that when I filled the bucket.

The wet part and the dry part were both in front of me and the line between them was completely straight. I put it there. I dropped the sponge back in the water and stood up.

I went in and washed my hands and looked in the mirror over the sink and cried for about two minutes.

Halfway through I stopped to check whether it showed. It did not show. The face was doing the level one, the street one, on its own, with nobody in the house. Then I went back to it.

It was not that she had not answered. She does not owe me an answer. I want that written down.

It was the word missing out of the second part.

Nothing was wrong. Nothing arrived.

I had read the same sixty pages twice. Sent a song to a person at work. Eaten bread standing up. Watched a man in Ohio die in the same corner. Explained the size of the universe to somebody who did not ask. Sat next to a man losing his house and decided mine was worse. Washed a door handle nobody has pulled since March. Washed one side of a car.

I did it in the mirror. I did not go and do it on the bed in the dark. There was nobody in the house.

Then I dried my hands and put the book back on the pile.

It goes on the top. It has always gone on the top.
