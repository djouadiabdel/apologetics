

Since about eleven I have not been able to get a full breath. It goes down two thirds and stops there. I have listened to my own chest like a doctor, which I nearly am, and there is nothing in it. Something in the corner has been saying the same thing since eleven and I have been talking over it.

"Four thousand one hundred and six," the angel says.

"What?"

"Nights."

He was assigned. He did not pick me and has never pretended otherwise. He sits where the light off the screen gives out, and he keeps a number, and he says it most nights around this hour, and I have never once asked him to.

The story ended a while ago. I am what came after.

I am the leg on the snake. I am also the man drawing it, the leg and then the tail and then the leg again, magnified, in detail nobody asked for. I finish a piece and cannot stop, so I keep going, and I print the rest at the back, and I label it the necessary part. A man closer to the priesthood than to the warmth of an XX chromosome sits down to paint a snake and then adds legs to it, and then adds a second set in case the first set was not clear.

"That is funny," he says. "Do more of those."

There are no readers. I do not read it myself. I spit at the sheet and what comes out is what comes, ugly, spreading thin off the roofs of the pages of the books I tear so that later on I can believe the thought was mine and that I might be smart. No. I am a copy of other pages. Nobody defended those either.

"I read them."

He does not count.

"I count everything," he says. "It is most of what I do."

I am honest like that. I have been through it enough times to sit an exam on it. There is no exam. There is no second reader.

My psychologist has a clock she can see and I cannot. Forty minutes in she says, you are doing the thing where you explain.

I know.

"Do you want to keep doing it?"

Nobody had asked me that before. I have been putting her in the chair of God for about a year and she has never once applied for the position. She is also paid. That is the part I turn over on the walk home, that the attention is real and is also invoiced, and I have not worked out whether that ruins it or makes it the only honest offer anyone has put to me.

There is an office and it keeps needing to be filled. It comes with a desk and a verdict and a light, and I bring the light myself.

There is an older file and it is still open. Two versions of the same thing are out there and they do not match, and there is no room anywhere they will ever be read side by side. I am the only one still keeping a copy.

Something else sits on the other side of me and tells me what to think. It has a nice feminine smile. I almost never look at it.

You are the only one who kept a copy, it says. That is the part you like.

He does not comment on that one.

Now it is the teacher.

"say something in italian"

"I am not Italian."

"you sound italian. then slavic. pick one"

She asks all the questions. I have never met anyone who asks so many and wants so little back. I sent her forty minutes of my voice explaining theosis, that heaven and hell are the same fire and the whole difference is whether you take it, and she listened to all of it, and then she asked my star sign so she could tell me it explained everything. She calls herself goldish. She goes by Ari, which is a lion.

She is learning German at night. Germany first, then Australia. Two rooms in the same country. In one of them a woman is drilling der die das at two in the morning. In the other one I am learning a language for a hospital that has never heard of me. Neither of us can say out loud in this country why we are doing it.

There is a boy in her class who will not speak a word of French to her and sings in it perfectly. She has no idea what to do about him. Neither do I.

At eleven I would have given a year off the end of my life for her name to come up on the screen. At midnight she sends a voice note about her sister and the price of the visa and something her students did, and she laughs in the middle of it, and I play it three times, and by the third time I feel nothing at all. The nothing is not peace. It is the floor going out from under the wanting, plank by plank, and I stand there on the joists checking whether I have stopped, the way you check a limb after a fall, and I am relieved, because if I have stopped then there is nothing to send and nothing to lose and I can go back to being a man with a project and a calendar with dates on it. Then at one it comes back all at once with the lights on, and it is not tenderness, it is closer to hunger, and I do not even want her, I want to be the thing she cannot put down, I want to be told, and then I think about typing something and my hands go cold and I put the phone face down on the bed like it belongs to somebody else.

"She is asleep."

"That is not what I asked."

"I know what you asked. I am not going to tell you."

I want to know what she thinks of me and every answer is bad. If she thinks well of me it is because she has the good version and I built it. If she never thinks about it, worse. If she has her own version of me and it is the accurate one, worst, and it probably is from where she is standing, and I will never get to argue.

I know what that is. I am afraid she will say no.

"No you are not."

He is right. I have been told no by strangers all my life and it costs nothing, a thumb moves on and the whole thing closes over. It is not the no. It is that she already has a version of me, and the moment she answers it sets, and I will not get to see it and I will not get to argue with it, and I will be walking around a city with somebody's file on me open and unreadable, which is a thing I have done before and did not survive well.

That is it. That is exactly it. I have been circling that for a year and it took four in the morning and an assigned angel to get it out of me.

"Four thousand one hundred and six," he says.

"You already said that."

"I know."

That was very good, it says. Write it down.

I write it down. I read it back twice. It is one of the better things I have written.

I cannot pray right because I am waiting on a verdict. I am waiting for a goal to be set and it is not going to be set for a long time.

The Hail Mary. The mother of us sinners. The one who fights the devil for us.

"You know it by heart."

"I know a lot of things by heart."

Some say praying to a woman is demonic, some say she is dead and gone, some assume I worship her, and not one of them is describing anything I actually do. She is the mother of my Lord. To deny that she is the mother of God is to deny who Jesus is. She did not make the divine nature. She carried it. Theotokos. God-bearer. I can hold that line for an hour against a man who has not asked me to, and I have, and I will again, and it is the only room in the house where I am never once afraid.

I have never said it.

I have beads coming in the post. A real rosary, ordered, paid for, shipped, in transit. I told myself I was waiting for a real one. I have ten fingers.

Then the psalms. Twenty nine of them, in French, on a calendar I built myself with dates on it, and how easy it would be, how fun, verse after verse for weeks until I can finally say David's whole psalm by heart, the one he wrote after Bathsheba, the one where he gives up hiding any of it.

You do not delight in sacrifice, or I would bring it. God does not want the burnt offering and says so plainly, and David works it out only after there is nothing left of him to burn, and hands over his heart because by then it is the one thing still on the table.

Let the bones you have crushed rejoice.

Then I thought about a bonfire.

Bone fire?

So I listen to a girl I have never met sing about a bonfire, and there is a line in it about wishing you both had the same set of evidence, and I sang it back in a room by myself at two in the morning and I did not have to change a word.

A broken and contrite heart you will not despise. I have the broken part. I keep checking the other word.

The thing in the corner has not stopped since eleven.

"Holy, holy, holy is the Lord Almighty."

Six wings. Two over its feet, two to fly with, two over its face. It has seen God and it has covered its own eyes and it has never once turned around to look at me.

"He cannot," the angel says. "He is covering his eyes. That is what the wings are for."

The only thing in this room that has seen God will not look at anything.

Woe to me. I am ruined. I am a man of unclean lips. That is the line. Isaiah sees the whole thing, the robe and the smoke and the door frames shaking, and the first thing out of him is about his own mouth, and then one of them comes down off the altar with tongs and a coal still burning in them and puts it on him.

Nobody is coming across this room with tongs.

"No," he says.

Three fifteen. I go to the window. Maybe one day I open it and the Theotokos is raining down over Algeria and the whole street has to stop and see her, and nobody could argue with me again, and I would not have to do anything, because it would all be settled from outside.

"Put your hope in a woman," he says. "Very modern of you."

Then I look at the faces I am given instead. Hundreds of strangers, a thumb moving, some of them answer.

I can write to a hundred people who have never heard of me.

I cannot write to one who has.

A stranger has no opinion of me yet.

They will see the work and they will value me. Maybe they will. What they value is that I am unique, and I am not unique, I still want the eyes of a stranger on me and I still want the goldish pillow to want my head on it.

"Then send something."

"To who."

"You know to who."

Four in the morning. Outside the first mosque starts, and then the one behind the market, and then all of them, and none of it is aimed at me either. The seraph does not stop for it. Everything in this country is calling out to someone and none of it is calling me, and nothing in this room is talking to me except the one I have decided does not count.

There is a message in the box for her. Unsent. Four days old. Nine words.

I read them four times. I sent them before I could take them apart.

Nothing happened. The phone lay there being a phone.

"Well," he says.

"Don't."

"I was not going to say anything."

The nine words were not this. This I am not going to send. I am going to leave it where she might find it, which is worse.

I know it will not be proof. Whatever comes back it will not be proof. If she answers warmly I will decide she is warm with everybody, and if she answers late I will time it, and if she does not answer at all I will have the apparatus built by morning and it will be a good one.

I will help, it says.

November is coming. The heart may heal. It may start again. It may only stop collapsing. And after November come the weeks of waiting for him to be born, the wintery fields screaming for a name to hold, and I am good at that part. I have always been good at the waiting part. I have four thousand one hundred and six nights of practice at standing at a window with the lamp in my own hand, waiting for something to come down over Algeria and settle it from outside, and this time it actually arrives, because that is the whole promise. Somebody comes and does it for me.

"Four thousand one hundred and seven," he says.

"It is not morning yet."

"It will be."
