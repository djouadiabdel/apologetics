The File

Three nights running I dreamt about her. On none of the mornings did I write to her.

On the twenty seventh I sent her forty minutes of my voice explaining theosis, that heaven and hell are the same fire and the whole difference is whether you take it. She listened to all of it. She said my accent sounds Italian. Then Slavic. She told me to pick one.

She is learning German at night. Germany first, then Australia. I am learning French for a hospital that has never heard of me. Two people in the same country doing the same thing for the same reason, and neither of us has said the reason out loud in this country to anybody.

The last conversation ran from ten until three. I asked questions for the first hour. She asked questions for the other four.

I have counted the number of times she has opened a conversation first. I am not going to write the number here.

I know her sign. I know what she thinks about feminism. I know the name of the city she wants to land in and the year she wants to land in it. I know how her voice goes when she is laughing at me on purpose.

There is a boy in her class who will not speak a word of French to her and sings in it perfectly. She has no idea what to do about him. Neither do I.

Two hours ago I sent her the essay about what I do to women. I did not think of it in those words at the time.

Since then I have opened the app eleven times.

I follow strangers. Hundreds of them, a thumb moving. Some of them answer.

There is a message in the box for her. Unsent. Four days old. Nine words.

I have done this before. There is an older file and it is still open. She is in another city with her own version of what is in it, and hers does not match mine. I am the only one still maintaining a copy.

Nobody asked me to maintain this one either.

I have not said the Hail Mary once.

A woman I have never met sings about a bonfire and about wishing you both had the same set of evidence. I have played it forty one times this week. The app keeps that number for me.

This file is four hundred and nineteen words long.

The message is nine.
