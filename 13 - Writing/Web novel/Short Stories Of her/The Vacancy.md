The Vacancy

My psychologist has a clock she can see and I cannot.

Forty minutes in she says, "you are doing the thing where you explain."

"I know."

"Do you want to keep doing it?"

Nobody had asked me that before. I have been putting her in the chair of God for about a year and she has never once applied for the position.

She is also paid. That is the part I turn over on the walk home. The attention is real. It is also invoiced. I have not worked out whether that ruins it or makes it the only honest offer anyone has put to me.

There is an office and it keeps needing to be filled. It comes with a desk and a verdict and a light, and I bring the light myself.

Aya held it for a long time. She still has a file open. Somewhere in another city she is walking around with a version of what happened in which I am the one who did it, and it is probably true from where she is standing, and I will never get to argue.

Now it is the teacher.

She says, "say something in Italian."

"I am not Italian."

"you sound Italian. then Slavic. pick one."

She asks all the questions. I have never met anyone who asks so many and wants so little back. I sent her forty minutes of my voice explaining theosis, that heaven and hell are the same fire and the whole difference is whether you take it. She listened to all of it. Then she asked my star sign so she could tell me it explained everything.

She is learning German at night. Germany first, then Australia.

Two rooms in the same country. In one of them a woman is drilling der die das at two in the morning. In the other one I am learning a language for a hospital that has never heard of me. Neither of us can say out loud here why we are doing it.

That is the part I keep going back to. Not her face.

"Ask me what I am afraid of," I tell her.

She does not. She sends a voice note about her sister and the price of the visa and something her students did, and she laughs in the middle of it, and I play it three times.

I cannot make her say it. That is the trouble with knowing a few real things about a person. I know she is a teacher, I know about the German, I know what she thinks about feminism and I know she has someone. I know enough that the made-up one will not say a line the real one would not say. She refuses in my head exactly the way she would refuse out loud. Politely. By changing the subject.

I put a light on her she did not ask for and cannot see.

I know what that is. I have read the man who explains what that is. He says the light is mine, that I am the one holding the lamp, that I hand a person the power to say whether I exist and then complain about the verdict.

Fine. I know. I am still holding it.

There is a woman who actually holds the office. She has held it since a room in Nazareth. She is the mother of my Lord. To deny that she is the mother of God is to deny who Jesus is. She did not make the divine nature. She carried it.

I never pray to her.

I have beads coming in the post. A real rosary, ordered, shipped, in transit. I am waiting on it the way I wait on everything.

Meanwhile the thing in the corner has not stopped since eleven.

"Holy, holy, holy is the Lord of hosts."

Six wings. Two over its feet, two to fly with, two over its face. It has seen God and it has covered its own eyes and it has never once turned around to look at me. Outside at four the first mosque starts and then the one behind the market and then all of them, and none of that is aimed at me either.

Everything in this country is calling out to someone and none of it is calling me.

"Ask me what I am afraid of."

Nothing.

God does not want the burnt offering. He says so plainly, and David works that out only after there is nothing left to burn, and hands over his heart because it is the one thing still on the table. A broken and contrite heart you will not despise. I have the broken part. I keep checking the other word.

She sang about a bonfire. All the evidence of two people going into it. I sang it back in a room by myself and I did not have to change a word.

Bone fire.

I am not going to send her this.

I am going to leave it where she might find it, which is worse, and which is the only reason it is any good.

"who is it for," she says.

"you."

"I am not there."

"I know."
