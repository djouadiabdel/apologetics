

----

I have an appetite for something complete.

Not for a thing. For something finished, that isn't missing anything, that doesn't need me and could never need me, and that could look at me once and settle the question I have not managed to settle in all the years I have been asking it, which is whether I am actually here.

I used to think the wanting was the defect. That a better man would want less, that if I could turn it down I would be fine, that the size of the appetite was the diagnosis. It isn't. Everyone has it. It's the vertical thing in me, the part that reaches past myself, and it is the only part of me I have never once managed to switch off.

Because it has no switch. That is the part nobody tells you. If the thing it was built for isn't there, or is there and I don't recognise it, the appetite does not lie down and wait for me to sort out my theology. It goes sideways. It looks around the room, takes the nearest available human, and puts them in the slot. It does this without asking me. By the time I notice, the installation is finished and I am already praying to someone who does not know they are being prayed to.

Deviated means redirected. Transcendence means the reaching. Vertical desire aimed horizontally. That's the whole thing that has been eating me, and it fits in one line.

The way I know it happened is the disproportion.

My grief was never her size. Sit with that arithmetic honestly for one minute. She was a girl. A girl with a phone and opinions and a family and a bad day on Tuesday. And when she was gone, the thing that came down on me was not the size of a girl. It was the size of God. It was out of all proportion to the object, and I kept explaining the excess by saying she was special, when the excess was never about her at all. That was the size of the hole she had been standing in. I wasn't grieving her. I was grieving the office she held.

And she could not do it. Not because she was cruel. Because nobody can. I handed a human being the weight of the infinite and she buckled under it, the way anyone buckles, and her buckling felt to me like betrayal, and she had never applied for the position. I never told her about it. I never told her that I had made her the answer to a question she had not been asked.

So it wasn't that I loved her wrong.

It's that I was not looking at her.

I was looking at what she could certify. She seemed self-sufficient to me, whole, like someone who already had the thing I was missing, and I believed that if she turned toward me some of it would transfer. The object is always a pretext. The real target is being. I didn't want anything she had. I wanted what she was, or what I had decided she was, which was never a person who existed and therefore was never available to be met. I was in a room with a woman and I was conducting business with a statue I had built of her.

And here is the part I resisted the longest.

Her unavailability was not the obstacle to the plan. It was the plan.

A god you can reach is not a god. If she had been easy, warm, simply and fully there, she would have become ordinary, and ordinary transfers nothing, because there would be nothing left in her worth transferring. Her distance was the proof of her superiority. Her superiority was the only thing that made her approval worth having. So the structure needed her to withhold. It could not have resolved. Every resolution destroys it. I was not waiting for her to say yes. I was building something that required her not to.

Which is why I keep writing that I am the same person, that I always end in the same place, that it is a circle. I'm right, and I am wrong about why. I am not choosing women. I am choosing a position, and the position has requirements, and it will keep finding someone to fill it. It's not a curse laid on me. It's a shape I keep building.

---
And now the thing I have been circling and would rather not write down.

I love the shapes.

When I try to describe any of this I never reach for a picture. Not a room, not a face, not the weather, not what anything smelled like. I reach for a circle. A mirror. A pattern. A slot. A triangle. An address. A verdict. Diagrams, every single time. And when someone hands me a shape that fits, something in me goes quiet in a way that nothing else has ever made it go quiet, and I have been reading that quiet as evidence that I understood something.

Maybe it is. But I know what else it is.

A shape is complete. A shape does not need me. I can see the whole of it at once, I can stand outside it, it does not move, it does not buckle, it will not leave. It has every single property I was looking for in her.

The diagram is the most available god there is.

That's my own answer to my own question. I like the shapes that fit because a shape that fits is a small finished thing, and I am starving for finished things, and a diagram will never fail me the way a person fails me. It certifies me. Not as loved. As someone who understands. And I would rather be the man who can see the circle than the man standing in it, because the man who can see it is outside, and outside is the only place I have ever felt safe.

Which means the understanding is the newest disguise. The circle can eat its own diagnosis. I can take the whole apparatus, the mediator and the triangle and the deviated transcendency and the whole beautiful machine, and build a very fine room out of it, and live in that room, and never do anything, and call it progress because the room is so well made.

And I gave it away myself. I said it in the question. Simple mirrors.

I answered it while I was asking it, which is apparently the only way I ever answer anything.

Because I wrote it before that too, and meant it as despair. I keep looking in the same mirror, at the same story, expecting to see a new reflection. Look at the object I reached for. Of everything available to me, I reached for the one thing whose entire function is to give me myself back. That was not a metaphor I selected. It was a confession I made without noticing. Every shape I build is a mirror. That's exactly why they comfort me, and exactly why nothing in them ever changes, because there has never once been anything in a mirror except me.

---
So I can quote the Beatitudes and I cannot receive them.

I got them right while crying. He who says that I am blessed, me the one in poor spirit, that I who mourn I will be comforted. And then in the very next breath, and I have read this back several times and I still don't like what it shows me, I wrote: and I want to be counted as righteous but I do not know what I am doing.

Look at what I did there. I took a gift and made it an entrance exam. I turned blessed are the poor in spirit into a rank I have to qualify for, and then failed myself for not qualifying, inside a single clause, without pausing, without noticing.

It's the only grammar I have. Everything I know about a superior being I learned inside the triangle, where regard is scarce, where it has to be won, where wanting it makes you weaker and being weaker makes you less worth regarding. So when the Beatitudes arrive I run them through the only machine I own, and the machine converts them into a standard. It cannot do anything else. It has never done anything else.

But there is no test in them. That's what I cannot get into my body. Every one of them says the same thing in a different key, which is that the condition I think disqualifies me is the qualification. Poor in spirit. Mourning. Hungry. Not become these things and then. Blessed are. Present tense. It isn't an instruction. It's news.

Blessed are those who mourn, for they will be comforted, is not something I can pass or fail. I was mourning. That was the entire entry requirement, and I met it by being in the exact state I was already in and hating myself for.

And I cannot diagram it. That's why it slides off me. There's no shape to stand outside of, because it isn't a structure, it's an address, and an address requires me to be the one addressed. Inside it. Received. With nowhere to stand and nothing to be clever about and no way to be the man looking down at the diagram of himself.

Every human being I have ever put in that slot became my rival. Not from cruelty. Arithmetic. She and I were reaching for the same finite thing, so her regard could only be bought at a cost, and my wanting it made me weaker, and my weakness made me less worth wanting. The triangle eats itself. There is no clever move inside it. I have tried all of them, and I am good at this, and I lost every time.

The one at the center of the Beatitudes is not competing with me for my own being. He isn't scarce. He is not made smaller by my having it. He is the only one who can stand in that slot without becoming the thing I have to defeat.

So the exit was never to want less. I asked what it would take for me to stop wanting happiness, and the question itself was the mistake. Nobody is asking me to shrink. The appetite was never the problem. The address was.

---
And Paul says, for now we see only a reflection as in a mirror, then we shall see face to face. Now I know in part, then I shall know fully, even as I am fully known.

He does not insult the mirror. He says the mirror is what we have for now. My shapes are permitted. They are the partial thing, and partial is not nothing, and I am allowed to see in part because there is no other way to see yet. But he says the mirror ends. He says there is a face on the other side of the glass, and that the glass was never the point.

Then the last clause, which I did not see the first four times I read it.

Even as I am fully known.

I ended a prayer with two words. See me. I wasn't quoting anything. I didn't know it was in there. But there is a sentence in Paul that answers it before I said it, and what it says is that the knowing has already been happening from the other side the whole time, that I am already fully known, and that the only thing still outstanding is for me to look up from the mirror and see back.
