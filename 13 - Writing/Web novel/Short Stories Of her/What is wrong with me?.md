I would like to say that I loved her and it went wrong. That sentence is available to me, it costs nothing, and everyone accepts it. But it is not what happened. What happened is that we misread each other completely, and neither of us wanted the misreading corrected, because the misreading was the part that felt like being known. That is a worse thing to have to say. There is no villain in it. There is no moment I can point to and say, here, this is where I should have chosen differently.

There is a line I resisted for a long time, which is that two beings never love at the same time. When I want her, she is indifferent. When my indifference finally arrives, it is exactly the thing that turns her toward me, and by then I am already somewhere else. My first reaction to that sentence was that it was too clinical to be true about me specifically. Other people, maybe. Not us. I understand now that the reaction was the whole diagnosis. Wanting to be the exception to a structure is the most reliable evidence that you are inside it.

We were each other's model and rival at the same time, which is a thing I could not have named then. I thought I was measuring myself against my life. I was measuring myself against her estimate of my life. Whatever we were supposedly fighting about stopped being the object early, and after that the object was only a pretext for the fight, and the fight was only a way of asking her whether I existed.

Because that is what I actually wanted from her, and it took me years and a French literary critic to admit it. I did not want anything she had. I wanted what she was. Or what I had decided she was, which is not the same woman and was never available to be met. She looked self-sufficient to me. She looked like a person who was already whole, who did not need to be told she was real. I wanted that fullness, and I believed that if she turned toward me, some of it would transfer. She was not a girl I loved. She was a mediator of a self-sufficiency I did not have, and I would have done the same thing to whoever occupied that position.

And this is the part I have the hardest time writing down. Her unavailability was not the obstacle. It was the proof. If she had been easy to be with, she would have been ordinary, and her approval would have been worth nothing, and I would not still be here writing about her. The resistance was what made her divine. Girard corrects Freud on this and the correction is the only thing that has ever removed any shame from it. I did not want to suffer. I never wanted to suffer. I wanted her being, and I accepted the suffering as the price of standing close to it. That is not a broken person choosing pain. That is a person choosing the structure that keeps the mediator transcendent, which is a thing you can only stop doing once you can see it.

I built my ambition out of her. I told myself, and I wrote it down, that the person I want to be is a person who is not sad because of her, and that this person is someone who accomplishes things. I thought that was recovery. It is not. It means she is still the one assigning the value. Every exam, every plan, every version of France, all of it is addressed to her. She does not have to be present for this. She is more present in her absence than she ever was in the room, because an absent mediator cannot disappoint you and cannot be corrected.

I wrote the whole thing down without knowing it. There is a café scene something and the other hears something entirely different, and both of them walk away happy. I thought I was writing a love story progressing forward, for Ippo and Kumi but I cannot warn them. A tragedy, and I built it from the inside, from memory, without recognising my own architecture.

And there is one more. In the Meg story there is a moment where Alberto meets a woman whose real name was hidden from him, who surprised him, who did not fit the test he was running. For one moment he was not conquering and not with her, because she interested him. That is the only unmediated encounter in anything I have ever written, the one moment where the triangle collapses and there are just two people.

I did not finish the story.
