# What now:?

## STEP 4: Hunt for visuals (one beat at a time)

Open your manga source (digital or scanned). Go beat by beat.

### For MANGA PANELS:

**Don't overthink it.** You're looking for panels that match the _emotion_ or _action_ of the beat, not necessarily the exact line you're saying.

- If you're talking about **Rui being indifferent early on** → find any panel where Rui looks neutral, distant, unengaged
- If you're talking about **Rui consumed later** → find a panel where she's crying, desperate, close-up on her eyes
- If you're talking about **Hina's desire infecting Rui** → find a panel of Hina looking at Natsuo, THEN a panel of Rui watching them

**How many panels per beat?**

- **Short beat (5-10 seconds of talking):** 1 panel with slow Transform movement
- **Medium beat (10-20 seconds):** 2-3 panels, each held for 4-7 seconds
- **Long beat (20-30 seconds):** 3-5 panels OR one panel with a very slow zoom + dissolve to a second angle of the same scene

**Save as you go:** Drop each panel into Track 2 at the right timecode. Don't wait until you've "found them all" — you'll lose track.

## STEP 5: Add Transform to EVERY manga panel

This is what makes your video not bland.

**Default recipe for a single panel (5-10 second hold):**

1. Add the panel to Track 2
2. Add **Transform** effect to it
3. Click the **keyframe button** (little diamond) at the start of the clip
4. **Zoom in slightly** on a meaningful detail (a character's eyes, two characters in frame, the central object)
5. Move the playhead to the END of the clip
6. Click the keyframe button again
7. **Adjust the zoom or position slightly** — either zoom in a bit more OR pan left/right to include more of the panel

**What this does:** The panel now slowly moves instead of sitting static. It feels alive.

**How much movement?**

- If the beat is **emotional or introspective** → VERY slow push-in (barely noticeable)
- If the beat is **building tension** → slightly faster push-in or pan across two characters
- If the beat is **a reveal or realization** → start tight on a detail, pull out to show the whole panel

**Do this for every manga panel.** It will take time the first few videos. Then it becomes muscle memory.

---

## STEP 6: Add Dissolve transitions between manga panels

Your manga panels should NOT hard-cut to each other (unless you're doing a rapid montage).

1. Make sure two manga panels overlap by **0.5 to 1 second** on the timeline
2. Go to **Compositions** (menu or effects panel)
3. Drag **Dissolve** onto the overlapping section
4. Adjust the dissolve length to 0.5-1 second

This makes it feel like pages turning instead of a slideshow clicking forward.

**Exception:** During FAST MONTAGES (like listing all the girls who fall for Natsuo), you DO want hard cuts. Speed and rhythm matter there. No dissolves.

## STEP 7: Add music (and keyframe the volume)

Pick **one** background track. Instrumental. Low-energy. Something that doesn't fight your voice.

(Anime OSTs work great — look for "lo-fi," "chill," or "ambient" versions on YouTube. Or royalty-free options: Epidemic Sound, Artlist, or even YouTube Audio Library.)

1. Drop the music into Track 1 (below your voiceover)
2. **Lower the music volume to -18dB to -24dB** so it sits under your voice
3. Add **Volume Keyframes**:
    - **Raise volume during silent moments** (between sections, when a manga panel is just sitting)
    - **Lower volume when you're speaking**
    - **Fade out completely at the very end** (corner drag on the audio clip)

**Pro move:** If you have a BIG thesis moment (like "The obstacle IS the desire"), you can **briefly drop the music to silence** right before you say it, then bring it back after. Creates a "pause for effect" feeling.
