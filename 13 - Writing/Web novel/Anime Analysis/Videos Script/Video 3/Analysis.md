# Mimesis: 

A mediator is **the person who makes you want something you wouldn't have wanted without them**.

Not a middleman. Not a negotiator. Not someone who delivers a message between two people. Forget every normal use of that word. In Girard, "mediator" means one thing only: **the person whose desire (or whose being) tells you what is worth wanting**.

Here's the simplest possible example.

You're walking through a market. You see a jacket. You think nothing of it. You keep walking. Then you see a man you respect — someone whose life looks like the life you want — and he's wearing that jacket. Suddenly you want the jacket. The jacket didn't change. _You_ didn't change. What happened is that a third person — the man you respect — stood between you and the jacket, and his relationship to the jacket made the jacket desirable.

That man is the mediator. He didn't tell you to buy the jacket. He didn't even know you were watching. He just _had_ the jacket, or _wanted_ the jacket, and because you unconsciously look to him as a model for what a good life looks like, his desire became your desire.

The mediator is the person you're secretly copying.

The critic wants "the real Jesus." That's the object. But the critic doesn't go directly to the gospels and form an independent opinion. The critic goes to Paul's letters, sees a version of Jesus he doesn't like, and then constructs his "real Jesus" _in opposition to Paul_. Paul is the figure whose version of Jesus the critic is reacting against. Paul is determining what the critic wants — just in reverse. Whatever Paul says, the critic says the opposite.

Paul is the mediator. Not because the critic admires Paul — he hates Paul. But hatred and admiration have the same structure in Girard. Both mean: **this person is determining what I want.** The critic's entire theology is organized around Paul. Remove Paul from the conversation and the critic has no position, because his position is "not-Paul."

That's what mediator means. The person — loved or hated — around whom your desire is organized.

----

**CONCEPT 1: THE MIMETIC TRIANGLE (the base)**

The standard triangle: Subject → Mediator → Object. The subject wants the object because the mediator wants it (or has it, or points to it). The key is that the arrow from subject to object _passes through_ the mediator. The desire is not direct.

**The triangle applied to Paul's critics:**

Subject = the critic (secular skeptic, Muslim apologist, whoever). Object = "the real Jesus" — the version of Jesus they want to exist. Mediator = Paul.

Here's what makes it work: the critic cannot reach the Jesus they want without going _through_ Paul, because Paul's letters are the earliest documents we have. Paul stands between the critic and the Jesus they want to construct. He's in the way. So the critic has to dismantle Paul before they can reach the object. They think they're arguing about doctrine (the object). They're actually locked in a rivalry with Paul (the mediator) who blocks access to the object.

The deformation: in normal mimetic desire, the subject imitates the mediator's desire. Here, the subject _inverts_ the mediator's desire — "whatever Paul says, the real Jesus said the opposite." But inversion is still imitation. The critic's entire theology is still organized around Paul's claims, just negated. Remove Paul and the critic has nothing to push against. The triangle collapses.

---

**CONCEPT 2: THE PSYCHOLOGICAL CIRCLE (Concept 11)**

Same triangle, but now we watch what happens to the subject's _self-perception_.

The triangle: Subject → Mediator → Object. But in internal mediation (where the mediator is close enough to be a rival), the subject cannot admit that the mediator is his model. To admit it would be to confess dependence. So the subject _reverses the story_: "I desired first. The mediator is the one copying me. Or worse — the mediator is the one corrupting the thing I love."

This is the psychological circle: the subject accuses the mediator of exactly what the subject himself is doing. The accusation is a projection. "Those who judge condemn themselves" because the judgment reveals what the judge is most anxious about in himself.

**Applied to Paul's critics:**

The critic accuses Paul of importing foreign ideas into Christianity. But the critic is importing modern secular assumptions, or Islamic theology, or Enlightenment rationalism into the text. The critic accuses Paul of selectively reading Jesus. But the critic cherry-picks gospel verses while ignoring the parallels you learned in Track 1. The critic accuses Paul of constructing a Jesus that serves his agenda. That is exactly what the critic does when he builds a "real Jesus" who conveniently agrees with whatever the critic already believes.

The deformation of the triangle: the arrow from subject to mediator, which was originally admiration (the subject needs the mediator to reach the object), has been _inverted into accusation_. But the structure hasn't changed. The subject is still organized around the mediator. He's just now organized around _destroying_ the mediator rather than imitating him. The triangle is the same shape. Only the emotional sign has flipped from positive to negative. And that flip — admiration becoming hatred while the structural dependence remains — is what Girard calls ressentiment.

---

**CONCEPT 3: NEGATIVE IMITATION (Concept 28)**

Now the triangle deforms further. The subject doesn't just hate the mediator — he defines his entire identity _against_ the mediator.

Standard triangle: Subject → Mediator → Object. In negative imitation, the subject says: "Whatever the mediator desires, I desire the opposite. Whatever the mediator affirms, I deny." The subject believes this makes him free. But look at the triangle — the mediator is still determining the subject's desire. The subject has just added a negative sign. He's still copying. He's copying in reverse.

Girard's image: the rebel's uniform. Every rebel wears a uniform — it's just the inverse of the establishment's uniform. The punk who shaves his head to reject bourgeois grooming standards is still organized around bourgeois grooming standards. He just negated them. Remove the establishment and the punk has no identity.

**Applied to Paul's critics:**

The "Paul vs. Jesus" critic says: "Paul taught atonement, so the real Jesus didn't. Paul taught divinity of Christ, so the real Jesus didn't. Paul taught law-free gospel, so the real Jesus didn't." Every single claim is a negation of Paul. The critic has no positive content independent of Paul. His entire theology is: take Paul, negate. That is negative imitation.

The deformation: the triangle hasn't lost any sides. The subject is still connected to the mediator, still connected to the object through the mediator. But now the subject _believes_ the triangle doesn't exist. He thinks he's reached the object (the real Jesus) directly, independently, on his own terms. He can't see that his "independent" conclusion is entirely determined by the mediator he rejected. The triangle has become invisible to the person inside it. That invisibility is what makes negative imitation so powerful — and so hard to argue against, because the person genuinely does not see their dependence.

---

**CONCEPT 4: THE SKANDALON (Concept 47)**

Now the triangle reaches its extreme deformation. The object disappears entirely.

In the standard triangle, there's a subject, a mediator, and an object. In the skandalon, the rivalry between subject and mediator has become so intense that the object is forgotten. The subject is no longer trying to reach "the real Jesus." He's just trying to destroy Paul. The rivalry _is_ the relationship. The mediator has become both the obstacle and the goal — you can't get past him, and you can't stop trying.

This is the double bind: the mediator blocks the subject's desire, but the subject cannot abandon the mediator because the mediator is the only thing giving the desire its intensity. The more Paul blocks the critic's access to the Jesus they want, the more important Paul becomes to the critic. The more the critic attacks Paul, the more central Paul becomes to the critic's worldview. The skandalon is the trap where fighting the mediator only tightens the grip.

**Applied to Paul's critics:**

Look at the behavioral evidence. The Muslim apologist cannot stop making videos about Paul. The secular skeptic writes book after book about Paul. The hyper-skeptical scholar (like Livsey from your transcript) builds an entire career around denying Paul existed. If Paul is a nobody, a fraud, a corruptor — why can't they simply walk away and go build their theology without him? Because Paul is their skandalon. He is the stumbling block they cannot get past and the figure they cannot stop returning to.

The deformation: the triangle has collapsed into a line. Subject ↔ Mediator. The object ("the real Jesus") has evaporated. What remains is pure fascination-repulsion between the critic and Paul. And this is why these arguments never end — because the argument itself has become the object. The rivalry sustains itself.

And here's the Pauline convergence: Paul himself used this word. 1 Corinthians 1:23 — the cross is a _skandalon_. The cross refuses to play the game of mimetic rivalry. It doesn't compete for power. It doesn't fight back. And precisely because it doesn't fight back, it becomes the permanent stumbling block. You can't defeat something that isn't competing with you. You can only keep tripping over it.

---

**CONCEPT 5: NOVELISTIC CONVERSION (Concept 49)**

Now the triangle breaks. Not deforms — breaks. And what replaces it is not a better triangle but the absence of the triangle.

In every great novel, Girard says, the hero eventually _sees the triangle_. He sees that his desire was borrowed. That the mediator was never a god or a devil but just another person caught in the same structure. That the object he pursued was never really the point. This seeing happens at the threshold of death — literal or metaphorical — because only death strips away the self-deception that keeps the triangle invisible.

**Applied to Paul:**

Before Damascus, Paul's triangle: Subject = Saul the Pharisee. Mediator = the law, the Pharisaic tradition, institutional approval. Object = righteousness, standing before God, the identity of the true Israelite. Saul pursued righteousness _through_ the law. The law mediated his access to God. And when the early Christians threatened to rearrange that triangle — claiming Jesus was the new mediator, not the law — Saul persecuted them. Because in internal mediation, the rival who threatens your mediator threatens your entire identity.

Damascus is the conversion. The triangle breaks. Saul sees that the law was never the mediator it promised to be. Philippians 3:4-8 is the confession: "Whatever was to my profit I now consider rubbish." That word _skybala_ — it's the death of the mediated self. Every credential, every achievement, every thing that organized his desire — gone. Not just devalued. Seen for what it was: borrowed desire dressed up as righteousness.

What replaces the triangle is not another triangle. It's direct encounter. "It pleased God to reveal his Son in me" (Galatians 1:15-16). No mediator standing between Paul and Christ. No institution, no credential, no performance. Just the encounter.

And this is why Paul's critics cannot forgive him. His conversion is a standing accusation against anyone still inside the triangle. If Paul — the most credentialed, most zealous, most "inside" Pharisee of his generation — could see through the triangle and call it rubbish, then no one's credentials are safe. His conversion doesn't just threaten their argument about Paul. It threatens their relationship with their own mediators — whatever those mediators are.

And here's why that distinction matters for your video. If Paul had just taught different ideas, you could argue with him the way you argue with any thinker — point by point, calmly, on the merits. But that's not what happens. What happens is rage. What happens is "Paul hijacked Christianity." What happens is people building entire careers around destroying him. That level of intensity doesn't come from a doctrinal disagreement. It comes from someone threatening the structure through which you access the thing you most want.

---

**THE PATTERN ACROSS ALL FIVE:**

Notice what happens as you move from Concept 1 to Concept 5. The triangle starts intact (basic mimetic desire), then the emotional sign flips (psychological circle), then the subject starts imitating in reverse (negative imitation), then the object vanishes entirely (skandalon), and finally the triangle itself breaks (conversion). It's a progression — each concept is a further deformation of the same original structure. You don't need five separate frameworks. You need one framework at five stages of intensity.

This is exactly what you did with Meursault. You showed that the askesis (denial of desire) has three components — method, cost, lie — and Camus skipped all three. Same logic here: the anti-Paul accusation has a mimetic structure — triangle, projection, negative imitation, skandalon — and the critics skip all of it. They think they're doing independent scholarship. They're inside the triangle and can't see it.
