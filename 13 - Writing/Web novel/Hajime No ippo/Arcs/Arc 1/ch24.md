# Chapter 24

His hands were firm on the bench, feeling the rectangular shape of it, his shoulders screaming because he was using them to push the bench backward — and there was only a wall to be pushed into. His fingers started to shake as the force increased until his right arm gave up and slipped, his body tilting sideways, his left hand gripping the left edge of the bench while his left torso lifted to compensate for the weight shift.

His thumb caught the corner of the wood. Pain surged through his hand in a clean bright line and all he could do was try to balance himself, but that was a mistake too — his back hit parallel to the ground, the heavy bench across his chest, and he lay there. Jaw clenched. Unable to look to his right, toward the ring, toward the thing. He kept himself inside all that heaviness, hoping no one would look at him, feeling the eyes poking his side and turning it all numb.

When he gathered enough strength to lift the seat off his chest he ended up on his knees. He raised his head toward the ring.

No one had looked at him.

He sighed. A smile broke across his face — the involuntary kind, the kind that comes from being invisible at the exact moment invisibility is a gift. He touched his head with his right hand, fixed his headband, pushed his hair back into place, and looked at the ring.

---

The spar was already moving.

Imai pressed forward. Slow. Deliberate. The same footwork Itagaki had watched during the warm-up — dense, mechanical, nothing in it that invited you to watch twice. He moved the way a man crosses a room he's crossed a thousand times, each step placed where the last one was placed, the rhythm so flat it made the air around the ring feel still.

Nobody wants to take a hit from Ippo. Itagaki knew that the way he knew the weight of his own hands. He had seen everyone who stepped into a ring with Ippo try to get close, and he had seen them all on their knees — shocked by the weight of it, the transfer from the back foot to the front, the hips turning, the shoulders contracting, the opposite arm pulling to keep the force sealed inside the punch. Everyone who ate that shot wore the same face afterward: the face of a man who touched something heavier than he expected and couldn't put it down fast enough.

So Imai was right to be careful. The slowness made sense. The caution made sense.

What didn't make sense was Ippo.

Ippo was moving but the movement had a delay in it, a fraction of a second where his body arrived somewhere and his eyes arrived later, as if they were looking at something else on the way. Itagaki had watched Ippo spar dozens of times. He knew the rhythm — the peekaboo, the weight shifts, the way Ippo's torso swayed like a door on a hinge, always closing toward the opponent. That rhythm was there. But it was wearing something on top of it, something heavy and invisible, the way a coat worn indoors makes a man look like he's about to leave.

_Senpai. You're in the ring. Be in the ring._

He wanted to say it out loud. His mouth opened and closed around nothing.

---

The darkness around the ring pressed in.

The ring was the only bright spot in the gym — the overhead lights pouring down onto the canvas, turning the ropes white, catching the sweat on both fighters' skin. Everything outside the ropes lived in shadow. Itagaki was in the shadow. The bench was in the shadow. The crowd lining the walls — Aoki, Kimura, the press row, the younger boxers who had stopped their own training to watch — all of them were in the shadow, and the shadow was eating the edges of the light on Ippo's side, little by little, the way water darkens the hem of a shirt from the bottom up.

Imai stood in the center of the light. It sat on him seamlessly, as if the ring had been built around his body and the lights had been angled to find him. He wasn't doing anything to earn it. He was just standing where the light was, and the light was staying.

Itagaki's right thumb was bleeding. He hadn't noticed when it started — the bench corner had caught the skin and opened something, and now the blood was pooling in the crease of his knuckle, warm and slow. He lifted the thumb to his mouth and put it between his lips, tasting copper, and the gesture made him look like a child watching something he wasn't supposed to see.

He kept his eyes on the ring.

---

The first jab came and it was nothing. A feint — Imai's left extending halfway and pulling back, testing the distance, checking Ippo's reaction. Ippo didn't flinch. Didn't blink. The peekaboo held.

The second was a feint too. Same extension, same pull-back, same flat rhythm. Itagaki's eyes tracked it automatically, the speed-trained part of his brain cataloguing the timing the way a metronome counts without caring what song is playing.

Then the third.

It was not a feint.

Imai's left arm extended fully — a beautiful line from shoulder to fist, the wrist snapping at the end, the kind of jab that textbooks photograph because every joint is where every joint is supposed to be. It traveled toward Ippo's face and Itagaki waited for the slip, waited for the shoulder roll, waited for the thing Ippo always did when punches came at his head — the small dip to the right that made the punch pass over his ear like a bird flying too close.

The sound came first. Before his eyes processed the image his ears caught the impact — a wet, heavy crack that roared across the ring and hit every edge of it, the kind of sound a body makes when leather meets skin and the skin doesn't move in time.

Itagaki screamed.

Not a word. A sound — _HUH_ — ripped from somewhere behind his lungs, his body reacting before his mind could build a sentence around what it had just seen. His thumb fell from his mouth. His face opened — jaw dropping, eyes going wide, the muscles in his cheeks losing all instruction.

Ippo was hit.

All of it — the scouting, the preparation, the shadow boxing, the peekaboo, the weight that pressed the canvas into the earth — all of it undone by a single left hand. A jab. A left that he let a rookie land.

He heard Aoki before he saw him — a scream, eyes wide, hands holding his head as if the head might leave without them. Kimura was next to him with his arms crossed, his eyes widened but his body still, the controlled alarm of a man who has seen enough fights to know the difference between a flash and a fracture. Takamura stood with his arms crossed too, watching, his face showing nothing except the slight widening of his eyes — and even that was restrained, held back, the way Takamura held everything back when the thing happening was serious enough to deserve silence.

Itagaki's thumb was bleeding onto his fist. He didn't feel it. He was staring at Ippo's head — at the angle it had turned, at the way the impact had pushed it to the side — and trying to understand how the thing he had just seen could exist in the same world as the thing he believed.

_He hasn't sparred in a while. That's all. He's rusty. That's all it is._

His eyes went to Miyata.

He didn't decide to look. His eyes traveled there the way they always traveled to the thing that might explain what couldn't be explained. Miyata was standing where he'd been standing since the beginning — black hair, slight frown, hands resting under his chin, watching the ring with the expression of a man confirming a measurement he'd already taken. Nothing in his face had moved. Nothing in his posture had shifted. He looked the same way he'd looked when he walked in — careful, distant, already finished with whatever conclusion the rest of the room was still trying to reach.

Itagaki had a million things to say to him. A million questions shaped like accusations — _why are you here, what did you do to him, why does your face look like that when his looks like this_ — and every one of them died in his throat because his eyes snapped back to the ring, back to Ippo, because what Ippo did next was more important than anything Miyata's face could tell him.

Ippo balanced himself.

His head came back to center. His feet reset — left foot forward, right foot back, the weight settling into the stance the way a house settles onto its foundation after a storm shakes it. His gloves came up. His eyes found Imai.

And Itagaki removed his thumb from his mouth and gripped the bench with both hands because the look on Ippo's face was one he recognized — not from the gym, not from sparring, but from the fights, the real ones, the ones under the lights with the crowd screaming and the blood on the canvas and the referee counting and Ippo getting up anyway.

_Now Imai's child play ends._

Ippo stepped forward.
