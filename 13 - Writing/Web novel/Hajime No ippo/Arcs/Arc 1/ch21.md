# Chapter 21 — The Bench

---

The wood under him was warm from his own weight.

That was the first thing Itagaki noticed — not the gym noise, not the shuffle of feet across the canvas, not Kamogawa's voice cutting through the room like a blade dragged across stone. The bench. The flat, splintered surface of the gym bench, which had been cold when he first sat down and was now the temperature of his body, as if the bench had decided to keep him.

He'd been told to sit.

Not asked. Told. And his legs had obeyed before his pride could object, which was the worst part — that his body agreed with the order even while the thing behind his ribs was still standing, still stepping forward, still saying _let me._

Itagaki pressed his palms flat against the wood and felt the grain catch on the skin of his fingers. Somewhere to his left, a heavy bag swung and the chain groaned. To his right, the ropes of the ring creaked under someone's weight. The gym was full and moving and no one was looking at him.

He was supposed to be watching.

Scouting — that's what it was. That's the word a smart fighter would use. Imai was warming up on the far side of the gym, rolling his shoulders, loosening the muscles in his neck with slow, deliberate turns. The kind of warm-up that didn't rush. The kind of warm-up that assumed the room would wait.

Itagaki's eyes went to him the way a thumb goes back to a cracked phone screen. Not because he wanted to look. Because he couldn't stop.

---

Imai's frame first. The trunk. Itagaki had seen him on tape, had watched grainy Inter-High footage on his phone so many times the compression artifacts felt like part of Imai's body — the pixelated shoulders, the blocky fists. But tape lies about mass. In person, Imai was thicker through the midsection than Itagaki expected. Not fat. Packed. The muscle sat low on his hips like ballast in a ship, the kind of weight that doesn't move when you want it to move and doesn't budge when someone else tries to move it.

Itagaki looked down at his own legs. Looked back up. Hated that he'd compared.

His eyes returned to Imai like the loop of a video he couldn't close.

The footwork next. Imai began shadowboxing — nothing flashy, nothing theatrical, just the quiet mechanical repetition of a body that had done this ten thousand times and would do it ten thousand more without complaint. Weight transferred from back foot to front, the hips turning, the shoulders following. Itagaki searched for the seam. The hitch. The place where the rhythm stuttered and a faster man could slide through.

He didn't find it.

Imai's footwork wasn't beautiful. Wasn't Ippo-level, where you could hear the music in the weight shifts. It was _good._ Reliable. The kind of footwork that doesn't lose fights even if it doesn't win them. And Itagaki kept looking, kept replaying the same three-step sequence in his mind — left, pivot, right — the way you zoom into a photo looking for something that will make you feel better, some flaw in the frame that proves the picture isn't what it looks like.

He found nothing. His stomach tightened.

Then the hands.

Imai threw his jab and Itagaki watched the extension — the snap of the wrist, the rotation of the shoulder, the speed at which the fist returned to guard. He watched it again. And again, because Imai's rhythm was predictable enough that the jab kept coming at the same interval, and each time it did, Itagaki's chest got a little quieter.

Imai's hands were fast.

Not faster than his. Speed was Itagaki's whole identity, the one thing he carried into every ring and every conversation and every comparison — _I am faster._ But the gap was smaller than he'd assumed. The distance between Imai's jab and his own was not a canyon. It was a crack in the pavement. Still a gap. Still real. But small enough that the wrong night, the wrong round, the wrong second of hesitation could close it.

_That's his warm-up speed,_ Itagaki told himself. _That's him loose. In the ring it'll be different. In the ring the gap opens up._

The reframe came fast, automatic, the way your mouth says _she's not even that pretty_ before your brain finishes processing what your eyes already know. And the speed of the reframe was itself the proof that the observation had landed. You don't rush to dismiss what doesn't threaten you.

His eyes went back. Kept going back.

What Imai didn't do — that was the part Itagaki's mind started to work with. Imai didn't feint. Didn't vary his rhythm. His shadowboxing followed a pattern: jab, jab, cross, step back, reset. Jab, jab, cross, step back, reset. Workmanlike. Efficient. The same way a man sweeps a floor — not because he loves the floor but because the floor needs sweeping and he is the one with the broom.

Itagaki saw the pattern. Saw it repeat. And here — quietly, involuntarily, like a reflex he didn't ask for — something in his mind shifted. Not confidence exactly. Something lower than confidence. Something closer to recognition. _I could time that. I could read that loop before it finishes and be inside it before his hands come back._

The thought coexisted with the fear. Both were true. He was afraid of Imai's speed and certain he could read Imai's rhythm, and neither cancelled the other, and neither was a lie.

And then he looked at Imai's face.

He didn't mean to. The eyes just traveled upward the way they do when you've studied someone's body long enough that the body becomes data and the face becomes the question. Imai was shadowboxing and his expression was —

Not menace. Not focus. Not the kind of clenched intensity Ippo wore when he entered the ring, where every muscle in his face tightened as if his skull was trying to hold something in.

Comfort.

Imai looked comfortable. He looked like the gym was his living room and the shadowboxing was something he did between meals. He looked like he was supposed to be the one warming up while other people sat on benches and watched. Like there was no version of this room where he was the one sitting down.

Itagaki's fingers curled into the wood of the bench.

_You walked in here off the street. You walked in here and everyone rearranged the room for you. You didn't earn this. You didn't spend years watching Ippo's back getting smaller down the road and wondering if your legs would ever be fast enough to keep up. You just showed up and the door was open and you walked through it._

He knew it wasn't fair. He knew Imai had trained. Three Inter-High titles wasn't luck. Three wins over Itagaki himself wasn't luck. But fairness wasn't what this was about. This was about the way Imai looked when he shadowboxed — like belonging was something he wore rather than something he chased.

Itagaki stared at the space Imai's fist had just vacated. The air was still moving where the punch had been. He could almost see the shape of it.

His eyes flicked to where Ippo was. Just for a second. Just to check.

Ippo wasn't looking at him.

His eyes flicked to where Ippo was. Just for a second. Just to check.

Ippo was wrapping his hands. Slow, methodical, the tape circling his knuckles the way it always did before something that mattered. He wasn't looking at Itagaki. He wasn't looking at Imai either.

He was looking at the ring.

And then he stood up.
