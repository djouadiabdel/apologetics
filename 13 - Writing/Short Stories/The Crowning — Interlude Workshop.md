# Interlude — The Stair

Workshop only. The abstract thesis has been removed, the waiters have been re-dumped as one continuous population, and *What did she say?* has been moved forward. The chapter file remains untouched.

I tried again on the next flight.

The first memory remained first. The last remained last. I could put the others between them in the order they happened and say what the weather had been, where the hand had rested, what I had done immediately afterwards. But when I tried to place a distance from one to the next, the line came apart. I used my fingers. Each finger held one until I moved to the following hand, and then the first five were only fingers again.

I could remember without measuring.

I had learned patience.

The stairs widened at the next turn. People lived along the outer wall where the stone was least wet. They had blankets folded beneath them, shoes repaired in several colours of thread, cups set on steps as though each had been put down for a moment. Nothing was permanent. Everything was worn.

A woman sitting near the turn held a sentence written across the inside of her wrist. She read it whenever the warmth changed.

*I will come when I am able.*

The writing had blurred where her thumb passed over it, but the words were still legible. She said the person who gave them had meant them. I believed her.

Three steps below, a man kept his sentence in his mouth.

*When the house is ready, there will be a place for you.*

He said it once, exactly, and did not improve the wording when I asked what kind of readiness had been intended.

Across from him, the third was shorter.

*Yes. I cannot tell you when.*

The person carrying that one smiled after saying it. Not with hope. With the ordinary recognition of hearing a familiar voice correctly reproduced.

Nobody there had been refused. That was what held them.

The woman with the writing asked what sentence I carried.

I told her you had stayed.

She waited.

I said you had stood behind my right shoulder at the low wall. When I moved toward you, you stopped and did not move backward. You had sat across from the empty place while the cracked cup leaked. You had continued down after taking off what had been on your head. On the next flight you had put your hand around two of my fingers and kept them until the landing.

Every one of those things happened. I added nothing.

What did she say?

My hand went to the pocket inside the coat.

There was nothing there. The pages had gone through the second gate. I ran two fingers along the lining and found the place where the smallest sheet used to adhere.

I said a relation cannot be reduced to its quotations. Behaviour has a grammar prior to speech, and insisting on a sentence can become a way of refusing what somebody has communicated because it arrived in a form you did not choose.

The woman listened.

What did she say?

I took my hand out of the pocket.

The man with the house gave me the method they used there. He said to prepare only what would remain useful if nobody arrived. Do not rehearse the arrival. Do not test the warmth while working.

There was dirt collected where the wall met the steps. I cleared it with the side of my hand, one stair and then the next. The dirt was damp and came away in rolls. I carried it to a crack and pressed it in with my thumb. When the crack was full, I found another.

My breathing settled. The wetness left my hands. I stopped checking whether the warmth had moved.

The method worked.

The old man had sat down near you. He had his hands joined behind him even there, which forced his shoulders forward and looked uncomfortable. I told him I was not remaining indefinitely. I would ask when I had learned to wait without requiring an answer.

Who made that the condition?

I showed him my hands. They were dry. That was not an answer to his question, but he had asked the wrong question. A condition is judged by whether it prepares a person for the act, not by who first formulated it.

He looked at my hands until I put them away.

I slept with my back against the outer wall.

When I woke, you were no longer beside the old man. The cup on the stair below me had been moved. The people were in different positions, or I remembered them in different positions, and I could not establish which.

I asked the woman with the sentence how long I had slept.

She did not know.

Had I spoken?

She had not heard me.

Had I moved from the wall?

She had not been watching.

I asked the man with the house. He had been asleep too. I asked the person with the shortest promise, who said there had been no reason to count.

Nobody had watched me do it.

I had not checked the warmth. I had not climbed toward the upper floor. I had not rehearsed anything. I had waited without complaint for an interval nobody could recover, and therefore there was no account of it outside me.

My palms were wet again. I told myself it was from the dirt in the cracks. The dirt had dried.

I knew this movement. The wet hands, the window, the other room. I had restored the distinction and could name what was happening while it happened. I stood and went upward, counting each stair. At the turn I could not say how many I had crossed. I went back toward the others, stopped, then went up again.

At the turn there was a dull place on the wall at the height of my shoulder. No soot. No threads. Only stone darkened by bodies resting in the same position. I put my shoulder against it to look down. The worn place fitted the coat from the shoulder seam to the upper arm.

Below me, nobody needed instruction. Every person had the sentence, the method, and enough work for another interval. I could add nothing.

My feet had already turned downward.

I returned because returning was the first decision I had made there that was not governed by duration.

Later I gave your answer under my breath.

I began it with my name. I left a pause before the yes because an immediate answer would not have shown that you understood what the waiting had cost. The pause was too long. It made you sound reluctant. I began again and shortened it.

Somebody stepped around me to reach a cup.

He had been close enough to hear both versions. He took the cup and went back to his place without looking at me.

You were sitting two stairs below the old man. I had not seen you return. I remained where I was because the next movement needed to be yours.

You took off one shoe. The skin at the back of your heel had split. You pressed it with your thumb, looked at what came away on the thumb, wiped it inside the shoe, and put the shoe back on.

Then you stood and continued down.

You passed the woman with the sentence. You passed the man with the house. Neither tried to stop you.

There was no gate at the bottom.

My hand went into the empty pocket again.

I told myself I was ready and followed.
