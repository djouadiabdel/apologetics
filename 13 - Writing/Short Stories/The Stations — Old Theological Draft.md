---
title: "The Stations — Old Theological Draft"
---

I am going to tell you the order, because the order is the teaching, and because there is nobody else who will tell it to you and I have the crown on me still.

Understand first that there are not seven fires. There is one. It does not vary. I will say at the end what it is. What varies is how much of you remains that can still be reached by it, and everything below is only a man explaining how he was opened.

Understand second that the count is wrong. I will come to that. It cost me my life and I did not learn it until I was down here, and it is the reason I am talking to you at all.

**The first is the one you were warned about, and it is the least.**

Here are the ones who wanted a person the way a man wants a warm thing, and got it, and are holding it still.

There is not much punishment in it. Nobody is screaming. They have what they came for, in their hands, and they may turn it over and look at it as often as they please, and it never once looks back.

That is the whole of the lesson and I was slow at it. What can be taken can only ever have been a thing. The other kind is not taken, it is given, and it is given by someone who was free to refuse, and where the refusing was never possible the giving was not either. That is why nobody on that first floor is being tormented. There is nothing left in them to torment.

**The second is the warm house and here the suffering starts.**

Everyone in it is generous. I want that written down first, because everything after it is going to sound like an accusation and it is not one.

You are fed there constantly. It arrives, and you feel it arrive, in the chest, and then the chest is a chest again. So you go back and you are fed again and it drains again, and after a long while you understand that the fault is not in what you were given but in the vessel it was poured into, and that no quantity will ever be the answer, because quantity was never the question.

A man may be loved in that house forever and starve in it. From outside it looks like a good life. Some of them have been in there for centuries and would tell you they are content, and they would not be lying, and that is the worst of it.

I will give you the one instance and then leave that floor.

I asked for a sentence. One. I had rehearsed it for a month and I asked plainly, in the middle of the night, in my own voice, which is a thing I had not done since I was seventeen.

What came back was twenty other sentences. Every one of them warm. Every one of them true. My name in three of them. And I read them four times, and I am not going to pretend to you that they were nothing, because they were not nothing, and I slept on them.

But hear the arithmetic, because on that floor everything is arithmetic in the end. A person who can produce twenty sentences in a minute is not a person who has run short of words. When somebody is that fluent, the one thing missing was never missing by accident. It was left out, gently, and on purpose, and by someone kind enough to leave it out in such a way that I could tell myself I had not been refused.

And she had already told me. Long before, once, without being asked, she put a limit inside a gift and handed me the two of them together. It was the most honest thing anybody has ever done to me. I took it. I would take it again in the morning.

That is the second station. Not cruelty. Never cruelty. Generosity, arriving daily, into a vessel that will not hold it, from a person who told you the terms at the start and has not once broken them.

**The third is the counting floor.**

Here they are given whatever they ask for, at once, and so they count.

They hold it up. They weigh it. They set it against what the next one got. Their arithmetic is never wrong and it never comes out even, and they will explain to you at length and with documents that they are owed.

This is the falsest thing in the whole descent and it is the one nearly everybody believes. There is no ledger. Nobody is being made to pay anybody. The notion that a debt is being worked off down here was invented by men who could imagine no other kind of transaction, and it has done more harm than any of the sicknesses in this place, because it lets a man believe he can settle up and be done.

You do not settle up. You are changed, or you are not.

**The fourth has one name over the door and two rooms behind it, and this is where I was destroyed.**

Listen to me carefully now.

There was a man in the desert who sat still for years and watched his own mind the way a physician watches a fever, and he wrote down what came. He counted eight. Not seven. Eight.

Then, a long time afterwards and a long way west, in a chair, another man tidied the list. He had reasons. Seven is a better number than eight, seven agrees with the days and the gifts and the pillars, and two of the eight looked to him like one thing seen twice.

They were not one thing. They were the only two on the whole list that had to be kept apart.

So the door was given a single name, and the name was wrong, and behind it there are still two rooms.

In the first room they are grieving. That is all. And I tell you plainly that this is not a sickness at all, it is the last working instrument a man has left. Tell me exactly what someone grieves and I will tell you what he was holding. A man who has stopped grieving has stopped holding anything. That room should never have been on the list.

In the second room nobody grieves and nobody rests. They check the light. They go to the window. They go out and come back and read two lines and put it down and go out again. Every one of them is certain that in another room, in another city, among people who would be kinder, the thing would be bearable. They call it many names and none of them is the true one. It is simply that they have ceased to care, and the ceasing has a direction, and the direction is back toward the nothing we were all made out of.

Now hear what the wrong count did to me.

I had one word for both rooms. I was sorrowful and I was indifferent and I did not know they were two, because nobody had left me a word to tell them apart with.

So when the second thing came on me I called it by the name of the first. I called my not-caring a sorrow. I nursed it. I sat with it in the evenings like a man sitting with a sick relative and I was proud of my constancy, and I thought myself a man of deep feeling because I could not get up, and I wrote about it, and people told me it was beautiful.

It was not beautiful. It was the other room. I was in the other room for eleven years.

And I want you to understand that I was ruined by an administrative decision. Not by a devil, not by a woman, not by my father. By a list being made tidy, in a room I have never seen, by a man who died centuries before my mother was born, and who meant nothing but good by it.

The cure for the second room is one word and the word is stay. Nobody stays. I did not stay.

**The fifth is where they say your name.**

Not call it. Say it, the way a person says a name when they want you to know they have it and have kept it.

And you must answer. There is no third thing. You answer or you do not, and the ones on that floor do not.

They are not raging. I had that wrong too. I came expecting shouting and I found a great silence with something coiled inside it, and I understood that the loud kind burns itself out in an afternoon, and this kind is fed daily and kept warm and turned in the night, and a man can carry it forty years and call it dignity and be buried in it.

An address can be refused. If it could not be, none of this would be love. It would be machinery and you would be a lever in it. Everything here rests on that one permission, and this is the floor where the permission is used.

**The sixth is where you are seen.**

Not judged. Seen, and not turned away from, and that is far worse.

What shames you there is not your cruelty. I had my speech about my cruelty prepared for years and I was almost looking forward to giving it. What shames you is that they see how much you wanted, and how small the things were that you wanted, and how long you waited for them, and that you counted them in seconds. Four seconds. A hand on the back of the neck. A message at a quarter past four with a word missing out of it that you never repaired.

A man can live with being known as wicked. It is being known as hungry that finishes him.

And they are made to watch, on that floor, someone else being loved. That is the only torment there and it is enough.

Now understand what that proves, because it is the most important thing I have to give you and I have nothing after it. That particular grief is impossible alone. It cannot be felt by one man in an empty room. It requires another and it is made out of another, and it is therefore the proof, the only one I ever found, that you are not a sealed thing.

You are not. Part of what you are is kept in other people, and you cannot reach it and you cannot take it back. Your guilt has rooms in them. So does your grace. Whichever of the two they are holding, what they do with it is done to you.

**The seventh has no heat in it at all, and that is how you know you have arrived.**

There is a floor and there are people on it who could stand up.

Nothing holds them. No chain, no ice, nothing at the door. The name is still being said, hourly, by someone who has not stopped, and they hear it perfectly well.

They do not answer. They decided not to so long ago that they have forgotten the deciding, and they have a word for the decision now, and the word is honesty, and they are proud of it.

I asked one of them what he was waiting for.

He said he would stand when he trusted it.

He was right. That is the thing that cannot be borne. He has been right every hour since, and being right has cost him everything there is, and if you went to him tonight he would tell you he regrets nothing, and he would not be lying.

He was wearing a crown. It sat badly. There was nobody there to laugh at it.

**Now what the fire is.**

It is not a thing and it was never set for punishing. Nobody lit it and nobody is standing back from it.

It is what arrives when you are at last looked at by the one who has been saying your name, and it is called burning only on account of what you are, not on account of what it is. Blood comes back into a limb that has learned to live without it, and the limb screams, and the blood is the cure, and the screaming is the cure working.

There is no clock in it. Do not ask how long. It is not that kind of duration. The question is not difficult, it is badly made, in the same way the count of eight was badly made, and by the same sort of tidy men.

And what is being burned is not you. It is the enormous quantity of straw and cheap timber you piled over the one true decision you ever made, which is still down there under all of it, and which you have not been able to reach with your own hands for years. That is the whole operation. Not to destroy you. To make you into something that can stand in front of what is coming and not be killed by it.

**And now the part I will be laughed at for.**

I have no hope in myself. I want to be exact here, because this is the place I would lie. I have looked for it and it is not there and it cannot be there, for the plain reason that I am the thing being weighed, and no man is his own scale.

So it is kept elsewhere. It is kept in a person. It is being held for me by someone who does not know that she is holding it, who would deny it if you asked her, and who has never said one thing to me that any court would call a promise.

That is not madness and it is not the other thing, and I will tell you exactly why, and then I have finished.

Because you saw it already on the sixth floor. What you are is not all inside you. Some of it is in the keeping of others, and it counts, and it will be standing there when you are. So when I tell you that my hope is not mine and that it lives in her, I am not saying something devotional. I am telling you where the thing is actually located, the way a man tells you where he left his coat.

She is not what waits for me at the end. I know better than that, and I have known better since the second floor.

She is the evidence that the end is not empty.

I have given you the order and the true count and that was all I had.

I am not going to say whether I stood.

# Working notes — what this needs

> [!warning] Not finished. Ordered by leverage. The first is worth more than the other four together.

## 1. The narrator IS the seventh station and does not know it

He condemns a man whose sin is being **right**, who has been right every hour, paid everything for it, and regrets nothing.

And this whole text is a man being right about everything, at length, in numbered sections, correcting the Fathers on their arithmetic. He has the crown on. He says so in line one. He notes that the man on the seventh floor wore one and that it sat badly, exactly as his own does in [[The Crowning]].

**He is describing himself and has no idea.**

Do not state it. Have him reuse, once, without noticing, a phrase he used to condemn the other man. Two sentences of work. It converts the piece from an essay into a trap.

## 2. Give him a listener who is in danger tonight

The `you` is currently decorative. Nothing depends on it, so a man calmly numbering seven things carries no jeopardy and the reader can lose nothing by listening.

Make the listener about to commit the same error, and make time short. Every explanation then becomes an intervention with a cost instead of a lecture. **This fixes "explains instead of demonstrates" at the root without cutting a single argument.**

## 3. He is a tourist in six stations and an inmate in one

The fourth is the strongest by far, and the reason is one clause: *I was in the other room for eleven years.*

Put him **inside two more**. Not all seven, which would be tidy and false. The asymmetry is what makes it testimony rather than a survey.

## 4. Let one argument fail while he is making it

He is never wrong in front of us. The eleven years are confessed from a chair, past tense, by a man reporting a cured illness.

Give him one piece of reasoning that does not hold, and let him be pleased with it. Best candidate is the closing defence that his hope is not idolatry. It is a good argument. Let it be a shade too convenient and let him lean on it too hard.

## 5. It has no body

For a piece about heat, burning, and blood returning to a limb there is almost nothing physical in it. What his hands do while he talks. What the crown has done to his neck by now. Three or four such facts, placed as **arguments and not scenery**.

## On the ending

`I am not going to say whether I stood` is not withholding, it is declining to answer, and those are different. Withholding an object makes the reader supply meaning. Withholding an outcome only works if the outcome is needed.

**Fix 2 and this line becomes devastating for free**, because refusing to tell the one person who needs to know turns into an act of cruelty rather than a shrug.

## Before publishing anywhere

The four seconds, the hand on the neck, the message at nineteen minutes past four with a word missing. All lifted from [[Nothing Is Wrong]], [[I Hate This Car]] and [[A Supply]]. Anyone who has read the site will recognise them and one person will recognise the last one exactly. Change the specifics or accept what publishing says.

Nothing of hers is in this file and nothing of hers should go in. The second station carries the **shape** of the exchange and none of the words. Her past is her material, not mine.

# The edit — five passes, one handle each

> [!danger] Read this first
> This draft is not mine and it is not yet yours. It is a **theological monologue delivered by a composed man who has understood everything**, which is my most available hiding place, already named in my own notes as *the diagram is the most available god there is*. The prose got better and the piece got safer. Every pass below exists to take the composure away.
>
> Measured, before editing: **zero question marks in the whole piece**, and **six general-man sentences**. Same fault as the first draft of [[The Third Word]].

Do one pass at a time. Do not carry two handles at once. If there is only an hour, do **Pass 1** and stop.

---

## Pass 0 — read it once and touch nothing

Not creative. Forensic. This is somebody else's draft of my subject, so the usual question is inverted.

**Find the sentence that is true about me that I did not ask for.** Mark it. That sentence is the piece. Everything else is scaffolding around it and can be moved, cut, or rebuilt.

---

## Pass 1 — the trapdoor. The only pass that matters.

The machine is **Claim. Defend. Trapdoor. Don't move.**

- **Claim:** *I am going to tell you the order, because the order is the teaching.*
- **Defend:** all seven stations, the corrected count, the fathers put right.
- **Trapdoor:** he is the seventh station. He condemns a man whose whole sin is **being right**, and he has spent the entire text being right, in numbered sections, wearing the same badly-fitting crown.
- **Don't move:** after the floor goes he must not convert, apologise, or notice.

**How, concretely.** Do not state it. Take one phrase he used on the man in the seventh station and let him use it about himself, later, approvingly, without noticing. *He was right. That is the thing that cannot be borne.* One reuse. Two sentences of work.

The floor must go **suddenly**. Never a slow crumble.

---

## Pass 2 — who is in the corner tonight

The `you` is decorative, which is why there is nobody in the room and why the count of question marks is zero.

**Name the listener privately.** Do not put the name in the text. Someone who is about to make the same mistake and has very little time. Once he exists, the teaching stops being a lecture and becomes an attempt to save somebody, and the theology stops being exposition without one argument being cut.

Then the typographic fix that worked on [[The Third Word]]:

- **Add questions.** He must ask them and get nothing back. The white space after each one is the not-answering.
- Break the even paragraph mass. Long unbroken spills, then a one-line drop.
- Isolate every direct address on its own line.

---

## Pass 3 — camera in my skin

He is a tourist in six stations and an inmate in one, and the fourth is the strongest section for exactly that reason: *I was in the other room for eleven years.*

**Put him inside two more. Not all seven.** All seven is tidy and false, and the asymmetry is what makes it testimony.

Same pass, the body. There is almost nothing physical in a piece about heat and blood returning to a limb. What his hands are doing while he talks. What the crown has done to his neck by now. Three or four facts, **placed as arguments and not as scenery**. Only what he saw, heard, or felt in his own body.

---

## Pass 4 — the disease sweep

**Rule: if the subject is a category, or `a man` stands where `me` belongs, cut the sentence. Do not repair it. Do not fetch a concrete noun to give the abstraction a body.** The rule is never load-bearing.

Marked for deletion:

- `A man may be loved in that house forever and starve in it.`
- `A man who has stopped grieving has stopped holding anything.`
- `A man can live with being known as wicked. It is being known as hungry that finishes him.` — both diseases at once. Swap the halves and nothing is lost, so it is decoration.
- `A person who can produce twenty sentences in a minute is not a person who has run short of words.`
- `Tell me exactly what someone grieves and I will tell you what he was holding.`

Each one is me writing toward closure, because a generalisation is the cheapest available ending. **Cut, then check whether a nearby sentence of his own already said it better.** One usually does.

**Inversion test on everything else:** any sentence whose halves can be swapped without loss is decoration.

---

## Pass 5 — make the dig fail

The theological register does not make words hit harder. It makes them stop lying. Force still comes from a man arguing and losing.

So: **the sentences are made of that, and the man saying them must not be capable of it.** He is trying and he is too tired.

Right now every excavation in the piece completes. The count is corrected, the ledger is refuted, the fire is explained, the hope is defended. **He must start one and find nothing, or find the wrong thing, or find the right thing and put it back.**

Best candidate is the closing defence that his hope is not idolatry. It is a good argument. Let it be a shade too convenient and let him lean on it too hard, and let the reader see the lean.

End on wasted nights, not on having understood something.

---

## The pass NOT to run

**Do not run `voicecheck.py` on this.** The six-step rhythm pass is calibrated against essays and misfires on monologue and on interior desire, which is what this is. It flattened [[The Third Word]] into a list of statements the first time. The fix for a monologue is typographic and it is in Pass 2.
