# The Crowning — Novella

## How to use this edition

1. Open one chapter and edit its prose normally—delete words, replace phrases, add paragraphs, or move sentences exactly as you would in any Obsidian note.
2. The boxes at the bottom are suggestions. You do **not** colour or format your writing.
3. Do whichever suggestions help. Ignore any suggestion that feels false.
4. A chapter may already be right. Keeping it unchanged is an editorial decision, not unfinished work.

## Colour key

> [!danger] CHANGE
> Rewrite or shorten the named passage if you agree that it does not yet sound like you.

> [!warning] CHOOSE
> Make the named decision. You can write `MY CHOICE: keep`, `cut`, or explain a different choice.

> [!question] ADD IF TRUE
> Add the requested detail only if something real comes to you. Never invent material merely to satisfy the prompt.

> [!success] KEEP
> You may rewrite the wording, but preserve the named event or physical clue.

The colours are already produced by Obsidian's callout boxes. You never need to select a font colour or type coloured text.

## What the job is in each chapter

| Chapter   | The main job                                                                                                                                |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| I         | Make the first meeting and refused hand sound like your natural telling.                                                                    |
| II        | Choose which nightmare image is truly yours and make the guilty wish honest.                                                                |
| III       | Decide which warm-house scene is necessary and whether the paper-kissing admission is true.                                                 |
| IV        | Replace one elegant ledger example with an embarrassing calculation that feels personal.                                                    |
| V         | Test the old rule he learned from hurt: giving instead of asking, without turning the former woman into the culprit.                        |
| VI        | Rewrite the second call of his name from inside the body; test whether her crown removal and brief touch express proximity without a title. |
| VII       | Re-dump the four seconds without polishing; keep only bodily ugliness that feels true.                                                      |
| Interlude | Dump the waiting as an unwitnessed bodily interval; he has no promise to quote, and the technique must work without moving him.             |
| VIII      | Let his platform lecture become vain or awkward and let the reader laugh without his permission.                                            |
| IX        | Keep love and grief alive while the fire destroys their use as evidence, payment, or accusation.                                            |
| X         | Test the titleless room and her final step; do not convert either into a coded promise or explain the final question.                       |

## Whole-book tests

- [ ] Her body remains evidence, never a verdict supplied by him.
- [ ] She has a cost and an existence unrelated to his desire.
- [ ] Her affection is real where she gives it, but no gesture is promoted into a title she did not give.
- [ ] The former relationship explains his defensive rule without becoming a trial of the former woman.
- [ ] The fire increases his love while leaving him nothing with which to purchase an answer.
- [ ] Three or four ugly admissions remain ugly; later prose does not redeem them.
- [ ] The reader gets to laugh at him at least once without his permission.
- [ ] *Seven is a holy number, the number of Matthew and Mark* remains exact and unexplained.
- [ ] The crown obeys paper physics in every appearance.
- [ ] The last page does not explain crowns, recite the physics, or know which question is coming.
- [ ] The final man appears capable of obeying an answer, including *go*.
