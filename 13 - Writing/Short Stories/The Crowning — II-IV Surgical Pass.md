## II — What Cannot Be Returned

### 1. `02:47` — cut the resolution

**Now**
> I returned the shape. The man received it without thanks because I had not given him anything. The joy continued from his arms as we walked away.

**After**
> I returned the shape. The man received it without thanks. The joy continued from his arms as we walked away.

His expectation of gratitude stays exposed. Nobody resolves it for him.

### 2. `02:81` — end on what she learned

**Now**
> Not by force. That is the part I cannot get anyone to understand. She let me near and I went farther than the permission went, so gently that I could call every separate step an accident. Now there is a piece of her that is my doing. Some of what she must be careful about, she learned being careful with me. I built a caution in another person out of the way I received what she freely gave.

**After**
> Not by force. That is the part I cannot get anyone to understand. She let me near and I went farther than the permission went, so gently that I could call every separate step an accident. Now there is a piece of her that is my doing. Some of what she must be careful about, she learned being careful with me.

*so gently that I could call every separate step an accident* already exhibits the defence. The final sentence explained it.

### 3. `02:99` — remove the maxim

**Now**
> It was not attached to me. It was hers now. It was not a thing I was holding that could be set down and walked away from. It was a shape in somebody, and shapes in people are not property, and there is no counter to return them to. What I put in her became part of what she is. It may still be there when I am nothing.

**After**
> It was not attached to me. It was hers now. It was not a thing I was holding that could be set down and walked away from. It was a shape in somebody. What I put in her became part of what she is. It may still be there when I am nothing.

Irreversibility now arrives as fact rather than as rule.

### 4. `02:127` — cut the diagnosis

**Now**
> The formulation pleased me. My arms lowered.
>
> I thought understanding had released them. I did not consider that turning the burden into a sentence was another way of holding it.[^c2-rg1]
>
> This time you noticed.

**After**
> The formulation pleased me. My arms lowered.
>
> I thought understanding had released them.
>

*This time you noticed* now lands directly against his belief that he was free. She supplies what the cut sentence used to.

## III — The Warm House

### 5. `03:5` — restore the preserved wording

**Now**
> Everyone in it is generous. Everything after this will sound like an accusation and it is not one.

**After**
> Everyone in it is generous. I want that said before we enter, because everything after it will sound like an accusation and it is not one.

Without *I want that said*, the sentence is the author framing the floor. With it, the narrator is preparing his defence before delivering his prosecution.

### 6. `03:27` — leave the hesitation unexplained

**Now**
> The woman hesitated for the first time.
>
> Not because she lacked proof. Nothing on that floor runs short. She was allowing me the chance to hear what I had asked.
>
> I repeated it.

**After**
> The woman hesitated for the first time.
>
> Not because she lacked proof. Nothing on that floor runs short.
>
> I repeated it.

He no longer reads her motive correctly, and *I repeated it* becomes the whole answer.

### 7. `03:37` — end at the breastbone

> You watched my hands. I had them pressed flat against my breastbone as though I could keep the warmth in by covering the place it escaped.

**After**
> You watched my hands. I had them pressed flat against my breastbone.

The cold-paper admission at `03:103` supplies the meaning later, from his body rather than his commentary.

### 8. `03:53` — cut the doctrine

**Now**
> At last she moved the chair back into its place. She did it without offence. The room lost nothing by your refusal, and you owed it no performance of gratitude.[^c3-r1]

**After**
> At last she moved the chair back into its place. She did it without offence.
>
> I disliked how easily the exchange ended.

The non-coercive giving is now carried by the gesture. His dislike of it follows with nothing in between.

### 9. `03:97` — cut the paragraph entire

It was left out gently and on purpose by somebody kind enough to leave it out in a way that allowed me to pretend I had not been refused.

## IV — The Ledger

### 10. `04:19` — cut the pre-emption

**Now**
> They will explain at length and with documents that they are owed. The documents are excellent. The figures can be checked. The falsity is not in a number. It is in the belief that somewhere a final column exists in which a person can be made even with another person and walk away unchanged.[^c4-r1]
>
> I had brought my own ledger.

**After**
> They will explain at length and with documents that they are owed. The documents are excellent. The figures can be checked.
>
> I had brought my own ledger.

The only structural fault in IV. With this gone, `04:101` arrives as an arrival:

> You do not settle up. You are changed, or you are not.
>
> I wish that sentence were mine.

That wish is now true. It also gives a cleaner pivot: the floor's documents are excellent, and he brought his own.
