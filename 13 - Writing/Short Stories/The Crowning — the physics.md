# The physics of the descent

Rules only. If a rule is not obeyed on the page the reader will feel it before they can name it. If a rule is *stated* on the page the story turns into a manual.

---

## 1. The place

**1.1** Seven floors, each below the last, connected by stairs. **Seven gates.** Six of them stand between one floor and the next. The seventh stands at the bottom of the seventh floor and opens onto the fire, and it is the only opening onto the fire there is.

**1.1a** The stairs are not neutral passage. The stair beyond the sixth gate and before the seventh floor carries a settled population. It has no gate of its own. Nothing there is asked and nothing there can be paid, which is why more is lost on it than on any floor.

**1.2** There is a heat at the bottom, past the seventh. It is the only source of warmth in the whole structure and everything else in the place is at the temperature of whoever is touching it.

**1.3** Everyone has a body here and it works. Temperature, weight, sound, sweat, the arms getting tired. This is not a place of souls in the air. Anything that happens has to happen to skin first. That rule is not negotiable and it is the reason the piece works at all.

**1.4** No common clock. Duration exists, but cannot be fixed into a shared chronology. Nobody here can tell you how long, and the question is not hard, it is badly built. Travellers still count recurrences and borrow units; the numbers produce no agreed when.

---

## 2. The gates

**2.1** Each gate wants one thing. The traveller does not choose what. The gate chooses, and it chooses the instrument by which that floor's sickness was practised.

| Floor | What is practised there | What the gate takes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | wanting a person as a warm thing | what he took out of her, and it will not detach |
| 2 | being fed and never filled | her letters |
| 3 | the accounts, the comparison, the owing | the ledger |
| 4 | the two rooms behind one name | his knowledge that he is the worst |
| 5 | the cold refusal to answer an address | his name |
| 6 | watching someone else be given it | the count |
| 7 | wanting to be known as the man who got it right | the asking |

**2.2** The first gate is not a gate. It takes nothing, because what it asks for cannot be returned. What you put into a person becomes part of what they are and there is no counter to hand it back at. Its only function is to show you the theft and make you carry it the rest of the way.

**2.3 The law of refusal, and it is the important one.** Refusing a gate does not send you back and does not throw you out. **You stay on that floor and you begin to understand it.**

That is the whole punishment. Understanding is what happens to people who will not pay.

Consequences, all load-bearing:

- Every authority on a floor is somebody who did not leave it.
- The most articulate person in this place is on the seventh.
- Fluency about a condition is evidence of residence in it, not of recovery from it.
- A man can build an entire body of teaching out of a gate he would not walk through, and the teaching will be accurate, and people will write it down.

**2.4** Refusal is never dramatic and is never framed as refusal. It arrives as a better reason. The refuser is always the most reasonable person in the room and can prove it.

---

## 3. The loop

> [!note] The loop began as an invention and is now canon. The draft plants it physically before naming it: the turn taken too early, the broken stair avoided without looking, the gate sentence delivered too cleanly, the door expected to move the old way, and the exact wear of the seventh stair under his foot. The later recognition earns the explanation.

**3.1 He has done this before.** Not his first descent. The order and the count he is so fluent about were learned by repetition and not by revelation.

**3.2 The causal chain, which is what makes it worth keeping.**

1. He refuses a gate.
2. Refusal keeps him on the floor until he understands it.
3. Understanding makes him fluent.
4. Fluency eventually locates the correct payment and he goes on down.
5. At the seventh he produces the perfect reason every time.
6. The incomplete descent resets the payments and **does not reset the fluency.**
7. So he starts again with seven instruments and a better theology than last time.

**His expertise is the evidence against him.** A man cannot narrate all seven floors like a resident and be passing through.

**3.3** After a refusal he goes back up carrying what he would not surrender, and everything the earlier gates took is returned at the top, because payment does not hold if the descent does not complete. The letters come back. The ledger comes back. The name comes back.

**3.4 What is planted.** Physical residue of earlier attempts, never explained at first: stair edges he knows the wear of without looking; a turn taken before the corridor gives him a reason; a gate speech that comes out too smooth and too fast; irritation when a door opens in a different direction; the number of steps and change of stone known before the seventh gate appears. Do not add the discarded twenty-minutes line. The body already knows enough.

**3.5 This is why the heat never gets closer.** It is not receding and it is not moving. He is on the near side of the only gate that opens onto it, and every route on this side runs parallel. Walking toward it feels exactly like walking toward it.

**3.6 And here is what has never happened before.** He cannot cross a gate he has refused. **Grace can cross it toward him.** The Lamb comes through from the far side, which is not something the structure owes him and not something his twelve attempts earned, and it is the only event in the whole book that breaks a law.

---

## 4. The other traveller

**4.1** She is not being led and never was. She was already descending. He came down and found her already here and made that mean something.

**4.2** She pays gates. He does not see her pay them, because the paying is not a public act and because he is talking.

**4.3** She is ahead of him on the ones that matter. She put her crown against the wall at the bottom of the fifth flight without discussion and without looking back at it. He noted the time.

**4.4 She does not need anything from him.** His belief that he is saving her is the assumption that survives longest and it is not corrected inside this text.

**4.5 What she does with her body.** This is where the reader learns the truth, so it must be reported precisely and interpreted wrongly.

Behaviours available, all of them neutral on paper:

- when he explains, she watches his hands and not his face
- she angles away rather than turning away
- she lets him pass instead of walking beside him
- she stops when he comes closer, and does not step back
- she waits with her weight on one leg
- once she puts her hand out and takes it back
- she looks at him the way you look at somebody carrying something too heavy for what it turns out to be

**Never write pity. Never write pathetic.** He supplies those words himself, at speed, and defends them. Report the body and the distance and let the reader do the arithmetic he cannot. Never confirm him and never correct him.

**4.6** She answers exactly once, on the fourth floor, and the words must be heard: *Weren't you grieving before too?* When he begins the distinction again, she says, *I heard you.* That is where her silence begins. For the rest of the descent the reader remembers a voice he has replaced with his own.

---

## 5. The theologian

**5.1** He is the man the narrator intends to become. The one who puts the counts back together, who joins the two halves of the Church, who is finally the person who got it right. Small, old, white cassock, hands behind his back at a window.

**5.2** He is therefore not a stranger and not a fantasy. He is a shape the narrator built out of his own ambition over many years, in detail, on purpose.

**5.3 And somebody else is standing in it.** The Lamb takes the shape a traveller will accept instruction from, and for this man there is exactly one such shape, which is himself arrived. He would not have listened to anything else, and this is the joke and it is not a kind one.

**5.4** So the reveal is not a costume change. It is that the voice he thought was his own future was another's the entire time, and he was being accompanied on every floor while believing he was alone and teaching.

**5.5** He never explains at the reveal. He speaks on the floors and is silent at the end. The moment he confirms the lecture, the man was right, and the story dies.

**5.6** He appears four times and refuses something each time. He refuses to say how long he has been standing there. He speaks to her and not to the narrator, once, inaudibly. He refuses the one question that is the narrator's own.

---

## 6. Address, and what keeps anybody here in being

**6.1** Nothing here is indestructible. There is no part of a person that argues against going back to the nothing they were made from.

**6.2** People persist because they are being addressed and the addressing does not stop.

**6.3** An address can be refused. It has to be refusable or none of this is love, and the whole structure rests on that one permission.

**6.4** Refusing does not kill you. It thins you. Nobody dies down here. They attenuate, and the attenuated ones are calm, and they are the ones who have been here longest.

**6.5** A name is said twice. Never three times. That is not cruelty and it is not a countdown, it is that the third would be coercion and coercion is the one thing this place cannot do.

---

## 7. What the narrator's instrument can and cannot do

Give the reader this rule early and never state it, and the whole book becomes solvable.

**7.1 His measurements are reliable, until the sixth gate.** Every number in his account is true. Seven things counted twelve times. Eleven years in the other room. Pockets checked eleven times on one flight of stairs. Eleven four-second events, with dates. Four seconds, twice. The ninth time at the window in one hour. If he counts it, it happened that many times. The sixth gate then takes the arithmetic and leaves the memories, so from that point he counts and arrives nowhere. The failure is real. He is not lying about a number, he no longer has the instrument. On the stair he reads that loss as patience.

**7.2 His interpretations are not.** Every claim about what another person feels, wants, means, or is about to do is manufactured, arrives instantly, and is defended well.

**7.3** So the reader can trust the physics and cannot trust the psychology, and the gap between those two is where the story actually lives.

**7.4** He can perceive his own body accurately and cannot draw the obvious conclusion from it. He knows the thing on his head is warm, gives under the thumbs, keeps the marks, lifts half a beat late in moving air, and curls at the edge in heat. He has the correct general principle. He never runs it on himself.

**7.5 Explanation is his vice, not the book's method.** Keep an explanation when it convicts him by being wrong, or when its polished accuracy proves that he has remained on a floor. If it correctly tells the reader what the adjacent events already show, cut it. The prose cannot secretly perform the act for which the story judges him.

**7.6 His composure is real and must not be roughed up everywhere.** Ordered syntax is the shelter he has built and the voice he uses to teach. The story is the scheduled failure of that shelter. Between failures he may be as lucid, balanced and qualified as he likes.

**7.7 The syntax gives way eight times.** Each failure is attached to a physical fact, never to a thesis:

1. first floor: the warm wrist moves against his sleeve and his arms close
2. second floor: he puts his mouth to the folded page in the dark
3. fourth floor: the crack reaches his chest and both versions use his mouth
4. fifth floor: the name is said a second time and he lets it pass
5. sixth floor: the four seconds
6. seventh floor: the half-second before he puts the crown back on
7. the fire: the blood restores the body
8. the waking: the paper becomes dust in his hand

At those points he repeats, corrects himself, abandons clauses and loses hierarchy. The break is brief. Composure returning afterwards is part of the evidence.

**7.8 Self-interruption is not decoration.** Roughly once per chapter, he catches himself performing the account and continues anyway. An unfinished clause is useful only where the body has outrun the explanation.

**7.9 The deletion test.** Remove an explanatory sentence and read the sentences on either side. If they touch, the explanation was decoration. If they do not, an event is missing; write the event rather than restoring the explanation.

**7.10 The seven-place count is a bodily tic, not another diagram.** It is established in the first chapter as chest, hip, mouth, shoulder, throat, fingers, head, once with each hand. It begins again in the ledger room and is interrupted by her movement. After the room laughs on the seventh floor, he performs the whole route with both hands but supplies no number, because the sixth gate has taken arithmetic and not sequence. In the fire the right hand begins it without permission and closes at the throat. It does not return after the crown becomes dust. Do not place it on every floor and do not explain what a witness thinks of it.

## 8. The vices, corrected

Evagrius, in the desert, counted eight: gluttony, lust, avarice, sadness, anger, acedia, vainglory, pride.

Gregory, in the chair, made three changes and not one.

- acedia folded into sadness
- **pride lifted out of the list entirely and set over it as the root**, with vainglory left standing as one of the seven
- **envy added, which was never on the desert list at all**

| Floor | Gregory's seven | On the desert list |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | lust | yes |
| 2 | gluttony | yes |
| 3 | avarice | yes |
| 4 | sadness, with acedia hidden inside it | as two separate things |
| 5 | anger | yes |
| 6 | **envy** | **no** |
| 7 | **vainglory** | yes, and kept apart from pride |

**Pride is not a floor. Pride is the building.** It is what the seven stand in and what the descent is inside of, and this is why the structure has no eighth storey and no basement below the seventh.

**8.1 The sixth floor is the vice the West invented.** The man who came down to correct the count is destroyed on the one floor his corrected count does not contain. The tidy man in the chair, whom he blames for eleven years of his life, is the only person who ever gave him a word for the worst thing that has ever happened to him.

**8.2 The seventh is the doubling he cannot see.** Wanting to become the man who reconciles the two halves is a vocation. Wanting to be **known as** the man who reconciled them is the seventh floor. He cannot reliably tell them apart, and the figure he has built to instruct him is made out of exactly that confusion.

**8.3** None of the seven is named in the prose. Ever.

## 9. The crown

**9.1** It is paper. It is a drawing of a crown, in his own hand, done on a night he remembers exactly.

**9.2** It obeys paper physics at all times and he never once concludes paper. Warm at his own temperature and never any other. Soft under the thumbs, and the marks stay. Half a beat late in disturbed air. Dark at one edge and curling in heat.

**9.2a Where it is.** On his head for the whole descent, and the warmth is always measured against his own skin under it. **Not his chest.** His chest is where the letters were, and the two must never be compared to the same body part. After the waking it is in his arm, which is the first time in the whole book he has held it rather than worn it, and that is the tell.

**9.3 The asymmetry, and it is the only supernatural fact in the structure.**

**A crown can be taken off alone. A crown cannot be placed by the wearer.**

Removal is a solitary act and it is available to anybody at any moment. That is what abdication is. She lifted hers off with both hands and set it against the wall at the bottom of the stairs and walked on, and nothing prevented her, and nothing was going to.

Placing is not solitary and there is no version of the movement one person performs on their own head. Which means his problem was never that he is stuck with it. **His problem is that he wants one and cannot give it to himself.**

**9.4 So the seventh gate does not want the crown.** It wants him to turn round, hold it out, and ask.

**The gate opens on the asking. Not on the answer.**

She can say no. She can say leave. The gate still opens, because her answer was never the currency and she owes him no salvific act. This is how §10.4 and this section stay in the same book: he depends on her and she is not the cure, and both of those are true because what he needed from her was the chance to be refused.

**9.5** The real one is on her, and it is made of light, and at first it takes her face. That image is dangerous on purpose: he still sees glory before he sees a person. By the final page the light thins enough for her mouth to become visible and remain unreadable. The glory does not get to erase the woman.

**9.6 Her crown removal is not her disappearance.** After she leaves it cold against the wall, she later chooses two of his fingers, leads him to the next landing, and releases them. The sequence is exact: she rejects the crown, chooses proximity, and chooses its limit. None of the three actions interprets either of the others.

Her touch is a gift in the present, not a coded promise about a future title. His task is to receive it without making it an instalment on marriage.

---

## 10. The ending condition

**10.1** The fire does not make him worthy. It makes him **able to ask plainly**. Those are different and the difference is the whole book.

The fire does not reduce his love. It returns the wanting at full strength and removes everything he could spend: pages, service, suffering, correctness, and the crown. He comes out wanting her more and with nothing to present as the price of an answer.

**10.2** No amount of understanding closes the question. He can be entirely healed and she can still not want him. She can also want his presence while refusing the name he wants for the relation. Affection, title, and marriage are not synonyms, and he cannot promote one into another for her.

**10.3 The most frightening truth available, and it is harsher than an unanswered proposal.**

Not that she might refuse him. That **the crowning, the explaining, the following and the suffering have already made him somebody she is right not to want**, and that becoming capable of love may mean hearing *leave me* and obeying it.

The purification does not produce a yes. It produces a man who can hear no.

**This remains physics. He never recites it.** The alternate lives show it, and the final distance between their bodies carries it. If he can formulate this judgment about himself on the last page, he has finished before the book ends.

**10.4** She is never the monster and never the cure. She is good, and kind, and free, and all three at once is the horror.

**10.5 The question is not necessarily the one he came down with.** He does not inventory possible questions at the end and does not diagnose how each one might manipulate her. A man capable of that diagnosis has already completed the change. The content remains genuinely unavailable until he opens his mouth.

**10.6** The text ends before we hear which. *I am going to open my mouth now.*

**10.7 And the last requirement, which is the whole test.** He must be capable not only of asking but of **obeying the answer.** A man who asks and then argues has not paid the gate. Everything in the book has to be arranged so the reader believes, in that final line, that he would go if she said go.

**10.8 What survives.** Love survives. Grief survives. Vocation survives. The fire burns their use as evidence. The grief cannot become a case against the former woman; the vocation cannot become proof that the world owes him recognition; love cannot become a claim on the present woman.

**10.9 The old relational rule.** Nobody deliberately taught it to him. He made it from hurt: do not ask again; give first; become useful; make refusal feel cruel; call the absence of a spoken refusal patience. This rule is what Christ breaks. Christ does not adjudicate the former relationship or erase what was loved in it.

**10.10 Why the unasked question holds up the building.** While the question remains his, its meaning remains his. He alone can say what the seven floors proved. Her answer would enter the account as a fact he did not author and reassess the descent backward. Asking is therefore not only risking *no*. It is surrendering sole custody of what his life means.

**10.11 The titleless room.** One alternate life must hold the presently unresolved case: she reaches first and wants him there, but refuses the name he brings. He is neither rejected nor crowned. The danger is that he treats her affection as a deposit and her limit as delay. The text does not decide for him whether staying is fidelity or another form of walking.
