# VII. Four Seconds

The sixth is where you are seen.

Not judged. Seen and not turned away from, which is worse.

What shames you there is not cruelty. I had my speech about my cruelty prepared for years and was almost looking forward to giving it. What shames you is how much you wanted, how small the wanted things were, how long you waited, and that you counted them in seconds.

Four seconds. A hand on the back of the neck.

A person can live with being known as wicked. It is being known as hungry that finishes him.

At the entrance stood a narrow table and a woman with a bowl of water. She asked what I had brought to be seen.

I began with cruelty. I gave her the deliberate acts first, then the acts whose deliberation I had hidden from myself, then the things omitted because omission leaves cleaner hands. I did not minimise. I named motive where I knew it and refused motive where claiming knowledge would have made the confession more impressive than the act.

It was a good confession.

The woman listened to the end. Then she dipped one hand into the bowl and touched two wet fingers to the centre of my forehead.

Not that, she said.

I thought she meant the confession had been incomplete. I began with the earliest thing I had left out.

Not what you did.

What, then?

What you wanted while you did it.

I told her wanting was already contained in motive.

She moved the bowl aside. On the table underneath it were small objects I had not seen: a chair pulled close to another chair; a door left open after everybody else had gone; a hand no larger than a coin resting at the back of a neck; a page with no writing on it except my name; two cups, one still full because nobody had arrived to drink it.

I reached for the hand.

The woman covered it with her palm.

Not yet.

I asked whose objects they were.

She said they belonged to anybody who recognised them.

Behind us, people moved through the entrance carrying elaborate accounts of what they had done. Each account was returned. Some argued that the account was necessary context. Some added worse acts in the hope of finally becoming too wicked to be examined for anything smaller. The woman waited for their lists to end and uncovered the objects again.

You came to the table. Your eyes moved across the chair, the door, the page, the cups. You did not reach for anything.

I told the woman that some people had disciplined their wants so completely that no object applied.

She looked at you rather than answering me.

The old man lowered his head. I mistook the movement for agreement.

The woman returned the little hand to the table and pointed toward the low wall.

At the front of the floor is a low wall. Everybody is eventually brought to it. Beyond it, ordinary rooms open and close in the dark. Nothing performed there is meant for us, which is why every movement can be seen.

I put both hands on the wall. The stone had no warmth of its own.

You stood behind my right shoulder. When I shifted toward you, you stopped. You did not move backward. I am recording that exactly.

The first room opened.

A young man was reading from pages held too close to his face. His argument was confused. He had mistaken a word in one language for a similar word in another and built the final claim on the mistake. I saw it before he finished the paragraph.

The people listening did not.

When he reached the end, an old woman in the front put both hands together and thanked him. She said the argument had returned something to her that learning had taken away. The young man lowered his pages and could not answer.

I gave the correction aloud. The wall returned my voice to me and none of it entered the room.

The young man would discover the error later. He would be embarrassed and revise the pages. The old woman would retain what the mistaken argument had given her. No correction would remove it.

The room closed.

In the second, a man had come to apologise. He did it badly. He began with intention, included circumstances that reduced his freedom, and said if more often than he said I. I marked each failure.

The person before him listened. Before the apology had improved, she crossed the room and held him.

He stopped speaking into her shoulder.

I said she had interrupted the necessary part. Forgiveness before an accurate account leaves the structure of the harm intact. The old man beside you heard me and did not answer.

The room closed.

In the third, somebody was chosen for work I could have done better. In the fourth, a letter arrived for a person who had not spent the morning checking whether it would. In the fifth, a hand reached across a table and took another hand without either person having to explain what the movement meant.

Each room supplied a reason. I found it. The young man had spoken from need rather than scholarship. The poor apology was received because of a history I could not see. The chosen worker possessed some ordinary quality the task required. The letter had been earned in ways unavailable to inspection. The hands belonged to people whose earlier asking had made the gesture possible.

The reasons were sound.

They changed nothing in my body.

Then the nearest room opened.

In the nearest room there was a man and somebody with him.

He was not a better man than me. I looked. I looked hard and for a long time. I want to say I was searching for a reason, and there was not one. I have gone over it since. There is still not one.[^c7-g1]

One thumbnail was black at the edge. That was what I found.

She came behind him while he worked at something in his hands, a strap or a buckle. She put her palm flat against the back of his neck, under the hair, and left it there.

He did not stop what he was doing.

He did not stop.

He kept working at the strap. He said something over his shoulder that was not about her and laughed at the end. Her hand remained and then went away. He never looked up.

Four seconds.

Four full seconds. I counted them because I count every one I have been given. There have been eleven and I know the dates and I could feel you behind my right shoulder and I lowered my head a little not enough to ask for anything there is an amount a man can move and still say he was only changing how he stood and the air reached the place below my hair and he received four and did not look up and did not know and will not remember it tomorrow and it cost him nothing it cost him absolutely nothing he did not even have to be present for it he was somewhere else the whole time and she gave it to him anyway and will give him another tonight and he will not notice that one either and I stood in the dark and wanted it so badly that my mouth filled and I swallowed and it came back and my hands went over the wall and I would have taken it from him I would have taken it out of the air I would have entered the room and taken it and I knew in that second that I would have and something in me that had been standing for a very long time went out from under me[^c7-g2]

and I called it running.

There is nowhere on that floor to run away to. I went along the wall with one shoulder striking it and my hands over my ears, which did nothing because this is not a floor you hear. At the first divider my foot landed badly. At the second I missed the floor and went down far enough to catch myself, then got up and continued.

The thing on my head moved when I ran. Not the way a weight moves. It went with my head and then went on going, half a beat late, lifting at the back the way a light thing lifts in disturbed air. I heard its edge touch my hair.

At the far end my knees struck stone.

There was spit hanging from my mouth. I wiped it with the back of my hand before I looked toward you. The hand left my mouth and went to the place I had opened to the air.

The thing was exactly as warm as the skin beneath it. Exactly the temperature of the place where her hand had rested on him.

I decided that proved the heat was spreading.

You came as far as the last divider and stopped. You looked at me the way you look at somebody carrying something too heavy for what it turns out to be. I know what I thought the look meant. I will not make you responsible for the thought.

The old man stood on the other side of the divider. He had been there while I watched. He looked down at me without taking his hands from behind his back.

Which part of that happened in you alone? he asked.[^c7-r1]

I said the grief was mine.

He looked through the opening toward the two people on the other side. I could not point to any part of it that had begun in me and ended there.

I told him I would rather do it alone. I had always done everything alone. I could be worked on privately and emerge without troubling anybody.

He said nobody comes out of anything alone. There was no private version and never had been. Wanting a private version belonged to one of the two rooms on the fourth floor, and I should know which.

I told him I did know. The desert man had known too. That was the point of restoring the eight.

The old man looked through the opening toward the room I had fled.

He did not write this one down, he said.

I thought I had misheard him.

The man in the desert, he said. There is no place for this in his eight.

I got to my feet. My knees had left two wet marks on the stone.

Then who put it here?

The man in the chair.

He said nothing else. He did not need to. The tidy man I had blamed for eleven years was the only one who had left me a word for the thing that had just taken my legs away. I had come down to repair his count, and the floor that destroyed me did not exist in mine.[^c7-rg1]

Here is the thing about the sixth burn that I have not been able to put anywhere.

It is not the same fire.

Everything else burns the way a held thing burns. It is in your hands and hot and you keep hold of it. This one comes off. I felt it being taken from me the way a dressing comes off skin that has grown into it. It was worse than carrying it. When it came away, there was less of me and the less was not a loss.

Something has to be shed. Somebody bled for the shedding to be possible. It is the oldest transaction and the only one down here that is not a transaction, because nothing went the other way.

The sixth gate took the count.

It asked me to give the four seconds first.

I said one.

The first second left the scene. The hand at the back of the neck did not move, but the beginning of its duration became impossible to locate.

I said two. Then three.

At four, the hand lifted. I still remembered the touch. I remembered seeing it begin and end. I could no longer place a measured distance between those events.

Then the gate wanted the eleven.

I gave ten quickly. The dates remained in their order, each attached to a room, weather, clothing, what had happened immediately before and what I did after. The memories were complete. Only the small mark I had made beside each was gone.

I held the last number.

Without it, I said, the others could no longer be described as insufficient. They would simply have happened.

The gate waited.

I looked back at the wall where the hand had rested on somebody else. The room beyond it had closed. There was only stone.

Eleven, I said.

The number went.

Not the memories. The dates remain. The gate took the arithmetic. On the stairs I tried to add the events and they would not stay in a line. I could remember every one without being able to turn them into a sum.

I tried subtraction. That failed too.

You waited at the next turn while I counted on my fingers and arrived nowhere.

## Theological material — Ratzinger

Full context: [[The Crowning — Ratzinger Sourcebook#V. The Immortality of the Soul and the Resurrection of the Dead — printed pp. 104–162]] and [[The Crowning — Ratzinger Sourcebook#VI. The Resurrection of the Dead and the Return of Christ — printed pp. 165–214]].

**Ecclesial idea:** There is no sealed, self-sufficient person. Human beings exist in and through relations, and Christian fulfilment draws the self out of isolation without dissolving its uniqueness. Envy is the narrator's obscene parody of this truth: another person's received tenderness enters his body as though communion were a quantity stolen from him.

**Short seed:** “Heaven is a stranger to isolation.” — Ratzinger, printed p. 235.

**Do not turn it into:** A claim that universal communion abolishes personal boundaries or makes another couple's intimacy available to him. Communion is not access.

**Try writing from:** Say the four seconds again without the word *envy*. Let him notice one ridiculous thing he does while counting—lips, fingers, posture, breath—and do not allow him to comment on how ridiculous it is.

## Desire material — Girard

Full context: [[The Crowning — Girard Sourcebook#2. The model becomes the obstacle]].

**Girardian pressure:** This is the book's purest mimetic triangle. The touch is not made false by imitation, but the rival's effortless reception magnifies it until the rival matters more than the touch. The narrator looks for proof that the man is better because he needs the obstacle to explain the man's possession.

**Short seed:** “The appearance of a rival seems to validate the desire.” — *I See Satan*, printed p. 10.

**Do not turn it into:** Her hand being an object passed between men. Girard's diagnosis must expose that reduction, never reproduce it.

**Try writing from:** For one paragraph, remove the woman from his attention and let him inspect only the man's neck, hands, competence, and failure to notice. At what instant does hatred become imitation?

## Sentence footnotes — Ratzinger + Girard

[^c7-g1]: **Girard — the rival's possession is being interpreted as superiority.** He looks for the difference that would justify why the other man receives the touch. The inability to find one does not free him; it makes the rival more fascinating. Keep the inspection petty and concrete.

[^c7-g2]: **Girard — the triangle becomes visible because syntax loses its alibi.** The rival validates the touch, the touch magnifies the rival, and the woman is nearly erased into the thing passing between them. This is exactly why the run-on should not be followed by an elegant explanation of envy.

[^c7-r1]: **Ratzinger — the self is relational rather than sealed.** The question does not deny responsibility; it denies the fantasy of a private version of suffering and purification. His desire involves real others, which is why “I would rather do it alone” cannot be innocent.

[^c7-rg1]: **The correction is ecclesial and anti-scapegoating at once.** Gregory's West named a vice the desert list lacked. Receiving truth from the man he blamed breaks both private expertise and the clean distribution of guilt. Do not explain the irony again after this paragraph.
