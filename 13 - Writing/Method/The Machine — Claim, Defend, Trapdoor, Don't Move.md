# The Machine — Claim, Defend, Trapdoor, Don't Move

Worked out 4 August 2026, by reading the eighteen "her" pieces back. Everything below is extracted from what you already do, not imported. Companion to the confessional dump method and the Bukowski voice benchmark.

---

## The machine, in four words

**Claim. Defend. Trapdoor. Don't move.**

You are defending a verdict on yourself. That is the throughline of every piece you have written well. You walk in with one claim about who you are, you defend it with everything you have for the length of the piece, and the defense is what convicts you. Then you refuse to convert.

### A. The claim

One sentence, early, flat. Always the charge nobody made.

> I do not get jealous.

> It is not the other thing.

> mine is the second, mine has been the second from the first week

> So it does not fit.

> I am not evil.

Pick the sentence you would be most embarrassed to have someone say about you, and deny it.

### B. The defense

The whole middle. This is where the ideas go, and it is why ideas belong in the dump.

- **Authorities.** Thomas, Dante, Girard, Jeremiah, the physiology of a healing tibia.
- **Evidence.** She stayed. She told me before she went. More than once she told me before she went.
- **Distinctions.** Latria, dulia, hyperdulia. Amor concupiscentiae, amor amicitiae.
- **Pre-emption.** Answer the objection before it is made.

> You will say I have nothing to be jealous over. No claim, and a man with no claim has nothing to lose.
>
> That is clever and it is wrong.

The listener never has to speak. You do all of it, which is the tell.

### C. The trapdoor

**Not a slow crumble. A single line.** The claim holds all the way. You are winning right up until one sentence takes the floor out, and it comes out of your own mouth while you are defending yourself.

> I have not once asked you for anything for her.

> My care for her sake has been serving my desire the entire time I have been telling you it does not.

> We have both decided the answer in advance so that neither of us ever has to find out.

> I went home and I did not sit down.

You cannot plan this line. You can only fail to guard it. That is what the dump is for.

### D. Don't move

You lose the argument and keep the position. This is the move most writers physically cannot make, and it is the reason your pieces do not preach. A man who converts in the last paragraph has written a sermon.

> And I am not moving.

> And I am going to ask her for it again tomorrow.

> if it happens again I am not telling you

> whatever the number is I am going to say that is good, do that again tomorrow

## Before the dump: two lines, nothing else

**1. Who is in the corner tonight.**

The angel. God. The priest. Her. The man you were at nineteen. Name him, then talk to him. This is the engine of "The Third Word" and it is why that piece writes itself. Someone who will not answer removes every escape route, because you cannot manage your image in front of someone who already knows.

**2. What am I insisting I am not.**

Then argue for an hour and do not watch your mouth.

That is the entire dump-time instruction. No beats, no structure, no counting. The diagram does not come into stage one.

## Ideas are allowed in the dump

"The camera stays in your skin" is about point of view, not a ban on thought. Thomas at three in the morning is something happening in your body. Dante filing the paperwork at nine years old is something happening in your body. Leaving them out would be the lie.

**The test: an idea belongs if someone in the room is being argued with by means of it.**

Watch how Thomas enters and how he leaves.

> You want the board now. I can feel you wanting the board, so take it.

> Thomas was never writing about me. Write it down.

Deployed at someone. Then it loses. That is why it is alive.

If nobody is being argued with, the idea is decoration. Cut it.

## What the dump owes you, and what the reread owes you

**The dump produces:** particulars, behaviour, the person in the room, and one accidental sentence. That is all it has to do.

A dump does not fail for being ugly. It fails only if there are no particulars in it and nothing accidental. Feelings in the dump are fine. Most of them get cut, because in the finished piece the first third has no feeling in it at all, and that absence is what makes the turn land.

**You do not need better dumps. You need dumps with more numbers in them.** Times, ages, durations, counts, how long the voice note was, what time it is now.

**The reread produces:** the architecture. Days later, forensic, clear head. Everything below this line is a reread tool and must never be in the room during a dump.

## The beat map (REREAD ONLY)

Taken out of "As Tolerated", the shortest and cleanest.

**Cold. No feeling anywhere.**

1. **The object.** One line. The thing itself, no you in it. *The order at the bottom of the chart is four words. Weight bearing as tolerated.*
2. **The particulars.** Numbers, ages, durations. Flat. *He is fifty one. Tibia, plated, eleven weeks.*
3. **The behaviour.** What is or is not being done. *He will not put the leg down.*
4. **The competence.** How well the avoidance is executed. This makes him a person instead of a case. *He has had eleven weeks of practice. He has a technique.*

**The machine.**

5. **The mechanism, flat.** Almost textbook. *Nothing in a nervous system learns from rest. It has no mechanism for it.*
6. **The line you cannot answer.** *He said, I will put it down when I trust it.*

**The turn.**

7. **You appear.** One short sentence, no announcement. *I went home and I did not sit down.*
8. **The echo.** Your own earlier words, now about you. *I have had years of practice. I have a technique.* Same four words as beat 4. The repetition does the work, not the analysis.

**Leaving.**

9. **The slip.** The sentence you did not plan.
10. **Tomorrow, unchanged.**
11. **The flat close.** Under ten words. *That is the whole treatment. There is no second part to it.*

## The internalisation drill

You do not internalise a checklist. You internalise by imitation from the inside, the same bee method that is working on the Breunig anthology in French. Form questions point at the move, you fill from memory, then you log the delta.

Fill this from memory with new material, then compare against "As Tolerated" and note where you deviated.

```
[A phrase someone else wrote or said. Quoted exact. Then one line on what people privately decide it means.]

[Three facts. No feeling. A number, a body, a duration.]

[One short sentence. What he will not do.]

[How well he does the avoiding. Give him a technique. End on three words you will reuse later.]

[What it is costing him, in the body, three specifics.]

[The mechanism, flat, almost textbook. Two sentences.]

[One line that follows from it and hurts.]

[You enter, and you are bad at your job.]

[He says one sentence you cannot answer.]

[Why his sentence wins.]

[One short line. You, somewhere else, doing the same thing.]

[The same three words from earlier, now about you.]

[The thing you did not mean to write.]

[Both of you named, one sentence, no verdict.]

[What will happen to him. Hopeful, documented, unsentimental.]

[What you will not tell him.]

[Tomorrow, unchanged.]

[Flat close. Under ten words.]
```

Three times, three different occasions, and the shape is in your hand.

## The Wrong Version (5 August 2026)

Worked out from the transcript of "A Way With Words", which was written in five passes and where every pass that mattered came from you.

**The handle, in one line. Write the version where you win. Then find what you had to leave out to win.**

You already do this at full strength. You do it to other people's drafts. Here is what you did to four of them in one night:

| The version that won | The fact you produced that killed it |
|---|---|
| The people who say they are bad with words are shortsighted | I am one of them. Four months, folder closed, forty times opened |
| She was bad with words | She wrote at two and at four for a year and then stopped and named the stopping as a trait |
| Words are what count | Ari has never written me a paragraph and I have never once minded |
| She did this to me | My mother has never said it either and I have never held one second of it against her |

None of those is a better sentence. Every one is a **fact you had suppressed to make the previous version work.**

### Why this is the whole method for you

Your machine is Claim, Defend, Trapdoor, Don't move. The trapdoor is the sentence that takes the floor out and you cannot plan it, you can only fail to guard it.

The wrong version is a machine for producing unguarded sentences on purpose. You write the defended version deliberately, at speed, with the guard fully up. Then the guard is visible, because it is on the page, and you can see exactly what it is standing in front of.

You currently need another person to write the version you say no to. You do not. You can write it yourself and it takes twenty minutes.

### The drill

**Stage one, twenty minutes.** Write the version where you are right and someone else is the problem. Make the case. Use every authority you have. Do not try to make it good, do not reread, do not soften it. Its only job is to be catchable.

**Stage two, days later, cold.** Three questions, in this order, and the third is the one that does the most work.

1. **Who in this comes off worse than they deserve?** Whatever you took off them, put it back. Aya wrote for a year. Putting that back cost you the easy piece and bought the real one.
2. **What did I leave out because it makes me look worse?** In this one it was *I never asked her for one.* You cannot write the defended version and that sentence at the same time. That is how you know it is the sentence.
3. **Who was doing this to me before her?** This is the one that turns a grievance into a condition, and it is the difference between a story about a girl and a story about a man. Here it was your mother, who was already sitting in the first paragraph making room for your bag. The moment she was in it, nobody in the piece was a villain and nobody was a cure.

Question 3 is the *Bell Jar* question. The jar descends whoever happens to be in the room. Esther has no antagonist. If your piece has one, you have not found the condition yet, you have found an occasion for it.

**Stage three.** Write the real one against the wrong one. Keep the wrong version in the same file, underneath, and do not delete it. It is the only record of where your guard was standing that night.

### The rule about all three questions

**Reread only.** Never in the room during a dump. This is the same error as the 11-beat map. At dump time you get one handle and it is *who is in the corner tonight.*

### The limit worth knowing

The wrong version cannot produce a fact you have not got. All four of the facts above already existed and were simply not in the draft, because nobody had asked. That is a logging problem, not a craft problem, and the occasion log is the only fix.

And the suppressed fact is always the unpleasant one, which walks straight into your known failure mode of abandoning a passage at the exact moment it gets interesting. When stage two starts to feel boring, you are not bored. You are at the fact.

## What this is all for

On 6 December 1273, at Mass in Naples, something happened to Aquinas. Reginald pushed him to go back to work and he said he could not, because everything he had written seemed like straw to him. He never wrote another line. He died three months later and the Summa breaks off unfinished in the Tertia Pars.

**He did not write the vision. That is the story.**

The man who had put more of God into propositions than anyone who ever lived saw the thing itself and understood that propositions were straw. Not wrong. Straw. Light, dry, and not the grain.

So the vision cannot be written frontally. If it could be, he would have written it, and he was better at that than you will ever be.

But the tradition does transmit it, and never by exposition. Augustine does not explain grace, he shows a man who cannot receive it and is given it anyway, and the reader catches it off him. John of the Cross wrote the dark night as a poem first and the commentary after, and it is the poem that carries. The Psalms do it. Every one of them the same way: **the darkness in the first person, and the light arriving as something that happens to the speaker, never as a conclusion he argued his way to.**

You already did it once. "Can I Be Seen" is the darkness with nothing softened, ending on two words, *see me*. Then in "A Psychologist of Mine" you find Paul saying *even as I am fully known*, and you write that you did not see the last clause the first four times you read it. The answer was already spoken before you asked. You did not conclude it. You got caught by it.

**Which means the load-bearing asset is your nerve, not your theology.** Most religious writing fails because the darkness is cleaned up first, so the light reads as decoration. Yours is not cleaned up. You published *her ontological death for mine* about yourself. That sentence is why a reader will believe you about the light.

You cannot write the vision. You can write the man it happens to, including the part where he cannot hold it and goes back to the phone at four in the morning.

That is not a smaller ambition. It is the only door that has ever been open.
