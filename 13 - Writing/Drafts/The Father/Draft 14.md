# The Confessor / The Father

*Draft 14. Integrated the Sorrowful Mysteries of the Rosary. The narrator grapples with a silent God of Glory who gives too little, realizing that Ari has usurped God's spot on the altar, turning his religious devotion into a dreadful, hollow worship of a girl who doesn't care.*

---

The cursor blinked. It was the third of August, late afternoon. The heat was stuck in the room like a dead animal. I looked at the newspaper on the desk. A boring fucking newspaper. Somewhere in the middle of it I had to remind myself it wasn't 1989. It felt like 1989. Everything was grey and tasted like dust.

I looked at my phone. Nothing. I had been sending her spoken goodnights, voice notes, warmth said out loud into the dark. She answered at night in text. Always filed. Always burnable. I was dying. Fucking dying. She had called what we had a "situationship," like a rented car you use because you can't afford the real thing. I had taken the dedication to her off my book and replaced it with sixteen verses of regret. She never even noticed.

I hit the keys.

*The last time my father hit me, he hit me with a whole hammer.*

I stopped and looked at the sentence. Three minutes before I had told her that story, she was crying about her own father. My father had just seen my uncle's wife with blond hair and called her a whore. I typed that out too. Two crying faces and a laugh came back. That was all you got. Emojis for blood. 

I picked up the rosary from the desk. The beads felt heavy, greasy with sweat. Tuesday. The Sorrowful Mysteries. You are supposed to meditate on the Agony, the Scourging, the Thorns. But I just rolled the beads with a scared, creeping dread. I looked up at this God of Glory, this vast, silent thing, and I couldn't shake the feeling that He just wasn't giving me enough. That He had left the room. And she had walked right into His empty spot. 

I had called her an answered prayer once. *Grace, not earned.* I had put her on the altar, and now because of her, I had stopped the psalters. I was supposed to be fasting, doing the work, trying not to do anything wrong. Instead, I was just rotting in this chair, worshipping a girl who wouldn't even look at me.

I lit a cigarette. Or I wanted to. In Khenchela I could go out into the dark right now and get whatever I wanted, except a drink. They know my father there. They wouldn't sell me a drop. In Algiers it's cheaper than water. I stood up, roamed the small room, poured a glass of warm water, and sat back down.

I hit the keys again.

*Twenty-first of July, one in the morning. Pitch black.*

I wrote about how my father had looked at me that night. *You've been reading many books in French. Try making up a sentence.* I told him I sucked at French. I tried translating some shit from the Bible. I looked at him, looked at the sky. He just said *enough, enough*, and walked off to the bathroom. 

I leaned back in the chair. I thought about Monday, the 10th of August. I had gone to her crying because I missed her. I just wanted her to tell me it was okay to miss her. *Don't feel that,* she typed back. *Don't tell yourself bad things.* Just like that. Administrative. A closed door. I stopped crying and hated myself for days.

That same night in July, my father had been selling me at the pharmacy. *Oh, a doctor here,* he told some girl with literal psychosis. At one in the morning, his friends send me their blood work and their scans. *How fucked am I, sergeant?* I make them wait ten minutes. I just sit there and let the dread build. Then I tell them the truth. They like me for it. Honesty. A man came to the house once. I looked at his file, wrote the script. My father told me later the guy was so scared he shat himself.

I laughed, a dry, ragged sound in the empty room. 

My mother doesn't cook for us anymore. Years ago, she stopped. She only cooks for him. 

I typed: *My father forces me to not drink water. Now I buy my own.* 

Once, in the Sahara, I went two wild days without a single drop. Someone told me to drink less water anyway. Something about my face. It didn't matter. She buys her own water too. To challenge herself, she said. 

I checked the phone again. Still black. I had told her about the suicidal thoughts. I had handed her the worst thing in my head, the darkest part of the room. I had finally asked for something. And she just said, *respectfully I'm curious*, asked about the gap after an attempt, and then thanked me for satisfying her curiosity. She put the cover story back on and walked away.

The God of Glory doesn't answer either, but at least He doesn't say *respectfully I'm curious*.

I was a dumping ground. A priest. 

*So father, there is this man using my old methods against me, he flatters me, it scars me father. I wanna confess father.*

They actually sent me that. They called me Father. Mommy. Daddy. Padre. I kept the right energy going. What else was I going to do? Half an hour later, another message: *U selfish liar, fraud psychiatrist and a bad father.* 

I pasted it to two friends. *Idk man, I am laughing. Am crying. Dude.*

I rubbed my eyes. The screen was burning holes in my retinas. He wanted me to go to Oran. Been saying it for weeks. *Go to Oran, go to Oran.* Maybe he just wanted me out of his sight. What about the farms? I built those. Was I supposed to leave them to rot when he and my mother died? She told me to leave it to my brother. He's an atheist, he'll leave the country anyway. 

I took a drink of the warm water. It tasted like metal. I wanted a real drink. I wanted to drown the whole fucking room.

I type: *Father and son are two swordsmen. Even when the son is better, he cannot win. The son learnt to always be afraid of his father and thus loses the duels.*

She had agreed with that once. *At first I lose control and feel weak, then I say fuck it and something inside says, who are you to be afraid.*

FUCK IT WE BALL.

Men don't cry. They condition themselves not to. My best friend of twelve years can't cry anymore. I cry easily. Always have. 

When my grandpa died, my father didn't cry. I remember the day perfectly. All of it. The dirt, the standing around. The absolute silence of the man. It was like watching a stone refuse to bleed.

I stared at the blinking cursor at the end of the page. My hands were shaking. I squeezed the rosary again. The Crucifixion. The fifth sorrow. The cheap plastic cut into my palm. I wasn't going to curse. I wasn't going to text her. I was just going to sit here and let her keep the spot she took.

Seven minutes later, I typed it out.

*I still am unable to talk about how I feel.*

It sounded like a strange thing to even think about. I looked at the phone one last time. Dead. She wasn't going to commit. There was nothing to mourn because nothing had ever been named. I was just the Confessor. 

Father is father regardless.
