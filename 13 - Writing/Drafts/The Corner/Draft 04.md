# The Corner

*Draft 4. Numbered fragments, out of order, after Mary Robison.*

---

**1.** I like being alone.

**2.** It is twenty past one and I have been walking this room for an hour. Bed, door, window, bed. I do not sit down when I talk to her.

**3.** The first thing a teacher ever said to me was that I was a dumb fuck.

**4.** At the gym I make them turn the music off. If there are three people in the room it is a crowded day.

**5.** I type him a line I wrote. I'm all here, I'm suffering. He types back, you're high nigga chill.

**6.** I have been walking rooms since I was small. I have never got tired of it. I do not know one other person who does it.

**7.** My father is down the hall. If he hears me he will do me four times, is how I put it, and I was laughing when I put it that way.

**8.** In la cité I used to carry a whole roasted chicken up the stairs and eat it by myself.

**9.** 105.99. Never 106.

**10.** I never go on first days. Not one, of anything, in my life. I wait until the thing is already running and then I walk in.

**11.** The first year of primary school. Not the preschool, the first year. I do not remember one thing about being inside that building. Not a face, not a desk, not the yard, not which way the door opened.

**12.** I remember being left there.

**13.** He types the word window and I read it as widow.

**14.** She asked what I was like on my first day of school. The introvert crying baby, or the one making friends.

**15.** I copied out the message the other one sent me when she turned me down. I need to choose healing instead of starting another relationship. I sent her the whole thing and wrote same script under it.

**16.** Growing up I had almost no social interaction. Not with the family either.

**17.** There are two years between my brother and me and I could not tell you what we did together in any of them.

**18.** In middle school I was the loudest person in the room.

**19.** I did the stupidest things I have done in my life in those three years, on purpose, so people would look.

**20.** If somebody annoyed me I went at him.

**21.** I cannot tell you the name of one person I ate with.

**22.** I have called it compensation since.

**23.** I do not like short hair. It makes my face seen.

**24.** On the tenth of August I saw thirty or more people. I could not tell you one of them.

**25.** I had not slept. I do not remember half of what I did.

**26.** There is a girl with psychosis who talks to me in the pharmacy because my father announced that there was a doctor in the house.

**27.** My father's friends stop me in the street to ask things.

**28.** A man came to the door once and I read his file and wrote out his script.

**29.** I look miserable in the hospital.

**30.** There was a night shift in July where nobody came. Not one patient, the whole night.

**31.** I found a corner and slept in it and it was the best sleep I have ever had.

**32.** In July I was walking in the university at eight at night. I was there because the library stays open.

**33.** Coming round the side of a building I saw a man standing and another man on his knees in front of him.

**34.** I did not stop and I did not turn around. I went to the library.

**35.** I drive alone on the roads at night and I go faster than I should.

**36.** Sometimes I go out at night for no reason except that there is nobody out at that hour. I like winter for the same reason.

**37.** Once I walked through the worst neighbourhood in the city on my own in the dark.

**38.** If it goes on too long I put the noise cancelling headphones on. The girls outside carried on the whole evening in August and without them I would have lost it.

**39.** Nobody asked me to sit there.

**40.** I do not remember the last time I was on a bus. If I am on one I stand in the corner of it and I do not look at anybody.

**41.** I am trying to act half dead.

**42.** At the end of July everyone left the house without telling me. I had it for three days and then the next week as well.

**43.** I ate what was in the fridge and then I ate what was in the freezer.

**44.** I read in the afternoon and I read at night.

**45.** I said out loud that the only person I was going to talk to that day was the book. Then I typed it to a girl I have never met.

**46.** They were the ones who left.

**47.** At night I am at the desk and the desk is in the corner and I am behind it.

**48.** The dormition of the Theotokos is on the wall in front of me. Mary on the bed, the apostles standing over her, Christ holding her soul like a small child.

**49.** The AC is playing its music. It has been thirty six hours and I am not tired.

**50.** There are crumbs on the desk. I touch one. The other one moves away from my hand and then it stands still and I eat the crumbs.

**51.** The music is in my ears and the hands are above my head but they are not, it is the lyrics.

**52.** I was in bed crying in the dark at two in the morning.

**53.** There is nothing to talk about. I am just crying while walking.

**54.** I converted in November. I have the contacts and I have not gone.

**55.** They will not let me go to Carthage on my own, and the man they would send with me prays five times a day and would want to know what I was going for.

**56.** Nine months. I am waiting for a companion I do not want.

**57.** In August we went to the coast. They kept asking me to come out and I told them to leave me alone.

**58.** The car in the morning and the car at night and nothing in between.

**59.** A girl I talk to every day was in the same city the whole time. She found out afterwards and said we could have met, and that if I had told her before. I had not told her before.

**60.** I did not want to be taken anywhere.

**61.** In Seraidi I finished the day at the hotel and they had the music going. I got up and walked away from it, far enough that it was only a sound, and I sat down on my own and looked at the view.

**62.** The view was better from where I was.

**63.** Then two men came out near where I was sitting. One of them was smoking. The other took a cigarette, put it in his mouth, leaned in and lit it off his friend's.

**64.** Then he put his hands on his waist and they stood there smiling at each other.

**65.** I could not take my eyes off them. I kept looking to see if they really were.

**66.** She told me to let her help. She said that if she had somebody she trusted she would cry in front of them, that she cries alone all the time and she also cries in front of people.

**67.** I told her that women know what to do with their feelings and that men hide it and die in their corner.

**68.** I said it like it was about men.

**69.** I told her once that being scared of silence means you feel like you have to perform to get something back. She agreed with me.

**70.** My brother was not home. I heard a voice and I turned around and there was nobody. The kitchen was empty. The window was open.

**71.** I like being alone.
