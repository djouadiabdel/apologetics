# The Corner

*Draft 3. Fourteen rooms, read out of the full transcripts rather than the extracted lines.*

---

I like being alone.

It is twenty past one in the morning and I have been walking this room for an hour. Bed, door, window, bed. I do not sit down when I talk to her. I am typing to my friend while I walk and what I type is I'm all here, I'm suffering, and something is making me into a thing that can carry a face that was never mine. He says you're high nigga chill. I keep walking. He says go to sleep like deadass. He types the word window and I read it as widow.

I have been doing this since I was small. I have never got tired of it and I do not know one other person who does it. The only thing that changed is that I keep it quiet now. My father is down the hall. If he hears me he will do me four times, is how I put it, and I was laughing when I put it that way.

I have always liked the quiet.

Growing up I had almost no social interaction. Not with the family either. There are two years between my brother and me and I could not tell you what we did together in any of them. When somebody tried to take an interest in someone near me I got a little jealous,.

I never go on first days. I have never gone on a first day or a first week of anything in my life. I wait until the thing is already running and then I walk in.

The first year of primary school. Not the preschool, the first year. I do not remember one thing about being inside that building. Not a face, not a desk, not the yard, not which way the door opened. I remember being left there. That is all of it. Everything else went and that stayed.

The first thing a teacher ever said to me was that I was a dumb fuck.

She asked me what I was like on my first day of school, whether I was the introvert crying baby or the one making friends. I did not answer her. I copied out the message the other one sent me when she turned me down, the whole thing, I need to choose healing instead of starting another relationship, and I sent it to her and I wrote same script under it.

In middle school I was the loudest person in the room. I did the stupidest things I have done in my life in those three years, on purpose, so people would look. If somebody annoyed me I went at him. I was not quiet once. I cannot tell you the name of a single person I ate with. I have called it compensation since.

At the gym I make them turn the music off. If there are three people in the room it is a crowded day. Most days it is the rings and the dumbbells at home in the evening, and then I eat, and then I read for three hours, or five.

In la cité I used to carry a whole roasted chicken up and eat it by myself. My friend can tell you about it. I weighed 105.99 that year. Never 106.

On the tenth of August I saw thirty or more people. I could not tell you one of them. I had not slept and I do not remember half of what I did. There is a girl with psychosis who talks to me in the pharmacy because my father announced that there was a doctor in the house, and my father's friends stop me to ask things, and a man came to the door once and I read his file and wrote out his script. I look miserable in the hospital.

There was a night shift in July where nobody came. Not one patient, the entire night. I found a corner and I slept in it and it was the best sleep I have ever had.

In July I was walking in the university at eight at night. I was there because the library stays open. Coming round the side of a building I saw a man standing and another man on his knees in front of him. I did not stop and I did not turn around. I went to the library.

Nobody asked me to sit there.

I drive alone on the roads at night and I go faster than I should. Sometimes I go out at night for no reason except that there is nobody out at that hour. I like winter for the same reason. Once I walked through the worst neighbourhood in the city on my own in the dark.

If I am somewhere with people and it goes on too long I put the noise cancelling headphones on. The girls outside carried on the whole evening in August and without them I would have lost it.

I do not remember the last time I was on a bus. If I am on one I stand in the corner of it and I do not look at anybody.

I am trying to act half dead.

At the end of July everyone left the house without telling me. I had it for three days and then the next week as well. I ate what was in the fridge and then what was in the freezer. I read in the afternoon and I read at night. At some point I said out loud that the only person I was going to talk to that day was the book, and then I typed it to a girl I have never met.

They were the ones who left.

I do not like short hair. It makes my face seen.

At night I am at the desk and the desk is in the corner and I am behind it. The dormition of the Theotokos is on the wall in front of me. Mary on the bed, the apostles standing over her, Christ holding her soul like a small child. The AC is playing its music. It has been thirty six hours and I am not tired.

There are crumbs on the desk. I touch one. The other one moves away from my hand and then it stands still and I eat the crumbs.

The music is in my ears and the hands are above my head but they are not, it is the lyrics.

I was in bed crying in the dark at two in the morning. There is nothing to talk about. I am just crying while walking.

In August we went to the coast. They kept asking me to come out and I told them to leave me alone. I spent it near the water, the car in the morning and the car at night and nothing in between. A girl I talk to every day was in the same city the whole time. She found out afterwards and said we could have met, and that if I had told her before. I had not told her before.

I did not want to be taken anywhere.

I have not gone to Carthage. I converted in November and I have the contacts and I have not gone, because my family said they would not let me go there alone, and the only man they would send with me prays five times a day and would want to know what I was going for. So it has been nine months. I am waiting for a companion I do not want.

In Seraidi I finished the day at the hotel and they had the music going. I got up and walked away from it, far enough that it was only a sound, and I sat down on my own and looked at the view.

The view was better from where I was.

Then two men came out near where I was sitting. One of them was smoking. The other one took a cigarette, put it in his mouth, leaned in and lit it off his friend's. Then he put his hands on his waist and they stood there smiling at each other. I could not take my eyes off them. I kept looking to see if they really were.

One of them told me to let her help. She said if she had somebody she trusted she would cry in front of them, that she cries alone all the time and she also cries in front of people, and she asked me to let her in on it.

I told her that women know what to do with their feelings and that men hide it and die in their corner.

I said it like it was about men.

I told her once that being scared of silence means you feel like you have to perform to get something back. She agreed with me.

My brother was not home. I heard a voice and I turned around and there was nobody. The kitchen was empty. The window was open.

I like being alone.
