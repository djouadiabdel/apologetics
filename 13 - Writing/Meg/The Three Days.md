They agreed on three days apart. Meg said it like a doctor. Faith heard it like a sentence.

---

**One**

Faith worked.

She had never been the kind to look at her own day and take it apart, and that morning she took it apart. Woke, sat, started inside the hour. Used the small steps Meg had built for her, the ones that let you quit at any point so you never have to be brave to begin.

By evening she had done more than she had done in a month. She felt the thing she had been told she would feel.

She did not think about who had built the steps.

Nobody is remembered for handing you a method. The method becomes yours the moment it works, and the hand that gave it becomes a period you were going through. Faith would have said she was grateful. She was busy.

Meg spent the day being aware of the phone.

She had taught Faith how to stand up without her. It had worked. She had made herself into the thing you use and then do not need, and she had done it on purpose, and she had done it because being needed was the only shape of love she trusted, and now the shape was gone and the love was supposed to still be there.

At ten she checked whether the message had been seen. It had.

---

**Two**

Faith woke with a clean thought and it frightened her.

None of it was hard. Everything Meg had ever told her was ordinary. Start small. Rest on a timer. Do sixty percent on a sixty percent day and call it full. Any book would say it. She could have found it alone.

So why had she needed a person to say it.

And under that, the other thought, the one that had teeth. She gave me the method because she wanted me. You feed the animal you are hunting. You give it the best thing you own and you wait.

Faith sat with that for a long time. It explained everything and it cost nothing to believe, which should have warned her.

Meg spent the day trying to work and thinking about being replaceable.

Not replaced. Replaceable. Faith had stood up without her. Anyone could have done that job. Meg had not been the reason for anything, and she did not know how to say what she was if she was not the reason.

She wanted to send something. She wrote it and did not send it. She wrote a shorter one and did not send that either.

Then she cleaned the room, which is what she does.

---

**Three**

It rained all day and neither of them worked.

Faith broke at ten in the evening, without warning, in the middle of a page. The studying was for a life she could describe and could not want. A steady job. Money for her parents. A version of herself that never has to ask anyone for anything.

She could see it. It was fine. It was somebody else's.

She cried the way you cry when the thing you are crying about is not in the room.

Meg broke earlier and quieter. She had done everything correctly for three days. There was no one to show.

By eleven they were both walking.

The rain had turned the streets to mud and the city had given up on itself. Faith went the long way to keep from arriving too fast. Meg went the short way and ran.

Faith had a speech. She had built it on the walk over, in order, with the part about being used first and the part about not needing anyone second and the part about coming anyway at the end. She said it under her breath at the corners.

They arrived at empty houses.

Meg stood in Faith's doorway with mud to the knee while Faith's mother asked twice if she was all right. Faith stood in Meg's kitchen and was offered tea. Two hours gone. Each of them somewhere the other had just left.

Meg looked for Faith where Faith goes. The bookshops. The two cafes. The one shop with the good window.

Faith looked for Meg where she was most afraid of finding her, which is a different kind of looking.

The rain stopped around one and the streets came back.

Meg gave up in a small square with a bench in it and sat down at the end of the bench, next to a stranger, and started humming. It was the song. It was always going to be the song.

The stranger put a hand on her arm.

Faith did not give the speech.

She had it whole. She had the order of it. She opened her mouth and her body did something else entirely, and afterward she could not have said what the first part was even about.

They kissed on a wet bench in front of whoever was awake. For the three days. For the ones before that.

---

Walking back, neither of them said the thing they had learned. Faith did not say that she could stand on her own and had hated every hour of it. Meg did not say that she had spent three days finding out she was a method with a face on it.

They took the long way because their shoes were finished anyway.

At the door Meg said, tomorrow we start again.

And Faith said, yes, and did not ask what they were starting.
