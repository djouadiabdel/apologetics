Meg woke cold and did not want the day.

Rain. Her brother had left before light and the house had made a production of it. Her mother went through the kitchen the way she always did, no thought at all for the girl still in bed. Meg had stopped minding years ago.

That was the first thing she got wrong. It was done before she opened her eyes. She had learned to live in the room instead of changing it.

The song came on. She let it play and felt the small lift it always gave her, which is not the same as being glad.

---

I looked at the ceiling. My cheeks were cold. I wanted something and could not name it, so I gave it the wrong name and got up.

Washed my face. Ate standing. Watched videos while I ate, one and then another, spending the day's attention before the day had started. I know what I am doing while I do it. Knowing has never once stopped me.

Coffee. Then the idea came.

What if I could focus for ten hours a day.

I want to be honest about where that came from. It did not come from wanting to work. It came from wanting to be finished. Ten hours in one day and the other days go empty. Nothing asked of me in them. That was the appeal.

There are two ways to be consistent. Do it every day, or do it on certain days and do it enormously. Both need momentum. Both break when the room changes.

The room is the part nobody believes. When I am home I build a way of being that fits the house, and it works, and then I travel and it is gone. Not weakened. Gone. My brain does not file the two places as two places. It files them as two worlds, and it wants whichever one gave it more.

It is like having two girlfriends. If you compare, the one in front of you is never the one being judged. You are grading her against a memory. The memory has no bad mornings in it.

Our brains are relative.

So I sat there with the coffee going cold and thought, I should make a cursed tool. Something that only works when I set the conditions. Only I can set them. Using it costs me something every time, and the cost is the point, because then the switch is not mine to argue with. I would just pay and go.

The trouble is the room again. I have divided it into two halves, work and rest, and my brain has never once agreed. It is one room. It has always been one room. And somewhere in the evening I start owing myself fun, because I have trained myself to earn it, and the earning made it worse. I taught myself to need the reward. Now the reward is a job.

Things wrong with the day, in order:

The head after waking. The first hour. The room that is not two rooms. The wanting of fun. The reward I built and cannot dismantle.

What I have learned is smaller than the list. Start. Five minutes is a start. Beat yesterday's five minutes the way you beat a weight, by one plate, and do not look at the whole stack. Rest badly on purpose. Sit and do nothing that gives you anything back.

Eat before the gym or the focus goes. Put the gym at the end, so the window closes at five and closes for a reason.

After the gym, nothing. No screen. Zero.

I read that back and it is a man building a machine so he does not have to sit still in a room he already lives in.

---

Meg wrote the list out again in the evening and it was shorter the second time.

She did not know yet whether she could hold a whole day. She knew she had spent the morning designing a way to be somebody by five o'clock.

Tomorrow she would try it. That was the plan and the plan was the point, the way the plan is always the point.
