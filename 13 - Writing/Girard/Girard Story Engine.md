# Girard Story Engine

Concept map, link table, and story clusters for the collection. Each story is one person who does not know what is happening to them, 2,000 to 2,500 words, first person.

**How to use.** Pick a cluster. Read only its assigned pages, twenty minutes. Write the story. Do not read ahead, and do not read the whole corpus first.

**Rule that governs everything here: the concepts are for me, never for the page.** No character names a mechanism. No narrator understands the structure. If a reader could extract the concept as a stated idea, the story failed.

### The rest of the corpus, not on disk

**Warning on this section.** I have not opened these books. Titles, dates and subject matter are reliable; **chapter titles are from memory and page ranges are deliberately absent.** Verify against the book when you acquire it, and do not cite anything here as a source until you have.

Ranked by what they actually add to *this* project, which is fiction, not apologetics.

**1. *Resurrection from the Underground: Feodor Dostoevsky* (1963).** The most important missing one for the form you have chosen. It is Girard on the confessional narrator who explains himself and is wrong, which is the exact machine every story in this collection runs on. Underground man, the double, the conversion at the end. If you buy one, buy this.

**2. *A Theater of Envy: William Shakespeare* (1991).** Girard working through characters and scenes across most of the plays instead of through theory. The richest supply of concrete situations in the whole corpus, and the best model for how to carry a mechanism without stating it. Adds the collapse of *degree* and hierarchy, and the switching of desire in *Midsummer Night's Dream*.

**3. *Violence and the Sacred* (1972).** The engine underneath the crowd. Sacrificial crisis, undifferentiation, the monstrous double, sacrificial substitution. Required for any story where more than three people are involved. Nothing in the three books on disk covers the monstrous double properly.

**4. *The Scapegoat* (1982).** The four stereotypes of persecution, read off medieval texts written by persecutors who did not know what they were writing. That is a story template on its own, and it is the closest Girard comes to handing a novelist a form.

**5. *Job: The Victim of His People* (1985).** One man against a community that has already decided. The cleanest single-situation book he wrote, and it maps onto a single story more directly than anything else.

**6. *Anorexia and Mimetic Desire* (2005).** Short. Rivalry conducted through the body and through refusal. Directly usable and almost nobody uses it.

**7. *Battling to the End* (2007).** Escalation to extremes once the brakes are gone. For the terminal stories only.

**8. *Evolution and Conversion* (2004) and *Quand ces choses commenceront* (1994).** Interviews. Fast way to hear him say a concept plainly. Not needed for fiction.

**9. *Sacrifice* (Vedic texts), *Celui par qui le scandale arrive*, *Critique dans un souterrain*, *Mimesis and Theory*, *The Girard Reader*.** Completeness. Skip until the collection exists.

## 2. The concepts, as a disease and not a list

Read down. Each stage is produced by the one above it. The ordering is Girard's own: he calls the hero's career a descent from the upper regions of a novelistic domain to the lower ones, and treats the novels as portions of one continuous structure.

> **How to use this, and how not to.** It is a severity scale, not a plot. It tells me how deep a character is, and which concepts live next to each other so I know what will turn up in the story uninvited.
>
> **It is not a route anybody has to walk.** If I use it as a ladder, every story becomes a man moving from stage three to stage five and the whole book reads as a diagram. Most stories should not move the character down the scale at all. He is simply at a depth, and the story is what one week at that depth looks like. Movement is one setting on the outcome dial in §4, not the default.

**Stage 0. Ground.** Triangular desire. The mediator. External mediation, where the model is too far to collide with and nothing goes wrong. *DDN I, pp.1–12.*

**Stage 1. The turn.** Internal mediation, the model close enough to reach for the same thing. Model becomes obstacle. The double bind, *imitate me* and *do not imitate me* in one order. Scandal, the obstacle you keep going back to. *DDN I pp.9–12 · Things Hidden III.1 pp.290–294 · I See Satan ch.1 pp.7–18.*

**Stage 2. The circuit closes.** Double mediation, each is the other's model. Doubles, the two become indistinguishable while insisting they are opposites. Desire without object, where the thing itself drops out. *DDN IV pp.99–104 · Things Hidden III.2 pp.299–325.*

**Stage 3. The erotic complications.** Coquetry, arousing in order to refuse. Concealment, whoever shows loses. Flaubert's law, two never love at the same time. Masochism, then the switch to sadism. *DDN IV pp.105–112 · DDN VIII pp.176–192 · Things Hidden III.3 pp.326–335.*

**Stage 4. Spread.** Contagion. Anyone who tries to cure it catches it worse. Alternation, the symptoms cycling. *DDN I and IV · Things Hidden III.2 pp.305–310 · I See Satan ch.2 pp.19–31.*

**Stage 5. Divinization.** Metaphysical desire, wanting the other's being rather than the other. Deviated transcendency, the person put in God's slot. *DDN II pp.53–82 · I See Satan ch.3 pp.32–48.*

**Stage 5b. The monstrous double.** The rival stops being a person and becomes a figure, both fascinating and hideous, and the subject cannot look away. This is the missing link between the private stages above and the group below, and **it is not properly covered in any book on disk.** *Violence and the Sacred, the chapter on mimetic desire and the monstrous double.*

**Stage 6. The group.** Undifferentiation, where everyone becomes interchangeable and the collapse of distinctions is itself experienced as the crisis. Then all against one. The victimage mechanism. The crowd that agrees and calls its agreement a judgement. The four stereotypes by which a persecuting text gives itself away. *I See Satan ch.7 pp.82–94, ch.12 pp.154–163 · Things Hidden I.1 pp.3–47 · Violence and the Sacred on the sacrificial crisis · The Scapegoat on the stereotypes.*

**Stage 6b. No brakes.** Once the sacrificial machinery stops working, nothing damps the escalation and it runs to the end. *Battling to the End.*

**Stage 7. Exits, three false and one real.** Askesis, self-denial that is really a longer route to possession. Engineered non-desire, which is pride at its most advanced. Curing someone else. And conversion, which only ever happens at the end of resources. *DDN VII pp.153–175 · DDN XI pp.256–289 · DDN XII pp.290–314 · Things Hidden III.5 pp.393–402.*

## 3. Link table

What pulls what. Use this to find the second and third concept once the first is chosen.

| If the story starts here | It naturally drags in | Do not also add |
|---|---|---|
| Internal mediation | model as obstacle, scandal | anything from stage 6 |
| Double mediation | concealment, Flaubert's law | coquetry (redundant, it is a form of it) |
| Coquetry | metaphysical desire, contagion | masochism (too close) |
| Concealment | askesis, masochism | conversion |
| Contagion | the failed curer, the crowd | metaphysical desire |
| Metaphysical desire | deviated transcendency, the absent rival | the group stages |
| Masochism | divinization of the withholder, the switch | coquetry |
| The crowd | scandal, the founding murder | the erotic stages |
| Conversion | everything above it, as history | any second exit |

**Three concepts maximum per story.** The reason is not purity, it is room. Others will arrive on their own while you write, and those unplanned ones are usually the best thing in the piece. A story loaded with five has no space left for anything to happen by accident.

## 4. The generator

**One concept does not equal one story.** One concept equals a family of stories, and the family is produced by spinning five dials against it. This is the actual engine. The clusters further down are just spins I already did.

### The five dials

**1. Position.** Who is telling it. Subject, the one desiring · Model, being imitated and usually unaware of it · Object, the one being wanted · Bystander, who catches it by watching.

**2. Outcome.** Nothing changes, and he is only more fluent about it by the end · False conversion, everything changes and nothing does · The switch, he moves to the other seat and runs the same machine · Escalation, it goes to the end · Real conversion, and only at the end of resources.

**3. Awareness.** No idea · Has a theory and the theory is wrong · **Has the correct theory and it changes nothing**, which is the cruellest and the most modern.

**4. Relation.** Lover · friend · sibling · parent · teacher · colleague · stranger.

**5. Span.** One evening · one week · years compressed into 2,000 words.

Four positions by five outcomes by three awarenesses by seven relations by three spans is 1,260 stories per concept, and there are thirty-six high-yield concepts. That is the number you were reaching for.

### Worked example: concept 24 alone, four stories

**24a.** Subject / no idea / nothing changes / lover / one evening. A man will not say the thing. He has a reason. The evening ends and he still has the reason.

**24b.** Model / wrong theory / real conversion / lover / years. Told by the one being concealed from. She has spent years reading a man who was managing what she saw, and her theory about why he is distant is wrong in an interesting way. She gets out.

**24c.** Bystander / correct theory and it changes nothing / escalation / siblings. He watches two brothers do it, understands the mechanism completely, can describe it accurately, and does nothing, and it goes all the way.

**24d.** Subject / wrong theory / the switch / colleague. He has always been the one who waits. He gets the position where he is waited for, and does exactly what was done to him, and calls it having learned something.

Four stories, one concept, no repetition. Do this to any of the thirty-six.

### The distribution rule for the collection

Some convert, most do not. If everyone converts the book is propaganda and if nobody does it is a pose. Girard's own position is that conversion is real, rare, and never arrives on a good day. So across fifteen stories: **two real conversions, both late in the book. Two or three false conversions. The rest split between nothing changes, the switch, and escalation.**

The reader should not be able to predict which one they are getting.

## 5. Worked clusters

Spins I already did. Each has the concepts, the reading, the situation, and the thing the narrator is wrong about. The wrongness is the engine. He states his reason early and the reader watches it fail to account for his behaviour.

**1. The Spot.** Concealment + Flaubert's law + askesis. *Read DDN IV pp.109–112.* A man will not say the one thing. He has an elaborate reason. Every scene is a chance to say it and a different excuse for not doing so. **Wrong about:** thinking the silence is dignity.

**2. The Cure.** Contagion + the failed curer. *Read DDN I on contagion; I See Satan ch.2 pp.19–31.* He sets out to talk a friend down from an obsession, and by the end wants the thing himself. **Wrong about:** believing he is outside it.

**3. The Recommendation.** External mediation collapsing into internal. *Read DDN I pp.9–12.* Someone he admires from a distance moves into his life. Admiration was fine at range. **Wrong about:** calling the new bitterness a discovery about the other person's character.

**4. The Ledger.** Metaphysical desire + deviated transcendency + counting. *Read DDN II pp.53–82.* A man keeps a private tally of a relationship. Seconds, replies, who began. **Wrong about:** thinking the count measures her.

**5. The Twins.** Doubles + double bind. *Read Things Hidden III.1 pp.290–294, III.2 pp.299–305.* Two people become identical while each insists they are nothing like the other. **Wrong about:** the difference.

**6. The Absent One.** Chain triangles + rivalry with someone not present. *Read DDN IV pp.99–107.* He competes with a person who is dead, gone, or possibly invented. **Wrong about:** believing that winning is available.

**7. The Gift.** The model who never withholds. *Read DDN IV pp.109–112; I See Satan ch.13.* Someone gives him unconditional regard and he treats them with contempt for it. **Wrong about:** reading the generosity as weakness.

**8. Desire Without Object.** *Read Things Hidden III.2 pp.310–325.* Two men are still fighting after the thing they were fighting over is gone, and neither notices it went. **Wrong about:** what the fight is for.

**9. The Switch.** Masochism into sadism. *Read DDN VIII pp.176–192; Things Hidden III.3 pp.326–335.* He has been the one who waits. He becomes the one who is waited for, and does the same thing. **Wrong about:** thinking he has recovered.

**10. The Agreement.** Scandal + all against one. *Read I See Satan ch.1 pp.7–18 and ch.7 pp.82–94.* A small group settles, and the settling requires one person. **Wrong about:** believing the group reached a judgement.

**11. The Fast.** Askesis. *Read DDN VII pp.153–175.* He gives something up, and the giving up is a purchase. **Wrong about:** calling it discipline.

**12. The Conversion That Wasn't.** False conversion. *Read DDN V pp.113–138 on Rênal.* He changes completely and nothing changes. **Wrong about:** thinking something happened.

**13. The Drop.** Real conversion. *Read DDN XII pp.290–314.* Last in the collection. He is out of resources and stops. **Wrong about:** nothing, for four sentences, and this is the only story in the book where that is true.

### Clusters that need a book I do not have

Write these later, after acquiring. Listed so the map is complete rather than shaped by what happens to be on my disk.

**14. The Face.** The monstrous double. *Needs Violence and the Sacred.* The rival stops being a person he can describe. He is repelled and cannot stop looking, and he keeps arranging to be where the other one is. **Wrong about:** thinking the fascination is hatred.

**15. The Four Signs.** The stereotypes of persecution. *Needs The Scapegoat.* Written by a narrator who is one of the persecutors and does not know it, reporting the crisis, the accusation, the mark on the victim, and the resolution, entirely as fact. **Wrong about:** everything, and never corrected inside the story.

**16. The Comforters.** *Needs Job.* One person against everyone he knows, all of whom are being kind. **Wrong about:** believing the kindness is separable from the verdict.

**17. The Same.** Undifferentiation. *Needs Violence and the Sacred.* A group where the distinctions quietly stop holding, and everyone reacts to the sameness with panic they explain other ways. **Wrong about:** the cause of the tension.

**18. The Body.** *Needs Anorexia and Mimetic Desire.* Rivalry conducted entirely through refusal and appearance, with no stated rival. **Wrong about:** thinking it is about control.

**19. The Underground.** *Needs Resurrection from the Underground.* The purest version of the form. A narrator who explains himself for 2,000 words with total fluency and is wrong in every paragraph. Write this one after reading that book, not before, because it is the model for the whole collection.

**20. No Brakes.** *Needs Battling to the End.* Two parties past the point where anything slows them. Short, late in the book, and the only story with no domestic setting.

## 5. Rules for writing them

1. No character names a mechanism. No narrator has the vocabulary.
2. The narrator may name his feelings. He may not be right about them.
3. He does not learn. Except in cluster 13, and there only at the very end.
4. Accurate behaviour, wrong explanation. The reader assembles the truth from the gap.
5. Report what happened, do not describe what it meant. Material from `The Log.md` goes here.
6. Under 2,500 words. Finished before the next is started.
7. No epigraphs from Girard, no dedication to Girard, nothing that tells the reader where this came from.
