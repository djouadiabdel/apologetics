# IX. The Fire

It is not a thing and it was never set for punishing. Nobody lit it and nobody is standing back from it.[^c9-r1]

It is what arrives when you are at last looked at by the one who has been saying your name, and it is called burning only on account of what you are, not on account of what it is. Blood comes back into a limb that has learned to live without it, and the limb screams, and the blood is the cure, and the screaming is the cure working.

There is no clock in it. Do not ask how long. It is not that kind of duration. The question is not difficult, it is badly made, in the same way the count of eight was badly made, and by the same sort of tidy men.

And what is being burned is not you. It is the enormous quantity of straw and cheap timber you piled over the one true decision you ever made, which is still down there under all of it, and which you have not been able to reach with your own hands for years. That is the whole operation. Not to destroy you. To make you into something that can stand in front of what is coming and not be killed by it.

I turned to give all of that to the old man, because I had it in order and it was good and I wanted it heard.

He was no longer beside you.

I looked back toward the window. He was not there either.

I went along the seventh calling the only name I had for him, which was not a name. The people at their desks did not look up. The man on the platform had begun again. Somewhere behind me, a pen kept pace with his voice.

The route back to the stairs was open. I knew what would happen if I took it. At the top the pages would be against my chest again. The ledger would pull at my shoulder. My name would fit around me. I would count seven things twelve times and begin with a better explanation.

You remained at the gate.

For the first time, you were facing something other than me.

Then there was light along the bottom of the door.

The gate did not open. I watched the line around it. No hinge moved, no latch withdrew, no space appeared between the door and its frame.

The light came through anyway.

It crossed the stone toward us, and there was a body standing in it on our side of the closed gate.[^c9-r2]

Behind me, the pen stopped.

The man on the platform said that every barrier is permeable to its proper object. He began explaining that the gate had therefore not been broken. His voice remained calm, but the people at the desks were no longer writing. One by one they turned toward the warmth.

He raised his voice.

The warmth reached the platform. His crown threw no reflection. He put both hands around it and continued speaking, faster now, establishing the distinction between an exception and a contradiction.

Nobody wrote the distinction down.

He called for the young woman by name.

She stood.

For one moment I thought she was answering him. Then she stepped away from the desk and faced the body in the light.

The man on the platform was still correct. The gate had not opened. Nothing in its construction had failed. The thing on our side had simply crossed from the direction no resident had been able to travel.

It was standing.

It had been killed. This is the one place in the whole account where I have no equipment. It was a small thing and it had been killed and it was standing up, and the killing had not been undone, it was still in it, it was on it, the throat, and it was standing.

And it had seven eyes.

I counted them. I have counted everything on the way down here and this is the only count I have ever made that came out right the first time. Seven. Not eight and not six and not a tidy man's seven that used to be eight. Seven, in a body, looking at me out of all of them at once.

One eye held the colour of the old man's eyes at the window. Another held the slight delay before his apology. I do not know how an eye can contain a delay. I am reporting what I saw.

The white of the cassock remained around the small body for a moment, not as cloth but as the memory of a shape no longer needed. The hands behind the back were the last part to go. They opened at his sides.

I had spent years constructing the person I would obey. I had selected age, office, patience, learning, the visible proof of a life whose arguments had been received. I had made him small so that power would appear disciplined and dressed him in white so that ambition could pass for service. I had prepared every inch of him as carefully as I prepared a sentence.

Another had accepted the shape without accepting one claim I had made for it.

Every floor I have described to you, it had already been on. Every one. The old hand at the ledger was it. The man at the window was it. The voice on the fifth saying my name twice and not a third time was it.

I had built the shape of the theologian myself: the cassock, the hands, the patient posture, the old age in which the world had finally listened. But the one standing inside the shape had never been me. I had been accompanied by another through the whole descent while explaining to you that I was the guide.

I got down. Not out of reverence. There was no decision in it, the legs went.

The crown came off my head as I went down. It fell onto the stone beside me and moved. I could hear it against the floor. It was shaking the way a light thing shakes when something very large is coming up beneath it.

Then the blood.

I am going to do this properly because it is the only part of the whole descent that was done to me rather than by me.

It came onto the back of my hands first and it was warm and that was all it was for a moment, warm, like being touched by somebody who has been out in the sun. Then it went in. Not through a cut. In, the way heat goes into a hand that has been out in the cold too long and has stopped reporting, and the first thing that happens is not pain, the first thing that happens is that the hand starts existing again, and you find out how much of it had gone quiet without telling you.

Then it hurt.

No, first the hands came back. Under the nails, then every fingertip separately, the stone in every line of the palms and I tried to lift them and could not because there was too much hand, every joint there at once, every small bone moving against the next, the wrists beating where the body had been trying the doors from inside.

Then the right elbow. A cold place in it I had carried so long I thought that was simply where an arm ended. The heat entered and the arm went out from under me and I was on one shoulder and looking at you through my fingers.

Not nearer.

Not gone.

I tried to decide which one meant—

The blood reached my throat.

The right hand moved without being told: chest, hip, mouth, shoulder. At the throat it closed and would not go farther.

The first sound was not a word. The next almost had your name in it, though I do not know your name down here, though I have not written it, though— I bit it apart.

Then the jaw opened. Every explanation was in there. I had been holding all of them with my teeth. They struck together and I tasted blood from the tongue and could not tell which blood was mine, which had come in through the hands, whether that distinction still—

I pissed myself and only knew because it went cold under my hip.

It went up the arms and into the chest and it found every place I had shut and it opened them in order, and each one screamed as it came back on, and the screaming was not the injury, I understood that at the time and I am not saying it now because it sounds well, I understood it while it was happening. The blood was the cure. The screaming was the cure working. I was not being punished. I was being reperfused.[^c9-g1]

The chest was worse because it had continued functioning while parts of it disappeared. The heart had learned a shortened route. It beat around certain rooms, sending only enough blood through them to keep their doors from being declared closed. When the full circulation returned, those rooms expanded against the ribs.

The second floor opened first. Every gift I had received arrived again without the stories I had attached to its limits. Warmth struck the chest and left, struck and left, and every departure was clean. Nothing had been stolen on the way out. I had been making a wound out of the fact that a gift ends.

The ledger followed. I felt its absent weight along my hip and all the quantities I had lost at the sixth gate tried to return. They could not become numbers. They entered as people instead. Every column acquired a face I had claimed not to be judging.

Then my name entered, the whole of it, not spoken from outside now but carried in the blood to every place that had refused it. The body answered before the mouth. The back arched. The hands opened.

And the whole of it, the entire enormous thing, was longing.

That is what it is made of. Not fire. Wanting. Every want I had put down and pretended out of existence returned with the circulation: the four seconds, the pages, the sentence rehearsed for a month, the hand at the back of the neck, every answer I had converted into a promise and every boundary I had renamed as delay, all of it alive at the same time, at full strength, in a body, and there was no position I could lie in.

And you. Not crowned. Not waiting at the end of anything. You with your hand on the wet wall instead of in mine. You saying *I heard you* and making me hate three ordinary words because there was nowhere inside them to put a distinction. You bareheaded on the stair, taking two of my fingers and then taking your hand back. I wanted the hand back. I wanted your voice and the weight of you near me and the name we had not agreed to give any of it. The fire did not make that want smaller. It made it impossible to confuse with peace.

Then the older rule came back.

Nobody had taught it to me. I made it out of what hurt: do not ask again. Give first. Give more. Know what is needed before it is spoken. Become so useful that refusal would look like cruelty, then call yourself loving because you never made the refusal come out of anybody's mouth. My hands began looking for work while I lay there. One reached for pages that were gone. The other opened and closed on the stone.

There was nothing left to give.

I wanted you more.

There was also the wanting to be the old man.

It had hidden better than the others because it wore usefulness. I wanted the two halves brought together. I wanted old wounds closed. I wanted people to love what was true because somebody had made it beautiful enough to approach.

And I wanted the somebody to have my name.[^c9-g2]

The fire did not separate those wants for me. It turned both to full strength and left them touching. Vocation and the wish to be seen having a vocation occupied the same piece of flesh. I could not burn one without feeling the other burn beside it.

On the platform, the seated man was still explaining. His voice broke on a word and began the sentence again. The warmth had reached his folded legs. He could have stood.

I did not see whether he did.

The thing on the stone beside me had gone dark along one edge and was curling in toward the middle, slowly, the way something does when the heat has got properly into it. I watched it do that with my face on the ground. I thought, that is what fire does to metal.

I have never in my life seen what fire does to metal.

And it went on.

And the next part is the only good news I have.

And while it was at full strength I went looking, because I am the way I am and I do not stop being the way I am even for that.

I went through myself for the second room. The not-caring. The going to the window, the two lines and putting it down, the other city and the kinder people. I went through myself for it the way you go through a coat you are sure you left something in.

I searched the ordinary places first. The wish to sleep after waking. The moment after reading two lines when the hand puts the page down before the mind has refused it. The belief that work would become possible in a cleaner room. The calm voice used to describe a life already being abandoned.

I found memories of all of them. A memory is not a room. I entered each and struck the walls. Grief answered from several. Shame answered from more. Wanting answered from all of them.

The wet-handed man was not there.

The window was not there.

The coat had no other city in its pockets.

It is not in me.

I could not find it anywhere and I looked properly and I had the whole of myself open at the time, every room of it, nothing shut, and it was not there.

The other one was still in me. The grieving. It was exactly where it has always been and nothing was happening to it. It was not being burned. It was not on the list of what was being burned. I lay there and grieved the entire time and the fire went round it the way water goes round a stone.[^c9-r3]

I tried to turn the stone over for an accusation. There was no underside. What happened had happened. What I had loved remained loved. The fire would not make a case from it.

Two rooms. I had it right. I had been right from the fourth floor and was right now, and being right had done nothing for me in eleven years. Knowing there are two rooms is not the same as having only one of them left. This did while I lay on the stone what correctness could not do in a decade.

And I will tell you the mechanism because I worked it out lying down and it is the only piece of this I have that nobody handed me.

You cannot not-care while you are burning like that.

That is all it is. The second room needs a low flame. It grows in a man who has gone slightly cold and has not noticed, and the whole of its power is that it feels like sorrow, and sorrow is warm, and the moment something turns the wanting all the way up the not-caring has nowhere to stand. It is not driven out. There is simply no room for it in a body where everything is on.

So the cure for the second room was never the word stay. Stay is what you do while waiting for this. I have been telling people the wrong thing for years, and I was very good at it, and they wrote it down.[^c9-rg1]

It went on until it stopped being able to burn me.

Not because it went out. It did not go out, it is still going, it is what the warm at the front of this account has been the whole time. It stopped because there stopped being anything left in me that had a temperature. Everything in me that could be burned had been.

What remained was not that you were mine. The fire gave me nothing about your answer. It was only this, and I could not make it better:

I love you.

I do not know what you will do.

There was no second half.

I lay on the stone in the middle of the fire and it did not hurt anymore and I have never been so awake.

## Theological material — Ratzinger

Full context: [[The Crowning — Ratzinger Sourcebook#VII. Hell, Purgatory, Heaven — printed pp. 215–238]]. Read especially printed pp. 228–232.

**Ecclesial idea:** Purgatory is not an external punishment imposed from a distance. Ratzinger identifies the fire with encounter with the Lord: Christ's intimate presence burns through closure and makes the person capable of God and of communion. Grace does not spare transformation; transformation is grace becoming victorious in the person. The communion of saints accompanies rather than watches from safety.

**Short seed:** “He himself will be saved, but only as through fire.” — 1 Corinthians 3:15, quoted by Ratzinger on printed p. 228.

**Do not turn it into:** Pain proving sincerity, blood purchasing the woman, or the narrator's suffering making him Christlike enough to deserve her. The Lamb acts; the narrator receives what he could not perform.

**Try writing from:** Begin when light crosses the still-closed gate. Write only what changes in the platform, the pen, the crown, and his body. Afterward, permit at most one sentence of doctrine. If the scene already says the fire is a person, cut the explanation that says it again.

## Desire material — Girard

Full context: [[The Crowning — Girard Sourcebook#The Catholic guardrail]] and [[The Crowning — Girard Sourcebook#6. Conversion]].

**Girardian pressure:** The narrator wants suffering to consecrate him: enough pain might prove the love, vindicate the vocation, and make him a figure worthy of being received. Girard's warning is exact here. Self-sacrifice can hide the desire to make oneself sacred. The actual Lamb therefore does not crown his suffering; it ends his authorship of the sacrifice.

**Short seed:** “The desire to sacralize oneself.” — *Things Hidden*, printed p. 236.

**Do not turn it into:** A denial of Christ's sacrifice, Eucharistic sacrifice, or the redemptive meaning of suffering united to Christ. This card detects the narrator's imitation of sacrifice for prestige; it does not define Catholic atonement.

**Try writing from:** When the blood first touches him, let him expect one admiring witness or one confirming sentence. Then deny him that composition. What happens to the body when nobody is watching the suffering in the manner he prepared?

## Sentence footnotes — Ratzinger + Girard

[^c9-r1]: **Ratzinger — purgatory is encounter, not an apparatus.** Printed pp. 228–231 identify the judging fire Christologically: the Lord's presence transforms rather than an external mechanism punishing. The idea is exact, but the opening may say it before the event earns it. Test how much the Lamb's arrival can carry unaided.

[^c9-r2]: **Ratzinger — grace crosses the boundary from its own side.** The gate remains closed because the narrator has not asked; Christ's presence is not therefore imprisoned by the narrator's refusal. This preserves both the law of freedom and the priority of grace.

[^c9-g1]: **Girard — beware self-consecration through pain.** The humiliation and pain must not become evidence that the narrator suffered exceptionally well. “I understood that at the time” risks returning mastery to him inside the one event he could not author. Consider whether the body can know before the sentence does.

[^c9-g2]: **Girard — metaphysical ambition finally names itself.** The vocation may be real; the desire that the reconciler bear his name is also real. Keeping both in one piece of flesh is stronger than purifying the vocation into innocence.

[^c9-r3]: **Ratzinger — suffering is not all one substance.** Love and grief can remain while closure is burned away. This prevents purification from becoming apatheia or emotional erasure. It also prevents grief from counting as merit: the fire protects it without rewarding him with her.

[^c9-rg1]: **Accurate teaching was never participation.** Girard explains why disciples can copy a cure their teacher has not undergone; Ratzinger makes transformation the grace of encounter itself. This is the payoff of the seventh floor, so let “they wrote it down” close the mechanism without another summary.
