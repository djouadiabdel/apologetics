# II–IV candidates

Step 5 of the work order. Candidates only, sorted by confidence. Nothing has been changed in any chapter file. Every line is quoted so the decision can be made without opening three files.

Tested against the operational law in [[The Crowning — Revision Map]] and against the voice standard in [[The Crowning — VII Composite]].

## Three findings that change the map

### 1. `04:19` pre-empts the old man and breaks `04:103`

> The falsity is not in a number. It is in the belief that somewhere a final column exists in which a person can be made even with another person and walk away unchanged.

Eighty lines later the old man says:

> You do not settle up. You are changed, or you are not.

And the narrator says:

> I wish that sentence were mine.

He already said it, at the top of the chapter, at greater length. *I wish that sentence were mine* is the best line in IV and it currently follows a paragraph in which he articulated the same thesis unaided.

This is structural, not tonal. Cutting `04:19` from *The falsity* onward restores the old man's sentence as an arrival and makes the wish honest. Keep *The documents are excellent. The figures can be checked.*

### 2. `03:97` is only half-cut by the current ruling

The map cuts the fourth sentence. The first three deliver the same verdict:

> The limit had been inside the gift from the beginning. Nothing was withdrawn. Nothing promised was withheld. ~~I took what was offered, separated the warmth from the boundary that gave it a shape, and called the boundary an injury.~~

Removing only the last sentence leaves the moral finding intact in the three before it. Either cut the paragraph entire, or accept that the verdict stays and cut nothing. Recommend the former: `03:99` (*I had the pages with me... folded small in against the chest where warmth goes in and does not stay*) follows immediately and is the real sentence.

### 3. The knowledge-verb search misses III's two verdicts about her

Both contain no cognition verb and both fail the law's third clause:

> `03:27` She was allowing me the chance to hear what I had asked.

> `03:53` The room lost nothing by your refusal, and you owed it no performance of gratitude.

The first has him correctly reading her motive and correctly grading his own request. The second has him stating the floor's theological point about non-coercive giving, accurately, in his own voice, pre-fire. This is the catechism risk arriving in the narrator's mouth instead of the old man's.

In both cases the surrounding behaviour already carries it. `03:25` *The woman hesitated for the first time* is the evidence. `03:55` *I disliked how easily the exchange ended* is the exposure.
