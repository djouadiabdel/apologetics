# I. The Warmth

Workshop only. This is the existing chapter after the locked callback map, not a cold re-dump. The chapter file remains untouched.

It is dark and I had been walking for order when I found you already on the stairs.

I thought there was nobody else in here. Then I took a turn before the corridor had given me any reason to take it, and there you were below me, waiting with your weight on one leg. You looked first at my hands and then at what I had on my head. I knew what the look meant at once. I am very fast at knowing what a look means.

The stair was narrow enough that one of us had to move. You went down two steps and stood against the outer wall. I took that for an invitation to join you. It may only have been an attempt to let me pass. I joined you anyway.

The wall was wet. Your sleeve darkened where it touched the stone and you looked at the mark. I told you the water was harmless. I had no evidence for that. The dark had already given me the corridor, the turn, and the broken stairs in the correct order, and after three correct things a person begins to believe the fourth has also been supplied.

I offered you my hand.

You looked at it.

Then you placed your own hand on the wall and continued down.

I went first. There was room for that interpretation too.

I have called it a crown since the hour I put it there. I have been waiting for you to make the name true.

They have been called seven fires, never seven hells. There is one fire. I know what I am talking about. The theologian of a time to come is in here waiting for you, and there is something wrong with the count, and the second thing is there too. I will come to that. It cost me my life and I did not learn it until I was down here, thinking about the crowned woman, and it is the reason I am talking to you at all.

There is a warmth ahead of us. Everything I am about to tell you is measured against it and you should know what the ruler is. It is not much. It is the difference between a room with somebody in it and a room without one, and I have spent enough time in both to tell them apart in the dark.

A thing that is warm is burning. Nothing is warm on its own. I am going to use that twice.

So there is a fire ahead of us and somebody is keeping it.

And the second time. This on my head is warm too. It has been warm since the hour I put it on and it has never once gone cold, not on the stairs, not in the rooms where my hands went numb, not on the fifth floor where everything went quiet. I have tested it the way I test everything. It is exactly as warm as the back of my own neck and it stays exactly as warm as the back of my own neck.

So there is something in it.

There has to be. Nothing is warm on its own.

There are gates. That is the thing nobody tells you. There is one after every floor. It chooses what it takes, always the thing you would have chosen last.

I came down carrying seven things. I have counted them since. Twelve times, and it is seven every time.

Seven is a holy number, the number of Matthew and Mark.

Two made weight. The pages lay against my chest and the ledger pulled at one side of my body.

I counted by touching the places where the seven announced themselves. Chest, hip, mouth, shoulder, throat, fingers, head. I did it once with the left hand and once with the right. Seven both times.

You waited through the second count. On the third, you began walking.

I finished before following. A number should not be abandoned merely because the person it is being demonstrated to has left.

You let me pass instead of coming alongside me.

That was sensible. The first stair had a broken lip near the wall. I stepped over it. You stopped.

A loose piece lay against the riser. You picked it up and placed it into the missing part of the stair. It did not fit. You turned it once, then left it beside the wall.

You wiped the white dust from your fingers and looked at my hands again.

Farther down, the stairs divided around a square column and joined again. Both flights sloped toward the same warmth. You chose the left. I told you the right was shorter.

There was no way to see where either one ended.

You remained on the left.

I took the right quickly enough to prove the choice had been mine. The flight turned twice, narrowed, and brought me out behind you. You had not hurried. You did not look surprised to see me there.

I explained that distance behaves differently below. You listened with your face toward the next landing.

That was the first time the warmth failed to get closer. I did not measure it then because I already knew the reason: the corridor was longer than it looked. The second time, I blamed the thickness of the walls. By the time I had enough examples to understand the rule, I also had enough explanations not to.

At the next landing I knew where the stone changed beneath the left foot. I shortened the step before it came into view.

The stair beneath us had been worn lower at its centre by feet travelling down. The returning wear was nearer the wall.

You looked at my feet.

I had placed them automatically in the returning wear.

I moved to the centre before the next step.

Footsteps began below us.

They were climbing. One foot dragged slightly after every third step. The sound came through the column, crossed beneath us, and entered the returning wear near the wall.

You stopped.

I counted fourteen steps before the person should have reached the turn. Nobody appeared. The footsteps continued upward through the space where we stood.

The dragging foot passed me first. I felt its vibration in the stone beside my right heel. Then the other. The rhythm was familiar enough that I knew what the next step would sound like before it came.

I placed my foot against the wall to compare.

The ascending step and mine landed together.

You looked at my face then, not my hands.

I told you the column was returning an echo from above. The explanation required sound to arrive before we made it, but time was unreliable here and therefore the objection proved less than it would elsewhere.

The footsteps went on without us.

When they faded, I could no longer remember which one of us had first begun moving again.

I began to tell you the order.
