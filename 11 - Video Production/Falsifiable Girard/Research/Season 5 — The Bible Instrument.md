@@ THE SKELETON

## The shape of the season

Three layers, kept strictly apart, and the separation is the design.

| layer | the question | where it lands |
|---|---|---|
| **1a** | do these texts interlock like independent reports, or collide like legends | run 1, and a control run |
| **1b** | when somebody alleges a contradiction, is it one, and what does the rescue cost | 671 allegations, nine gates |
| **2** | is the historical case for the resurrection honestly built | **not** whether it happened |

## What this season will not do, said once and meant

**It will not argue that the resurrection happened.** Layer 2's own rulebook
fixes that ceiling before any scoring, and episode 21 reads it out. The best
available outcome there is "the case is honestly built", which is a fact about
arguments and not about history.

**It will not defend inerrancy.** The study costs inerrancy in its second week
and does not try to buy it back.

**Inerrancy** is the doctrine that the biblical texts contain no errors of any
kind, including in matters of history and detail, not merely in what they teach.
It is a strong position and it is not the only Christian one. Plenty of readers
hold that these documents are reliable witnesses that also get details wrong,
the way any two honest witnesses do.

>> That distinction runs under the whole season. **A finding that one of two texts
>> is wrong about a fact is fatal to inerrancy and survivable for reliability**,
>> and conflating the two is what makes this argument unwinnable in public.

>> **And every headline in every file carries the same label: one coder, not
>> blinded, who recognises every text on sight.** No amount of process moves it
>> past that, and the season says so roughly once an episode, because a study
>> that states its ceiling twice and then forgets it is worse than one that never
>> stated it.

## The three things to watch for

**One.** Season 4 ended on an objection it could not answer: four texts about one
week, written by the victim's own partisans, compared to what? **This season has
the answer Season 4 lacked, and it is sitting in a folder called
`apocrypha`.**

**Two.** A falsifier fires in this season **on the person running it**, by name,
in the headline. Watch what he does next, because the structural argument he
makes is either the best reasoning in six seasons or an escape hatch, and the
episode makes you decide.

**Three.** The refrain has a new form here. **A check that cannot fail prints
reassurance**, and this season finds two fourth-century versions of it, plus one
father who states the opposite and gets it right in 340 AD.

@@ EPISODE 1 — A DIFFERENT STUDY, THE SAME POSTURE

## The stake

A different repository, and the question everybody actually came for, and the
first act is to fence off three quarters of it. **This episode is about refusing
to answer more than your instrument can**, and why the fences are what make the
numbers mean something. The season's promise: the fences get tested, one of them
fires on the person running it, and the last episode counts what the fences
bought.

## Collecting Season 4

Nine rounds, two passes, and everything that worked belonged to the half of the
instrument disclosed as compromised before it was built. The season closed on a
question it had deferred all the way through:

>> **Four documents about one week, written by the victim's own partisans. Can
>> they be compared to anything at all?**

That is not a rhetorical objection. It is the reason the dissent result could
never be cleanly interpreted, because "the Gospels contain more objection
language than Greek myth" has an obvious explanation that has nothing to do with
Girard: **the Gospels were written by people on the victim's side.**

## The answer Season 4 did not have

You compare them to **other documents written by partisans about the same events
and the same person.**

They exist. They are called the apocryphal gospels, and there are enough of them
to build a corpus: the Gospel of Peter, the Protevangelium, the Infancy Gospel of
Thomas, the Acts of Pilate. Written by Christians, about Jesus, in the same
tradition, with the same commitments.

>> **That is a control**, and it is the thing every measurement in Seasons 2, 3
>> and 4 was missing. The four-bucket instrument compared myth to tragedy, which
>> are different genres by different people about different things. This compares
>> a document to its nearest possible neighbour.

Take a moment on how much that changes, because it is easy to nod at and it is the
reason this season exists.

In Season 4 the comparison was Gospels against Greek myth. When the Gospels came
out different, there were a dozen available explanations and no way to choose:
different language, different century, different genre, different translators,
different purpose, and yes, possibly the thing being tested. **A difference between
two groups that differ in a dozen ways tells you almost nothing**, because you
cannot attribute it.

Here the two groups differ in far fewer ways. Same religion, same subject, same
narrative form, overlapping episodes, both written by partisans. **When they come
out different, the list of candidate explanations is short enough to argue
about**, and a short list is the entire difference between a measurement and an
observation.

That is why a cruder instrument here can be worth more than a refined one there,
and episode 22 makes that comparison explicitly once both are on the table.

## The device: the three layers, and why they are fenced

| layer | the question it asks | what it can conclude | what it cannot |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1a** | do the texts interlock or collide | this looks like independent reporting, or it does not | **nothing about whether the events happened** |
| **1b** | is an alleged contradiction real, and what does the rescue cost | how much the accusers' list actually costs | **nothing about inerrancy surviving** |
| **2** | is the case for the resurrection honestly built | the argument is sound or unsound | **nothing about whether a miracle occurred** |

**Read the right-hand column, because the fences are the design.** Every one of
those exclusions is a place where a study like this normally leaks. You test
transmission and then quietly talk as though you have tested the events. You cost
an accusation and then talk as though you have defended a doctrine.

## The ceiling, stated now rather than in a footnote

**One coder. Not blinded. Who recognises every text on sight.**

The person coding these items has read the Gospels, knows the famous arguments,
and can tell Mark from the Gospel of Peter in one sentence. **Every classification
runs through a mind that already knows which corpus it is holding**, and no
procedure invented in this season fixes that.

So every headline carries the label **strong structured pilot, not a confirmatory
result**. Not as modesty. As a fact about what the numbers can bear.

The distinction between a pilot and a confirmatory study is worth having properly,
because it is not a matter of degree. **A pilot asks whether the measurement can
be made at all**, and its output is a procedure plus an estimate of how hard the
procedure was. A confirmatory study asks what the answer is, and its output is a
number people are entitled to act on.

The two look identical on the page. Same tables, same percentages, same charts.
**What separates them is entirely upstream**: whether the coding was blind,
whether there was more than one coder, whether the sample was fixed before
anybody looked.

>> So calling this a pilot is not a hedge to be dropped once the numbers look
>> good. **It is a statement that the numbers were never eligible to be acted
>> on**, whichever way they came out, and it was true before any of them existed.

## The clinical anchor

**Triaging into three streams and refusing to let them mix.**

Chest pain goes to one pathway, the social problem to another, the medication
review to a third. It is the same patient and they are genuinely connected, and
the reason you keep the streams apart is that **a confident answer on one gets
spent on the others** if you let them run together.

The commonest failure is not misdiagnosis. It is a resolved chest pain being
treated as a resolved admission.

## The character anchor

**Rashomon.**

Four people describe one killing in a wood and the accounts do not fit. Kurosawa's
film is famous for being about truth being unavailable, which is the reading
everybody takes away and is not quite what the film shows.

**Watch what the accounts still share.** There was a man, a woman, a bandit, a
grove, a death. The accounts collide on motive, on who struck, on who is to
blame, and they interlock on the furniture of the event.

>> **That distinction is this whole season.** Not "do the witnesses agree",
>> which real witnesses never do, but **which layer they disagree on**, and
>> whether the disagreements are the kind that independent people produce or the
>> kind that invention produces.

## What would count against this

1. **The apocrypha being a bad control.** They are later, they had the canonical
   gospels in front of them, and some are a different genre. **Episode 5 argues
   this properly**, and the answer is not that the objection is wrong.
2. **The layers not actually being independent.** If the Layer 2 arguments turn
   out to need Layer 1 accuracy, the fences do not exist and the framing is
   wrong. **A falsifier is registered for exactly that**, and episode 21 reports
   that it signalled.
3. **One coder being fatal rather than limiting.** It might be. The honest
   position is that no number here survives a hostile reading of that fact, and
   the season keeps saying so.

## Handing off

**Episode 2 takes an apologetic argument** that has been made for two hundred
years and asks the question nobody asks it: what would it look like if it were
false?

## Questions

1. Name another control for the Gospels, better or worse than the apocrypha, and
   say what it would cost.
2. The three layers are fenced. **Which fence do you expect to leak first?**
3. If one non-blinded coder is a ceiling, what would the next study need? Be
   specific about who, and about what they would not be told.

@@ EPISODE 2 — AN ARGUMENT THAT HAS NEVER BEEN TESTED

## The stake

The undesigned coincidences argument is two hundred years old and has never been
tested. **You will watch it get what every argument deserves: categories,
controls, and a stated ceiling**, before anyone is allowed to be impressed.

## Collecting episode 1

Three fenced layers, a control that Seasons 2 to 4 never had, and a ceiling
stated in advance: one coder, not blinded.

## The argument, which is old and genuinely clever

It is called **undesigned coincidences**, and it goes back to William Paley in
1790. The shape is this.

One gospel says something odd and unexplained. Another gospel, elsewhere, in
passing, for no apparent reason, supplies the fact that makes it make sense.
Neither text flags the connection.

**The worked example, and it is a good one.** John says Jesus asks *Philip* where
to buy bread for the crowd, which is a strange person to ask. Luke, in a
completely different passage, mentions that the feeding happened near
**Bethsaida**. John, elsewhere again, mentions that **Philip was from Bethsaida**.

>> Three texts, no cross-reference, and the odd detail resolves. **The claim is
>> that forgery does not produce this shape**, because a forger supplies the
>> explanation where the puzzle is.

Work that last clause, because it is the argument's actual engine and it is
usually stated too fast.

If you are inventing a story, you write the explanation next to the puzzle. That
is what writing is. You do not leave an unmotivated detail in chapter six and
place its explanation in a different book by a different author, because you would
have no reason to and no way to coordinate it.

**What produces that shape is several people describing one world they all know
about, none of them writing for the others.** Each mentions what is relevant to
their own telling, and the details fit because the world was consistent, not
because anybody made them fit.

>> **So the claim is not "this is too clever to be invented."** It is narrower and
>> better: **invention has a characteristic shape, and this is a different
>> shape.** Which is a testable claim the moment you have some invented texts to
>> compare against, and episode 5 goes and gets some.

## Why it has never been an argument you could lose

Here is the problem, and it is the reason this study exists.

**Nobody has ever counted the ones that do not work.**

The literature is a collection of the successes. Paley found some, Blunt found
more, McGrew found more again, and each is presented as a case. **There is no
denominator and there is no control.**

| what the literature has | what it does not have |
|---|---|
| a list of coincidences that resolve | how many gaps were checked |
| each one argued in detail | how many gaps failed to resolve |
| the strongest cases | **whether legends do this too** |

**A list of hits with no misses and no comparison is not evidence.** It is a
scrapbook, and you can build one for any text long enough to have odd details in
it.

That last sentence is testable and you should test it, because it decides how much
of this argument survives.

Take any long novel with more than one book in the series. Find an unexplained
detail. Then go looking, anywhere in the other books, for something that would
explain it. **You will find something**, because long texts contain enormous
numbers of details and any two details can be connected by a reader willing to
connect them.

The question the apologetic literature never asks is how often that happens **by
chance in a text nobody claims is reporting anything.** Without that rate, a
handful of coincidences in four gospels is a number with nothing to compare it to.

>> **The undesigned-coincidence argument may well be right.** Nothing here says it
>> is wrong. It says that as currently presented it is **unfalsifiable**, because
>> no possible finding could count against it, and a version that could come back
>> negative has never been built.

Which is this series' own sentence, arriving in the apologetic literature rather
than in a research repository.

>> **A check that cannot fail prints reassurance.**

Two hundred years of collected coincidences is reassurance, printed steadily, by a
procedure that has never once returned a negative because **nobody built the thing
that could return one.** That is the same defect Season 2 found in a lexicon and
Season 3 found in a null test, and it is sitting in an argument that predates both
by two centuries.

## What turning it into an instrument requires

Three things, and each one is a whole episode later.

1. **A denominator.** Every cross-checkable gap, counted, whether or not it
   resolves.
2. **A control.** The same procedure run on texts everybody agrees are legendary.
3. **A rule that stops the coder supplying the answer.** A fit counts only if a
   *text* provides the reconciling fact.

>> **The third one is the sharpest, and it is where this study departs from the
>> tradition it is testing.** In the apologetic literature the connection is made
>> by the apologist. Here, if the coder has to supply it, it is not a fit.

## The device: the same gap, scored two ways

| | John asks Philip | scored as |
|---|---|---|
| the coder knows Philip is from Bethsaida and says so | supplied by the coder | **not a fit** |
| John 1:44 states Philip is from Bethsaida, on the page | supplied by a text | **FIT** |

**The finding is identical. The provenance is not**, and provenance is the only
thing that separates evidence from a good reader.

## The clinical anchor

**Corroborating a history from two relatives interviewed separately.**

The daughter mentions a fall three weeks ago. The son, asked about something
else, mentions the new blood pressure tablet started a month ago. **Neither of
them connected those and you did**, and the connection is real.

Now notice the discipline that makes it evidence rather than a story: **you write
down what each person actually said, separately, before you join them.** A note
that records only your synthesis has destroyed the thing that made it convincing.

## The character anchor

**A continuity error that turns out not to be one.**

A character has a scar in episode 9 and no scar in episode 4, and the internet
calls it a blunder. Then somebody points out that episode 6, in a throwaway line
nobody cared about, has him mention a fight.

**Nobody wrote that as a payoff.** It resolves because the writers were tracking
one world. And the reason this is the right anchor is that **fandoms also produce
hundreds of these that do not resolve**, and nobody makes a video about those,
which is precisely the missing denominator.

## What would count against this

1. **Undesigned coincidences being explicable by literary dependence.** If Luke
   read John, the coincidence is a borrowed detail. **The study handles this by
   tagging independence**, and episode 6 reports that all five canonical fits are
   between independent texts.
2. **The whole argument proving less than it seems.** Even at its best it shows
   the texts behave like independent reports of something. **It does not show
   what.** A shared legend, independently recorded, produces the same shape.
3. **A denominator being impossible in practice.** It has not been built, and
   episode 17 treats that as the honest ceiling on the whole layer.

## Handing off

**Episode 3 builds the classification**, four categories, and the interesting one
is the category that exists to stop the coder helping.

## Questions

1. Build an undesigned coincidence for a novel you know well. **How hard was
   it?** That difficulty is the argument's real strength or its real weakness.
2. Why is "a shared legend, independently recorded" not excluded by any result in
   this layer?
3. **Before episode 3:** how would you count a gap that neither resolves nor
   collides? It is the most common case and it decides the denominator.

@@ EPISODE 3 — FOUR CATEGORIES, AND THE ONE THAT STOPS YOU HELPING

## The stake

The category whose whole job is to stop you helping the answer you like.
**Blinding starts with the labels**, and this episode shows the trap being closed
before the data arrives.

## Collecting episode 2

Undesigned coincidences are a two-hundred-year-old argument with no denominator,
no control, and a habit of letting the person making the argument supply the
connection. Turning it into an instrument means fixing all three.

## Every cross-checkable gap gets exactly one of four labels

The unit is not a text and it is not a verse. It is a **gap**: a place where two
texts describe the same episode and one of them says something the other does
not, or says it differently.

You walk the **pericope**, which is the one piece of jargon worth adopting here. A
pericope is **a self-contained episode in a gospel**: the burial, the feeding of
the five thousand, the empty tomb. Not a chapter, which is a later editorial
division imposed on the text, and not a verse. **It is the unit the story itself
has.** Comparing gospels means lining up the same pericope in each and reading
across, and the word exists because the alternative is saying "the bit where" for
the rest of your life.

You walk it, you find every such place, and you label it.

**FIT.** One text supplies a fact that makes the other text's oddity make sense,
and the supplying is done by the text rather than by you.

**COLLISION.** The two texts assert things that cannot both be true. Split into
**hard**, where no reading reconciles them, and **soft**, where a reading exists
but it is strained.

**AGREEMENT.** The two texts say the same thing. This sounds like the good case
and it is the least informative label on the list, because agreement is what
copying produces as readily as truth.

**NULL.** The gap neither resolves nor collides. One text mentions a detail, the
other is silent, and nothing follows either way.

## Why NULL is the category that makes this a study

Almost every gap is a NULL, and the temptation is to stop recording them, because
recording a nothing feels like clerical work with no payoff.

**NULL is the denominator.** It is the count of chances the texts had to
interlock and did not, and the count of chances they had to collide and did not.
Without it you have a numerator floating in space, which is the exact condition
the apologetic literature is in and the reason episode 2 called it a scrapbook.

>> **The interlock ratio is fits divided by fits plus collisions.** A text that
>> produces five fits looks impressive until you learn it also produced five
>> collisions, and unremarkable until you learn it had four hundred chances to do
>> either.

So the boring label is the one carrying the argument, and a coder who gets bored
and stops logging NULLs has quietly rebuilt the scrapbook while feeling
efficient.

And notice the specific way that failure arrives, because it is not a decision
anybody makes. Nobody thinks "I shall stop recording nulls." What happens is that
the interesting items take longer to code, the nulls take seconds, and after two
hours the threshold for what counts as a gap worth logging has drifted upward
without any moment where it moved.

>> **The denominator does not get abandoned. It gets eroded**, and the erosion is
>> invisible in the output, because a coding sheet with fewer nulls looks exactly
>> like a corpus with fewer gaps.

## The rule that does the real work

A difference is a FIT **only if a text supplies the reconciling fact.**

That sounds like a technicality and it is the entire methodological content of
the study. In the apologetic literature the connection is drawn by the person
writing the book, and the reader cannot tell whether the connection was in the
texts or in the author's knowledge of them. Here that difference is made
procedural: **name the verse that supplies it, or it is not a fit.**

And the rule is symmetric, which matters more than the rule itself. It blocks the
defender inventing a harmony, and it blocks the sceptic dissolving a collision by
saying it feels fine. Both moves are the same move: supplying from outside what
the text does not supply. The study calls this the **Ehrman guard**, and every
later layer inherits it.

**Bart Ehrman** is a New Testament textual scholar, probably the best-known
sceptical voice on how the gospels were transmitted, and the guard carries his
name because of an objection of his about harmonising. Faced with two accounts
that will not fit, a defender can always construct a third story in which both
are true. Ehrman's point is that doing this is **writing a new gospel**: the
reconciling narrative appears in none of the texts, so you have solved the problem
by inventing a document nobody has.

>> **Naming the guard after a critic is deliberate.** It is a rule taken from the
>> other side and then **applied in both directions**, which is the only version
>> of it worth having, and episode 15 is what happens when somebody measures how
>> often he broke it himself.

>> **Never relax it in one direction only.** A guard that binds your opponent and
>> not you is not a guard. It is a rhetorical device with a procedural costume on,
>> and it will return whatever you already believed with a number attached.

## The device: one gap, four possible labels

| what the texts do | label | what it contributes |
|---|---|---|
| John's odd detail explained by Luke's aside | **FIT** | numerator |
| Mark says the women told nobody, others say they told | **hard COLLISION** | numerator, other side |
| a reading reconciles them but strains | **soft COLLISION** | numerator, discounted |
| both say Jesus was buried | AGREEMENT | almost nothing |
| one names an angel, the other does not | **NULL** | **the denominator** |

## The clinical anchor

**The difference between a sign and a symptom, and who supplied it.**

A symptom is what the patient reports. A sign is what you elicit. Both go in the
notes and they are recorded separately, and the reason is exactly this study's
rule: **an examination finding you produced is a different kind of evidence from
a complaint the patient volunteered**, and a note that blends them cannot be
audited later.

The failure mode is familiar. You suspect a diagnosis, you ask a leading
question, the patient agrees, and it is written down as a symptom. Nothing false
was recorded and the provenance has been destroyed.

## The character anchor

**A fan theory that quotes an episode, against one that does not.**

Two people explain the same odd moment in a show. The first says "this makes
sense because of what she said in season two, episode four," and gives the line.
The second says "this makes sense because he was obviously lying."

**Both explanations might be correct.** Only one of them can be checked by
somebody who does not already agree, and the difference between them is not
cleverness. The first person is pointing at the text and the second is pointing at
their own reading of it.

## What would count against this

1. **NULL being unbounded.** How many gaps are there really? The answer depends
   on how finely you slice a pericope, and **nobody has fixed the slicing rule
   independently.** This is the denominator problem arriving early and it is not
   solved in this season.
2. **Soft collisions being a dumping ground.** A category for "collides but a
   reading exists" is exactly where a motivated coder puts the inconvenient
   items. The study reports hard and soft separately for this reason, which
   limits the damage without removing it.
3. **The Ehrman guard being unfalsifiable in practice.** Deciding whether a text
   "supplies" a fact is itself a judgement. **The guard reduces the judgement, it
   does not eliminate it**, and episode 15 is what happens when somebody measures
   how much judgement is left.

## Handing off

**Episode 4 tries to blind the coder**, which cannot be done, and the interesting
part is what gets built anyway and what the tooling can and cannot hide.

## Questions

1. Take a gap you know from the Gospels. **Label it, then say who supplied the
   label.**
2. AGREEMENT is described here as almost uninformative. Construct the case where
   it is not.
3. **Before episode 4:** you cannot unknow the texts. Design the best blinding
   you can anyway, and name what it fails to hide.

@@ EPISODE 4 — BLINDING SOMEBODY WHO CANNOT BE BLINDED

## The stake

The coder knows the material and cannot un-know it. **This episode is about
blinding somebody who cannot be blinded**, a problem far beyond this project, and
the tooling answer to it.

## Collecting episode 3

Four labels, a rule that a fit must be supplied by a text, and a guard that binds
the defender and the sceptic identically. All of it still runs through one mind
that knows every text on sight.

## The problem, stated without flinching

The coder is going to read a passage and know immediately whether it is Mark or
the Gospel of Peter. Not by an act of scholarship, by recognition. The canonical
texts are among the most familiar documents in the language.

**And the hypothesis under test is that the canonical texts behave better.** So
the person applying the labels knows, for every item, which way the label needs to
fall for the prediction to pass.

That is the textbook setup for bias, and no amount of good intention touches it,
because the bias does not operate through intention. It operates through **how
long you look before you accept an answer**, which is the mechanism Season 4
episode 10 named and which is invisible from the inside.

## What gets built anyway

Two mechanisms, both enforced by tooling rather than by resolve.

**Order blinding.** A script emits every item in shuffled order, so the coder does
not work through Mark and then work through Peter. The identities of the sources
are written to a separate file, `key.json`, and that file is sealed until scoring.

The sealed key deserves a sentence on why it is a file rather than a promise.

A coder who keeps the mapping in his head is not doing anything dishonest, and he
is also carrying the tally as he goes. He knows the canon is at four fits and
the apocrypha at none, and the next borderline item arrives into a mind that
knows what it would do to the running total. **The bias is not in wanting a
result. It is in knowing the score while you play.**

Putting the mapping in a file the coder does not open removes the scoreboard. It
does not remove recognition, which is the next section, and it removes something
real, which is why it is worth the ten lines of code.

**Label blinding.** The coder does not see which witness is which gospel while
coding. The items arrive as anonymous witness codes.

The value of this is real and it is narrow. It removes the **run effect**, where
your standards drift across a block of items from one source and you calibrate to
that source without noticing. It does nothing about recognition.

## What the tooling cannot hide, and the study says so

>> The texts carry their own content, so the run **cannot be truly blinded to
>> canon against apocrypha the way a naive coder could be.**

Read that as the honest sentence it is. A witness code hides the label. It does
not hide that this passage has a talking cross walking out of the tomb, which
occurs in exactly one document in the corpus and which the coder will recognise
in four words.

So the blinding is partial by construction, and the study's response is not to
claim otherwise. It is to state the ceiling on every headline and to separate the
findings by how much they depend on judgement, which is episode 7 and is the most
useful thing in the layer.

**That separation is the real answer to unblindable coding**, and it generalises
past this study, so it is worth stating as a rule.

When you cannot remove a bias, **sort your findings by how much of them the bias
could touch.** Some results depend on a judgement call and some are facts on the
page. A study that reports both at one confidence has hidden its weakest number
inside its strongest, and a study that sorts them lets a hostile reader keep the
half that survives.

>> The alternative, which is what usually happens, is a limitations paragraph at
>> the end saying the coding was not blinded. **That is true, unusable, and costs
>> the author nothing**, because it does not tell a reader which specific number
>> to discount.

## The device: what each mechanism actually buys

| mechanism | removes | does not remove |
|---|---|---|
| shuffled order | drift across a block, calibrating to one source | recognition of any individual item |
| sealed key | scoring while watching the tally move | knowing which way the prediction needs it to fall |
| witness codes | the label | **the content** |
| **nothing available** | | **that the coder knows the hypothesis** |

**The bottom-left cell is empty and the study leaves it empty.** That is what an
honest limitations section looks like: not a paragraph of hedging, a row with
nothing in it.

## The clinical anchor

**Reporting imaging blind to the clinical details, and why nobody actually does
it.**

A radiologist given no history reports what is on the film. A radiologist told
"query pneumonia, day three post-op" finds the consolidation faster and misses the
thing nobody asked about. The literature on this is not subtle: **context improves
sensitivity for the suspected diagnosis and reduces it for everything else.**

And you cannot run a service on blind reporting, because the context genuinely
helps. So the compromise is the same one here: **you do not pretend the reporter
is blind. You record what they were told.**

## The character anchor

**A blind audition with the chairs turned around.**

It works, and it demonstrably changes who gets picked. And it does not survive
somebody walking out and singing a song they released last year, because the
mechanism hides the face and not the voice.

**Nobody concludes from that failure that the chairs are pointless.** They
conclude that the chairs handle one specific bias and that a famous contestant is
a different problem needing a different answer, and that the show should stop
claiming the audition was blind when it was not.

## What would count against this

1. **Partial blinding being worse than none.** A defensible position: it produces
   the *appearance* of rigour while the main bias runs free, and a reader may
   discount less than they should. **The counter is that the ceiling is printed
   on every headline**, which is a real answer only if readers read headlines.
2. **The run effect being the smaller problem.** Almost certainly true.
   The mechanisms address the bias that could be addressed, which is not the same
   as addressing the bias that matters.
3. **A second coder being available and not used.** The project decided in July
   that one coder is the design and the runs are pilots. **That is a resource
   decision recorded as such**, not a methodological argument, and it should not
   be dressed as one.

## Handing off

**Episode 5 builds the control**, which is the part Seasons 2 to 4 never had, and
the objection to it is strong enough that the episode spends most of its time
there.

## Questions

1. Design a blinding scheme that would hide the *content*. Is the result still
   the same study?
2. Is partial blinding worse than none? Argue it properly, then decide.
3. **The empty cell.** Name one thing that would fill it, and say what it costs.

@@ EPISODE 5 — THE CONTROL, AND WHY IT IS THE WHOLE STUDY

## The stake

The apocryphal control, and why it is the whole study. **Seasons 2 to 4 refined
an instrument with no control; this one refuses to**, and the episode shows what
a control buys that refinement never can.

## Collecting episode 4

Order blinding and a sealed key, both enforced by tooling, and an empty cell where
the main bias sits. Which brings us to the thing that makes the numbers mean
anything at all.

## The question a control answers

Suppose the canonical gospels interlock at some rate. Suppose they collide at some
rate. **Those numbers are meaningless in isolation** and it takes a moment to see
why, because they feel like facts.

A rate of one hard collision per thousand words sounds low. Low compared to what?
Compared to a transcript? Compared to four independent police statements? Compared
to a novel? **You have no idea whether it is low, and neither does anybody else**,
until you run the same procedure on texts whose character is not in dispute.

And notice this cuts at the sceptic exactly as hard as at the defender, which is
what makes it a control rather than a debating point.

The sceptic's version of the same error is "the gospels contradict each other,
therefore they are unreliable." **Compared to what?** Four independent accounts of
a real event contradict each other constantly. Police statements from the same
incident disagree about the number of assailants, the colour of a car, the order
of events. **Contradiction is what independent testimony looks like**, and a set
of accounts with no contradictions in it is evidence of collusion rather than
truth.

>> So both sides are quoting a raw count and calling it a verdict. **The defender
>> says the collisions are few and the sceptic says they exist**, and neither has
>> a baseline, and the argument cannot terminate because there is nothing in it
>> that could.

## The control, and it is a good one

Six apocryphal texts. The Gospel of Peter, the Protevangelium of James, the
Infancy Gospel of Thomas, the Acts of Pilate, and two more.

These are the right control for a specific reason. **They are not a random
different genre.** They are Christian documents, about Jesus, written by people
with the same commitments, in the same tradition, narrating overlapping events.
The Gospel of Peter has a passion and an empty tomb. The infancy gospels have a
childhood.

>> So the comparison is not "sacred text against secular text" or "ancient against
>> modern". It is **partisan documents about Jesus against other partisan
>> documents about Jesus**, which is exactly the objection Season 4 could not
>> answer, made into a measurement.

## The objection, which is serious and does not go away

The apocrypha are **later**, and they **had the canonical gospels in front of
them.**

That is not a quibble. If the Gospel of Peter is working from Mark, then its
collisions with Mark are one text reworking another rather than two independent
traditions diverging. The control is not a clean sample of "what legend looks
like". It is a sample of what legend looks like **when it is deriving from the
thing you are comparing it to.**

The study records this rather than answering it, and it records a specific
instance: the two infancy gospels are tagged as independent by the frozen rulebook
and are **probably literarily related.**

## What the study does with an objection it cannot answer

It states which way the objection cuts, and notices it cuts both ways.

If the infancy texts are related, their collisions are one author reworking
another, not independent invention. That weakens "independent legends collide".
**And two related texts that still contradict each other on whether a teacher dies
is arguably worse for their reliability rather than better.**

>> **Recorded, not resolved.** That phrase appears in the results file and it is
>> the correct posture. An objection you cannot answer gets written down at full
>> strength, with its consequences in both directions, and left standing.

## The device: what makes a control good, applied here

| property a control needs | do the apocrypha have it |
|---|---|
| same subject matter | **yes**, Jesus, overlapping episodes |
| same commitments | **yes**, written by believers |
| same rough genre | **mostly**, narrative gospel form |
| independent of the test corpus | **no**, and this is the wound |
| character not in dispute | **yes**, nobody defends the talking cross |

**Four of five, and the missing one is the important one.** That is a good
control, honestly described, which is a rarer thing than a perfect one.

## The clinical anchor

**A negative control on an assay, and what a missing one costs.**

You run a test and it comes back positive. Without a negative control you do not
know whether the reagent produces that signal on plain buffer. **The result is
uninterpretable, not weak.** There is no partial credit.

And the specific failure here is the one that matches the apocrypha problem: **a
negative control contaminated with the analyte.** It is still a control, it still
catches gross problems, and it will systematically understate your effect because
the thing you are controlling with has some of the thing in it.

## The character anchor

**A mockbuster.**

*Transmorphers* exists because *Transformers* exists. Watching them side by side
tells you a great deal about what a competent film does, because the cheap one is
copying the same brief with none of the craft.

**And it tells you nothing about how two independent studios would have handled
robots**, because there is only one brief and one of them is chasing it. That is
the shape of the objection and it is why the control is good for embellishment and
weak for independence.

## What would count against this

1. **The control being contaminated, as above.** The study's own strongest
   critique of itself in this layer, and it is not resolved.
2. **Six texts being too few.** They are, and the corpora on both sides are small.
   Episode 8 shows how small, when four units out of twenty-two turn out to move a
   verdict on their own.
3. **The apocrypha being selected.** They were not chosen at random from the
   apocryphal literature. They were chosen because they narrate overlapping
   episodes, which is necessary for the comparison and **is still a selection made
   by the person running the study.**

## Handing off

**Episode 6 runs it**, and the result splits cleanly into a half that is
trustworthy and a half that the person who produced it tells you not to trust.

## Questions

1. Name a control for the Gospels that is **not** derived from them. What do you
   lose?
2. A contaminated negative control understates the effect. **Which direction does
   that push this study's conclusion, and does that help or hurt the hypothesis?**
3. **Before episode 6:** predict which half of the result will be the weak one.

@@ EPISODE 6 — THE RESULT, AND THE ELEVEN TIMES

## The stake

The control is four-fifths right, and the missing fifth is the part that matters,
recorded and not resolved. **This is what an honest control looks like when it is
imperfect**, and the run's first real numbers arrive.

## Collecting episode 5

A control that is four-fifths right, with the missing fifth being the one that
matters, recorded and not resolved. Thirty-nine coded items across five pericopes.

## What came out

The prediction had five parts and four of them held.

**Direction holds.** Within each matched comparison the canon interlocks more than
the apocrypha. On the empty tomb it is 0.25 against 0.00. On the feeding of the
five thousand against the infancy miracles it is 0.67 against 0.00.

**Hard collisions hold.** The apocrypha collide harder, at 0.95 per thousand
words against the canon's 0.22.

Notice what that second number admits. **The canon is not clean.** Mark's ending,
where the women say nothing to anyone, is logged as a hard collision against the
other accounts, by the person hoping the canon would win. It is in the numerator
where it hurts.

That single logging decision is worth more than the paragraph around it, and here
is the test for whether it was honest. **The rescue was available.** Mark's ending
is textually disputed, the manuscript tradition is genuinely messy there, and a
coder who wanted to could have excluded the item on those grounds and written a
defensible sentence explaining why.

>> **He did not, and the item sits in the canon's own collision count.** That is
>> what it looks like when a rule written in advance binds somebody who would
>> rather it did not, and it is the only reason any of the other numbers in this
>> table are worth reading.

**Embellishment holds, and it is the strongest thing in the run.** One
embellishment in the canon, which is Matthew's raised saints coming out of the
tombs. Eight in the apocrypha. Adjusted for length that is **0.22 per thousand
against 2.54, about eleven times.**

**Independence holds.** All five canonical fits are between texts that do not
depend on each other, mostly John against the **Synoptics**.

That word needs unpacking, because the whole prediction rests on it. Matthew, Mark
and Luke are called the Synoptic gospels, from a Greek word meaning **seen
together**, because they can be laid out in three parallel columns and read
across. They share wording, order and whole episodes, and the standard explanation
is that **Matthew and Luke both used Mark.**

John is the odd one out. Different structure, different episodes, largely
different material, and no accepted account has him copying the other three.

>> So a fit between two Synoptics is nearly worthless as evidence, because copying
>> explains it. **A fit between John and a Synoptic cannot be explained that way.**
>> All five canonical fits are of the second kind, so the interlock advantage is
>> not an artifact of copying, which was the obvious deflating explanation and it
>> is ruled out.

## The prediction that failed

**Magnitude fails on the canon's own side.** The floor was set at 0.60 before
scoring. The canon comes in at **0.50**, and only two of its four pericopes clear
the floor.

And the reason is worth sitting with, because it is not a technical failure. **The
passion narratives are collision-dense enough to drag the canon's own interlock
rate below the floor its defender set for it.** The empty tomb scores 0.25. That
is the single most theologically loaded pericope in the corpus and it is the worst
performer on the canonical side.

There are two readings of that and the episode should give you both rather than
pick.

**The uncomfortable one.** The instrument does worst exactly where the tradition
needs it most, and a defender would have to explain why the central claim is
supported by the shakiest pericope in the corpus.

**The interesting one.** Collision density is what you would expect from several
independent people describing a chaotic morning nobody understood at the time.
The accounts of a car crash disagree more than the accounts of a dinner party, and
the disagreement is not evidence that the crash was invented.

>> **Both readings are available from the same number**, and nothing in this run
>> chooses between them, which is why the episode reports the number and stops.

>> The prediction that broke is the one flagged softest in advance, which is
>> either good calibration or a sign that the soft flag was doing the work of a
>> hedge. **Episode 7 is the author deciding which.**

## The device: the two halves of one result

| finding | depends on the coder's judgement? | trust |
|---|---|---|
| the apocrypha embellish 11× more | **no**, these are facts on the page | **high** |
| the apocrypha hard-collide more | mostly no | high |
| the canon interlocks more | **yes, entirely** | **low** |
| the canon's ratio is 0.50 | yes | low |

**A schoolmaster dies in one infancy gospel and merely blushes in the other.** A
walking cross comes out of the tomb and speaks, in one document and nowhere else.
The boy who withers a playmate kills him in one version and heals him in another.

Those are not interpretations. You do not need to know the hypothesis or trust the
coder to check any of them, and that is what makes the embellishment finding the
solid part of the run.

## The clinical anchor

**Inter-observer variability, and which findings survive it.**

Two radiologists agree almost perfectly on whether there is a fracture. They agree
much less on whether a lung field shows early consolidation. **Same imaging, same
training, different amounts of judgement in the call.**

So a study reporting both should not report one confidence for both. The fracture
count is nearly a fact and the consolidation count is a measurement of the
observers as much as the patients, and a paper that averages them into a single
result has hidden its own weakest number inside its strongest.

## The character anchor

**A fishing story growing between two tellings.**

The fish gets bigger. That is the recognisable shape of a story being retold by
people invested in it, and everybody knows it on sight, which is exactly why it
works as a measure.

**Now notice what the measurement actually is.** Not whether the first telling was
true. Only whether the second telling grew, and by how much, against a baseline of
somebody retelling the same event without growth. The canon grows once in four and
a half thousand words. The apocrypha grow eight times in three thousand.

## What would count against this

1. **Thirty-nine items.** It is a very small run, and episode 8 measures exactly
   how fragile a run this size is.
2. **The 11× being about genre rather than truthfulness.** The infancy gospels are
   arguably a devotional genre where embellishment is the point, not a failure. If
   so, the measure is detecting genre and calling it legend.
3. **The empty tomb scoring worst.** The pericope the whole tradition cares most
   about is the one where the canon performs worst on its own instrument, and
   **that is either honest reporting or a sign the instrument mismeasures exactly
   where it matters.**

## Handing off

**Episode 7 is the author telling you which half of his own result to disbelieve**,
and it is the most useful thing in the layer.

## Questions

1. Mark's ending is logged as a hard collision against the coder's own interest.
   **What would you have done, honestly?**
2. Is 11× a fact about legend, or about genre? Say what would separate them.
3. **Before episode 7:** which number in the table above would you attack first if
   you wanted to destroy this study?

@@ EPISODE 7 — THE NUMERATOR IS WHERE THE THUMB RESTS

## The stake

The coder admits where his own thumb rests, and names the mechanism. **Naming
your bias with a mechanism is different from confessing a vibe**, and this
episode shows the difference.

## Collecting episode 6

Four predictions held, magnitude failed on the canon's own side at 0.50 against a
0.60 floor, and the run splits into a half that is checkable and a half that is
not.

## The sentence this episode exists for

From the results file, written by the person who produced the number:

>> **The fit-ratio half is the least trustworthy thing here, and I produced it.**

Then the reason, which is the part worth memorising.

**The fits logged for the canon are the famous ones.** Bethsaida and Philip.
Passover and the green grass. John's "we". These are the undesigned coincidences
that appear in every book on the subject, that the coder has read about, and that
he went looking for because he already knew they were there.

Read that list again and ask a question the apologetic literature never asks.
**How many gaps were checked to find those three?**

Nobody knows, because the checking happened across two hundred years, distributed
across dozens of readers, and only the successes were written down. Paley
published what he found. He did not publish the afternoons that produced nothing,
and neither did anybody after him.

>> So the canonical fit count is not the output of a search. **It is the output of
>> two centuries of searching, filtered for hits**, being re-logged in an
>> afternoon by somebody who inherited the filtered list. And it is being compared
>> against a corpus nobody has ever searched even once.

## Why that is fatal to the number and not to the study

Think about where selection can enter the interlock ratio. The ratio is fits
divided by fits plus collisions. **Collisions announce themselves.** Two texts say
incompatible things and you notice, whether or not you were looking.

**Fits do not announce themselves.** A fit is a connection between two passages
that do not reference each other, and you only see it if you go looking in the
right place. A coder who has read the apologetic literature knows exactly where to
look in the canon, and has read nothing at all about undesigned coincidences in
the Gospel of Peter.

>> **So the numerator of the canon's ratio is precisely where a knowing coder's
>> thumb rests, and there was only my thumb.** A blind coder scanning every gap
>> without knowing the literature would very likely log fewer, and the canon ratio
>> would fall.

## The asymmetry, stated as a mechanism

| | canon | apocrypha |
|---|---|---|
| coder has read a literature listing the fits | **yes, extensive** | **no, none exists** |
| effort spent looking for fits | high, and directed | low, and undirected |
| collisions found regardless of effort | yes | yes |
| **net effect on the ratio** | **inflated** | not inflated |

The bias does not need anybody to log a false fit. **Every fit logged can be
genuine and the number still comes out wrong**, because the search was not equally
thorough on both sides, and an unequal search produces an unequal count from
identical texts.

That is a cleaner statement of the problem than "bias", and it is checkable in
principle: give somebody the apocrypha and the apologetic method and see how many
undesigned coincidences they find. **Nobody has done that**, and until somebody
does, the canon's ratio is an upper bound on itself.

## The one-line honest reading

>> **The apocrypha demonstrably embellish and hard-contradict far more than the
>> canon, which is robust. The canon appears to interlock more, which is real in
>> direction and inflated in degree by who did the coding.**

Two clauses, two different confidences, in one sentence, and neither one is thrown
away. **That sentence is what this whole series has been trying to teach how to
write.**

Look at what it refuses to do, because the refusals are the craft.

It does not drop the weak half, which would be cleaner and would misrepresent the
run. It does not report both halves at one confidence, which would be easier and
would hide the strong finding inside the weak one. It does not hedge the strong
half to make the sentence feel balanced, which is the commonest failure and the
most respectable-looking.

>> **"Real in direction, inflated in degree" is a specific claim about a specific
>> number**, and a hostile reader can act on it. That is the test for whether a
>> caveat is doing work: **can somebody who distrusts you use it?** A caveat they
>> cannot act on is decoration.

## The clinical anchor

**A radiologist who knows the clinical question.**

Told "query fracture", they find the fracture. The fracture is really there. And
the pneumothorax at the top of the film, which nobody asked about, gets missed at
a measurably higher rate.

**Nothing false was reported.** The report is accurate about everything it
mentions. The search was directed, and a directed search returns a biased sample
of what was there, which is a different failure from misreading and is invisible
in the report itself.

## The character anchor

**A highlight reel.**

Every clip is real. Nothing is staged, nothing is edited to deceive, and the
player genuinely did all of it. And you cannot learn anything about how good they
are, because you are watching a sample selected by somebody who was looking for
good moments.

**The fix is not honesty.** The person cutting the reel was honest. The fix is a
denominator: how many minutes did they play.

## What would count against this

1. **This being excessive self-flagellation.** The fits are real, they are
   independent, and P4 confirmed they are not a copying artifact. **A reader might
   reasonably weight them higher than the author does.** That reading is
   available and it is not obviously wrong.
2. **The bias being measurable and not measured.** The check exists and is
   describable in one sentence. It was not run. **Describing a check you did not
   run is cheaper than running it**, and this episode should be read with that in
   mind.
3. **Direction surviving anyway.** Even a heavily discounted canon ratio beats
   0.00, because the apocrypha logged **no fits at all.** Zero is hard to reach by
   thumb.

## Handing off

**Episode 8 runs a second, tighter control** eight months later, and two more
falsifiers fire, one of which tells you how much of this depends on four
sentences.

## Questions

1. Design the experiment that would measure the search asymmetry. What does it
   cost to run?
2. The apocrypha logged zero fits. **Is zero evidence, or is it the absence of a
   literature?**
3. Rewrite the one-line honest reading as a headline a publisher would accept.
   **What did you have to lose?**

@@ EPISODE 8 — FOUR SENTENCES THAT MOVE THE VERDICT

## The stake

A leave-one-out check shows the verdict rests on four sentences. **Four sentences
in 39 items, flipping a study**, and the discipline of saying so out loud instead
of hoping nobody notices.

## Collecting episode 7

The interlock ratio's numerator is where the coder's thumb rests, admitted by the
coder, with the mechanism named as an unequal search rather than a dishonest one.

## The second control, run much later

A new run, scored in August against a rulebook frozen on 5 August, with the
scoring procedure committed the same morning **before any unit was coded**. It
measures **restraint**: not whether texts collide, but whether they decline to
embellish where a legend would.

Twenty-two units. And this time three falsifiers were registered, and the third
one is a technique worth the whole episode.

## H3 fires, and it is the same failure as run 1

**The gap between the two sets is 0.225, against a threshold of 0.25 fixed before
any text was read.**

The direction is right and the size is not. And the pre-registration says exactly
what that means, in words written in advance:

>> *direction without magnitude, which is what failed P5 in run 1 and it fails
>> here too.*

**Two independent runs, eight months apart, on different units, failing in the
same specific way.** That is worth more than either failure alone, because a
repeated failure with a shared shape is a finding about the instrument rather than
about a run.

## H5 fires, and this is the technique

**Leave-one-out.** Take your twenty-two units. Drop one. Recompute the headline.
Put it back, drop the next, recompute. Twenty-two times.

The question it answers is brutally simple: **does any single item, on its own,
change the verdict?**

Four of the twenty-two do.

Dropping the unit for Mark's longer ending takes the gap from 0.225 to 0.259 and
H3 stops firing. So does dropping Emmaus. So does dropping either of the two least
developed units in the Gospel of Peter, from the other side.

>> So the verdict of this run rests on **four sentences of text**, and if any one
>> of them had been coded differently, or excluded on a defensible technicality,
>> the round would have reported the opposite.

Notice that two of the four sit on each side. That detail matters and it is easy
to skim past.

If all four fragile units were canonical, you would suspect the coder of leaning
on the canon. If all four were apocryphal, the same suspicion the other way.
**Two and two is what fragility looks like when it comes from sample size rather
than from a thumb**, and it is the only reassuring thing in the episode.

>> **What it is not is a defence of the result.** A verdict that four sentences can
>> flip is a verdict about twenty-two units, and twenty-two units cannot carry a
>> claim about a literature. The right conclusion is not "the finding is wrong",
>> it is **"this run was never large enough to have a finding"**, which the
>> pre-registration could have worked out in advance and did not.

## Why running it first is the whole point

The pre-registration required leave-one-out to be run **before the headline ratio
was computed.** Not after. Before.

Think about what that ordering buys. Run it afterwards, with the headline in view,
and every one of those four units becomes a candidate for re-examination. You
would look again at Mark's longer ending, and you would find something arguable,
**because there genuinely is something arguable about Mark's longer ending.**

**Run it first and the fragility is a property of the design rather than a
temptation.** You learn that your sample is too small before you learn which way
it fell.

## The device: what leave-one-out tells you that a p-value cannot

| question | answered by |
|---|---|
| could this have happened by chance | a p-value or a null |
| is the effect big enough to matter | a pre-set threshold |
| **does the whole result rest on one item** | **leave-one-out, and nothing else** |

**The third row is invisible to the first two.** A result can be significant, clear
the magnitude bar, and still be one coding decision away from reversing, and no
amount of statistical machinery on the aggregate will tell you.

## The clinical anchor

**A meta-analysis that hinges on one trial.**

Twelve studies pooled, a clean forest plot, a significant summary estimate. Remove
the 1997 trial and the effect vanishes.

**That is a standard sensitivity analysis and it is standard because it fires
often.** The pooled number was never a summary of twelve studies. It was one study
with eleven pieces of decoration, and the only reason anybody knows is that
somebody removed them one at a time.

## The character anchor

**Jenga, near the end.**

The tower is standing. It looks like a tower. And there are four blocks in it such
that pulling any one brings it down, while the other forty do nothing.

**"It is standing" and "it is stable" are different claims**, and the only way to
tell them apart is to try removing pieces, which is a thing you have to decide to
do while it is still standing.

## What would count against this

1. **Twenty-two units being too few for the technique to mean much.** True. With a
   sample this small, some fragility is arithmetically guaranteed. **The finding
   is not "this study is unusually fragile", it is "this study is too small", and
   that was knowable in advance.**
2. **H3's threshold being set too high.** 0.25 was fixed in advance, and a
   threshold of 0.20 would have passed. **Whether 0.25 was honest is checkable by
   asking what run 1's failure would have needed**, and it is the same shape.
3. **Two failures being a pattern of one instrument.** Both runs were designed by
   the same person with the same instincts. **Correlated failures are not
   independent evidence.**

## Handing off

**Episode 9 leaves Layer 1a entirely** and takes up the question everybody
actually argues about, with a docket compiled by people who want the answer to
come out badly.

## Questions

1. Run leave-one-out mentally on run 1's five pericopes. **Which one carries it?**
2. Why must leave-one-out run before the headline? Name what goes wrong otherwise.
3. **Two runs, same failure shape.** Is that stronger evidence or correlated
   error? You cannot have both.

@@ EPISODE 9 — LETTING YOUR OPPONENTS WRITE THE LIST

## The stake

The contradiction list is built from hostile compilations, taken entire, so the
hard items cannot be skipped. **Letting your opponents write the list is the
strongest move available**, and this episode shows what it costs and what it
buys.

## Collecting episode 8

Two runs, eight months apart, failing the same way: direction without magnitude.
And a leave-one-out check showing the second verdict rests on four sentences.

Layer 1a asked whether the texts interlock. Layer 1b asks the question people
actually argue about. **When somebody alleges a contradiction, is it one, and what
does the rescue cost?**

## The first decision, and it settles the study's honesty

You need a list of alleged contradictions. There are two ways to get one.

**You can compile it yourself.** You know the texts, you know the hard places, and
you would produce a thoughtful list. And every reader who distrusts you would be
right to, because you also know which items you can answer.

**Or you can take the list from people who want it to hurt.**

The docket here is **671 raw allegations**, aggregated from three hostile
compilations: the Skeptic's Annotated Bible, Infidels.org, and EvilBible. Not a
sample of them. The aggregation, entire.

>> **The docket is hostile-sourced and complete, so the coder cannot skip the hard
>> ones.** They are on the list because somebody who wanted the New Testament to
>> look bad put them there, and removing one requires a stated reason on the
>> record.

## What that buys, and what it does not

It buys the one thing selection bias cannot survive: **the person being tested did
not choose the test.**

And it costs something real, which is why almost nobody does it.

A hostile list is full of bad entries. Items that misread the verse, items that
allege a contradiction where one side is a question, the same complaint four times
over. **You have to work all of them**, seriously, and write down the reason each
one fails, and that is a great deal of tedious labour producing nothing anybody
wants to read.

The temptation is to filter first: drop the obviously bad entries, keep the
serious ones, and work those properly. **That is fatal, and the reason is that you
would be the one deciding which are obviously bad**, which hands back exactly the
control that taking the list was meant to give away.

And it changes what the result is a statement about, in a way worth being precise
about. This is a **ceiling on the accusers' case**, never a floor on the truth.

If the strongest available compilation of objections yields two hard problems, you
have learned something about that compilation. **You have not learned that there
are only two problems in the New Testament**, because a contradiction nobody has
noticed is not on anybody's list, and no procedure here would find it.

That asymmetry is permanent and it is worth naming precisely, because it is the
kind of thing that gets lost the moment a number is quoted.

**A hostile docket bounds the accusers and says nothing about the texts.** It is
the difference between "your best shot failed" and "there was nothing to hit."
Those two sentences are often said as though they were one sentence, and only the
first is available here.

>> The version that would say something about the texts is the denominator study
>> in episode 17, which walks every cross-checkable pair whether or not anybody
>> complained about it. **It has not been built**, and until it is, every number
>> in this layer is a fact about three websites.

## The clinical anchor

**An incident reporting system, and what it can never measure.**

You can count everything that was filed. You can categorise it, trend it, and
present it to the board. And the denominator is not errors, it is **errors
somebody chose to write up**, which is a different quantity moving for different

So a falling incident rate is compatible with improving care and equally

## The character anchor

@@ EPISODE 10 — SAME SUBJECT, SAME RESPECT, SAME TIME

## The stake

671 allegations, three hostile sources, one rule applied to all of them. **A
ceiling on the accusers' case instead of a floor**, and the inversion that makes
the comparison honest.

## Collecting episode 9

671 allegations from three hostile compilations, taken entire so the hard ones
cannot be skipped, producing a ceiling on the accusers' case rather than a floor
on the truth.

Now the definition, because everything downstream is arithmetic on it.

## The definition, and it is Aristotle's

Two texts contradict when they assert **P and not-P** about:

- **the same subject**,
- **in the same respect**,
- **at the same time**,

and no licensed reading makes both true.

Those three qualifiers are the whole content, and each one blocks a specific
mistake that ordinary argument makes constantly.

## Working the three qualifiers

**Same subject.** "Jesus healed one blind man" and "Jesus healed two blind men"
contradict only if they describe the same healing. If they are two occasions, both
are true and nothing has happened. So before anything else you have to establish
that the two texts are talking about one event, and a surprising number of alleged
contradictions die here without any special pleading.

**Same respect.** This is the one that does the most work and causes the most
trouble. "The mustard seed is the smallest of all seeds" and modern botany
disagree only if the text is making a botanical claim rather than a proverbial
one. **A word used in two senses generates a fake contradiction**, and so does a
rhetorical "all" read as a strict quantifier.

**Same time.** "Circumcision is required" and "circumcision is nothing" are both
in the New Testament, and they belong to a stated change of covenant. Before
against after is not P and not-P.

And notice that the third qualifier is the one most open to abuse, in a way the
first two are not. **Same subject and same respect are questions about the texts.
Same time invites you to supply a history.**

"These belong to different periods" is a claim, and it is checkable when the text
states the change and unfalsifiable when it does not. The circumcision case is
fine because the New Testament argues about the transition explicitly and at
length. **An appeal to different periods where no text marks a period is the same
move as inventing an event**, and the cost scale in episode 13 prices it
accordingly.

## Why this is not a defensive trick

Because the same three qualifiers destroy defences as readily as accusations, and
the study is about to demonstrate that on the church fathers.

>> **A distinction of respect that nobody can attest is not a distinction, it is a
>> wish.** Saying two texts use a word in different senses obliges you to show
>> where the second sense is attested, and if you cannot, you have supplied from
>> outside what the text does not supply, which is the guard from episode 3
>> pointed at yourself.

The rulebook requires it explicitly: **every appeal to a difference of sense must
state the specific distinction and cite where the alternative sense is attested.**
Not "these could mean different things", which is always true of any two words.

The requirement to **cite where the alternative sense is attested** is the whole
weight of that rule, and it is worth seeing why it is not a formality.

Any word in any language can be argued into a second sense. Given a motive, you
can propose that "day" means an age, that "all" means many, that "brother" means
cousin. **Every one of those proposals is possible and some of them are correct**,
and the difference between the correct ones and the convenient ones is whether the
sense turns up somewhere else, in a text nobody was arguing about.

>> **Attestation is what stops a sense-distinction being free.** And a project
>> that requires it of its opponents and not of itself has built a machine for
>> agreeing with itself, which is what episode 15 catches this project doing at a
>> measured rate of 67.6%.

## The device: the same allegation through the three qualifiers

| allegation | qualifier that decides it | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| one blind man against two | same subject | dies if two occasions, and there is no evidence either way |
| faith against works | same respect | two emphases, not P and not-P |
| circumcision required against nothing | same time | stated change of covenant |
| **Judas hanged against Judas burst open** | **all three hold** | **survives, and it is one of two** |

**The bottom row is the shape of a real one.** Same man, same death, same moment,
and no attested sense in which hanging is falling headlong.

## The clinical anchor

**Two results that look contradictory until you check the units.**

A calcium of 2.2 and a calcium of 8.8 are the same measurement in different units.
A glucose taken fasting and one taken after lunch are not in conflict. A potassium
from a haemolysed sample is not disagreeing with the one from a clean draw.

**Most apparent conflicts in a set of notes are resolved by asking what was
measured, in what units, at what time**, which is precisely same subject, same
respect, same time. The remainder, after those three questions, is the interesting
list, and it is always much shorter than it looked.

## The character anchor

**Two people arguing about whether a film is long.**

One saw the theatrical cut and one saw the extended edition. They are not
disagreeing about anything. They have been arguing for twenty minutes and both are
correct, and the argument only ends when somebody asks which version.

**Almost every argument you have ever watched fail to resolve has this shape**, and
the three qualifiers are a procedure for finding out in the first minute rather
than the twentieth.

## What would count against this

1. **The definition being too strict.** Under it, a great many real tensions in
   the texts are not contradictions, and somebody could reasonably say the strict
   definition protects the texts by construction. **The answer is that it is
   Aristotle's and not the project's**, and that it is applied to defences too,
   which episode 18 shows costs the defenders more than it costs the accusers.
2. **"Licensed reading" being where all the judgement hides.** It is. That is
   what the nine gates in episode 11 exist to constrain and what fires a falsifier
   in episode 15.
3. **Same-respect being unfalsifiable in the hard cases.** Sometimes nobody can
   settle whether a word is used in two senses. **Those items should be contested,
   not dissolved**, and whether they were is the thing episode 15 measures.

## Handing off

allegation can fail, applied in a fixed order.

@@ EPISODE 11 — NINE GATES

## The stake

Nine gates, ordered, with the two most judgement-heavy flagged in advance.
**A falsifier set on the gates' own share before any gate is applied**, which is
how you keep a rubric from becoming a thumb.

## Collecting episode 10

Same subject, same respect, same time, and no licensed reading that makes both
true. A definition twenty-three centuries old, applied symmetrically.

An allegation now has to pass nine gates before it counts as a real clash. Each
gate is a specific way the definition can fail, and they are applied in order.

## The nine

**A0, not an assertion.** One side is a question, a command, a wish, a curse, a
hypothetical, or the reported speech of a character the text does not endorse.
Job's friends say a great many things the book of Job exists to contradict.

**A1, not the same subject.** Different events, persons, places or occasions.

**A2, not the same respect.** Different sense of a shared word, a rhetorical "all"
read as strict, figurative against literal, legal against biological, role against
person.

**A3, not the same time.** Different period or dispensation, a stated change of
covenant, before against after.

**A4, omission only.** One text is silent where the other speaks. **Silence is not
denial, and naming one of two is not asserting only one.**

**A5, tension not negation.** Two emphases in tension without P and not-P. Love
against wrath, faith against works, sovereignty against freedom.

**A6, text against doctrine.** The clash is with a later dogma such as inerrancy
or a systematic theology, not between two texts. **This gate is important and
counterintuitive:** an item that embarrasses a doctrine but leaves both texts true
is not a contradiction between texts, and it is filed as such.

**A7, designed juxtaposition.** The tension is an intentional literary pairing.
Proverbs 26:4 and 26:5 tell you not to answer a fool and then to answer a fool, on
consecutive lines, on purpose.

**A8, reference failure.** The cited verses do not say what the entry claims, or
the citation is broken.

## A8 is the gate that grades the accusers

Most of the gates are judgements. **A8 is not.** You open the verse and either it
says what the entry claims or it does not.

That makes it the one gate whose count is an objective finding about the
compilations rather than about the coder, and the study reports it as such: **five
entries misdescribe their own citations, four put a question on one side.**

Those are small numbers and they are worth having precisely because they are
checkable by anybody in ten minutes, which almost nothing else in this season is.

And there is a second reason to care about A8 beyond its size. **It tells you
about the process that produced the list.**

An entry that misdescribes its own citation was written by somebody who did not
open the verse. Not somebody who read it and disagreed about its meaning, which
would be an ordinary dispute. **Somebody who copied a claim from another list.**

>> So A8 is a small window onto how these compilations are actually assembled, and
>> what it shows is the same thing episode 12 measures at scale: **a literature
>> propagating by copying rather than by checking.** Nine items is not many. The
>> mechanism it exposes is what produces the other two hundred.

## The device: the gates ordered by how much judgement they take

| gate | judgement required | who can check it |
|---|---|---|
| **A8** reference failure | **none** | anybody with a Bible |
| A0 not an assertion | very little | anybody |
| A7 designed juxtaposition | some | a reader of the genre |
| A1 same subject | real | contested case by case |
| A3 same time | real | contested case by case |
| **A2 same respect** | **maximal** | **almost nobody** |
| **A5 tension not negation** | **maximal** | **almost nobody** |

**The two bottom rows are flagged in the rulebook, in advance, as the danger.**
The pre-registration says so plainly: *Tier A is where my judgement concentrates.
A2 and A5 are where almost anything can be called a difference of sense.*

And having named them as the danger, it requires their counts reported on their
own line so a reader can discount them, **and sets a falsifier on their share.**
Episode 15 is that falsifier firing.

## The clinical anchor

**Exclusion criteria applied at screening, in a fixed order.**

Age, then renal function, then the drug interaction, then consent. The order is
fixed so the reason for exclusion is recorded consistently, and so that two
screeners exclude the same patient for the same stated reason.

**And the criteria that require judgement are the ones you audit.** Nobody
re-checks the date of birth. Everybody re-checks "unable to comply with follow-up",
because that is the criterion where a screener's convenience can hide.

## The character anchor

**A bouncer with a checklist, and the one line that is not on it.**

ID, dress code, capacity, guest list. Four objective gates, and everybody in the
queue can see whether they passed each one.

**Then there is "not tonight, mate."** That is the gate doing the real work, it is
unappealable, and the person applying it could not fully explain it if asked. Every
system of rules has one, and the honest thing is not to pretend otherwise but to
**count how often it gets used.**

## What would count against this

1. **Nine gates being too many.** More gates means more ways to dismiss an
   allegation, and a defender who wanted to dissolve everything would design
   exactly this. **The counter is that they come from the definition rather than
   from convenience**, and that A6 in particular costs the defence its doctrine.
2. **The order mattering.** An item that could fail at A1 or A2 gets recorded at
   whichever comes first, and the ordering was chosen by the coder.
3. **A6 being a dodge.** Filing "this contradicts inerrancy" as not-a-contradiction
   is defensible and it is also exactly what somebody protecting a doctrine would
   do. **The study's answer is that it does not protect the doctrine**, and
   episode 17 says so at the top.

@@ EPISODE 13 — WHAT A RESCUE COSTS

## The stake

671 to 210 to 153, with a five-level price list for rescues. **What a rescue
costs, counted in advance**, and why the counting had to exist before the
rescues did.

## Collecting episode 12

671 to 210 to 153, with 27% of the accusers' list being the same complaint
recounted. Judgement enters at the near-dedupe and dominates at the gates.

Now the 51 items that survive. They are real clashes between two assertions, and
the question is no longer whether there is a problem. It is **what fixing it
costs.**

## The scale

Five levels, and the point of it is that "is it a contradiction" is the wrong
question and "what do you have to believe to make it go away" is the right one.

**Cost 0, dissolved.** The plain text shows no contradiction. Mentioning one of
two is not denying two.

**Cost 1, licensed.** A rescue exists, is directly cited, and is uncontested. A
lexeme genuinely has that range. A custom is genuinely attested.

**Cost 2, contested.** A real rescue exists but rests on a debated reading or a
reconstruction. A live difficulty, not a knockout.

**Cost 3, ad hoc.** The reconciliation needs an event no text states, or an
emendation of the text. Ehrman's phrase for this is **writing a new gospel**, and
it is the right phrase.

**Cost 4, hard.** No non-ad-hoc rescue on any channel. A genuine contradiction.

## The Ehrman guard, stated once and applied to everybody

>> **A rescue counts as cost 1 or below only if it cites its licence:** a lexeme, a
>> verse, a custom, a source. **If the only harmony available is invented events or
>> emended text, it is cost 3, no matter how plausible it sounds.**

The last clause is the one that bites. Plausibility is not licence. A reconstruction
can be entirely reasonable, the sort of thing that probably did happen, and still
be cost 3, **because the question is not whether it is true but whether a text says
it.**

That separation is unfamiliar enough to be worth a second pass, because most
argument runs the two together.

Suppose two accounts of a journey disagree about where somebody stopped, and you
propose they stopped twice. **That may well be what happened.** People do stop
twice. The proposal is not far-fetched and a historian might accept it without
blinking.

**It is still cost 3**, because no text says they stopped twice, and the proposal
was generated by the need to resolve the disagreement rather than by any evidence.
The scale is not measuring whether your story is true. **It is measuring where
your story came from**, and a story generated by the problem it solves has no
independent standing however plausible it sounds.

And the guard runs both ways, which is the part that separates this from
apologetics with extra steps. It blocks the defender inventing a harmony **and it
blocks the coder dissolving an item because it feels fine.** Both are supplying
from outside what the text does not supply.

## Why the scale is better than a verdict

A binary verdict on a contradiction produces an argument that never ends, because
both sides can always say the word. The scale converts the argument into a
question with an answer: **name the channel your rescue runs on, and cite it.**

That reframing does something specific to the debate. It stops being about whether
the Bible has contradictions and starts being about the **price list**, and a price
list can be audited item by item by somebody who disagrees with you about
everything else.

And it changes what winning looks like, which is the deeper effect.

Under a binary, both sides are trying to establish a fact about the Bible, and
neither can, so the argument runs forever on the same examples. Under a scale,
**both sides are trying to establish a price for a specific item**, and prices can
be compared, aggregated, and revised one at a time.

>> A defender who accepts the scale has conceded that some items cost something. A
>> sceptic who accepts it has conceded that most cost very little. **Neither
>> concession is available in the binary version**, which is why the binary
>> version is the one that never moves.

## The device: one item at four prices

| proposed rescue for the same clash | channel cited | cost |
|---|---|---|
| the two texts describe different occasions, and a text says so | verse | **1** |
| the word has a second attested sense, here is the lexicon | lexeme | **1** |
| the word could mean something else | **nothing** | **3** |
| there was an intervening event nobody records | **nothing** | **3** |
| no rescue on any channel | | **4** |

**Rows three and four are the ones that sound fine out loud.** They are the ones
you hear in sermons and in debates, and they are priced at 3 because a sentence
that could be said about any two texts whatsoever has no evidential content.

## The clinical anchor

**Grading an adverse event rather than declaring one.**

Nobody asks whether the patient had a side effect. They grade it: mild, moderate,
severe, life-threatening, fatal. Five levels, defined in advance, applied by
somebody who did not choose the drug.

**The grading is what makes the data poolable.** A trial reporting "some adverse
events" tells you nothing, and a trial reporting grade 3 and above tells you what
the drug costs. And the discipline that makes it work is that the grade is fixed
by criteria rather than by how the reporter feels about the drug.

## The character anchor

**A repair quote.**

You do not ask the mechanic whether the car has a problem. You ask what it costs to
fix, and the answer sorts itself into a scale everybody understands: a bulb, a
service item, a part that needs ordering, an engine rebuild, **write it off.**

**And the useful conversation is always about the itemised list**, not about
whether the car is "fine". Somebody who says the car is fine and somebody who says
it is scrap can both look at the same quote and find they are arguing about one
line on it.

## What would count against this

1. **The scale rewarding ingenuity.** A clever defender can always find *some*
   channel, so a well-read apologist scores lower costs than a less-read one on
   identical texts. **The scale measures the state of the defence literature as
   much as the texts.**
2. **Cost 3 being the interesting category and the fuzziest.** The line between a
   reconstruction and a licensed reading is exactly where the argument lives.
3. **The guard being applied by one person.** Symmetric in principle, and applied
   by somebody with a view. **Episode 15 measures how much that mattered**, and
   the answer is: enough to fire a falsifier.

The standard move is that more people spoke than any single gospel records, so

a crowd accuses somebody and different witnesses remember different voices.

believe something no text states. That is available and it is not free.

merely that none has been proposed. That is a very high bar and it gets harder the
more defenders have written, because every century of apologetic literature adds
candidate rescues. **An item can move from 4 to 3 forever and never move back.**

@@ EPISODE 16 — THE TWO THAT SURVIVE, AND A CORRECTION AGAINST THE STORY

## The stake

Two items survive at the top price, and one is corrected downward against the
story the season would have preferred. **Correcting against your own narrative in
public** is the posture the whole project exists to demonstrate.

## Collecting episode 15

F4 fired at 67.6% against a 40% ceiling, the dissolution rate is unreliable, and
the hard count survives on a directional argument you were asked to judge.

Two items are left. They are worth working properly, because everything else in
this layer is a number and these are texts.

## Judas, and the correction that went the wrong way

Matthew has Judas hang himself. Acts has him fall headlong and burst open in a
field. Same man, same death.

This item was originally priced at **cost 4**, which is the top of the scale and
means no rescue exists on any channel. It was **revised down to 3.**

**The revision went against the story.** Cost 4 is the stronger position and the
sharper finding, and the study gave it up.

The reason is narrow. Cost 4 means **no** rescue exists on any channel. And one
does: you can say the rope broke, or the branch gave, and the body fell.

>> **That rescue is worth nothing and it exists.** No text states it. It is
>> invented to fit. And the scale has a level for exactly that, which is 3, so the
>> item belongs at 3 and the sharper number was wrong.

The correction was propagated everywhere it appeared, including into the ledger
and the evidence file, **and the Bayes calculation gained a cost-3 case chosen
before the recomputation was run**, so the correction could not be tuned.

Two words there need unpacking.

**Bayes** is the arithmetic of updating a belief with evidence. You start with how
likely you thought something was, you observe something, and it moves you. The
project runs one to ask how much the whole ledger shifts the case, **and it is the
part of the study most sensitive to choices**, because you must supply the
starting number yourself.

Which is why the ordering matters. Lowering Judas from 4 to 3 changes an input to
that calculation. **The value the calculation would use for a cost-3 item was
fixed before the recomputation ran**, so it was impossible to pick the value that
made the correction painless. You correct the item, then you find out what it
costs, in that order, and not the reverse.

## Peter's accusers, and why the Greek closes it

The usual harmonisation says there were more challengers than any one gospel
reports, so Mark names one and Luke names another and both are true. That is an
A4 move, silence is not denial, and it is normally strong.

**It fails here on grammatical gender**, and this is a tier V finding, checked
against the Greek text rather than taken from a commentary.

The second challenger is gender-marked in all three. Mark has a **definite feminine**
article with the servant girl. Matthew has a **feminine** other. Luke has a
**masculine** other, and Peter answers him with a masculine **vocative**, "Man".

A vocative is simply **the form a word takes when you are addressing somebody
directly.** English barely has one, which is why the term is unfamiliar: we say
"Man!" the same way we say "a man". Greek marks it in the word itself, so **the
grammar records who is being spoken to**, not just who is being spoken about.

That is what makes this item close rather than arguable. Peter is not described as
answering a man. **He addresses one, and the form of the word he uses carries the
sex**, so the detail is in the grammar rather than in a narrator's summary that
could be loose.

So the texts do not merely name different people. **They mark different sexes for
the same challenge in the same sequence**, and multiplying challengers cannot
absorb it without adding an event.

## Why this is the layer's best moment

Because the check ran both ways and was recorded both ways.

Two other items were checked against the same Greek text and **cut in favour of
the defence.** One rescue that had been scored as dead turned out to be alive.
Another item narrowed to a smaller objection than the compilations claimed. And
one check **corrected an error the coder had made himself** about which case a
Greek noun was in.

>> **A verification procedure that only ever confirms your side is not a
>> verification procedure.** This one produced two findings for, two against, and
>> one self-correction, and all five are in the same file.

The self-correction is the most useful of the five and it is worth saying what it
was. The coder had claimed a Greek noun was in one case when it is in another, and
the error was found by writing the check as a **script** rather than as a
sentence.

That is a transferable technique and it is the same lesson the toolchain in this
series keeps teaching. **A claim written as prose cannot be run.** A claim written
as code gets executed against the actual text, and it either produces the result
you said it would or it does not.

>> Two **patristic** rescues were killed by the same script, and **writing them as
>> code caught two further errors in the coder's own argument** on the way. A
>> claim you can execute is a claim that can embarrass you, which is precisely
>> what makes it worth more than a footnote.

**Patristic** means belonging to the church fathers, from the Latin for father. The
fathers are the Christian writers of roughly the first five centuries, the ones
who were arguing about these texts while the arguments were new: Origen,
Chrysostom, Augustine, Eusebius, Jerome. Episodes 18 to 20 grade them, and the
word turns up here because two of the rescues being scored in this layer turn out
to be theirs rather than modern.

>> Which is itself a finding worth keeping. **Four of the rescues scored in this
>> study are patristic**, so the common line that harmonisations were invented
>> recently to plug holes is simply false, and it should stop being said.

## The device: the two survivors, priced

| item | the clash | the best rescue | why it costs 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Judas** | hanged against fell headlong | the rope or branch broke | **no text states it** |
| **Peter's accusers** | feminine against masculine challenger | more challengers than reported | **requires an unnarrated extra challenge** |

**The one abnormal result that changes management.**

Forty numbers come back on a screen and thirty-nine are noise you will never think
about again. One of them changes what you do tomorrow.

**And the skill is not finding abnormal numbers**, which is trivial, it is knowing
which ones are load-bearing. A study that reports all forty with equal emphasis has
not done the work, and a study that reports two out of fifty-one is claiming

## The character anchor

**A plot hole that actually breaks the story, against the hundred that do not.**

Every long-running show has continuity errors, and fans catalogue them by the
hundred, and almost none of them matter because the story does not rest on them.

**Then there is the one where a character knows something they could not know**,
and it matters because the plot turns on it. The difference is not how wrong the
detail is. It is **what depends on it**, which is why the study reports the core
separately.

@@ EPISODE 17 — TWO OUT OF WHAT

## The stake

Two survivors, and the question that decides what they mean: two out of what?
**The most important question in any rate**, asked at the exact moment it matters
most.

## Collecting episode 16

Two items at cost 3, one corrected downward against the story, one closed by
grammatical gender checked in the Greek. Now the question that decides whether
either number means anything.

## The hole

**Two hard contradictions out of what?**

Against thousands of cross-checkable claim-pairs, two is remarkable. Against
forty, two is bad. **Same numerator, opposite verdicts**, and nothing in this
study settles which frame you are in.

The pre-registration specifies the design for a denominator. **It has not been
built.** So the defensible claim is still about the accusers' list rather than
about the texts, and the study's own front page says so.

## Why a denominator is genuinely hard here

It is not laziness, and understanding why is more useful than the complaint.

To count cross-checkable claim-pairs you must first decide **what a claim is.** Is
"Jesus went to Jerusalem" one claim or three? Does every clause count? Every
proper noun? The answer changes the denominator by an order of magnitude and there
is no principled place to stop.

And the unit has to be fixed **in advance and independently**, because a
denominator chosen after you know your numerator is not a denominator. It is a
dial.

Watch how easily that dial turns. You have two hard contradictions. You want to
know whether that is good. You start counting claim-pairs, and every time you make
a granularity decision you can feel which way it moves the ratio.

**Slice finely and the denominator explodes and two looks like nothing.** Slice
coarsely and it shrinks and two looks serious. Neither slicing is wrong, both are
defensible in a methods section, and **the person choosing knows what each one
does to the answer.**

>> This is Season 4 episode 10's problem exactly, arriving in a different project:
>> **free parameters plus a target in view.** And the solution available there was
>> to enumerate every choice rather than make one, which nobody has yet tried
>> here.

>> This is the same problem as episode 3's NULL category, arriving at full scale.
>> **The denominator is the count of chances**, and chances are only countable once
>> somebody defines what a chance is.

## The lead that exists and has not been used

There is a fourth-century enumeration of the cross-checkable set: the **Eusebian
canon tables**, which index which passages of which gospels parallel each other.
Built for a completely different purpose, by somebody with no stake in this
question, seventeen centuries before it was asked.

**That is a sampling frame, not a denominator.** Pericopes are not
proposition-pairs, and converting one to the other is the whole difficulty again.
But a stake-free enumeration by a disinterested party is exactly the kind of thing
you want, and it needs its own pre-registration before anybody touches it.

The distinction between a sampling frame and a denominator is worth having, since
they get confused constantly and the confusion is expensive.

**A denominator is the count of chances.** A sampling frame is a list you can draw
from so your sample is not chosen by you. The canon tables give you the second: a
principled list of parallel passages, compiled by somebody with no interest in
this question, from which you could draw at random.

That is genuinely valuable and it solves a different problem. **It stops the
analyst choosing which passages to examine**, which is exactly the failure that
sank run 1's fit ratio in episode 7. It does not tell you how many claim-pairs
exist inside those passages, which is still the open problem.

>> Worth noticing anyway: **the fix for episode 7's disease has been sitting in a
>> fourth-century manuscript apparatus the whole time**, and nobody in this project
>> connected the two until the denominator question forced it.

## What the study is allowed to say without one

**About the accusers' list:** everything above. 671 to 153 to 51 to 2, with the
dissolution rate disqualified and the hard count structurally surviving.

**About the New Testament:** almost nothing quantitative.

>> **A count without a denominator is not a low number or a high number. It is not
>> a rate at all**, and the honest move is to stop calling it one.

## The device: the same numerator, two frames

| denominator | 2 hard contradictions is | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 40 cross-checkable pairs | 1 in 20 | **bad** |
| 400 | 1 in 200 | unremarkable |
| 4,000 | 1 in 2,000 | **remarkable** |

**Nobody knows which row is true**, and every public argument about biblical
contradictions is conducted as though the row were obvious to whoever is speaking.

## The clinical anchor

**A rate with no population denominator.**

Twelve cases this year. Up from eight. The room reacts.

**Twelve out of how many admissions?** If the catchment grew by half, twelve is a
fall. And the reason this happens constantly is that the numerator is easy to
count and lives in an incident system, while the denominator requires somebody to
go and get the activity data, which is dull.

**The easy number gets published and the hard number decides what it means.**

## The character anchor

**A batting average with no at-bats.**

Four home runs. Is that a good season? You cannot say, and everybody knows you
cannot say, because in sport the denominator is published as a matter of course
and nobody would accept the numerator alone.

**In almost every other domain we accept the numerator alone constantly**, and the
only difference is that sport made the habit of publishing both.

## What would count against this

1. **The denominator being unbuildable in principle.** If "claim" cannot be
   defined non-arbitrarily, no denominator exists and this layer is permanently a
   statement about lists. **That is a real possibility and the study does not rule
   it out.**
2. **The Eusebian route smuggling in a frame.** Eusebius indexed parallels
   according to his own sense of what parallels are, which is a theory of the
   texts.
3. **This objection applying to the accusers too.** It does, and harder. **671 is
   also a numerator with no denominator**, and it is quoted far more often.

@@ EPISODE 18 — THE FATHERS, GRADED ON THE SAME SCALE

## The stake

The church fathers get graded on the same scale as the modern harmonies, missing
denominator and all. **Older does not mean better**, and the grades come back
flatter than anyone's tradition predicts.

## Collecting episode 17

No denominator, so no rate, so the claims are about the accusers' list rather than
about the texts. Which leaves one thing this layer can still do honestly: turn the
instrument on the defence.

## The design, and it is stricter than it needed to be

Twelve of the hardest items on the docket, expanded to nineteen, chosen by
**mechanical criteria written to the file before any patristic source was
fetched.**

That ordering matters for the same reason it always does. Choose the items after
reading the fathers and you have chosen the items the fathers happen to address.
Choose them first, mechanically, and **the coverage becomes a finding rather than
an artifact.**

Then the patristic answer to each is graded on a fixed scale: **GOOD, PARTIAL,
BAD, ABSENT, AGAINST.**

## The headline, and it inverts what everybody expects

>> **The fathers cover MORE of the hard items than modern harmonies do, and they
>> answer them WORSE.**

Both halves are surprising and the second explains the first.

The fathers engage more because they were writing in a culture where the
objections were live and being pressed by people they had to answer. They answer
worse because **their answers are the ones that do not survive being said aloud
now**, and modern defenders have quietly stopped making them.

That is not a discovery about the fourth century. It is a discovery about the
present: **the modern harmony literature is smaller than the ancient one because
it has been filtered**, and the filtering removed the answers that embarrass
rather than the questions that are hard.

And the filtering is not a conspiracy, which is what makes it worth understanding.

Nobody decided to drop the weak answers. What happened is that each generation
repeated the answers that worked in argument and quietly stopped repeating the
ones that got laughed at. **Selection by embarrassment, operating over
centuries**, with no author and no moment where anything was suppressed.

>> The result is a literature that looks stronger than its tradition actually was,
>> **because the failures were not refuted, they were forgotten.** Which means a
>> modern defender is inheriting a curated case and has no way of knowing what was
>> curated out, unless somebody goes back and grades the originals.

There is a version of this in every field, including this one. **Seasons 2 to 4
exist because a research repository kept its failures**, and almost nothing else
does.

## The worst finding is a silence

Augustine's *De consensu evangelistarum* works the Judas material in detail. The
thirty pieces of silver, the potter's field, the misattribution of Zechariah to
Jeremiah. He is thorough, and he is engaging with the hard version.

**He never mentions Acts 1:18.**

That is the verse with the falling headlong and the bursting open. It is the other
half of the contradiction, it is the reason the item is at cost 3, and the most
thorough ancient harmony of the gospels works the pericope around it and does not
touch it.

**Verified**, not inferred from a summary.

## The gate that catches everybody

Augustine's solution to the staff problem, where the disciples are told to take a
staff in one gospel and not in another, is **gate A2 with no licence**: a
difference of sense with nothing attested to support it.

**A2 is the gate that fired F4 against this project two episodes ago.**

>> So the same gate is abusable by the accuser, by the defender, and by the coder,
>> **and all three have now been caught by it in this season.** That is the
>> strongest evidence the instrument is symmetric, and it is the only kind of
>> evidence that counts: it caught the person holding it.

## And one father who helps the defence, which the headline hides

A survey reporting that the fathers answer worse should be read with suspicion,
and the check is whether anything in it came out the other way. One thing did, and
it is worth stating because it costs the season's headline something.

**Papias, writing around 110 to 130, says Mark wrote down what he remembered
accurately but "not in order."**

That is a very early witness attesting, by name, that these texts were arranged
**thematically rather than chronologically**, and that the arrangement was known
and unremarkable at the time.

>> Which matters directly, because a large family of alleged contradictions is
>> about **ordering**: this gospel puts the temple cleansing early and that one
>> late, this healing before that sermon. Under the study's own rules, a text
>> whose genre does not claim chronological order is not asserting one, **and a
>> non-assertion cannot contradict.**

So Papias supplies exactly what the guard demands: **a licence from outside the
argument**, cited, early, and made for reasons that have nothing to do with any
modern dispute. That is a cost 1 rescue on a whole class of items, offered by a
father, and it makes several of the accusers' entries weaker rather than the
defence's answers.

**It changed no cost in this study**, because rescoring on a finding discovered
mid-survey needs a new pre-registration. **It is reported against the story
anyway**, which is what the season keeps asking of everybody else.

## The device: what the grading found

| grade | what it means | roughly how many |

| GOOD | a licensed dissolution that survives | **two** |
| PARTIAL | answers part of the item | some |
| **BAD** | an answer that fails on its own terms | **five** |
| **ABSENT** | the item is worked and this half is not mentioned | **the Judas silence** |
| AGAINST | the father concedes the point | Origen |

## The clinical anchor

**A textbook that omits the complication.**

The current edition describes the procedure cleanly. The 1974 edition describes
the same procedure and spends two pages on a complication the modern text does not
mention.

**The complication did not stop happening.** It stopped being written about,
because the answer that used to be given for it is no longer one anybody wants in
print. **The older text is more honest and more wrong at the same time**, and you
learn most by reading both.

## The character anchor

**A remake that quietly drops the hardest scene.**

The original had a sequence nobody could shoot well and it shows, and everybody
remembers it being clumsy. The remake simply does not have it.

**The remake is better and it is also evidence that the scene was unfilmable**,
which is a fact about the story you can only recover by noticing an absence.

2. **Grading ancient answers by modern standards.** Genuinely unfair in one

3. **A survey finding only failures being suspect.** The study says this about

@@ EPISODE 19 — VERDICT FLATNESS, ON BOTH SIDES

## The stake

Verdict flatness on both sides, caught by the same scale. **When your instrument
flattens everyone, the interesting question is what the flattening is measuring**,
and this episode asks it.

## Collecting episode 18

The fathers cover more hard items than modern harmonies and answer them worse, and
Augustine's staff solution failed the same gate that fired a falsifier against
this project.

Now the property underneath all of it, which is the most transferable idea in the
season.

## The defect, named

**Verdict flatness.** An output that does not vary with its input.

If a procedure returns the same answer whatever you feed it, **it is not reading
the input**, and its answer carries no information regardless of whether the
answer is true.

This is the series refrain arriving in its sharpest form, and it is worth saying
the whole sentence rather than pointing at it.

>> **A check that cannot fail prints reassurance.**

Verdict flatness is what that sentence looks like from outside: a stream of
confident verdicts, each one individually defensible, none of them responsive to
the case. **The reassurance is real and it is being printed rather than earned**,
and from inside there is no difference, which is why the only reliable test is to
ask what input would have changed the output.

## Augustine states it, in the fourth century, as a rule

Letter 82, and it is the cleanest example anywhere in the material.

When a scriptural text appears to be in error, Augustine says, the possibilities
are: the manuscript is faulty, the translator is bad, or **I have failed to
understand.**

Three options, exhaustively listed. **"The author erred" is not on the list.**

>> **That is a decision procedure whose output cannot depend on the text.** Feed
>> it a real error and it returns one of three, and feed it a scribal slip and it
>> returns one of the same three. It cannot come back negative, and Augustine
>> wrote it down as a rule rather than merely practising it.

And this is not a stray aside. It is the load-bearing premise of his long dispute
with Jerome over Galatians 2, which is itself worth knowing about, because **Jerome
and Augustine disagreed**: Jerome held Paul's rebuke of Peter was staged, Augustine
held it was real. **The fathers were not one voice**, and anybody quoting "the
fathers" as a bloc is quoting a selection.

## The same defect on the other side, mirrored

The skeptic compilations have their own flatness, and this season already measured
it. **27% of the list is the same complaint recounted.** Five entries misdescribe
their own citations. Four put a question on one side, meaning the entry alleges a
contradiction where one half is not an assertion at all.

**A compiler who checked would have caught all of those.** The list is produced by
a procedure whose output does not depend on reading the verse carefully, which is
the same defect wearing the opposite jersey.

>> **The cost scale catches both**, and that is the argument for having a scale at
>> all rather than a verdict. A binary lets a flat procedure hide, because flat
>> procedures produce confident binaries. A price list forces you to name a
>> channel, and a flat procedure has no channel to name.

## Chrysostom, and the argument that predicts nothing

Around 390, Chrysostom makes Layer 1's own argument: the evangelists' agreement
proves their honesty, and their disagreement proves they did not collude.

**Read that twice.** Agreement confirms and disagreement confirms. As stated it
cannot come back negative, on any evidence, ever.

And this is the point in the season where it should be uncomfortable, because
**that is very close to the argument this study is running.** The difference is
the two things Chrysostom did not have and could not have had: **a control and a
denominator.** With them, "disagreement is what independence looks like" becomes a
measurable prediction, because you can ask how much disagreement, of what kind,
compared to what.

**Use him that way, never as a cheap shot.** He is not being stupid. He is running
the right intuition without the equipment that turns it into a test, and the
equipment took seventeen centuries to arrive and this project still lacks half of
it.

## The device: flat against responsive

| procedure | what changes the output | flat? |
|---|---|---|
| "faulty manuscript, bad translator, or my failure" | **nothing** | **yes** |
| "agreement confirms, disagreement confirms" | **nothing** | **yes** |
| a list that never checks its own citations | nothing | **yes** |
| **the cost scale** | which channel supplies the rescue | **no** |
| **the nine gates** | which qualifier fails | no, and F4 measured how much |

## The clinical anchor

**An observation chart where every set of numbers is identical.**

Six hours of hourly obs and the pulse is 80 at every one of them. Respiratory rate
18, every time. Blood pressure to the same digit.

**No patient does that.** A real pulse wanders by a few beats between readings
even in somebody perfectly well, and a column of identical numbers is not a stable
patient, it is a chart filled in from memory at the end of the shift.

>> And notice what makes it detectable, because it is the whole diagnostic.
>> **Not one of those numbers is abnormal.** Every single value is defensible, the
>> patient may genuinely have been fine, and the chart is still worthless, because
>> **a measurement that does not vary was not taken.**

## The character anchor

**A reviewer who gives everything five stars.**

Their five-star review of a genuinely great film is worthless, and the film is
still great. **Nothing about the flatness makes the verdict false.** It makes the
verdict uninformative, and those are different failures, which is why this
criticism does not touch whether Augustine was right about any particular passage.

## What would count against this

1. **Flatness sometimes being correct.** If a text really is reliable, a procedure
   that keeps returning "reliable" is tracking reality. **The problem is that you
   cannot tell the two apart from the output**, which is the whole argument and
   also its limit.
2. **Augustine being quoted out of context.** The letter is a genuine methodological
   statement and it is also a letter, written to a person, about a dispute. **Tier
   V on the quotation, and the reading is an interpretation.**
3. **This project having its own flatness.** It should be asked. **F4 firing is
   evidence against it**, because a flat procedure does not produce a disqualifying
   number about itself.

## Handing off

**Episode 20 finds the exception**, one father who states the criterion this whole
project runs on, against his own interest, in the fourth century.

## Questions

1. Test something you believe for verdict flatness. **What input would change your
   output?** If none, say so.
2. Chrysostom's argument needs a control and a denominator. **Design his control.**
3. Is "the author erred" a legitimate thing to exclude in advance, if you already
   hold the text is inspired? Argue it fairly.

@@ EPISODE 20 — A DEFENDER WHO STATES THE CRITERION

## The stake

One father states the falsification condition against his own interest. **The
rarest thing in apologetics: a defender who tells you what would count against
him**, and what it does to the rest of his verdicts.

## Collecting episode 19

Verdict flatness on both sides, in the same season, caught by the same scale. And
a survey that has found almost nothing but failures, which is a condition that
should make you suspicious of the survey.

## The exception

Eusebius, writing around 340, in a work answering apparent contradictions between
the gospels, on the resurrection accounts.

He is discussing a charge that John conflicts with the others about time and
place, and he says the charge

>> "really would have been justifiable... **if John had kept the same time and the
>> same place**".

**Same time. Same place. Same respect.** That is Tier A. That is episode 10's
definition, stated by a defender, in the fourth century, **as the condition under
which the objection against him would have been correct.**

## Why this is the best thing in the patristic material

Because of who says it and when.

He is not conceding the item. He is naming, in advance, **what would have made his
opponents right.** He is stating his own falsification condition while arguing the
other way, which is the precise habit whose absence this project has been
recording on both sides for five seasons.

>> **It acquits Eusebius of the flatness charge laid on Chrysostom.** Chrysostom's
>> argument could not come back negative. Eusebius named the exact circumstance in
>> which his would have.

And the value of that is not that he was unusually honest, though he may have
been. **It is that the sentence is checkable seventeen centuries later.**

Once a defender says "the objection would be justified if X", anybody can go and
look at whether X obtains. The argument has acquired a handle. **You do not have
to trust him, assess his character, or weigh his tradition against another
tradition.** You go and read John and check the time and the place.

>> That is the entire practical value of stating a falsification condition, and it
>> is why this series keeps returning to it. **It converts a claim about a text
>> into an instruction anybody can follow**, and instructions outlive the people
>> who wrote them in a way that authority does not.

## And the same author, in the same work, doing the opposite

This is why the episode is not a victory lap.

Eusebius also proposes that a troublesome variant is not original, and gives as
grounds that it contained **something contradictory to the evidence of the other
evangelists.**

**That is harmonisation driving textual criticism.** The text is judged
inauthentic because it disagrees, which means disagreement can never survive as
evidence, which is flatness at the level of the text rather than the doctrine.

And elsewhere he answers the empty-tomb tangle by **multiplication**, deriving the
number of appearances from the number of evangelists: four evangelists, therefore
four occasions and four women. **The count comes from the sources rather than from
the events**, which is worse than the modern harmonies that get criticised for the
same move.

>> **One author, one work, both things.** The best falsifiability statement in the
>> material and one of the worst pieces of reasoning in it. **People are not
>> consistent, and a survey that sorts sources into good and bad has not read
>> them.**

## The warning the study writes against itself

A pattern made of one instance is not a pattern.

The study lists P6 anyway, and states why: **everything else in the survey is a
failure, and a survey that finds only failures should be suspected of having
looked only for them.**

**That sentence is the most self-aware thing in six seasons.** It is a researcher
noticing that his own results have a suspiciously uniform sign, and treating the
uniformity as a warning about his method rather than as a finding about his
subject.

## The device: Eusebius, both ways

| what he does | where | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| states the falsification condition against his own interest | Nicetas-Marinus 5 | **the best in the material** |
| judges a variant inauthentic **because** it disagrees | *Ad Marinum* | flat, at the text level |
| derives four appearances from four evangelists | *Ad Marinum* 4 | worse than Lindsell |
| the corpus contradicts itself on gospel order | flagged by its own editors | |

## The clinical anchor

**A colleague documenting their own complication in their own notes.**

Nobody made them. The note says what happened, when, and what they did about it,
and it is the strongest evidence in the file precisely because it runs against the
interest of the person writing.

**And that same colleague will have other notes that are thin and defensive.**
People are not uniform, and a reviewer who decides someone is reliable and then
reads everything through that decision has stopped reading.

## The character anchor

**An opponent conceding a point on record.**

In a debate, the concession is the only moment worth quoting, because everything
else either side says is what they came to say.

**And it does not make them right about the rest.** A person can be scrupulous in
one paragraph and sloppy in the next, and the concession is evidence about the
paragraph, not about the person.

## What would count against this

1. **One instance being nothing.** Said by the study about itself. **Tier V on the
   quotation**, which is worth something: the passage was fetched from an edition
   after being quoted off a blog, and reading the edition corrected three claims
   the project had made.
2. **Translation carrying the argument.** "Same time and the same place" is a
   translation, and the fit with Tier A may be partly the translator's.
3. **Selection.** This is the best thing found in a survey of nineteen hard items.
   **How many defenders were not surveyed?** Almost all of them.

## Handing off

**Episode 21 leaves Layer 1 entirely** for the question everybody actually wants
answered, and the first thing that layer does is fence off the answer.

## Questions

1. Eusebius states the criterion and violates it in the same work. **What do you
   do with an author like that?**
2. "A survey that finds only failures should be suspected of having looked only
   for them." **Apply that to this whole series.**
3. **Before episode 21:** what could a study of the resurrection case honestly
   conclude? Write it down before you are told.

@@ EPISODE 21 — THE LAYER THAT CANNOT ANSWER THE QUESTION

## The stake

The same father judging a text inauthentic because it disagreed, and the warning
that a layer built for consistency cannot answer the question it is being asked.
**Knowing what your layer cannot answer is the last skill**, and the season
states it plainly.

## Collecting episode 20

One father stating the falsification condition against his own interest, and the
same father judging a text inauthentic because it disagreed. And a warning that a
survey finding only failures may have looked only for them.

## The ceiling, fixed before any scoring

Layer 2 asks whether the historical case for the resurrection is honestly built.
Its rulebook fixes, in advance, what it can and cannot conclude.

**It can test** whether the claimed facts are supported, whether consensus claims
are verifiable, whether alternatives are fairly eliminated, and whether the case
is circular.

**It cannot test whether a miracle occurred.**

The reason is stated plainly and it is not a dodge. **Once the facts are fixed, the
inference is dominated by the prior on miracles**, and no source check touches a
prior. Two people who agree on every fact in the case and disagree about whether
miracles happen will disagree about the conclusion, and nothing in a bibliography
moves that.

>> **The best possible outcome of Layer 2 is "the case is honestly built."** Never
>> "it happened." That is written down before any scoring, so it cannot be quietly
>> upgraded afterwards.

The reasoning behind that ceiling deserves working, because it is not obvious and
it is not a concession to sceptics.

A historical argument moves you from evidence to a conclusion. **How far it moves
you depends on what you thought was possible before you started.** Somebody who
holds that miracles do not occur will read any quantity of testimony as evidence
that something else happened, and they are not being unreasonable, because a
sufficiently improbable claim requires proportionate evidence.

**And no amount of checking citations touches that.** You can verify every source
in the case, find every claim supported, and two readers will still land in
different places, because the disagreement was never about the sources.

>> So the ceiling is not modesty and it is not scepticism. **It is a statement
>> about which disagreements a bibliography can settle**, and this one is not
>> among them. What a bibliography can settle is whether the case is built out of
>> things that are actually there, which is worth doing and is a smaller job.

## The rule that carries the whole layer

Layer 1 had structural protection: hostile compilers chose the docket, so the
coder could not skip hard items.

**Layer 2 has no such protection**, because there is no hostile compilation of
objections to a specific apologetics website. So one rule does all the work:

>> **The apologetics site is the object under test and never the docket source.**

Objections come from **named critics only**. If a session takes the objection list
from the site being examined, the site has chosen which objections it will answer,
and **Layer 2 is void rather than weakened.**

The key auditor named in advance is **Dale Allison**, chosen specifically because
he affirms the core facts while rejecting the methodology, so both halves of him
always get reported.

## G1 fired, at two levels

**G1 said:** if the empty-tomb consensus claim cannot be verified from published,
checkable data, the site's strongest citation fails and the fact rests on an
unauditable premise.

**Level one.** The site asserts that most scholars, including non-Christian ones,
accept the tomb was found empty, **and cites no published source for it.**

**Level two, and this is the worse one.** The underlying survey behind that
consensus claim is one its own author described in 2012 as **"mostly
unpublished."**

So the strongest citation in the strongest section rests on a bibliography nobody
can inspect. **Cost 4**, for an unauditable warrant, which is the same discount
this project applied to its own run-1 fit ratio.

**Apply your own discount to yourself first, then to others**, and the number is
the same number.

## The falsifier that signalled

**G4** asks whether the surviving Layer 2 arguments need detail-level gospel
accuracy. If they do, **Layers 1 and 2 are not independent and this project's
whole framing is wrong.**

It signalled without firing. **Reported against the project's own interest**, and
left open.

## The device: what Layer 2 can say

| question | answerable | how |
|---|---|---|
| are the claimed facts supported by published sources | **yes** | check the citations |
| is a consensus claim verifiable | **yes**, and G1 fired on it | find the survey |
| are alternatives fairly eliminated | yes | read the critics directly |
| is the case circular | yes | trace the warrants |
| **did it happen** | **no** | **prior on miracles dominates** |

## The clinical anchor

**A guideline citing an unpublished dataset.**

The recommendation is grade A. You follow the citation and it goes to a conference
abstract, or to data held by the group that wrote the guideline.

**The recommendation may be entirely correct.** And you cannot check it, and grade
A means checkable, so the grade is wrong even if the advice is right. **Those are
separate facts** and conflating them is how a whole field ends up standing on four
unpublished trials.

## The character anchor

**A Wikipedia claim whose citation leads to a dead link.**

The sentence has been there for nine years and is quoted everywhere. Follow the
footnote and the page is gone, and the archived version does not say what the
sentence says.

**The claim might still be true.** What has collapsed is the reason anybody had for
believing it, and the difference between those two is invisible until somebody
clicks.

## What would count against this

1. **The site being a soft target.** It is one apologetics website, not the
   scholarly case. **Grading it does not grade Wright or Habermas properly**, and
   the study should not be read as though it did.
2. **The consensus claim being true anyway.** Very possibly. **G1 is about the
   warrant, not the fact**, and an unauditable warrant for a true claim is still
   unauditable.
3. **No control existing for Layer 2, at all.** Layer 1 had the apocrypha. **There
   is no corpus of comparably-argued cases**, and none can be built, which is a
   permanent ceiling rather than an unfinished task.

## Handing off

**Episode 22 closes the season** and adds up what three fenced layers actually
bought, which is less than the question deserves and more than the argument
usually gets.

## Questions

1. **Write the sentence Layer 2 is allowed to conclude.** Then write the one people
   will quote. Notice the gap.
2. G1 fired on an unauditable warrant. **Find one in something you believe.**
3. If no control can be built for Layer 2, should it have been attempted at all?

@@ EPISODE 22 — WHAT THREE FENCED LAYERS BOUGHT

## The stake

The season's promise comes due. The fences are counted: what they bought, what
they refused, and the series' most useful sentence. **A cruder instrument with a
control beats a refined instrument without one**, and you have now watched both
halves of that sentence.

## Collecting episode 21

A ceiling fixed in advance that Layer 2 cannot test whether a miracle occurred, an
inversion rule doing all the protective work alone, and G1 firing at two levels on
the strongest citation in the case.

## The season, added up

| layer | what it established | what it cost |
|---|---|---|
| **1a** | the apocrypha embellish about **11×** more, robustly | magnitude failed twice, direction only |
| **1a control** | restraint holds in direction | **H3 and H5 fired**, four units carry the verdict |
| **1b** | **2 at cost 3, 0 at cost 4**, from a hostile docket of 671 | **F4 fired**, dissolution unreliable, **inerrancy gone** |
| **patristic** | the fathers cover more and answer worse | one father states our own criterion |
| **2** | the strongest citation rests on an unpublished bibliography | cannot test whether it happened |

**Four falsifiers fired across one season**, and every one of them fired on the
person running the study rather than on his opponents.

Set that against the first four seasons and the contrast is the finding. In
Seasons 2 to 4 the falsifiers were about the instrument: a null was badly built, a
round was under-powered, a metric measured the wrong half. **Here they are about
the person.** The dissolution rate rests on his own sense-distinctions. The
verdict rests on four sentences he coded. The blinding does not blind him.

>> **An instrument you can fix. A coder you cannot.** Which is why this season's
>> honest ceiling is lower than Season 4's despite the study being better designed,
>> and why the phrase "strong structured pilot" appears on every headline rather
>> than in a limitations section.

## The one sentence worth keeping

>> **The apocrypha demonstrably embellish and hard-contradict far more than the
>> canon, which is robust. The canon appears to interlock more, which is real in
>> direction and inflated in degree by who did the coding. And there is no
>> denominator, so none of it is a rate.**

Three clauses, three different confidences, nothing thrown away and nothing
promoted. **That is the whole method in one sentence**, and it took five seasons to
be able to write it.

## What this season answers that Season 4 could not

Season 4 ended unable to interpret its own best result, because four partisan
documents about one week had nothing to be compared against.

**This season had a control**, and the answer is genuinely informative: **partisan
documents about Jesus, written by believers in the same tradition, do not all
behave the same way.** The apocrypha embellish eleven times more. That comparison
was unavailable in every one of the first four seasons, and it is why the numbers
here mean more despite the instrument being cruder.

>> **A cruder instrument with a control beats a refined instrument without one**,
>> and Seasons 2 through 4 are 50 episodes of refining an instrument without one.

That sentence is the most useful thing in six seasons and it should be uncomfortable,
because it means most of the effort went to the wrong place.

Season 2 fixed the tokeniser, de-noised the lexicon, audited the corpus and chose
a metric for principled reasons. Every one of those was a real improvement and
**none of them addressed the question of what the measurement should be compared
against.** The instrument got sharper and the comparison stayed absent.

>> **Refinement is visible work and controls are invisible work.** You can see a
>> better lexicon. A control looks like a folder of texts nobody is studying, and
>> it is the folder that decides whether any number means anything.

Which is the practical lesson for anybody running a study of their own: **build
the control first, while it is still cheap and while you do not yet know which way
it will cut.**

## What is still missing, said plainly

- **The denominator.** The top priority, specified and not built. Without it every
  count in Layer 1b is a statement about a list.
- **A second coder.** The ceiling on everything, stated on every headline.
- **The apocryphal control on the contradiction count.** F3 is registered and
  **untested.** Nobody has run the nine gates on the Gospel of Peter, and until
  somebody does, "51 accepted" has no comparison.
- **190 items unscored**, the New Testament against the Old.
- **32 of 51 audits are tier K**, meaning real defenders and real arguments with
  citations unverified. A lead, not a fact.

## The clinical anchor

**Three tests answering three different questions, and the harm when they merge.**

The troponin, the echo and the angiogram answer different questions and a
consultant who lets a normal troponin reassure them about the valve has merged
three results into one feeling.

**The fences are the skill.** Not the tests, which anybody can order.

## The character anchor

**Three locked rooms, and the temptation to say you searched the house.**

You searched one room thoroughly, one partially, and looked through the keyhole of
the third. That is a real search and it is a specific claim, and the sentence
"I searched the house" is available, shorter, and false.

## What would count against this

1. **One coder, and it is stated here for the last time.** Nothing in this season
   survives a reader who declines to trust the coding, and the season has said so
   in every episode by design.
2. **Four fired falsifiers being a bad sign rather than a good one.** A study
   whose own checks keep firing may simply be a badly designed study. **That
   reading is available and the counter is thin:** the alternative design is one
   where the checks were never set.
3. **The whole season being about method rather than about God.** Deliberately. And
   somebody could fairly say that a study which fences off every interesting
   question has bought its rigour by declining to be about anything.

## What comes next

**Season 5b takes one allegation per episode** and works it to the floor: what
both sides say, what the Greek actually says, and what the rescue costs. It is the
most repeatable format in the series and it is where the price list stops being a
scale and becomes a set of worked examples.

## Questions

1. **Four falsifiers fired.** Good study or bad study? Defend it.
2. Write the denominator's pre-registration. **One page, before you know the
   number.**
3. Nobody has run the gates on the Gospel of Peter. **Predict the result**, then
   say what you would conclude from either outcome.
