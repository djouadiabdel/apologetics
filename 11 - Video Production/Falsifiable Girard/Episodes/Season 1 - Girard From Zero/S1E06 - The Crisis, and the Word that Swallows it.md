# The Crisis, and the Word that Swallows it

## The stake

The season changes gear here, from philosophy to evidence. From this episode on,
nothing rests on Girard sounding right. **Everything becomes a list you can
check**, and the promise of the rest of the season is that you can run every
check yourself.

## Collecting episode 5

Episode 5 ended with a mechanism that **operated before writing** and that
**nobody inside it could see.** Which leaves an obvious problem: if it is
invisible from the inside and prehistoric, how would anyone ever study it?

**Girard's answer is that it leaves residue.** The people who ran the mechanism
went on to tell stories about what happened, and the stories carry marks.

> From here the season stops doing philosophy and starts making **a list**,
> because a list is the only thing you can turn into code.

Source: Girard, *The Scapegoat*, chapter 2.

## The frame, and one sentence makes it testable

Girard says a text written **by the persecutors**, who do not know they are
persecutors, carries four marks. He calls them the stereotypes of persecution.

**And the thing to say slowly: they are not four themes.**

A theme is something a text is about. **These are four positions in one event**,
seen from different places, by people with different amounts of information.

| # | stereotype | whose view it is | one line |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **crisis** | the community's | everything that made us different has stopped working |
| 2 | **crimes** | the accusers' | here is what he did |
| 3 | **marks** | the accusers' | here is why it was him and not someone else |
| 4 | **violence** | nobody's, it is what happens | all of us, on one of him |

**Four episodes, one each**, because each of the four has its own vocabulary, its
own dominant word, its own way of going wrong, and its own number that comes back
later to do damage. Taking them in pairs hides exactly the thing worth seeing,
which is that **they fail differently.**

## Stereotype 1, and the part episode 4 did not do

**Episode 4 gave you the crisis as a phenomenon.** Plague, famine, sterility,
twins, doubles, civil strife. A community describing a world in which
distinctions have stopped working. Do not re-teach it.

**This episode is the other half, and it is the half nobody does.** You have a
phenomenon. Now turn it into something a machine can count, which means writing
down the actual words.

Here is the list, as it was written, before any text existed:

> plague · pestilence · famine · drought · blight · barren · sterile · sickness ·
> disease · **death** · twin · twins · double · doubles · likeness

Look at how the list is built, because the construction is doing two different
jobs at once. Some of those words name **the disaster**. Some of them name
**the loss of difference**, which is what the disaster means in the theory. Twins
and doubles and likeness are not calamities at all. They are in the bucket because
Girard says undifferentiation is the crisis, and twins are undifferentiation with
a face.

> **So the bucket already contains a theoretical commitment**, and it is a good
> one, and nobody scoring a text will ever see it. **This is what an
> operationalization is**: a claim about the world, compressed into a list, and
> then invisible for the rest of the project.

## The number, and it is the worst one in the instrument

`crisis` fires at **2.07 per thousand words.** Middle of the pack.

And it is **60% the single word `death`.**

**Sixty percent.** That is the highest domination of any of the four buckets, and
it is worth sitting with before you hear the other three, because it sets the
standard against which they will look healthy.

## Why that number is a problem and not a curiosity

Ask what `death` tells you about a text.

**Almost nothing**, because it is one of the most common words in narrative
literature of any kind. Epics are full of deaths. Tragedies are full of deaths.
Genealogies are full of deaths, in the flattest possible sense, because that is
what a genealogy is. Hymns mention death. Philosophy mentions death.

So a bucket built to detect **a community losing its distinctions** is, in
practice, six-tenths a detector for **somebody having died**, which is a different
claim and a much duller one.

> **The crisis bucket is the one place in the instrument where the theory is
> loudest and the measurement is weakest.** Episode 4 spent ten minutes on the
> most interesting idea in Girard, and this is what it looks like once it has been
> made countable.

## The character anchor

**Agent Smith.**

He starts as one agent among several, a distinct thing with a role. Then he copies
himself onto everybody, and by the end the street is full of Smiths, and the point
is not that Smith is strong. **The point is that difference has stopped
existing**, which is the crisis stereotype in one image.

And now the joke the instrument played on itself. **The bucket built to detect
undifferentiation lost its own differentiation.** Fifteen words went in, and one
of them replicated across the bucket until it was 60% of everything the bucket
reports. `plague` and `sterile` and `twins` are still in there, doing almost
nothing, the way the last non-Smith is still technically on the street.

## The clinical anchor

**Fever.**

It is a real sign, not noise and not an artifact, and a patient with a fever
genuinely has something going on. **And it barely narrows anything**,
because it is present in most of what walks through the door, so the differential
you had before you took the temperature is roughly the differential you have
after.

Fever is a symptom that is common enough to be almost uninformative, and that is
exactly `death` at 60%. **A word can be a correct member of a category and still
destroy the category's usefulness by being everywhere.**

## What would count against this

1. **`death` genuinely being the crisis marker in these texts.** A serious
   objection, and the strongest one available. In a plague narrative the deaths
   **are** the crisis, so the word is not a contaminant, it is the thing itself.
   **Nobody has read a sample of `death` hits in context to find out**, which is a
   day's work and would settle it.
2. **The bucket working anyway.** Domination is not the same as failure. Delete
   `death`, re-run everything, and see whether any result moves. **Never done**,
   for any bucket, in any round.
3. **60% being normal.** If every word-list in every field is dominated by its
   commonest member, this is a fact about language rather than a defect here.
   Plausible, and it would make the number less alarming without making the bucket
   any more useful.

## Handing off
