# Reading Oedipus the King

[[S1E15 - Reading the Bacchae|← Reading the Bacchae]] · [[S1E17 - Reading Apollodorus|Reading Apollodorus →]]

---
## The stake

The cleanest fit in existence, and the cleanness is the problem. **This episode
is about the moment a perfect example should make you more suspicious, not
less**, because the texts that fit too well are the ones that were chosen for
fitting.

## Collecting episode 15

The *Bacchae* is Girard's best example and it came apart in our hands: the
accusation stereotype was the weakest of the four, and the dissent turned out to
be in the wrong place.

**This play is the opposite problem.** It is the cleanest fit in existence, and
the cleanness is what should worry you.

## The story, for someone who has never read it

**Storr's translation, and the opening line to read cold is "My children, latest
born to Cadmus old."**

Thebes is dying. Crops failing, cattle dying, women miscarrying. **The city is
under plague and the play opens inside it**, with a delegation begging the king
to do something.

The king is **Oedipus**, who saved the city once before by solving the Sphinx's
riddle, and who is therefore exactly the man to solve this.

The oracle says: the plague is a pollution. The old king Laius was murdered and
the murderer is still here. **Find him and expel him and the city will live.**

So Oedipus opens an investigation. He curses the murderer publicly. He questions
the prophet Tiresias, who tells him to stop. He questions his wife's brother. He
questions a herdsman.

**And the investigation arrives at himself.** He killed Laius, who was his father.
He married Jocasta, who is his mother. Jocasta hangs herself. Oedipus blinds
himself with her brooches and is driven out.

## The structural feature nobody points at, and it is episode 3

**This is a detective story in which the detective is the criminal.**

Episode 3 was about rivals converging until you cannot tell them apart. Here the
collapse is not between two people. **It is between two roles inside one person.**
The investigator and the culprit, the healer and the pollution, the man looking
and the thing being looked for.

> **The play is a machine for collapsing a distinction**, and it runs for fifteen
> hundred lines doing exactly that. Which is a strange thing to find at the
> centre of a theory about the collapse of distinctions.

**Tiresias is in this play too**, and he does the same job he does in the
*Bacchae*: he tells a king a true thing the king cannot hear, and he is abused for
it. Worth pointing out across the two episodes.

## The four, laid out by Sophocles himself

| stereotype | and the text does it out loud |
|---|---|
| **crisis** | Thebes **under plague**. Crops, cattle, childbirth. **The first scene**, before any plot |
| **crimes** | **parricide and incest.** Two of Girard's four crime families, named, both difference-erasing in exactly his sense |
| **marks** | a **foreigner** who turns out to be native. **Lame** from infancy, his feet pierced. And the **king** |
| **violence** | **expulsion.** He is driven out, and the city recovers |

**Read the opening scene aloud.** A city on its knees under plague, begging its
king for a cure, **and the cure turns out to be his own expulsion.**

## The marks stereotype, all three directions at once

Worth dwelling on. Oedipus is simultaneously:

- **too low**: exposed as an infant, crippled, raised by strangers, a man with no
  known parents
- **too high**: the king, saviour of the city, married to the queen
- **the outsider**: a Corinthian, or so everyone including him believes

> **He is every category of victim mark in the theory, in one man, at once.**
> Girard did not have to reach for this. Sophocles handed it to him.

## Now run episode 12's causal diagram on it, because the play is strange here

Episode 12's device: myth tells it forwards, Girard reconstructs it backwards.

**And this play stages the reconstruction on purpose.** We watch the community
**assemble** the accusation, live, scene by scene, over the course of an
afternoon.

So which is it?

**Reading one, for Girard.** Look at them building it. The crisis came first, the
need for a culprit came second, and the crime was assembled to fit. The play shows
you the machinery.

**Reading two, against.** No. They are not assembling it, they are **discovering**
it, with witnesses and evidence and a herdsman who was there. **The investigation
is competent and the conclusion is correct.**

> **Say both and settle neither.** The play is the strongest evidence for the
> theory and the strongest evidence that the theory is reading a detective story
> as a lynching.

## The character anchor

**Zuko's Agni Kai with his father**, from the accusation side rather than the scar
side.

Everybody in that room can give you a lawful account of what happened. A boy spoke
out of turn in a war meeting, a challenge was issued under the rules, the duel was
fought, the outcome was the outcome. **Every procedural step is correct.** Nobody
present is lying about the sequence.

And what actually happened is a father needing a son to be the problem.

**That is the two readings of *Oedipus*, in one scene.** You cannot settle it by
checking whether the procedure was followed, because the procedure was followed.

## The clinical anchor

**A case conference that reaches the correct diagnosis and would have reached a
diagnosis regardless.**

The workup is real. The tests were indicated, the findings are genuine, the
conclusion fits. **And the meeting was convened because something had to be
decided by the end of it**, and everybody in the room knew that when they walked
in.

Being right does not tell you which of those two produced the answer, and the
notes will only record the first.

## The two numbers

It scores **21.23**, second highest of 72 texts.

And it holds the **highest `marks` score in the whole corpus, 9.58.**

Why? Because `marks` is **44% the word "king"**, and the title character is
addressed as king on nearly every page.

> **The text that best fits the theory is also the single strongest piece of
> evidence for the objection that the instrument is only detecting royalty.**

**Sit in that and do not resolve it.** Season 4 runs the experiment that settles
it, and **the result is not the one this episode makes you expect.**

## The thing about Sophocles nobody says

Here is a real problem for the theory, better coming from you than from a comment.

**Oedipus is guilty. He did it.** He killed his father and married his mother.
**The accusation is true.**

Girard's mechanism runs on victims selected arbitrarily, whose guilt is
manufactured by the peace that follows. And his cleanest textual fit is a case
where the charge is accurate and the investigation is sound.

> **What does a theory built on arbitrary selection do with a victim who actually
> did it?**

There is an answer available: the theory is about **the crowd's process**, not the
victim's record, and a real culprit can be scapegoated too. **Say that, and then say
that this project never tests it and the question stays open.**

And it is worth seeing how much that answer gives away, because it is a good answer
with a large bill attached. **If guilt is irrelevant to the mechanism, then
innocence cannot be the thing that distinguishes a Gospel from a myth**, and the
distinction has to live entirely in how the crowd behaves rather than in what the
victim did.

That is not a problem invented here. It is Girard's own position, stated in his own
sentence, and it is the sentence episode 12 read twice. **Hold on to it**, because
the instrument built in Season 3 fills its word-lists mostly with innocence
vocabulary anyway, and **Season 7 episode 4 is where that contradiction finally
does visible damage.**

## What would count against this reading

1. **The play being a detective story rather than a lynching.** Stated above and
   deliberately not settled. **It is the strongest reading against Girard in the
   whole season** and it comes from the text that suits him best.
2. **The instrument scoring it highly for the wrong reason.** It holds the highest
   `marks` score in the corpus and `marks` is 44% the word `king`. **A text whose
   title character is addressed as king on every page will score high whether or
   not anything Girardian happens in it.**
3. **Sophocles being one of Girard's sources.** If the stereotype list was built
   partly by reading this play, then finding all four in it is circular. **Nobody
   has traced which texts Girard had in front of him when he wrote chapter 2**, and
   until somebody does, question 4 below has no answer.

## Handing off

Two masterpieces, two readings, and in both cases the poetry and the structure are
inseparable from the content.

**Episode 17 reads the opposite of that**, and the opposite turns out to be a
natural experiment nobody set up on purpose.
