# You Want it Because He Wants it

— · [[S1E02 - The One Who Taught You is Now in the Way|The One Who Taught You is Now in the Way →]]

---
## The stake

Before any theory: this episode is about the wanting itself, and you are doing it
right now. Somewhere in your life there is a thing you want that you never wanted
before a specific person wanted it first, and you have experienced that wanting as
entirely your own. **By the end of these ten minutes you will be able to find it.**
That is the whole promise of the episode.

## The character

**Vegeta.**

And be careful with him, because the lazy version of this example is wrong and an
audience will catch it.

**The lazy version:** Vegeta only wants what Goku wants. That is false. Vegeta had
goals before Goku mattered to him. He wanted immortality. He wanted to be the
strongest Saiyan. He had a whole identity built on being royalty.

**What actually happens is more interesting.** A low-class Saiyan turns out to be
stronger than the prince. And from that moment Vegeta's existing ambition is
**re-routed.** He does not acquire a new goal. Every goal he already had gets
re-expressed as a comparison with one specific person.

> **That is the more accurate claim and the more useful one: the model does not
> hand you a desire. The model gives your desire a scoreboard.**

You can prove it from the story. Every time Vegeta reaches a level he previously
called the summit, he is not satisfied for a single episode, because Goku reached
it too. **The target moves because the target was never a number.** It was a
person.

**Second anchor: Salieri in *Amadeus*.** He does not want music. He wants to be
the one who has it. When he sees that Mozart's manuscript has no corrections, he
does not hear beauty. He hears a verdict on himself.

Hold Salieri, because he comes back at the end of this episode and he is doing
something Vegeta is not.

## The claim, stated plainly

Girard's first move is that desire has **three** parts, not two.

The ordinary picture has two: a **subject** who wants, and an **object** wanted.

Girard says that for almost everything humans fight over there is a third part: a
**model**, sometimes called a mediator. The model wants the object, or seems to,
or simply has it. And the subject learns to want it from the model.

```
                    MODEL
                   /     \
        imitates  /       \  wants
                 /         \
            SUBJECT ------- OBJECT
                  the visible line
```

The line from subject to object is the one everybody sees. **The other two do the
work.**

**Draw this on camera and leave it up**, because in episode 2 it deforms, and the
whole hinge of the season is what happens to the top vertex.

## What this does NOT cover, and the theory is worthless without this

Here is the objection that arrives in the first thirty seconds, and the episode
must answer it or the whole thing is over-broad.

**You want water because you are thirsty.** Nobody modelled that. A newborn wants
milk with no social input whatever. If mimetic desire covers everything, it
explains nothing.

**Girard's answer is a distinction, and you should state it hard.**

| | driven by | example | can two people share it peacefully? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **appetite / need** | the body | thirst, hunger, sleep, warmth | **yes**, if there is enough |
| **desire** | a model | status, a career, a person, recognition | **no**, and the reason is the next section |

> **The theory is about the second column only.** Somebody who says "Girard
> thinks I only eat because others eat" has been handed the theory badly. **Say
> the limit out loud in episode 1**, because the credibility of everything after
> it depends on the audience knowing you know where it stops.

## Why this is not just "people are envious"

Envy is a feeling somebody has. Girard is not describing a feeling. He is
describing a **structure that produces the feeling**, and it runs whether or not
anyone involved is a bad person.

**The clinical version**, which may land faster. Nobody chooses to have referred
pain. Gallbladder disease presents in the right shoulder. The patient is not
confused and is not lying. The wiring routes it there.

Mimetic desire is a claim of that kind: **the wanting is routed through someone
else, and the person doing the wanting experiences it as their own, directly,
with no sense of a detour.**

Vegeta does not think "I am copying Goku." He experiences pure, first-person,
burning ambition.

> The structure is **invisible from inside it**, and that is not an inconvenient
> side effect. The theory requires it, because a mechanism everyone could see
> operating would stop working.

**Mark that as a tier-K claim on camera.** Girard says this repeatedly across
several books and this series has not gone and verified the page. **Say "I have
not checked the page for this one"** rather than implying you have. That habit
costs you nothing here and it is the habit the entire series is about.

## The distinction that decides everything after this

**This is the load-bearing section of episode 1**, and it is the one most
summaries of Girard leave out, which is why most summaries make episode 2 sound
false.

Not every model becomes a rival. Girard says which ones do, and it depends on one
thing: **can you and the model actually collide?**

**External mediation.** The model is out of reach. A dead legend. A fictional
hero. A star you will never meet. A kid who trains like his favourite fighter is
imitating a model, thoroughly, and **no rivalry is possible**, because the model
is not competing for anything the kid can touch. This kind is stable. It can last
a lifetime. It is mostly benign and it is what we usually call admiration.

**Internal mediation.** The model is **in your world**. Same room, same field,
same year, same prize. Now the imitation puts you on a collision course, because
what he wants is finite and you are now reaching for it.

**And now go back to Salieri, because this is why that story is a tragedy rather
than a career.**

> If Mozart had lived two hundred years before Salieri, Salieri could have
> worshipped him for a lifetime and been happy. **The problem is not Mozart's
> genius. The problem is that Mozart is in the next room, at the same court,
> competing for the same commissions.** Same admiration, moved close, becomes
> poison.

**Vegeta is internal mediation.** Goku is right there, same species, same fights,
same scoreboard.

> **Episode 2 is entirely about internal mediation.** When you hear "the model
> becomes the rival," it does not mean every model. It means the ones close
> enough to be reached.

## Two consequences that are not obvious

**Desire is contagious.** If wanting is learned from a model, it spreads, and one
person wanting a thing makes it more wantable. **Notice that this is a real
prediction and it could be false.** If wanting came from the object's properties,
crowd interest would tell you nothing about whether you should want it. Anyone
who has watched an auction, a bidding war on a house, or a queue forming outside
a shop has watched the prediction hold.

**Desire has nothing to reach.** If the object was only ever a signal of the
model's attention, then getting it does not end anything. Vegeta beats the level
and the level moves. **And this one is testable in your own life**, which is what
question 1 is for.

## What would count against all this

The series is about falsifiability, so episode 1 has to model the question rather
than just asserting a theory.

**What would break mimetic desire?** Three candidates, and say them:

1. A desire that is **intense, sustained and socially costly**, held by someone
   with no model available, in a domain nobody around them values.
2. Getting the object producing **lasting satisfaction**, routinely, rather than a
   moved target.
3. Rivalry that **does not intensify** as the two parties get closer and more
   similar. Girard predicts it should get worse, not better.

> **And here is the honest part. Nothing in this project tests any of the
> three.** The instrument built in Season 2 starts at the four stereotypes, which
> are episodes 6 to 9. **Everything before that is a premise the instrument
> assumes rather than tests**, and a critic will say so if you do not.

> **And here is the promise that pays for the wait.** Before this season ends you
> will watch the instrument touch four real texts, and in the last episode you
> will watch one of them break it: the least persecution-shaped story in Greek
> literature will produce the most persecution-shaped score in the corpus. **You
> will see the failure with your own eyes, and you will see what saves the
> instrument.** That is the deal: no test until episode 18, and a real one when
> it comes.
