# The Rule Girard Actually Wrote

## The stake

This one is about a habit, and the habit is the series. Somebody finally opened
the books and checked the page numbers. **What came back is a lesson in how much
of what everyone believes about a thinker was never checked against the page**,
and one of the corrections changes the argument itself.

**This episode exists because somebody finally opened the books.** Every page
number quoted in this series came out of the project's own files and had never
been checked against a copy. When they were checked, three of four needed a
correction, and one of them turned out to be about the argument rather than the
page.

## Collecting episode 10

Episode 10 built a ladder. Level 1, each vocabulary appears. Level 2, all four
appear in the same text, which is nearly worthless because **all four fire in 90%
of 72 Greek and Roman texts.** Level 3, all four converge on one passage, more
than chance would put them there.

**And it said level 3 is the claim.** This episode asks whether Girard says that,
and the answer is on a page nobody in eight rounds had read.

## And now the thing that was found by opening the book

**This is tier V. Go and check it, the passage is four pages into chapter 3 of
*The Scapegoat*.**

Girard states his own decision rule for identifying a persecution text, and it is
worth reading in full because nobody in this project had read it until the
citations were verified:

> "Each time an oral or written testament mentions an act of violence that is
> directly or indirectly collective we question whether it includes the
> description of a social and cultural crisis... The juxtaposition of more than
> one stereotype within a single document indicates persecution. **Not all the
> stereotypes must be present: three are enough and often even two.**"

**Read the last sentence again and put it on the ladder.**

That is a **level 2** rule. Presence in a document. And it is not even the strict
version of level 2, because it does not ask for four. **It asks for two.**

> **Girard's own procedure sits on the rung this episode has just shown is nearly
> worthless**, and it sits one rung lower than the version that was measured.

Now apply the project's own number to it. All four buckets fire in **90% of 72
Greek and Roman texts**, including texts with no persecution in them anywhere. If
all four are present in nine texts out of ten, then **"three are enough and often
even two" classifies almost the whole of Greek literature as a persecution
document.**

**That is not an objection somebody invented. It is Girard's stated rule meeting
this project's measurement, and the measurement was made without anybody noticing
it bore on the rule.**

## The device: put the ladder next to the source

**Same three rungs, one extra column**, and the column is what the citation check

> **Read the second and fourth rows.** They are two rungs apart, and the project
> spent eight rounds on the top one believing it was on Girard's.

## What follows, and be careful, because two things do not follow

**It does not follow that Girard is wrong**, because a rule of thumb for reading a
medieval chronicle is not the same as a claim about what the mechanism produces.
He may well hold that the event generates all four and that a surviving text
preserves only some. **Say that out loud, because it is the strongest defence and
it is probably correct.**

**And it does not follow that this project was wasting its time.** The opposite.

> **Level 3 is this project's invention.** Girard did not ask for co-location on a
> passage. **The instrument has been testing something harder than its source
> requires**, for eight rounds, and failing at it.

Which reframes every failure ahead of you.

At the end of Season 3 the tempting sentence is "Girard's claim did not show up."
**The honest one is longer and says two things.** The strengthened version of his
claim did not show up. **And the version he actually stated is refuted by this
project's own 90% figure, in the other direction.**

Hold both. **Neither is comfortable and both are checkable.**

## The move that settles it, and it takes one sentence

Here is the question that makes the whole thing tractable, and it is the question
to ask of any detection rule anybody ever hands you.

> **Run it against the case it is supposed to distinguish.**

Girard's rule says two or three stereotypes in a document indicate persecution. So
run it on **the Passion**, which is the standard name for the last part of the
gospel story: the arrest of Jesus, his trial before the Jewish authorities and
then Pilate, the crowd calling for his execution, and the crucifixion. From the
Latin for suffering, and nothing to do with the modern sense of the word.

**That episode is the one text Girard's whole theory has to treat differently**,
so it is the right thing to point a persecution detector at.

**Crisis**: a city under occupation, a festival crowd, an authority in conflict
with a popular teacher. **Crimes**: blasphemy, which is Girard's third crime family
exactly. **Marks**: a provincial and a carpenter's son who is simultaneously a
claimed king, both directions at once. **Violence**: a crowd, calling out together,
and a public execution.

**Four of four. Emphatically.** Episode 12 works through it properly, and it is not
a grudging pass.

> So the rule fires on the Gospels **as hard as it fires on anything**, and by its
> own terms it must, because Girard says the Gospels describe the same event. **A
> rule that classifies both sides of your distinction as positive is not a
> distinction.**

**And Girard knows this.** That is the part that turns a gotcha into a finding. He
never claims the four stereotypes separate myth from Gospel. He puts the whole
difference somewhere else, in one sentence about a dissenting voice, and this
series reads that sentence in episode 12.

> **The four stereotypes were never the discriminating instrument. They are the
> detector.** They tell you a persecution is being described. They cannot tell you
> who is describing it or whether the describer knows what he is doing.

So the finding is not that Girard contradicts himself. **It is that the first three
rounds built an ever better detector and tested it as though it were a
discriminator.**

The theory says it cannot be one. **And the sentence saying so was on the shelf
the whole time.**

**Round 4 is the round that finally builds the discriminator.** Everything before
it was sharpening the wrong tool, and the person sharpening it could have known
that from chapter 3 of *The Scapegoat* and page 3 of *I See Satan*.

## The character anchor

**A recipe you have cooked from memory for years.**

You know it. You have made it forty times, you have taught it to somebody else,
and you could write it out on request. **Then you find the card**, and it says
180 degrees where you have always used 200, and it does not mention the step you
have always thought was the whole trick.

The dish you have been making is not bad. It may genuinely be better. **But it is
yours**, and you have spent years attributing it to somebody else, sincerely,
without ever once putting the card next to the pan.

**That is what happened here**, and notice the specific thing that made it
possible: **the recipe was quoted from memory of a summary**, and everybody in the
chain was quoting the same summary.

## The clinical anchor

**Citing a guideline you have never opened.**

Everybody has done it. You know what the guideline says because you learned it
from a senior, who learned it from a talk, which was based on the guideline. **The
chain is usually accurate and it is never checkable at any single link**, and the
gap between "what the guideline says" and "what everybody knows it says" widens by
one small step per retelling.

The uncomfortable part is that this is normally harmless. **It stops being
harmless the moment you build an instrument out of it**, because an instrument
does not know it is repeating a summary and will happily produce four significant
figures on top of one.

## What would count against this

1. **The chapter 3 sentence being a rule of thumb rather than a claim.** Argued
   above and it is probably right. **The counter is that this project's task is
   precisely the reader's task in that sentence**, which is deciding from a
   document whether persecution is being described.
2. **Girard saying the opposite somewhere else.** Very likely, across a fifty-year
   body of work, and **nobody has looked.** One verified sentence is one verified
   sentence. **This episode is not entitled to say "Girard's position is" on the
   strength of a single passage**, and neither is the objection it raises.
3. **The 90% figure being an artifact.** It comes from this project's lexicon, and
   Season 1 episode 8 showed `marks` is 44% the word `king`. **If the buckets fire
   on genre rather than on persecution, then 90% is a fact about the instrument**
   and cannot be used against Girard's rule.

> **Objection 2 is the honest ceiling on this episode.** It found one sentence.
> Finding one sentence is not scholarship, it is the first ten minutes of
> scholarship, and the series should say so while reporting it.
