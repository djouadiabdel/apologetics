# Myth is the Story the Crowd Tells Itself

[[S1E11 - The Rule Girard Actually Wrote|← The Rule Girard Actually Wrote]] · [[S1E13 - What Girard Says the Gospels Do|What Girard Says the Gospels Do →]]

---
## The stake

You have read myths your whole life as stories about gods and monsters. This
episode reads one as a press release written by the people who won. **Once the
reversal is shown to you, you will not be able to unsee it, and it works on
modern stories too**, which is what the incident report in this episode is for.

## Collecting episode 5, and the whole episode falls out of it

Episode 5 established two things that sit uncomfortably together.

**One:** the mechanism is invisible from inside it. It only works because the
community cannot see what it is doing.

**Two:** the community afterwards tells stories about what happened.

> **So a myth is what that invisibility looks like written down.**

That is the organizing sentence of this episode. Not "myths are lies." Not "myths
are primitive." **Myth is an accurate report by people who could not see the one
thing that mattered.**

## The character

**The Usual Suspects.**

The story you are told for two hours is coherent, detailed, internally consistent,
and **assembled by the person with the most to gain from you believing it.** It
is only visible as a construction in the last ninety seconds, when you see where
the details came from.

That is the shape of the claim, and the reason the film works is the reason the
claim is hard to test: **from inside the telling, a constructed account and a
true one look identical.**

**Second anchor: the Death Note ending, from the point of view of ordinary
people.** Somebody dangerous is gone. Order returns. The account that circulates
afterwards is not a lie anyone told. **It is the only account available to people
who were not in the room.**

## The teaching device: run the causation both ways

**This is the thing to put on screen**, because it converts the claim from a
mood into something you can apply to any story in ten seconds.

**What the myth says happened:**

```
he committed the crime  ->  so we expelled him  ->  so the plague lifted
```

**What Girard says happened:**

```
the crisis  ->  we needed somebody  ->  we expelled him  ->  the crisis ended
                                                                    |
                                     so he must have caused it  <----+
```

**Same events. Same order in time. Opposite direction of causation.**

And notice the crucial detail: **the accusation arrives last** in Girard's
version, even though it appears first in the telling. The community is not lying
about the sequence. It is **explaining a real correlation with the only
explanation available to it.**

> That is the whole of Girard's reading of myth in one diagram, and you can hand
> the audience the diagram and let them run it on any story they like.

## What that predicts about the shape of myths

A claim about what a text **omits** is a claim you can go and check.

| what the myth contains | what it leaves out |
|---|---|
| the crisis, in vivid detail | that the crisis was ordinary rivalry, spreading |
| the crime, as established fact | any doubt at all about whether he did it |
| the marks, as the explanation | that the marks were selected afterwards |
| the violence, as necessary | that the violence was arbitrary |
| the victim becoming a god | that the community killed him |

**And the omission Girard cares most about: nobody in a myth ever suggests the
victim might be innocent.**

## The universal negative, which is the sharpest thing he ever wrote

*I See Satan Fall Like Lightning*, page 3. **Read it aloud twice.**

> "not merely the innocence of the victims versus their guilt, but the fact
> that, in mythology, **no one ever questions this guilt**. In the Gospels, the
> revealing account of scapegoating emanates not from the unanimous crowd but
> from a dissenting few. Initially, Jesus' disciples almost surrender to the
> mimetic power of the many, but on the third day, thanks to the Resurrection,
> they secede from the deluded mob and proclaim the innocence of their Lord.
> **In mythology no dissenting voice is ever heard.**"

**Now point at the load-bearing half**, because it is routinely misread and the
misreading wasted four rounds of this project.

**The differentia is NOT "the victims are innocent."** Girard rules that out
himself, **in the same sentence**: *not merely the innocence of the victims versus
their guilt.*

**The differentia is "no one ever questions."**

> It is a claim about **the crowd's verdict**, not about **the victim's
> character.** Those are two different things and everything in Seasons 3 and 4
> turns on the difference.

## Why this shape is a gift to anyone trying to test it

> A **universal negative** is the most falsifiable shape a claim can take. *This
> never happens, anywhere, in any of them.* **One genuine counterexample kills
> it.**

Compare that with almost everything else in Girard, which is a claim about
tendencies and readings and cannot be cornered. **This one has an edge you can
walk up to.**

That is why Season 3 builds an entire instrument aimed at this single sentence,
and why the failure of that instrument is worth four episodes.

## The number to plant, and it is the best self-catch in the project

When the project finally built a word-list for that sentence, the list split into
two halves.

**One half about the victim being innocent:** innocent, guiltless, no fault,
falsely accused, done nothing.

**One half about the crowd being divided:** division, some said, others said, not
all, spare him.

**Across four rounds of testing, the innocence half carried nothing at all and
the division half carried everything.**

> **And Girard's own sentence, quoted above, says innocence is not the
> differentia.** The instrument was built mostly out of innocence vocabulary
> anyway.

**That detonates in Season 7 episode 4**, and it is the strongest self-catch in
the whole project: **the word-list contradicted the sentence it was built from,
and nobody noticed for four rounds.**

## The clinical anchor

**The incident report written by the team involved.**

It is coherent. It is complete. Every fact in it is true, the timeline is right,
and it explains how the outcome followed from the circumstances. **It is also
written by the people whose account it exonerates**, and it is the document that
enters the record.

Nobody lied. **The account is simply told from the only position available to the
people telling it**, which is the position that cannot see itself acting.

## What would count against this

1. **A myth containing a sustained dissenting voice.** One is enough. The claim is
   universal.
2. **Myths that describe the violence as arbitrary**, in their own voice, without
   a later author's irony. Girard needs the account to be from inside.
3. **The causal reversal failing to fit.** If myths routinely establish the crime
   independently of the crisis, with evidence, the diagram is wrong.

> Number 1 is the one Season 3 goes after, and **what happens is stranger than
> either passing or failing.**
