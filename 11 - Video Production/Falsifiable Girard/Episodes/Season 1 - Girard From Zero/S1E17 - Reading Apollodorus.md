# Reading Apollodorus

[[S1E16 - Reading Oedipus the King|← Reading Oedipus the King]] · [[S1E18 - Reading the Odyssey, the Control|Reading the Odyssey, the Control →]]

---
## The stake

Myth with the storytelling stripped out. If the stereotypes survive that
stripping, they were never a style, they were a structure. **This episode is the
closest thing the season has to an experiment**, and its result is genuinely
ambiguous, which is the most honest thing about it.

## Collecting episodes 15 and 16

Two masterpieces. Two texts where every word is doing work, where the poetry and
the structure and the irony are inseparable from the content.

**This episode reads the opposite of that**, and the opposite turns out to be a
natural experiment nobody set up on purpose.

## What it is

The *Bibliotheca*. A systematic prose summary of **the whole of Greek myth**,
compiled around the first or second century AD. **Frazer's translation**, which is
the one `get_apollodorus.py` pulled off Theoi and the one every word count in this
episode was taken from.

Not a story. **A catalogue of stories.**

Everything is in it: the theogony, the houses of Argos and Thebes and Athens, the
labours, the war, the returns. All of it, at speed, one after another, with no
description, no scene-setting, no dialogue worth the name, no digression, and no
opinion.

## Read a page aloud and let the audience hear the speed

A character is born, marries, kills somebody, is transformed, and dies **in four
lines.** Then the next one. Then the next.

> **Every sentence is an event.** There is no connective tissue, because
> connective tissue is what a summary removes.

## The character anchor

**A Wikipedia plot summary.**

Everybody has read one. In episode nine, X kills Y; Z discovers the betrayal and
leaves. Same events, same order, same characters, **and none of the thing you
actually watched it for.**

Now use it as a test, because that is what this episode is going to do with
Apollodorus. **Take something you love and read its plot summary.** Whatever
survives the summary was in the events. Whatever evaporates was in the telling, and
you have just separated the two with no theory and no instrument.

**The *Bibliotheca* is Greek myth's plot summary**, written eighteen centuries
before anybody thought of doing that on purpose.

## And here is why this text is worth an episode

**Apollodorus is what myth looks like with the storytelling taken out.**

Same events. Same characters. Same crimes and killings and expulsions. **The plot
without the telling.**

Which sets up a question the corpus can actually answer:

> **Are Girard's four stereotypes in the EVENTS, or in the TELLING?**
> 
> If they are in the events, they should survive the removal of the poetry. **If
> they are an artifact of tragic rhetoric, they should vanish here.**

Nobody designed that experiment. It is sitting in the corpus by accident, which is
worth saying out loud, because a natural experiment is the most valuable thing a
corpus can contain and **you only ever get one by noticing it.** Nobody could have
funded this. Nobody could have commissioned a poet to write a version of Greek myth
with the poetry removed. **Somebody in the first century did it for reasons of his
own, and the control group has been sitting on a shelf ever since.**

## The answer, and it cuts two ways

Mythography scores **13.95.** Tragedy scores **13.88.**

**A dead heat.** Strip out the poetry, the meter, the irony, the choral odes and
the characterisation, and the stereotype vocabulary is **exactly as dense.**

**Reading one, and this is the one the project took.** It is a **confound.** A
rate per thousand words rewards plot per word, and Apollodorus is nothing but
plot per word, so it scores like a tragedy without being one. Here is the
measurement that supports it, taken much later with a **neutral** event-word list
containing nothing Girardian:

| genre | neutral event-words per 1000 |
|---|---|
| **MYTHOGRAPHY** | **28.65** |
| EPIC | 22.68 |
| OVID | 21.73 |
| TRAGEDY | 21.45 |
| CONTROL | 20.77 |

**It is the densest narrative prose in the corpus by a wide margin**, and that is
a real finding.

**Reading two, which the project never made, and it is worth saying on camera.**

The stereotypes **survived the removal of the telling.** If they were a feature of
tragic rhetoric, a mood produced by choral lament and heightened diction, they
should have thinned out in a bare summary and they did not.

> **That is arguably evidence FOR Girard**, and nowhere in eight rounds does
> anybody say so. The tie was recorded as a problem. **It can also be read as the
> stereotypes being structural rather than stylistic**, which is exactly what the
> theory claims.

Say both. **Do not pick.** And note out loud that the project only ever wrote down
the unflattering reading, which is its own kind of bias and worth noticing in a
series about bias.

**And notice which direction that bias runs**, because it is not the direction
anybody warns you about. The whole apparatus of pre-registration exists to stop a
believer flattering his own theory. Here the believer had a result that favoured
him sitting in front of him, and filed it as a confound, and never wrote the other
sentence down.

> That is not a virtue. **A researcher who systematically records one reading of
> his ambiguous results is biased whichever way he leans**, and being biased
> against yourself is still a way of not looking.

## The clinical anchor

**A discharge summary against the full notes.**

Every diagnosis is in the summary. Every drug, every procedure, every date.
**What is not in it is the reasoning.** The two days somebody was unsure, the
differential that was considered and dropped, the conversation that changed the
plan.

Read a hundred summaries and you will know what happened to a hundred patients and
almost nothing about how medicine is practised. **The events survive the
compression. The thinking does not**, and that is the experiment sitting in this
corpus by accident.

## And the twist that stops either reading being comfortable

Across all 73 texts, the correlation between event density and the Girard score is
**negative.** r = −0.243, p = 0.040.

**So density does not generally inflate the score. It deflates it.** Epic is dense
and scores low.

> The confound is real **for mythography specifically** and **unproven as a
> general mechanism.** Say plainly that the explanation does not exist. **This is
> an open problem in the project, not a solved one**, and pretending otherwise
> would be the exact thing this series is about.

## What would count against this reading

1. **The tie being too small to interpret in either direction.** It is 0.07, and
   **Season 3 episode 8 shows it sits inside a 17% noise floor.** Both readings
   offered above may be explanations of nothing, and that episode is where this one
   gets taken apart.
2. **Density explaining it entirely.** Measured, real, and it would make the
   survival of the stereotypes an artifact rather than evidence.
3. **The corpus imbalance.** Mythography is 8 files against epic's 36, for reasons
   Season 2 episode 4 shows have nothing to do with antiquity. **Nobody has
   reweighted and re-run.**
