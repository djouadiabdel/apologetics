## Collecting episodes 6 to 9

Four episodes, four stereotypes, four numbers. Crisis at 2.07 per thousand and 60%
one word. Crimes at 0.95, the rarest and the best built. Marks at 2.90 and 44%
`king`. Violence at 4.76, the commonest, with the unanimity phrases hidden inside
it.

**Say what kind of average those four are, because a viewer who goes and checks
will get different numbers.** Each is the **mean of the per-text rates** across
the 76 texts: score every text per thousand words, then average the 76 scores.
Pool the hits instead, dividing total hits by total words, and `crimes` comes out
at 1.02 and `marks` at 3.10. Both are defensible. **Only one is what these
figures are**, and the pooled version weights the long texts more heavily, which
is exactly what a corpus containing the *Odyssey* and a two-page fable should not
do silently.

**That is a list.** This episode is about the one word that turns a list into a
theory, and it introduces no new content at all.

## Take the four apart and they are worth nothing

Say this plainly, before a critic says it for you.

**Violent stories contain violence.** Not a finding. A tautology.

**Stories about kings contain the word king.** Also not a finding.

**Tragedies contain death.** Tragedies are about people dying.

**Myths contain accusations.** Myths are about people being accused.

> Any one of the four, found alone, in a text of the relevant genre, proves
> **nothing at all.** You could build a word-list for almost any theory and find
> it in Greek literature, because Greek literature is enormous and about extreme
> things.

## Three levels of claim, and only one of them is worth testing

This is the spine of the episode. **Draw it as a ladder.**

**Level 1. Each vocabulary appears in these texts.**
Trivially true. Nobody needs an instrument.

**Level 2. All four appear in the same text.**
This sounds much stronger. **It is almost as trivial, and here is the number
that proves it.**

When the instrument is finally built and run on 72 Greek and Roman texts, the
score for the weakest of the four buckets is **above zero in 90% of them.**

> **All four stereotypes are present in nine out of ten texts in the corpus.**
> Including the ones that are not about persecution at all. **Level 2 is nearly
> worthless as evidence**, and knowing that now is what stops you being impressed
> by it later.

**Level 3. All four converge on the same passage, more than chance would put
them there.**

**That is the claim**, and it is what "four faces of one event" means if it means
anything. One process, one place, one moment: the crisis, the accusation, the
marks, the killing.

> **The difference between level 2 and level 3 is the difference between a word
> list and a theory.** A word list says these terms show up in these books. A
> theory says they show up **in the same place, because one thing caused all
> four.**

## And here is what the season is really telling you

The project measured level 3 **four separate times, with four different
instruments, and failed every time.**

Not because the Gospels came out wrong. Not because the corpus was bad. **Because
the convergence was not there to find, or the instruments could not see it, and
after four attempts nobody can say which.**

Say that here. **It is not a spoiler, it is the reason to keep watching**, and it
is more honest than pretending the series has a triumphant ending.

## The clinical anchor, and it is not a metaphor

A raised white cell count means nothing on its own. Fifty causes.

A fever means nothing on its own. A hundred causes.

A raised lactate means nothing on its own.

**Sepsis is the claim that they arrive together, because one process produces all
of them.** That is what a syndrome is. It is a stronger claim than a symptom and
correspondingly harder to demonstrate.

**And you already know the objections to a syndrome**, which is why this anchor
is worth the time:

- *your three findings are common in sick patients generally*
- *you have defined a cluster that would appear by chance in any intensive care
  unit*
- *your criteria were built by looking at patients who already had the diagnosis*

> **Every one of those has an exact counterpart in this series.** The first is
> level 2 above. The second is what a null test is for, and it is Season 3
> episode 11. The third is why the word-list had to be written before the corpus
> existed, which is Season 2 episode 2.

## The character anchor

**A relay team.**

Nobody thinks the fastest individual leg tells you about the team. The claim
"this team is good" is a claim about the **weakest** leg, because the baton has to
go all the way round.

That is exactly the difference between **adding four numbers** and **taking the
smallest of them**, and it is the choice Season 2 episode 12 makes and the choice
Season 3 episode 2 destroys.

## One number to plant, and it is the strangest in the season

If the four stereotypes are four faces of one event, they should **rise and fall
together** across texts. A text high on one should tend to be high on the others.

**Season 3 measures the correlations between the four buckets across 76 texts.**

They come out between **0.13 and 0.38.** Crisis against marks is **0.13**, which
is close to no relationship at all.

> **Work out before Season 3 whether that helps Girard or hurts him**, because it
> genuinely cuts both ways and the episode will not tell you.
> 
> Low correlation means the four are **not redundant**. They are genuinely four
> different measurements rather than four names for one thing, which is what the
> theory needs. **And** it means they do not co-occur strongly, which is what the
> theory predicts they should.

## What would count against this

1. **Level 3 holding for texts that are obviously not persecution texts.** Then
   convergence is a property of narrative, not of the mechanism.
2. **The four buckets turning out to be nearly perfectly correlated.** Then they
   are one measurement with four names and the conjunction claim is empty.
3. **Level 3 failing everywhere.** Which is what happens, four times, and the
   honest question then becomes whether the theory is wrong or the instrument is
   blind. **Season 3 episode 19 leaves that open and so should you.**
