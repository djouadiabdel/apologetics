## The stake

Thirteen episodes without an objection is recruitment, and this episode exists to
break that. **You are being handed the other side's best case before the readings
start**, so that when you go into the texts you are reading with both cases in
your head instead of one.

## Collecting thirteen episodes, and noticing what is missing from them

You have now had thirteen episodes of one man's account of human violence, presented
by someone who finds it persuasive, with no opposition anywhere in it.

> **Thirteen episodes of a theory with no opposition is not teaching. It is
> recruitment**, and the audience should be told that this episode exists for
> that reason.

It comes **before** the readings on purpose. Meet the objections, then go and
look at the texts, so that you are reading with both cases in your head.

## The clinical anchor, and it is the whole episode

**A differential diagnosis.**

You do not confirm a diagnosis. You **list the alternatives and rule them out**,
and the list goes on the board before the treatment starts. A clinician who
considers one diagnosis and finds evidence for it has not diagnosed anything.
They have pattern-matched and then collected confirmations.

> **Everything in episodes 1 to 13 is evidence FOR one hypothesis.** Not one
> minute of it was spent on what else could produce the same pattern. **This
> episode is the differential.**

**The character version: House at the whiteboard.** The list goes up first. The
whole ritual of that show is that naming the alternatives is the work, and the
answer is worthless if the list was never written.

## Objection 1 — the readings are selective

The commonest and the hardest.

**The charge:** Girard picks the myths that fit. Greek myth is enormous. Choose
forty stories out of thousands and you can demonstrate anything, and nothing in
the method tells you which stories were rejected or why.

**Why it is serious:** it is unanswerable from inside the reading. Producing
another good reading is another selected example. You cannot escape a selection
problem by selecting more.

> **This is the objection the entire project exists to answer**, and it is why
> the text tiers were written down and committed before any scoring. Say so here,
> and then let Seasons 3 and 4 show how badly it went.

## Objection 2 — the founding murder has no evidence

**The charge:** the account requires that real communities really did kill real
victims and really did found their institutions on it, in prehistory. There is no
archaeological or ethnographic record of this happening.

**The strongest form**, and state it at its strongest: it is not that the evidence
is thin. It is that the claim is **structured so evidence is impossible**, because
the mechanism only works while nobody can see it, so any record would necessarily
be a record of the concealment.

> Notice that this is the same shape as the unfalsifiability charge, and notice
> where it lands: **squarely on the part of Girard that nothing in this project
> tests.** Episodes 1 to 5 are exactly that part. **Be honest about it here.**

## Objection 3 — the account of sacrifice is contested by people who study sacrifice

**The charge:** scholars of actual sacrificial systems, including biblical and
rabbinic sacrifice, argue that Girard's picture of sacrifice as disguised
scapegoating does not match how those systems describe themselves or how they
worked. Sacrifice in the Hebrew Bible is largely a system of gift, thanksgiving
and purification, and the scapegoat rite of Leviticus 16 is **one rite among
many** rather than the key to all of them.

**Mark this K.** The names to check are the scholars of ancient Judaism who have
written directly against Girard. **Fetch them, quote them accurately, or drop the
episode.**

## Objection 4 — the theological one, and it cuts from inside

**The charge, from Christians:** if the Cross is primarily **the unveiling of a
mechanism**, then Girard has turned salvation into a sociological demonstration.
The Cross reveals something rather than doing something.

**Why it belongs here** rather than in a theology series: it is a live dispute and
a Christian viewer will already be feeling it by episode 13. **Say it before they
do.**

## Objection 5 — the circularity, at its sharpest

Put the four stereotypes next to the theory's own escape route:

- a myth **with** all four stereotypes: the mechanism is visible
- a myth **with none of them**: the mechanism is perfectly concealed, **which is
  what myth does**

> **If both outcomes confirm, the theory has predicted nothing.** That is the
> objection in its most brutal form and it is not a strawman.

**The only answer is the one this project tried:** commit in advance to which
texts should score high and which low, and then **lose** if the controls win.
Season 3 episode 1 is what happened.

## The honest position to hold on camera

**You are not going to settle these. Say that.**

The series does one narrow thing: it takes **objection 1 and objection 5**
seriously enough to build something that could have said no, and reports what it
said.

> **Objections 2, 3 and 4 are untouched by anything in the next six seasons**,
> and a viewer who leaves thinking they were answered has been misled.

## What would count against this episode

Because an episode listing objections should be checkable like any other.

1. **An objection stronger than these five that is not on the list.** Very likely.
   **This is one person's reading of the critical literature, not a survey**, and
   nobody has gone through the Girardian journals to check what the standard
   replies are or whether they succeed.
2. **One of the five being a strawman.** Objection 2 is the one at risk: "no
   evidence for the founding murder" is stated here in its strong form, and Girard
   has a reply about the kind of evidence a prehistoric event could leave. **The
   reply is not represented here.**
3. **The objections turning out to be answered by the instrument.** They are not.
   **Nothing in six seasons touches objections 3 or 4 at all**, and the project
   only ever addresses objection 1 and half of objection 5.

> Say that last one out loud. **Two of the five objections in this episode are
> untouched by everything that follows**, and a viewer who watches the whole
> series should not leave thinking they were answered.
