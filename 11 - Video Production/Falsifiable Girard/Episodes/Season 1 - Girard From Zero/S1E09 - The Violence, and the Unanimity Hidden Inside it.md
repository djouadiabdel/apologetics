# The Violence, and the Unanimity Hidden Inside it

## The stake

The last stereotype is the crowd itself, and it carries the tell you can test
anywhere: nobody dissents. This episode shows you what unanimity looks like from
the inside, which is warmth. **You have been inside that warmth. The episode
gives you the tool for noticing it next time.

## Collecting episode 8

Three stereotypes down, and all three have been about **one person**: what is
happening to the community, what he is accused of, why he was chosen.

**This one is about everybody else.**

And collect the distinction from episode 7, because this is the bucket it protects

## Three components, and the third is the series

**The crowd itself.** Crowd, mob, throng, multitude, rabble.

**What it does.** Stones, expels, banishes, drives out, hunts, tears apart.

**Its unanimity.** With one voice. One accord. As one man. The whole city.

## And the third component is the rest of the series

**The unanimity is not a detail. It is the load-bearing part**, and here is why
that is a design decision worth explaining on camera.

When you look at the word-list in Season 2, you will find phrases in the
**violence** bucket that are not violence words at all: `with one voice`,
`one accord`, `as one man`, `the whole city`.

> **Those are unanimity words, and they are in the violence bucket because for
> Girard the unanimity is what makes the violence work.** Violence without
> unanimity is just a fight. Violence with unanimity is a mechanism.

And Girard's entire distinction between myth and Gospel, the claim that Seasons 3
and 4 spend eleven episodes failing to measure, is **only about whether that
unanimity holds.** Not whether there is violence. Not whether the victim is
innocent. **Whether anybody breaks ranks.**

Which means this bucket is doing two jobs under one name, and **it will be split
apart in Season 3** into a fifth bucket built entirely out of the crowd coming
apart. Everything the project eventually finds, and it is not much, lives in that
split.

And be clear about what kind of mistake the merger is, because it is not a
mislabelling. **The two things have completely different frequencies.** Violence
words are everywhere in Greek literature; unanimity phrases are rare and turn up
only when a text is describing a group acting as one. Put a rare, decisive signal
into the same bucket as a common, uninformative one and **the total is the common
one**, to within a rounding error, forever.

> Nobody chose that. It follows from addition, and it was locked in the day
> somebody decided which bucket `with one voice` belonged to.

## The character anchor

**"I am Spartacus."**

Everybody knows the scene and almost nobody notices what it is. A crowd is ordered
to identify one man. **The mechanism requires exactly one thing from them, which
is that they point**, and instead every man claims to be him.

Nothing about that scene is a rescue. It does not save Spartacus and the men know
it will not. **What it destroys is the unanimity**, and the reason it is one of
the most famous moments in cinema is that an audience feels, without being told,
that something enormous has been broken.

> **Hold that scene for four seasons.** It is the negative image of everything
> this instrument is built to find, and the whole of Season 3's dissent lexicon is
> an attempt to detect it in Greek.

## The clinical anchor

**Quorum sensing.**

Bacteria that individually do nothing at all. Each one releases a signal molecule,
each one measures how much of that signal is around it, and **below a threshold
every single cell stays inert.** Cross the threshold and they all switch on
together, and the population starts behaving as one organism.

The individual bacterium has not changed. **What changed is that it detected
agreement**, and agreement is the trigger rather than the accompaniment.

That is Girard's claim about crowds, in a system with no minds in it: the
destructive behaviour is not the sum of individual intentions, **it is switched on
by unanimity itself.**

> And that is the trap. **The healthiest bucket measures the thing Girard cares
> least about.** He is not claiming that persecution texts are violent. Everything
> in Greek literature is violent. **He is claiming something about the unanimity**,
> and the unanimity is a handful of phrases hiding inside the one bucket nobody
> would think to look in.

## The translation problem, planted here because it lands in Season 3

**The unanimity signal is phrases, and phrases are the first thing a translator
changes.**

`With one voice`, `one accord`, `as one man`, `the whole city`. Every one of those
is an idiom, and a translator rendering the same Greek as "everyone shouted" has
preserved the meaning perfectly and **destroyed the measurement completely.**

Compare that with the rest of the instrument. `Blood`, `king`, `death`, `murder`
are single common nouns, and any competent translator will use them because there
is nothing else to use. **The buckets built out of ordinary words are robust to
translation. The one carrying Girard's actual claim is not.**

> So the most theoretically important signal in the instrument is also the most
> fragile one, and **it is fragile in a way no amount of statistics can repair**,
> because the information was lost before the corpus existed.

**Season 3 episode 5 measures this**, once, on one paired play, and finds two
translators differing by 17.2% on total rate. Nobody has ever measured it on the
unanimity phrases specifically, which is where it would bite hardest.

## What would count against this

1. **Unanimity language turning out to be equally common in texts with no
   collective violence in them.** Then it is a rhetorical register, not a
   fingerprint. **Season 3 tests exactly this and the answer is not clean.**
2. **The unanimity phrases being too rare to measure.** They are phrases rather
   than words, in a corpus of translations, and a translator who writes "everyone
   shouted" instead of "with one voice" has erased the signal without changing the
   meaning. **This is the objection the project takes least seriously and it may
   be the strongest.**
3. **Putting unanimity in the violence bucket being simply wrong.** It merges a
   frequent, uninformative thing with a rare, decisive one, and the rare one
   disappears into the total. **Season 3 splits them out and everything the project
   finds comes from the split**, which is an argument that they should never have
   been together.
