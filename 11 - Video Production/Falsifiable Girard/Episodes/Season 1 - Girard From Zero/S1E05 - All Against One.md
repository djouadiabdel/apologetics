# All Against One

[[S1E04 - When Everyone Wants the Same Thing|← When Everyone Wants the Same Thing]] · [[S1E06 - The Crisis, and the Word that Swallows it|The Crisis, and the Word that Swallows it →]]

## The stake

This is the mechanism the whole project tests, and you have lived it at small
scale: a group under pressure finding one person whose expulsion restores the
peace. The family, the class group, the workplace. **This episode gives you the
shape of that moment, and the reason the peace it buys is real.

## Collecting four episodes, because this one is the payoff

**Episode 3** ended on the **monstrous double**: two rivals who have become
interchangeable, each experiencing the other as a thing wearing his face, and
reaching for inhuman language to describe him.

**Episode 4** ended on a crisis with **no internal brake**. Everyone against
everyone, a feedback loop that only points one way, and no mechanism inside the
system for stopping.

**This episode does one thing: it takes the monstrous double and gives it to an
entire community, pointed at one person.**

## The character

**Zuko's banishment.**

A room full of people. One person is marked, and the mark is on his face for the
rest of the series. The room's tension resolves onto him, and the room goes back
to functioning.

And here is the part to watch. **Nobody in that room afterwards describes it as a
mob.** They describe it as **justice**, and they are not lying.

**The harder and better anchor: any school you were ever in.** The mechanism does
not require a monster. It requires a room, a tension, and one person who can be
selected without anyone retaliating on their behalf.

## The claim, which is the famous one

The crisis of episode 4 has no internal brake. What ends it, on Girard's account,
is a **switch**: all against everyone becomes **all against one.**

The community selects a victim. Not for a reason that would survive inspection.
**For availability.** The violence that was flying in every direction goes in one
direction, is discharged, and stops.

## Stop here, because this is the weakest joint in the whole theory

**Do not smooth this over on camera.**

Everything up to this point has a mechanism you can watch operating. Copied
desire produces rivalry. Rivalry produces doubles. Doubles multiplied produce a
crisis. Each step follows from the one before and you can see why.

**And then: "and then everyone turns on one person."**

*Why one?* Why does all-against-all become all-against-one rather than continuing,
or burning itself out, or fracturing into factions that stabilise?

Girard's answer is that **the mob's imitation converges the way everything else
in the system converges.** A crowd copying each other's hostility will land on
the same target for the same reason a crowd copying each other's desire lands on
the same object. **The hostility is mimetic too.**

That is coherent. It uses no new machinery. **It is also the one step in the
sequence with no independent evidence behind it.**

> **A critic who wants to break Girard should attack here, not at the
> stereotypes.** The stereotypes are testable and this project spends eight
> rounds testing them. **The switch is not testable by anything in this series**,
> and it is the load-bearing joint between the psychology and the anthropology.

Say that out loud. It costs nothing, and it is what an honest teacher does.

## And then the part that makes this a theory about texts at all

This is the step people miss, and without it none of the rest of the series makes
sense.

**The peace afterwards is real.** The community genuinely feels better. The
violence has stopped. Whatever was tearing the place apart is no longer tearing
it apart.

So now run the community's own reasoning, from inside, with no bad faith
anywhere:

- before: chaos
- we removed him
- after: peace

> **He must have been the cause.** Look what happened when he was taken away.

**The mechanism produces its own justification, and it produces it after the
fact.** That is the sentence carrying this episode.

Nobody lied. Nobody conspired. The community observed a real correlation and drew
the only inference available to people standing where they were standing.

## Why the victim ends up sacred, which sounds strange and follows exactly

He caused the crisis, on their reading. **And he ended it.**

So he is simultaneously the worst thing that ever happened to this community and
the source of its order. That is not a contradiction they have to resolve. **It
is what a god looks like in the oldest layer of myth**: dangerous, set apart, and
the reason things work.

**Zuko again, and it is exact.** Banished as a disgrace, and the same story makes
him the one who restores the balance. **The story does not experience that as
odd.**

## The device: the mechanism as four states

**Draw it once and leave it up.** Everything in episodes 6 to 9 is a way of
detecting one of these four rows in a text.

| | the community | the victim |
|---|---|---|
| **1 crisis** | all against all, distinctions gone | not yet chosen |
| **2 selection** | needs one, not any one | acquires the marks retrospectively |
| **3 violence** | all against one, unanimous | killed or expelled |
| **4 peace** | distinctions return | becomes sacred, both curse and cure |

> **Row 4 is why nobody inside can see it.** The peace is real, it arrives on
> schedule, and it is the evidence the community uses to conclude that the victim
> was guilty. **The mechanism produces its own proof.**

## The clinical anchor

**A treatment given at the moment a self-limiting illness turns the corner.**

The patient recovers. The recovery is real. The timing is real. And the causal
story writes itself, and it will be repeated confidently for years, and there is
nothing dishonest anywhere in the chain.

> **That is what makes the mechanism hard to see from inside: the evidence for
> the wrong explanation is genuine.**

## Where this becomes countable

The four stereotypes in episodes 6 to 9 are Girard's list of **the traces this
mechanism leaves in a text**, and that list is the entire basis of the instrument.

**And here is the limit, said in this episode rather than later.**

Everything in episodes 1 to 5 is a claim about what happened in **real
communities, in prehistory, before writing.** Nothing in this project tests any
of it. No instrument can reach a mechanism that operated before there were texts.

> What the instrument tests is far narrower: whether the **residue** Girard says
> the mechanism leaves is detectable in texts, in the pattern he says it should
> be. **That is a smaller question, and it is the only one a corpus and a
> computer can ask.**

## What would count against this

1. **Crises that resolve without a victim**, routinely, in the historical and
   ethnographic record. Girard needs the victim to be the normal exit.
2. **Victims selected for properties that genuinely predict the crisis**, rather
   than for availability. If the selection turns out to be rational, the theory
   loses its point.
3. **Communities that do not sacralise the victim afterwards.** The double
   attribution, dangerous and saving, is the theory's fingerprint.

**None of the three is tested here.** Say it again.
