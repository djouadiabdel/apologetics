## Judas, and the correction that went the wrong way

Matthew has Judas hang himself. Acts has him fall headlong and burst open in a
field. Same man, same death.

This item was originally priced at **cost 4**, which is the top of the scale and
means no rescue exists on any channel. It was **revised down to 3.**

**The revision went against the story.** Cost 4 is the stronger position and the
sharper finding, and the study gave it up.

The reason is narrow. Cost 4 means **no** rescue exists on any channel. And one
does: you can say the rope broke, or the branch gave, and the body fell.

> **That rescue is worth nothing and it exists.** No text states it. It is
> invented to fit. And the scale has a level for exactly that, which is 3, so the
> item belongs at 3 and the sharper number was wrong.

The correction was propagated everywhere it appeared, including into the ledger
and the evidence file, **and the Bayes calculation gained a cost-3 case chosen
before the recomputation was run**, so the correction could not be tuned.

Two words there need unpacking.

**Bayes** is the arithmetic of updating a belief with evidence. You start with how
likely you thought something was, you observe something, and it moves you. The
project runs one to ask how much the whole ledger shifts the case, **and it is the
part of the study most sensitive to choices**, because you must supply the
starting number yourself.

Which is why the ordering matters. Lowering Judas from 4 to 3 changes an input to
that calculation. **The value the calculation would use for a cost-3 item was
fixed before the recomputation ran**, so it was impossible to pick the value that
made the correction painless. You correct the item, then you find out what it
costs, in that order, and not the reverse.

## Peter's accusers, and why the Greek closes it

The usual harmonisation says there were more challengers than any one gospel
reports, so Mark names one and Luke names another and both are true. That is an
A4 move, silence is not denial, and it is normally strong.

**It fails here on grammatical gender**, and this is a tier V finding, checked
against the Greek text rather than taken from a commentary.

The second challenger is gender-marked in all three. Mark has a **definite feminine**
article with the servant girl. Matthew has a **feminine** other. Luke has a
**masculine** other, and Peter answers him with a masculine **vocative**, "Man".

A vocative is simply **the form a word takes when you are addressing somebody
directly.** English barely has one, which is why the term is unfamiliar: we say
"Man!" the same way we say "a man". Greek marks it in the word itself, so **the
grammar records who is being spoken to**, not just who is being spoken about.

That is what makes this item close rather than arguable. Peter is not described as
answering a man. **He addresses one, and the form of the word he uses carries the
sex**, so the detail is in the grammar rather than in a narrator's summary that
could be loose.

So the texts do not merely name different people. **They mark different sexes for
the same challenge in the same sequence**, and multiplying challengers cannot
absorb it without adding an event.

## Why this is the layer's best moment

Because the check ran both ways and was recorded both ways.

Two other items were checked against the same Greek text and **cut in favour of
the defence.** One rescue that had been scored as dead turned out to be alive.
Another item narrowed to a smaller objection than the compilations claimed. And
one check **corrected an error the coder had made himself** about which case a
Greek noun was in.

> **A verification procedure that only ever confirms your side is not a
> verification procedure.** This one produced two findings for, two against, and
> one self-correction, and all five are in the same file.

The self-correction is the most useful of the five and it is worth saying what it
was. The coder had claimed a Greek noun was in one case when it is in another, and
the error was found by writing the check as a **script** rather than as a
sentence.

That is a transferable technique and it is the same lesson the toolchain in this
series keeps teaching. **A claim written as prose cannot be run.** A claim written
as code gets executed against the actual text, and it either produces the result
you said it would or it does not.

> Two **patristic** rescues were killed by the same script, and **writing them as
> code caught two further errors in the coder's own argument** on the way. A
> claim you can execute is a claim that can embarrass you, which is precisely
> what makes it worth more than a footnote.

**Patristic** means belonging to the church fathers, from the Latin for father. The
fathers are the Christian writers of roughly the first five centuries, the ones
who were arguing about these texts while the arguments were new: Origen,
Chrysostom, Augustine, Eusebius, Jerome. Episodes 18 to 20 grade them, and the
word turns up here because two of the rescues being scored in this layer turn out
to be theirs rather than modern.

> Which is itself a finding worth keeping. **Four of the rescues scored in this
> study are patristic**, so the common line that harmonisations were invented
> recently to plug holes is simply false, and it should stop being said.

## The clinical anchor

**The one abnormal result that changes management.**

Forty numbers come back on a screen and thirty-nine are noise you will never think
about again. One of them changes what you do tomorrow.

**And the skill is not finding abnormal numbers**, which is trivial, it is knowing
which ones are load-bearing. A study that reports all forty with equal emphasis has
not done the work, and a study that reports two out of fifty-one is claiming
exactly this kind of discrimination and had better be right about it.

## The character anchor

**A plot hole that actually breaks the story, against the hundred that do not.**

Every long-running show has continuity errors, and fans catalogue them by the
hundred, and almost none of them matter because the story does not rest on them.

**Then there is the one where a character knows something they could not know**,
and it matters because the plot turns on it. The difference is not how wrong the
detail is. It is **what depends on it**, which is why the study reports the core
separately.
