# An Argument that Has Never Been Tested

## The stake

The undesigned coincidences argument is two hundred years old and has never been
tested. **You will watch it get what every argument deserves: categories,
controls, and a stated ceiling**, before anyone is allowed to be impressed.

## Collecting episode 1

Three fenced layers, a control that Seasons 2 to 4 never had, and a ceiling
stated in advance: one coder, not blinded.

## The argument, which is old and genuinely clever

It is called **undesigned coincidences**, and it goes back to William Paley in
1790. The shape is this.

One gospel says something odd and unexplained. Another gospel, elsewhere, in
passing, for no apparent reason, supplies the fact that makes it make sense.
Neither text flags the connection.

**The worked example, and it is a good one.** John says Jesus asks *Philip* where
to buy bread for the crowd, which is a strange person to ask. Luke, in a
completely different passage, mentions that the feeding happened near
**Bethsaida**. John, elsewhere again, mentions that **Philip was from Bethsaida**.

> Three texts, no cross-reference, and the odd detail resolves. **The claim is
> that forgery does not produce this shape**, because a forger supplies the
> explanation where the puzzle is.

Work that last clause, because it is the argument's actual engine and it is
usually stated too fast.

If you are inventing a story, you write the explanation next to the puzzle. That
is what writing is. You do not leave an unmotivated detail in chapter six and
place its explanation in a different book by a different author, because you would
have no reason to and no way to coordinate it.

**What produces that shape is several people describing one world they all know
about, none of them writing for the others.** Each mentions what is relevant to
their own telling, and the details fit because the world was consistent, not
because anybody made them fit.

> **So the claim is not "this is too clever to be invented."** It is narrower and
> better: **invention has a characteristic shape, and this is a different
> shape.** Which is a testable claim the moment you have some invented texts to
> compare against, and episode 5 goes and gets some.

## Why it has never been an argument you could lose

Here is the problem, and it is the reason this study exists.

**Nobody has ever counted the ones that do not work.**

The literature is a collection of the successes. Paley found some, Blunt found
more, McGrew found more again, and each is presented as a case. **There is no
denominator and there is no control.**

| what the literature has | what it does not have |
|---|---|
| a list of coincidences that resolve | how many gaps were checked |
| each one argued in detail | how many gaps failed to resolve |
| the strongest cases | **whether legends do this too** |

**A list of hits with no misses and no comparison is not evidence.** It is a
scrapbook, and you can build one for any text long enough to have odd details in
it.

That last sentence is testable and you should test it, because it decides how much
of this argument survives.

Take any long novel with more than one book in the series. Find an unexplained
detail. Then go looking, anywhere in the other books, for something that would
explain it. **You will find something**, because long texts contain enormous
numbers of details and any two details can be connected by a reader willing to
connect them.

The question the apologetic literature never asks is how often that happens **by
chance in a text nobody claims is reporting anything.** Without that rate, a
handful of coincidences in four gospels is a number with nothing to compare it to.

> **The undesigned-coincidence argument may well be right.** Nothing here says it
> is wrong. It says that as currently presented it is **unfalsifiable**, because
> no possible finding could count against it, and a version that could come back
> negative has never been built.

Which is this series' own sentence, arriving in the apologetic literature rather
than in a research repository.

> **A check that cannot fail prints reassurance.**

Two hundred years of collected coincidences is reassurance, printed steadily, by a
procedure that has never once returned a negative because **nobody built the thing
that could return one.** That is the same defect Season 2 found in a lexicon and
Season 3 found in a null test, and it is sitting in an argument that predates both
by two centuries.

## What turning it into an instrument requires

Three things, and each one is a whole episode later.

1. **A denominator.** Every cross-checkable gap, counted, whether or not it
   resolves.
2. **A control.** The same procedure run on texts everybody agrees are legendary.
3. **A rule that stops the coder supplying the answer.** A fit counts only if a
   *text* provides the reconciling fact.

> **The third one is the sharpest, and it is where this study departs from the
> tradition it is testing.** In the apologetic literature the connection is made
> by the apologist. Here, if the coder has to supply it, it is not a fit.

## The device: the same gap, scored two ways

| | John asks Philip | scored as |
|---|---|---|
| the coder knows Philip is from Bethsaida and says so | supplied by the coder | **not a fit** |
| John 1:44 states Philip is from Bethsaida, on the page | supplied by a text | **FIT** |

**The finding is identical. The provenance is not**, and provenance is the only
thing that separates evidence from a good reader.

## The clinical anchor

**Corroborating a history from two relatives interviewed separately.**

The daughter mentions a fall three weeks ago. The son, asked about something
else, mentions the new blood pressure tablet started a month ago. **Neither of
them connected those and you did**, and the connection is real.

Now notice the discipline that makes it evidence rather than a story: **you write
down what each person actually said, separately, before you join them.** A note
that records only your synthesis has destroyed the thing that made it convincing.

## The character anchor

**A continuity error that turns out not to be one.**

A character has a scar in episode 9 and no scar in episode 4, and the internet
calls it a blunder. Then somebody points out that episode 6, in a throwaway line
nobody cared about, has him mention a fight.

**Nobody wrote that as a payoff.** It resolves because the writers were tracking
one world. And the reason this is the right anchor is that **fandoms also produce
hundreds of these that do not resolve**, and nobody makes a video about those,
which is precisely the missing denominator.

## What would count against this

1. **Undesigned coincidences being explicable by literary dependence.** If Luke
   read John, the coincidence is a borrowed detail. **The study handles this by
   tagging independence**, and episode 6 reports that all five canonical fits are
   between independent texts.
2. **The whole argument proving less than it seems.** Even at its best it shows
   the texts behave like independent reports of something. **It does not show
   what.** A shared legend, independently recorded, produces the same shape.
3. **A denominator being impossible in practice.** It has not been built, and
   episode 17 treats that as the honest ceiling on the whole layer.

## Handing off

**Episode 3 builds the classification**, four categories, and the interesting one
is the category that exists to stop the coder helping.

## Questions

1. Build an undesigned coincidence for a novel you know well. **How hard was
   it?** That difficulty is the argument's real strength or its real weakness.
2. Why is "a shared legend, independently recorded" not excluded by any result in
   this layer?
3. **Before episode 3:** how would you count a gap that neither resolves nor
   collides? It is the most common case and it decides the denominator.
