# The Falsifier Fires on the Person Running it

[[S5E14 - Two|← Two]] · [[S5E16 - The Two that Survive, and a Correction Against the Story|The Two that Survive, and a Correction Against the Story →]]

---
## The stake

The falsifier fires at 67.6% against a 40% ceiling, and it binds the coder, not
the instrument. **An instrument you can fix, a coder you cannot**, and this is
the episode where the season's real ceiling becomes visible.

## Collecting episode 14

51 real clashes, 2 at cost 3, 0 at cost 4, and a doctrine gone. All of it resting
on 102 rejections made by one person applying nine gates.

## The falsifier, written before any item was scored

> **F4.** If A2 plus A5 rejections exceed **40%** of all rejections, the
> dissolution is resting on my own sense-distinctions rather than on the texts,
> and Number A must be reported as unreliable.

A2 is "not the same respect". A5 is "tension, not negation". Episode 11 flagged
both as the gates where almost anything can be called a difference of sense, and
the rulebook set a ceiling on how much of the work they were allowed to do.

## It fired

**A2 plus A5 are 67.6% of all rejections**, against a ceiling of 40%.

Sixty-two entries dissolved by saying **"not the same respect"**. Seven more by
saying **"tension, not negation."** Two thirds of the dissolution rests on the two
gates the author himself named as the abusable ones.

And so the results file opens like this:

> **The honest first sentence of this study is not a number about the New
> Testament. It is that I dissolved 62 entries by saying "not the same respect",
> and I pre-committed to treating that volume as disqualifying. It is
> disqualifying.**

## What that costs, exactly

The dissolution rate is reported as **unreliable**. The number 51 cannot be quoted
as an estimate of how many real clashes exist. **The gate calls are not trustworthy
in aggregate**, and the person saying so is the person who made them.

Nothing in this season is more important than that paragraph existing, and nothing
in this season is easier to have quietly not written.

Think about how invisible the alternative would be. **F4 is a rule the author
wrote, in a file the author controls, about a threshold the author chose.** No
reviewer set it, no institution enforces it, and nobody outside the project would
ever have computed the ratio.

He could have reported 51 accepted items and two hard contradictions, with a
limitations paragraph noting that some rejections involved judgement, and every
sentence would have been true. **The A2 and A5 counts would simply not have been
added up.**

> **The only thing that made F4 fire was somebody doing the arithmetic on his own
> gates and then publishing the answer.** That is what a falsifier is: not a
> clever test, just a number you promised in advance to compute and to say out
> loud whichever way it came.

## The survival argument, which you have to judge

Then the file argues that the **hard count** survives, and the argument is
structural rather than an assertion, which is the only kind that should be
accepted here.

**An A2 or A5 error puts an item back in play at cost 1 or 2, and structurally
cannot create a cost 3 or 4.**

Follow it. A cost 3 means no channel supplies a rescue. An item rejected at A2 is
by construction an item where a channel **does** supply one, namely the sense
distinction that was used to reject it. So if that rejection was wrong, the item
returns as a **contested** clash with a stated sense distinction, which is cost 1
or 2 by definition.

Run as a worst case: **accepted goes from 51 to 120, cost 3 stays at 2, cost 4
stays at 0.**

> **This is either the best reasoning in six seasons or an escape hatch with good
> manners, and you have to decide which.** The test is whether the argument was
> available before the falsifier fired, or invented to survive it.

## The device: what a fired falsifier does and does not contaminate

| output | contaminated by F4 | why |
|---|---|---|
| the dissolution rate | **yes, fatally** | it is the thing F4 measures |
| the number 51 | **yes** | it is one minus the dissolutions |
| 27% duplication | no | arithmetic, before any gate |
| A8 reference failures | no | checkable by anybody |
| **2 at cost 3** | **no, and the argument is directional** | an A2 error cannot manufacture a 3 |

## The clinical anchor

**Auditing your own notes and finding you documented capacity in two-thirds of
cases with the same sentence.**

The sentence is not false. Each patient probably did have capacity. And a
formulation that appears in two thirds of your notes verbatim is not an
assessment, it is a habit, and the audit exists to catch the difference.

**The uncomfortable part is that you cannot tell from inside which of the two it
was**, on any individual note, including the ones where you remember thinking
about it.

## The character anchor

**A referee reviewing his own decisions and finding he overturned two thirds of
them on one rule.**

Nobody suggests he was cheating. And the plain reading is that he was applying
that rule far more than the rule can bear, and every individual call felt correct
at the time.

**What he does next is the whole thing.** The version where he publishes the
figure, and the version where he notices it and does not, produce identical match
reports.

## What would count against this

1. **The survival argument being an escape hatch.** The strongest objection to
   this episode. **The check is timing:** the directional property of A2 is a
   consequence of the gate definitions written in advance, not a new premise. **A
   reader who does not accept that should discount the hard count too**, and the
   study would rather they did that than trust it wrongly.
2. **67.6% being expected rather than alarming.** Most alleged contradictions
   really are sense confusions, so a high A2 rate may be the correct answer rather
   than a bias. **Then the 40% ceiling was set wrong**, and setting it wrong in
   advance is still better than not setting it.
3. **A falsifier firing being treated as a success.** It is reported as one here,
   and there is a real risk that "we fired our own falsifier" becomes a
   credibility move rather than a cost.
