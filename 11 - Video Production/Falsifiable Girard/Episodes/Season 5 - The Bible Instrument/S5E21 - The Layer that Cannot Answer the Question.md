## The ceiling, fixed before any scoring

Layer 2 asks whether the historical case for the resurrection is honestly built.
Its rulebook fixes, in advance, what it can and cannot conclude.

**It can test** whether the claimed facts are supported, whether consensus claims
are verifiable, whether alternatives are fairly eliminated, and whether the case
is circular.

**It cannot test whether a miracle occurred.**

The reason is stated plainly and it is not a dodge. **Once the facts are fixed, the
inference is dominated by the prior on miracles**, and no source check touches a
prior. Two people who agree on every fact in the case and disagree about whether
miracles happen will disagree about the conclusion, and nothing in a bibliography
moves that.

> **The best possible outcome of Layer 2 is "the case is honestly built."** Never
> "it happened." That is written down before any scoring, so it cannot be quietly
> upgraded afterwards.

The reasoning behind that ceiling deserves working, because it is not obvious and
it is not a concession to sceptics.

A historical argument moves you from evidence to a conclusion. **How far it moves
you depends on what you thought was possible before you started.** Somebody who
holds that miracles do not occur will read any quantity of testimony as evidence
that something else happened, and they are not being unreasonable, because a
sufficiently improbable claim requires proportionate evidence.

**And no amount of checking citations touches that.** You can verify every source
in the case, find every claim supported, and two readers will still land in
different places, because the disagreement was never about the sources.

> So the ceiling is not modesty and it is not scepticism. **It is a statement
> about which disagreements a bibliography can settle**, and this one is not
> among them. What a bibliography can settle is whether the case is built out of
> things that are actually there, which is worth doing and is a smaller job.

## The rule that carries the whole layer

Layer 1 had structural protection: hostile compilers chose the docket, so the
coder could not skip hard items.

**Layer 2 has no such protection**, because there is no hostile compilation of
objections to a specific apologetics website. So one rule does all the work:

> **The apologetics site is the object under test and never the docket source.**

Objections come from **named critics only**. If a session takes the objection list
from the site being examined, the site has chosen which objections it will answer,
and **Layer 2 is void rather than weakened.**

The key auditor named in advance is **Dale Allison**, chosen specifically because
he affirms the core facts while rejecting the methodology, so both halves of him
always get reported.

## G1 fired, at two levels

**G1 said:** if the empty-tomb consensus claim cannot be verified from published,
checkable data, the site's strongest citation fails and the fact rests on an
unauditable premise.

**Level one.** The site asserts that most scholars, including non-Christian ones,
accept the tomb was found empty, **and cites no published source for it.**

**Level two, and this is the worse one.** The underlying survey behind that
consensus claim is one its own author described in 2012 as **"mostly
unpublished."**

So the strongest citation in the strongest section rests on a bibliography nobody
can inspect. **Cost 4**, for an unauditable warrant, which is the same discount
this project applied to its own run-1 fit ratio.

**Apply your own discount to yourself first, then to others**, and the number is
the same number.

## The clinical anchor

**A guideline citing an unpublished dataset.**

The recommendation is grade A. You follow the citation and it goes to a conference
abstract, or to data held by the group that wrote the guideline.

**The recommendation may be entirely correct.** And you cannot check it, and grade
A means checkable, so the grade is wrong even if the advice is right. **Those are
separate facts** and conflating them is how a whole field ends up standing on four
unpublished trials.

## The character anchor

**A Wikipedia claim whose citation leads to a dead link.**

The sentence has been there for nine years and is quoted everywhere. Follow the
footnote and the page is gone, and the archived version does not say what the
sentence says.

**The claim might still be true.** What has collapsed is the reason anybody had for
believing it, and the difference between those two is invisible until somebody
clicks.

## What would count against this

1. **The site being a soft target.** It is one apologetics website, not the
   scholarly case. **Grading it does not grade Wright or Habermas properly**, and
   the study should not be read as though it did.
2. **The consensus claim being true anyway.** Very possibly. **G1 is about the
   warrant, not the fact**, and an unauditable warrant for a true claim is still
   unauditable.
3. **No control existing for Layer 2, at all.** Layer 1 had the apocrypha. **There
   is no corpus of comparably-argued cases**, and none can be built, which is a
   permanent ceiling rather than an unfinished task.
