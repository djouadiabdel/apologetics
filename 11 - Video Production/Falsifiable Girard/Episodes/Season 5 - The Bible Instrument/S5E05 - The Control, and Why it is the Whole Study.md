# The Control, and Why it is the Whole Study

[[S5E04 - Blinding Somebody Who Cannot Be Blinded|← Blinding Somebody Who Cannot Be Blinded]] · [[S5E06 - The Result, and the Eleven Times|The Result, and the Eleven Times →]]

## The stake

The apocryphal control, and why it is the whole study. **Seasons 2 to 4 refined
an instrument with no control; this one refuses to**, and the episode shows what
a control buys that refinement never can.

## Collecting episode 4

Order blinding and a sealed key, both enforced by tooling, and an empty cell where
the main bias sits. Which brings us to the thing that makes the numbers mean
anything at all.

## The question a control answers

Suppose the canonical gospels interlock at some rate. Suppose they collide at some
rate. **Those numbers are meaningless in isolation** and it takes a moment to see
why, because they feel like facts.

A rate of one hard collision per thousand words sounds low. Low compared to what?
Compared to a transcript? Compared to four independent police statements? Compared
to a novel? **You have no idea whether it is low, and neither does anybody else**,
until you run the same procedure on texts whose character is not in dispute.

And notice this cuts at the sceptic exactly as hard as at the defender, which is
what makes it a control rather than a debating point.

The sceptic's version of the same error is "the gospels contradict each other,
therefore they are unreliable." **Compared to what?** Four independent accounts of
a real event contradict each other constantly. Police statements from the same
incident disagree about the number of assailants, the colour of a car, the order
of events. **Contradiction is what independent testimony looks like**, and a set
of accounts with no contradictions in it is evidence of collusion rather than
truth.

> So both sides are quoting a raw count and calling it a verdict. **The defender
> says the collisions are few and the sceptic says they exist**, and neither has
> a baseline, and the argument cannot terminate because there is nothing in it
> that could.

## The control, and it is a good one

Six apocryphal texts. The Gospel of Peter, the Protevangelium of James, the
Infancy Gospel of Thomas, the Acts of Pilate, and two more.

These are the right control for a specific reason. **They are not a random
different genre.** They are Christian documents, about Jesus, written by people
with the same commitments, in the same tradition, narrating overlapping events.
The Gospel of Peter has a passion and an empty tomb. The infancy gospels have a
childhood.

> So the comparison is not "sacred text against secular text" or "ancient against
> modern". It is **partisan documents about Jesus against other partisan
> documents about Jesus**, which is exactly the objection Season 4 could not
> answer, made into a measurement.

## The objection, which is serious and does not go away

The apocrypha are **later**, and they **had the canonical gospels in front of
them.**

That is not a quibble. If the Gospel of Peter is working from Mark, then its
collisions with Mark are one text reworking another rather than two independent
traditions diverging. The control is not a clean sample of "what legend looks
like". It is a sample of what legend looks like **when it is deriving from the
thing you are comparing it to.**

The study records this rather than answering it, and it records a specific
instance: the two infancy gospels are tagged as independent by the frozen rulebook
and are **probably literarily related.**

## What the study does with an objection it cannot answer

It states which way the objection cuts, and notices it cuts both ways.

If the infancy texts are related, their collisions are one author reworking
another, not independent invention. That weakens "independent legends collide".
**And two related texts that still contradict each other on whether a teacher dies
is arguably worse for their reliability rather than better.**

> **Recorded, not resolved.** That phrase appears in the results file and it is
> the correct posture. An objection you cannot answer gets written down at full
> strength, with its consequences in both directions, and left standing.

## The device: what makes a control good, applied here

| property a control needs | do the apocrypha have it |
|---|---|
| same subject matter | **yes**, Jesus, overlapping episodes |
| same commitments | **yes**, written by believers |
| same rough genre | **mostly**, narrative gospel form |
| independent of the test corpus | **no**, and this is the wound |
| character not in dispute | **yes**, nobody defends the talking cross |

**Four of five, and the missing one is the important one.** That is a good
control, honestly described, which is a rarer thing than a perfect one.

## The clinical anchor

**A negative control on an assay, and what a missing one costs.**

You run a test and it comes back positive. Without a negative control you do not
know whether the reagent produces that signal on plain buffer. **The result is
uninterpretable, not weak.** There is no partial credit.

And the specific failure here is the one that matches the apocrypha problem: **a
negative control contaminated with the analyte.** It is still a control, it still
catches gross problems, and it will systematically understate your effect because
the thing you are controlling with has some of the thing in it.

## The character anchor

**A mockbuster.**

*Transmorphers* exists because *Transformers* exists. Watching them side by side
tells you a great deal about what a competent film does, because the cheap one is
copying the same brief with none of the craft.

**And it tells you nothing about how two independent studios would have handled
robots**, because there is only one brief and one of them is chasing it. That is
the shape of the objection and it is why the control is good for embellishment and
weak for independence.

## What would count against this

1. **The control being contaminated, as above.** The study's own strongest
   critique of itself in this layer, and it is not resolved.
2. **Six texts being too few.** They are, and the corpora on both sides are small.
   Episode 8 shows how small, when four units out of twenty-two turn out to move a
   verdict on their own.
3. **The apocrypha being selected.** They were not chosen at random from the
   apocryphal literature. They were chosen because they narrate overlapping
   episodes, which is necessary for the comparison and **is still a selection made
   by the person running the study.**

trustworthy and a half that the person who produced it tells you not to trust.
