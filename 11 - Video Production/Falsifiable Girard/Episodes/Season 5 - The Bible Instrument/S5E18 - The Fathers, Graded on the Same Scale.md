# The Fathers, Graded on the Same Scale

## The stake

The church fathers get graded on the same scale as the modern harmonies, missing
denominator and all. **Older does not mean better**, and the grades come back
flatter than anyone's tradition predicts.

## Collecting episode 17

No denominator, so no rate, so the claims are about the accusers' list rather than
about the texts. Which leaves one thing this layer can still do honestly: turn the
instrument on the defence.

## The design, and it is stricter than it needed to be

Twelve of the hardest items on the docket, expanded to nineteen, chosen by
**mechanical criteria written to the file before any patristic source was
fetched.**

That ordering matters for the same reason it always does. Choose the items after
reading the fathers and you have chosen the items the fathers happen to address.
Choose them first, mechanically, and **the coverage becomes a finding rather than
an artifact.**

Then the patristic answer to each is graded on a fixed scale: **GOOD, PARTIAL,
BAD, ABSENT, AGAINST.**

## The headline, and it inverts what everybody expects

> **The fathers cover MORE of the hard items than modern harmonies do, and they
> answer them WORSE.**

Both halves are surprising and the second explains the first.

The fathers engage more because they were writing in a culture where the
objections were live and being pressed by people they had to answer. They answer
worse because **their answers are the ones that do not survive being said aloud
now**, and modern defenders have quietly stopped making them.

That is not a discovery about the fourth century. It is a discovery about the
present: **the modern harmony literature is smaller than the ancient one because
it has been filtered**, and the filtering removed the answers that embarrass
rather than the questions that are hard.

And the filtering is not a conspiracy, which is what makes it worth understanding.

Nobody decided to drop the weak answers. What happened is that each generation
repeated the answers that worked in argument and quietly stopped repeating the
ones that got laughed at. **Selection by embarrassment, operating over
centuries**, with no author and no moment where anything was suppressed.

> The result is a literature that looks stronger than its tradition actually was,
> **because the failures were not refuted, they were forgotten.** Which means a
> modern defender is inheriting a curated case and has no way of knowing what was
> curated out, unless somebody goes back and grades the originals.

There is a version of this in every field, including this one. **Seasons 2 to 4
exist because a research repository kept its failures**, and almost nothing else
does.

## The worst finding is a silence

Augustine's *De consensu evangelistarum* works the Judas material in detail. The
thirty pieces of silver, the potter's field, the misattribution of Zechariah to
Jeremiah. He is thorough, and he is engaging with the hard version.

**He never mentions Acts 1:18.**

That is the verse with the falling headlong and the bursting open. It is the other
half of the contradiction, it is the reason the item is at cost 3, and the most
thorough ancient harmony of the gospels works the pericope around it and does not
touch it.

**Verified**, not inferred from a summary.

## The gate that catches everybody

Augustine's solution to the staff problem, where the disciples are told to take a
staff in one gospel and not in another, is **gate A2 with no licence**: a
difference of sense with nothing attested to support it.

**A2 is the gate that fired F4 against this project two episodes ago.**

> So the same gate is abusable by the accuser, by the defender, and by the coder,
> **and all three have now been caught by it in this season.** That is the
> strongest evidence the instrument is symmetric, and it is the only kind of
> evidence that counts: it caught the person holding it.

## And one father who helps the defence, which the headline hides

A survey reporting that the fathers answer worse should be read with suspicion,
and the check is whether anything in it came out the other way. One thing did, and
it is worth stating because it costs the season's headline something.

**Papias, writing around 110 to 130, says Mark wrote down what he remembered
accurately but "not in order."**

That is a very early witness attesting, by name, that these texts were arranged
**thematically rather than chronologically**, and that the arrangement was known
and unremarkable at the time.

> Which matters directly, because a large family of alleged contradictions is
> about **ordering**: this gospel puts the temple cleansing early and that one
> late, this healing before that sermon. Under the study's own rules, a text
> whose genre does not claim chronological order is not asserting one, **and a
> non-assertion cannot contradict.**

So Papias supplies exactly what the guard demands: **a licence from outside the
argument**, cited, early, and made for reasons that have nothing to do with any
modern dispute. That is a cost 1 rescue on a whole class of items, offered by a
father, and it makes several of the accusers' entries weaker rather than the
defence's answers.

**It changed no cost in this study**, because rescoring on a finding discovered
mid-survey needs a new pre-registration. **It is reported against the story
anyway**, which is what the season keeps asking of everybody else.

## The clinical anchor

**A textbook that omits the complication.**

The current edition describes the procedure cleanly. The 1974 edition describes
the same procedure and spends two pages on a complication the modern text does not
mention.

**The complication did not stop happening.** It stopped being written about,
because the answer that used to be given for it is no longer one anybody wants in
print. **The older text is more honest and more wrong at the same time**, and you
learn most by reading both.

## The character anchor

**A remake that quietly drops the hardest scene.**

The original had a sequence nobody could shoot well and it shows, and everybody
remembers it being clumsy. The remake simply does not have it.

**The remake is better and it is also evidence that the scene was unfilmable**,
which is a fact about the story you can only recover by noticing an absence.

## What would count against this

1. **Nineteen items being a small survey.** It is, and it is the hard tail rather
   than a representative sample by design.
2. **Grading ancient answers by modern standards.** Genuinely unfair in one
   direction, and the study's guard is symmetric on age: **age is not licence, and
   age is not a defect either.** No cost moved because a source was old.
3. **A survey finding only failures being suspect.** The study says this about
   itself in episode 20, and it is the right worry.
