**The most important question in any rate**, asked at the exact moment it matters

## The hole

**Two hard contradictions out of what?**

Against thousands of cross-checkable claim-pairs, two is remarkable. Against
forty, two is bad. **Same numerator, opposite verdicts**, and nothing in this
study settles which frame you are in.

The pre-registration specifies the design for a denominator. **It has not been
built.** So the defensible claim is still about the accusers' list rather than
about the texts, and the study's own front page says so.

## Why a denominator is genuinely hard here

It is not laziness, and understanding why is more useful than the complaint.

To count cross-checkable claim-pairs you must first decide **what a claim is.** Is
"Jesus went to Jerusalem" one claim or three? Does every clause count? Every
proper noun? The answer changes the denominator by an order of magnitude and there
is no principled place to stop.

And the unit has to be fixed **in advance and independently**, because a
denominator chosen after you know your numerator is not a denominator. It is a
dial.

Watch how easily that dial turns. You have two hard contradictions. You want to
know whether that is good. You start counting claim-pairs, and every time you make
a granularity decision you can feel which way it moves the ratio.

**Slice finely and the denominator explodes and two looks like nothing.** Slice
coarsely and it shrinks and two looks serious. Neither slicing is wrong, both are
defensible in a methods section, and **the person choosing knows what each one
does to the answer.**

> This is Season 4 episode 10's problem exactly, arriving in a different project:
> **free parameters plus a target in view.** And the solution available there was
> to enumerate every choice rather than make one, which nobody has yet tried
> here.

> This is the same problem as episode 3's NULL category, arriving at full scale.
> **The denominator is the count of chances**, and chances are only countable once
> somebody defines what a chance is.

## The lead that exists and has not been used

There is a fourth-century enumeration of the cross-checkable set: the **Eusebian
canon tables**, which index which passages of which gospels parallel each other.
Built for a completely different purpose, by somebody with no stake in this
question, seventeen centuries before it was asked.

**That is a sampling frame, not a denominator.** Pericopes are not
proposition-pairs, and converting one to the other is the whole difficulty again.
But a stake-free enumeration by a disinterested party is exactly the kind of thing
you want, and it needs its own pre-registration before anybody touches it.

The distinction between a sampling frame and a denominator is worth having, since
they get confused constantly and the confusion is expensive.

**A denominator is the count of chances.** A sampling frame is a list you can draw
from so your sample is not chosen by you. The canon tables give you the second: a
principled list of parallel passages, compiled by somebody with no interest in
this question, from which you could draw at random.

That is genuinely valuable and it solves a different problem. **It stops the
analyst choosing which passages to examine**, which is exactly the failure that
sank run 1's fit ratio in episode 7. It does not tell you how many claim-pairs
exist inside those passages, which is still the open problem.

> Worth noticing anyway: **the fix for episode 7's disease has been sitting in a
> fourth-century manuscript apparatus the whole time**, and nobody in this project
> connected the two until the denominator question forced it.

## What the study is allowed to say without one

**About the accusers' list:** everything above. 671 to 153 to 51 to 2, with the
dissolution rate disqualified and the hard count structurally surviving.

**About the New Testament:** almost nothing quantitative.

> **A count without a denominator is not a low number or a high number. It is not
> a rate at all**, and the honest move is to stop calling it one.

## The device: the same numerator, two frames

| denominator | 2 hard contradictions is | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 40 cross-checkable pairs | 1 in 20 | **bad** |
| 400 | 1 in 200 | unremarkable |
| 4,000 | 1 in 2,000 | **remarkable** |

**Nobody knows which row is true**, and every public argument about biblical
contradictions is conducted as though the row were obvious to whoever is speaking.

## The clinical anchor

**A rate with no population denominator.**

Twelve cases this year. Up from eight. The room reacts.

**Twelve out of how many admissions?** If the catchment grew by half, twelve is a
fall. And the reason this happens constantly is that the numerator is easy to
count and lives in an incident system, while the denominator requires somebody to
go and get the activity data, which is dull.

**The easy number gets published and the hard number decides what it means.**

## The character anchor

**A batting average with no at-bats.**

Four home runs. Is that a good season? You cannot say, and everybody knows you
cannot say, because in sport the denominator is published as a matter of course
and nobody would accept the numerator alone.

**In almost every other domain we accept the numerator alone constantly**, and the
only difference is that sport made the habit of publishing both.

## What would count against this

1. **The denominator being unbuildable in principle.** If "claim" cannot be
   defined non-arbitrarily, no denominator exists and this layer is permanently a
   statement about lists. **That is a real possibility and the study does not rule
   it out.**
2. **The Eusebian route smuggling in a frame.** Eusebius indexed parallels
   according to his own sense of what parallels are, which is a theory of the
   texts.
3. **This objection applying to the accusers too.** It does, and harder. **671 is
   also a numerator with no denominator**, and it is quoted far more often.
