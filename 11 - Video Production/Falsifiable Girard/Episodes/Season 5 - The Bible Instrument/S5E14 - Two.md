## The tally

Of those 51, priced on the scale:

**Two at cost 3.** How Judas died, and who accused Peter.

**Zero at cost 4.**

## What the two are

**Judas.** Matthew has him hang himself. Acts has him fall headlong and burst
open. Same man, same death, same moment, and the reconciliations available all
require an event neither text states, which is the definition of cost 3.

**Peter's accusers.** The gospels differ on who challenged Peter in the courtyard,
and the second challenger is **gender-marked in the Greek**, which closes the
usual harmonisation. Mark has a definite feminine article, Matthew has a feminine
pronoun, Luke has a masculine one and Peter answers with a masculine vocative.

Why gender-marking closes it is worth spelling out, because the harmonisation it
kills is normally a strong one.

The standard move is that more people spoke than any single gospel records, so
Mark reports one challenger and Luke another and both are true. **That works
whenever the texts merely name different people**, and it is not special pleading:
a crowd accuses somebody and different witnesses remember different voices.

**It stops working when the texts specify the same slot in the sequence and mark
it differently.** This is the second denial in all three, and Greek marks the
challenger's sex in the grammar rather than leaving it open. To keep the
harmonisation you now need a second challenge, of a different sex, in the same
slot, that nobody narrates. **Which is an invented event, which is cost 3.**

## Read the zero carefully, because it is doing less than it looks

**Zero at cost 4** does not mean there are no contradictions. It means that for
every accepted item, **some** rescue exists on **some** channel, even if that
rescue is expensive.

And cost 3 is not a pass. It is the study saying: to make this go away you must
believe something no text states. That is available and it is not free.

There is also a structural reason zero at cost 4 was always likely, and a study
that quotes the number without it is overselling.

**Cost 4 requires proving a negative**: that no rescue exists on any channel, not
merely that none has been proposed. That is a very high bar and it gets harder the
more defenders have written, because every century of apologetic literature adds
candidate rescues. **An item can move from 4 to 3 forever and never move back.**

> So "zero at cost 4" is partly a fact about the texts and partly a fact about
> how much ingenuity has been spent on them for two thousand years. **The
> honest reading is that the scale's top level is nearly unreachable by
> construction**, and the interesting line is therefore cost 3.

> **The honest sentence is not "the New Testament has two contradictions."** It
> is: **of 671 allegations compiled by people trying to find the worst, 153 are
> distinct, 51 are real clashes, and two of those cannot be fixed without
> inventing an event.**

## What it costs, said at the top rather than the bottom

**Inerrancy.**

A cost 3 means one of two texts is wrong about a fact. Judas either hanged or fell
headlong, Matthew and Acts say different things, and **the scale does not remove
it.** No amount of the rest of this study buys that back.

So a defender who wants to quote the number two has to also quote what the two
cost, and a study that produced the number and then defended inerrancy would be
contradicting its own output.

## The clinical anchor

**A screening result that requires confirmation.**

A positive screen is not a diagnosis and a negative screen is not an all-clear. It
is a number produced by a test with a known sensitivity, on a population with a
known prevalence, and it means something only in that frame.

**"Two" is a screening result.** Two out of an unstated denominator, from a test
whose specificity has just been shown to be unreliable, on a docket somebody else
compiled. Every one of those qualifiers is in the study and none of them survives
being quoted in a thumbnail.

## The character anchor

**A health inspection score in the window.**

Five stars. And the score is against a defined checklist, by an inspector who came
on one day, and it means the specific thing the checklist measures.

**Nobody reads it that way.** It gets read as "this place is good", and the gap
between what the number measures and what it is taken to mean is the entire reason
the checklist has to be published alongside it.

## What would count against this
