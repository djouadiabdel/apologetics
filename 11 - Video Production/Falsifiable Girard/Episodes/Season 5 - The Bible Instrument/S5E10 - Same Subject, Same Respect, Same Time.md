## The definition, and it is Aristotle's

Two texts contradict when they assert **P and not-P** about:

- **the same subject**,
- **in the same respect**,
- **at the same time**,

and no licensed reading makes both true.

Those three qualifiers are the whole content, and each one blocks a specific
mistake that ordinary argument makes constantly.

## Working the three qualifiers

**Same subject.** "Jesus healed one blind man" and "Jesus healed two blind men"
contradict only if they describe the same healing. If they are two occasions, both
are true and nothing has happened. So before anything else you have to establish
that the two texts are talking about one event, and a surprising number of alleged
contradictions die here without any special pleading.

**Same respect.** This is the one that does the most work and causes the most
trouble. "The mustard seed is the smallest of all seeds" and modern botany
disagree only if the text is making a botanical claim rather than a proverbial
one. **A word used in two senses generates a fake contradiction**, and so does a
rhetorical "all" read as a strict quantifier.

**Same time.** "Circumcision is required" and "circumcision is nothing" are both
in the New Testament, and they belong to a stated change of covenant. Before
against after is not P and not-P.

And notice that the third qualifier is the one most open to abuse, in a way the
first two are not. **Same subject and same respect are questions about the texts.
Same time invites you to supply a history.**

"These belong to different periods" is a claim, and it is checkable when the text
states the change and unfalsifiable when it does not. The circumcision case is
fine because the New Testament argues about the transition explicitly and at
length. **An appeal to different periods where no text marks a period is the same
move as inventing an event**, and the cost scale in episode 13 prices it
accordingly.

## Why this is not a defensive trick

Because the same three qualifiers destroy defences as readily as accusations, and
the study is about to demonstrate that on the church fathers.

> **A distinction of respect that nobody can attest is not a distinction, it is a
> wish.** Saying two texts use a word in different senses obliges you to show
> where the second sense is attested, and if you cannot, you have supplied from
> outside what the text does not supply, which is the guard from episode 3
> pointed at yourself.

The rulebook requires it explicitly: **every appeal to a difference of sense must
state the specific distinction and cite where the alternative sense is attested.**
Not "these could mean different things", which is always true of any two words.

The requirement to **cite where the alternative sense is attested** is the whole
weight of that rule, and it is worth seeing why it is not a formality.

Any word in any language can be argued into a second sense. Given a motive, you
can propose that "day" means an age, that "all" means many, that "brother" means
cousin. **Every one of those proposals is possible and some of them are correct**,
and the difference between the correct ones and the convenient ones is whether the
sense turns up somewhere else, in a text nobody was arguing about.

> **Attestation is what stops a sense-distinction being free.** And a project
> that requires it of its opponents and not of itself has built a machine for
> agreeing with itself, which is what episode 15 catches this project doing at a
> measured rate of 67.6%.

## The device: the same allegation through the three qualifiers

| one blind man against two | same subject | dies if two occasions, and there is no evidence either way |
| faith against works | same respect | two emphases, not P and not-P |
| circumcision required against nothing | same time | stated change of covenant |
| **Judas hanged against Judas burst open** | **all three hold** | **survives, and it is one of two** |

**The bottom row is the shape of a real one.** Same man, same death, same moment,
and no attested sense in which hanging is falling headlong.

## The clinical anchor

**Two results that look contradictory until you check the units.**

A calcium of 2.2 and a calcium of 8.8 are the same measurement in different units.
A glucose taken fasting and one taken after lunch are not in conflict. A potassium
from a haemolysed sample is not disagreeing with the one from a clean draw.

**Most apparent conflicts in a set of notes are resolved by asking what was
measured, in what units, at what time**, which is precisely same subject, same
respect, same time. The remainder, after those three questions, is the interesting
list, and it is always much shorter than it looked.

## The character anchor

**Two people arguing about whether a film is long.**

One saw the theatrical cut and one saw the extended edition. They are not
disagreeing about anything. They have been arguing for twenty minutes and both are
correct, and the argument only ends when somebody asks which version.

**Almost every argument you have ever watched fail to resolve has this shape**, and
the three qualifiers are a procedure for finding out in the first minute rather
than the twentieth.

## What would count against this

1. **The definition being too strict.** Under it, a great many real tensions in
   the texts are not contradictions, and somebody could reasonably say the strict
   definition protects the texts by construction. **The answer is that it is
   Aristotle's and not the project's**, and that it is applied to defences too,
   which episode 18 shows costs the defenders more than it costs the accusers.
2. **"Licensed reading" being where all the judgement hides.** It is. That is
   what the nine gates in episode 11 exist to constrain and what fires a falsifier
   in episode 15.
3. **Same-respect being unfalsifiable in the hard cases.** Sometimes nobody can
   settle whether a word is used in two senses. **Those items should be contested,
   not dissolved**, and whether they were is the thing episode 15 measures.
