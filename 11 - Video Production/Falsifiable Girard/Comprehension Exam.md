# Comprehension Exam — all five seasons

**The bar:** a reader who knows nothing about Girard, statistics or the New
Testament reads one episode and comes out able to answer **what** it establishes,
**why** it is true, and **how it relates** to what came before and after.

Not "can he film it" (the teachability exam). Not "is it correct" (the four
checkers). **Can a stranger arrive expert.**

## Procedure, fixed before scoring

**180 questions, written from the episode TITLES and the series index only.** No
episode body was opened while writing them. Each is scored:

- **A** — answered explicitly and completely
- **P** — the material is there but the reader must assemble it themselves
- **F** — not answerable from that episode

**Falsifier, fixed in advance: more than 18 F, or more than 45 P+F, means the
series does not meet the bar and needs another explanation pass.**

## Contamination disclosure

**Severe, and stated plainly.** I wrote all 90 episodes. I cannot un-know them.
The mitigations are real but partial:

1. **Questions were generated from titles only**, in one pass, before any body was
   reopened. A title is what a viewer sees before watching, which makes it the
   fairest available prompt.
2. **Three questions per episode were forced** — one what, one why, one
   relationship — so I could not write only the questions I knew were answered.
3. **The relationship questions are the least contaminated**, because connections
   across episodes are the thing I am least likely to have deliberately written.

**A pass here is weak evidence. Only the failures count**, per the standing rule.

# RESULT

**178 A, 1 P, 1 F.** The falsifier did not fire, and **that result should not be
trusted at face value.**

## Read the contamination before reading the score

I wrote the 90 episodes and I wrote the 180 questions. **A 99% pass is what severe
contamination predicts**, not what a well-explained series necessarily produces.
The score is close to meaningless as evidence that the material is good.

**What the exam is still worth is the shape of what failed.** Under a bias this
strong, the questions that survive it are the ones I could not have written to
order, and exactly one category produced a real failure.

## The one failure, and it is the category that matters

**Q117 — "How does the noise floor relate to every comparison in Seasons 2 and 3?"
FAIL.**

Season 3 episode 8 computes the instrument's noise floor at **17.2%** and applies
it to exactly one confound, the mythography/tragedy tie at 0.5%. It then stops.
**A reader finishes the episode holding a ruler and is never shown what else to
measure with it.**

That is the obvious next question and nobody asks it, including me, across five
seasons of writing. Running the floor against every round-2 comparison, on the
same basis the 17.2% was computed (difference over the lower value), produces
three things that were not written anywhere:

| comparison | gap | |
|---|---|---|
| tragedy against control | 78.6% | far clear |
| tragedy against epic | 46.4% | clear |
| tragedy against Ovid | 40.9% | clear |
| epic against control | 22.0% | clears by 4.8 |
| **named tier separation** | **20.0%** | **clears by 2.8** |
| **Ovid against epic** | **3.9%** | **inside the floor** |
| **mythography against tragedy** | **0.5%** | **inside the floor** |

**The named tier separation is the finding.** P2/P3 is the only round-2 prediction
that passed cleanly, on texts named before scoring, and it clears the instrument's
own wobble **by under three percentage points.**

And **Ovid against epic at 3.9% is not a small difference, it is not a
difference** — which means the "Ovid ≈ epic ≈ mythography" half of P1 came out as
predicted and **could not have come out any other way.** That half of P1 was never
a test.

**All of it is now in S3E8.**

## Two questions that were wrong, not two episodes

Recorded because a scoring pass that never blames its own instrument is not a
scoring pass.

- **Q109** asked S3E6 about "the density problem". E6 is the **dilution** problem;
  density is E7. My question mis-located it.
- **Q113** asked whether S3E7 flags that the gap may be smaller than the noise
  floor. It does: *"Episode 8 asks whether the tie was ever big enough to need
  explaining."* **My grep tested for the words "noise floor" rather than for the
  idea**, which is the same literalism this series keeps catching in its own
  checkers.

## What to conclude, narrowly

**Not** that the series explains everything well. The exam cannot show that and its
author cannot be the one to certify it.

**What it does show** is that the what-and-why layer is saturated — every
definition, mechanism and reason a stranger would ask for is present at first use,
after three earlier passes went looking for exactly those. **The remaining gaps
are relational**, and they are of one specific kind:

>> **A number gets computed, explained, and applied to the case that produced it,
>> and never turned around and pointed at everything else already published.**
>> That is what Q117 caught, and it is invisible to every checker in this project,
>> because nothing is wrong on the page.

**The next exam should be relationship questions only**, and it should be written
by somebody who did not write the episodes.
