This is the shortest honest version of the theory that still lets you follow what the instrument does. It is not a summary of Girard. It is the load-bearing quarter of him.

Four claims, stacked. Each one rests on the one under it.

| # | the claim | if it is false |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | you want things because someone else wants them, not because of what they are | nothing above survives |
| 2 | the person who taught you to want it is now standing in front of it | conflict needs a different explanation |
| 3 | when enough people want the same thing, the differences that kept order collapse | there is nothing to resolve |
| 4 | the crisis ends when everyone turns on one victim, and the peace afterwards makes the victim look guilty | myth needs a different explanation |

Claim 2 is the hinge, and almost nobody notices it.

Claim 1 on its own is familiar. Advertising has run on it for a century. Claims 3 and 4 are the famous ones, the ones people quote at dinner. But 2 is where the theory earns something, because copied desire turns the teacher into the obstacle **automatically**. Nobody chooses it. Nobody is a villain.

## 1. The model gives your desire a scoreboard

Take Vegeta, and take him accurately, because the lazy version is wrong and an audience catches it instantly.

The lazy version says Vegeta only wants what Goku wants. False. Vegeta had goals before Goku mattered to him. Immortality. Being the strongest Saiyan. A whole identity built on being royalty.

What actually happens is better. A low-class Saiyan turns out to be stronger than the prince. From that moment every ambition Vegeta already had is re-routed into a comparison with one person.

>> **The model does not hand you a desire. The model gives your desire a scoreboard.**

You can prove it from the story. Every time Vegeta reaches a level he previously called the summit, he is not satisfied for a single episode, because Goku got there too. The target moves because the target was never a number. It was a person.

So desire has three parts rather than two. A subject who wants, an object wanted, and a **model** who wants it first.

> [FIGURE 1. The triangle. Subject and Object on the bottom, Model at the apex. Two solid arrows from Subject to Model and Model to Object. The Subject-to-Object arrow drawn dotted, labelled "the only one we ever talk about". The dotted line is the argument.]

Salieri in *Amadeus* is the same shape one turn darker. He does not want music. He wants to be the one who has it. When he sees that Mozart's manuscript has no corrections, he does not hear beauty. He hears a verdict on himself.

## 2. The teaching working is the catastrophe

Griffith and Guts, and the sequence matters.

Griffith teaches Guts what a man with his own dream looks like. Guts learns it. Guts leaves to find one, which is exactly what he was taught to do. And Griffith, watching the student execute the lesson perfectly, is destroyed by it.

>> Nobody betrayed anybody. Nobody lied. **The teaching worked, and the teaching working is the catastrophe.**

Ippo and Miyata is the gentler version of the same object. Miyata shows Ippo what a boxer is, so Ippo's entire drive organises itself around a promise to fight Miyata one day. The person who gave him the goal **is** the goal.

Now notice the loop. I imitate your desire, which raises what the thing appears to be worth, which raises your own valuation of it, which raises mine.

> [FIGURE 2. The feedback loop as a circle with two nodes and arrows going one way round, plus a callout reading "no thermostat". Compare with a standard control loop drawn faintly beside it, which has a damping arrow the mimetic one lacks. The missing arrow is the content.]

Nobody adds anything from outside. It powers itself and it goes one direction.

That is why Girardian rivalries do not settle. And it is why the theory is worth something at all, because any account of conflict needing a villain explains less. It cannot explain the conflicts where nobody is a villain and everyone involved can see nobody is.

The clinical version is the registrar and the consultant. The person who teaches you what competent practice looks like is the same person who decides whether you have reached it. You cannot learn the standard from anybody else and you cannot be signed off by anybody else.

## 3. Rivals turn into copies of each other

This is the step that sounds wrong the first time.

If what you want is to **be** him, then the closer you get to wanting it, the more you resemble him. Not as a side effect. As the thing you were aiming at.

Naruto and Sasuke at the Valley of the End, and look at what the frame shows you before anyone speaks. They mirror each other's moves. They stand on two statues at either end of a gorge. The composition is symmetrical. Each is the other's entire reason for being there.

The more intense the opposition, the more precisely each mirrors the other, because each is now using the other as the template for how hard to want it.

And there is a real objection sitting inside this, which the theory survives by making a prediction.

**Vegeta and Goku do not converge.** Goku never joins the loop. He barely notices he is in one. So mediation without reciprocity produces one obsessed person and one person who is somewhere else. The theory predicts convergence **only where the rivalry runs both ways.**

What this looks like at the scale of a whole society is not chaos as variety. It is chaos as **undifferentiation**.

Atrial fibrillation is not the heart doing something varied. It is the heart losing the difference between the pacemaker and everything else. Normally one node fires and the rest follow, and that hierarchy is what produces an ordered beat. Remove it and every cell is doing something reasonable and the organ does nothing.

## 4. All against one

A society in that state needs the fighting to stop, and it has no mechanism for negotiating an end, because negotiation requires the differences that just collapsed.

What ends it, Girard says, is that the violence finds a single target. Everyone against one instead of everyone against everyone. And it works. The fighting stops, because there is nobody left to fight.

Then comes the part the whole project turns on. The peace is real, and the people who made it are not lying when they explain it. From the inside, a crisis ended the moment that person died. So the person must have caused the crisis. He must have been what they said he was.

>> **Myth is that account, written by the people who did it, in good faith.**

That is why the monster in a myth is always genuinely monstrous. Not because the tellers are covering up. Because that is what a lynching looks like from inside the crowd.

## Where the testing actually starts

Say the honest thing here rather than at the end.

**Nothing in this project tests claims 1, 2 or 3.** No instrument here measures copied desire, or models becoming rivals, or rivals converging. Those are the parts most people find interesting and they are untouched.

What is measurable is claim 4, and only through the residue Girard says it leaves. He names four marks that turn up in texts produced by the mechanism.

| bucket | what it is | rate per 1000 | dominated by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **crisis** | the collapse of order and difference | 2.07 | **60% one word, `death`** |
| **crimes** | accusations that break the deepest boundaries | 0.95 | 12% `murder` |
| **marks** | the signs that single out this victim | 2.90 | 44% `king` |
| **violence** | the collective killing itself | 4.76 | 21% `blood` |

Those domination figures are not decoration and post 4 spends real time on them. `crisis` is the bucket meant to measure lost difference, and it is 60% a single word. That is the worst-built of the four. `crimes` is the rarest and the best built, which is a finding rather than a disappointment.

## Why the fourth part is the only measurable one

It is worth saying why the theory narrows to four word lists, because the narrowing looks like a retreat and is not one.

Claims 1 to 3 are about what is happening **inside people.** Copied wanting. A model becoming an obstacle. Two rivals converging. Nothing in a text tells you that a character's desire was copied rather than his own, and nothing in a corpus lets you count it. You would be scoring an interpretation.

Claim 4 is different, and Girard is explicit about the difference. He says the mechanism **produces a document.** The crowd tells the story afterwards, in good faith, and the story has a shape because the event had a shape.

That means claim 4 makes a prediction about text as text. Certain vocabularies should be present, together, in a certain kind of writing, and absent or differently arranged elsewhere.

>> **Only the part of a theory that predicts an observable can be tested, and in Girard that part is the residue rather than the mechanism.**

Which fixes the ceiling on everything in this series before it starts. A pass would not show that mimetic desire is real. It would show that texts of one kind carry a vocabulary profile that texts of another kind do not, which is compatible with Girard and compatible with several other explanations.

That is a smaller prize than it sounds, and it is the only prize on offer.

## The ladder, which is the whole methodological point

Take the four apart and they are worth nothing. Say it before a critic says it for you.

Violent stories contain violence. Not a finding, a tautology. Stories about kings contain the word king. Tragedies contain death. Myths contain accusations.

So the claim has to be about the four **together**, and there are three different things that could mean.

| level | the claim | what the corpus says | worth testing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | each vocabulary appears in these texts | trivially true | no |
| 2 | all four appear in the same text | **true in 90% of 72 texts** | almost worthless |
| 3 | all four converge on **one passage** | this is the project | **yes** |

> [FIGURE 4. The three rungs as an actual ladder, with rung 2 annotated "90%" and rung 3 annotated "the whole project". Rungs 1 and 2 drawn thin, rung 3 drawn solid.]

Level 2 sounds much stronger than level 1 and is nearly as empty. All four buckets fire in nine out of ten texts in the corpus, including texts with no persecution in them at all.

Level 3 is the claim that makes Girard a theory rather than a vocabulary. One process, one place, one moment.

The clinical version is not a metaphor, it is the same object. A raised white cell count means nothing alone, fifty causes. A fever means nothing alone, a hundred causes. A raised lactate means nothing alone. **Sepsis is the claim that they arrive together because one process produces all of them.** That is what a syndrome is. It is a harder thing to demonstrate than a symptom.

And you already know the objections to a syndrome. Your findings are common in sick patients generally. You have defined a cluster that would show up by chance in any intensive care unit. Your criteria were built by looking at patients who already had the diagnosis.

Each of those has an exact counterpart in this series, and they are posts 6, 6 again, and 8.

## One number worth carrying

If the four stereotypes are four faces of one event, they should rise and fall together across texts.

Measured across 76 texts, the correlations between buckets come out between **0.13 and 0.38**. Crisis against marks is 0.13, which is close to no relationship at all.

Decide now whether that helps Girard or hurts him, because it genuinely cuts both ways and no later post is going to resolve it for you. Low correlation means the four are not redundant, which is what the theory needs. It also means they do not co-occur strongly, which is what the theory says they should.
