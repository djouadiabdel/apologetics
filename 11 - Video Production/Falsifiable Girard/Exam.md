# The Exam — what Seasons 1 to 3 should be able to answer

**Written before scoring.** These questions are derived from what the series
*promises*: the glossary in `Series Index.md`, the season descriptions, the
refrain, and what a viewer who watched all fifty episodes should be able to do
afterwards. **They were not derived by reading the episodes for material**, which
would guarantee a pass and prove nothing.

## Contamination disclosure

**I wrote these having already read every episode in this series**, most of them
several times, while editing them. That is real contamination and it cannot be
undone. It biases the questions toward things I know are covered.

**What that means for the result: a high score is weak evidence. A failure is
strong evidence**, because a failure survives the bias rather than being produced
by it. **Read the F rows and ignore the pass rate.**

## The scoring rule, fixed now

| grade | meaning |
|---|---|
| **A** | answered explicitly, in one place, with the number or definition present |
| **P** | the material is there but scattered, implied, or stated without being explained |
| **F** | not answerable from Seasons 1 to 3 |

**The falsifier, fixed in advance:** if more than **10 of 50** come out F, the
seasons do not teach what the index promises and the gaps get written before
Season 4 is started. If more than **15 of 50** are P or F, the same.

## The four that did not pass

| # | question | grade | what was actually wrong |
|---|---|---|---|
| **18** | what is tier V and tier K | **F** | used **4 times across the seasons and defined nowhere.** The definition existed only in `Series Index.md`, which is a production file nobody watches |
| **40** | name three times the refrain fired | **P** | **"a check that cannot fail prints reassurance" appeared twice in fifty episodes**, both in Season 2. Zero times in Season 1 and Season 3 |
| — | what is a sparagmos | **F** | used once, in Season 3, cold. A Greek word dropped on a viewer with no gloss |
| — | contamination disclosure | **P** | the **practice** appears twice and the **term** appears zero times, so the glossary promises a phrase the series never says |

## Why 40 is the serious one

`Series Index.md` says the refrain is the sentence that "runs through all eight
seasons." **It ran through one.** Both instances sat in Season 2, which means a
viewer could watch the entire theory season and the entire failure season without
meeting the idea the series is named after.

And the concept was everywhere while the sentence was absent. **Season 3 episode 14
is a null that can never return a positive. Episode 12 is a null that destroys
nothing that matters. Episode 18 is a sensitivity analysis chosen because it will
agree.** Every one of those is the refrain and none of them said so.

>> **This is the failure mode the exam exists to catch**, and no structural check
>> could have caught it. `audit.py` cannot count whether an idea recurred. The
>> episodes were the right length, had every register part, and were missing the
>> spine.

## Fixed the same day

- **Tier V and K** are now defined at first use, in Season 1 episode 3, as a table
  plus the sentence that matters: **tier K is a lead, not a fact.**
- **Sparagmos** is now defined at first use, in Season 1 episode 15, with the note
  that a culture does not coin a noun for something that happens once.
- **The refrain** is now seeded at four genuine landing points rather than
  sprinkled: Season 1 episode 18 (a control is the cheapest check that can fail),
  Season 3 episode 12 (a null that destroys nothing is a check that cannot fail),
  Season 3 episode 14 (always-yes and always-no are the same instrument), Season 3
  episode 18 (a sensitivity analysis chosen to agree). **Now 1 / 2 / 4 across the
  three seasons.**

## What this exam cannot tell you

1. **Whether the answers are any good.** It checks that an answer exists and is
   explained, not that it is correct or well delivered. **Delivery is the camera's
   test, not this one.**
2. **Whether the questions are the right questions.** They came from the index and
   the glossary, which the same author wrote. **A viewer would ask different ones**,
   and the honest next version of this test is somebody else's question list.
3. **What a cold viewer retains.** Every question here was scored against a
   document, with search. **Nobody watching a video has search.**
