# The Video Spine

## Why the beat format failed

The scripts were built as a numbered list of beats, and a beat list has no internal logic. Nothing in it says which beat is load-bearing, nothing says what has to come before what, and nothing says when you are finished. So every beat became a reveal of equal weight, seven or ten in a row, and the viewer stopped following an argument and started waiting for the next trick.

A father's letter is not a list of insights about a passage. It has one thing to say and it spends the whole letter getting the hearer to the place where that one thing lands on him.

## The six moves

Six moves. In order. Every episode.

**1. The address. Under a minute.**
Open on the listener's condition, not on the text. Chrysostom does not open with the Greek. He opens with what is wrong with the people in front of him. Name the situation the passage is about to walk into. If you cannot name a condition, you do not have an episode yet.

**2. The text, read once, whole, no commentary.**
Read it and let it sit. Do not annotate while reading. The listener needs the whole shape before you touch any part of it.

**3. The difficulty, sharpened as hard as you can make it.**
State the problem in the strongest form available, the form your opponent would use. This is where the fathers are least like modern apologetics. They make the objection worse before they answer it, because an objection you have softened is not worth answering and the listener can feel you softening it. If the passage looks like it contradicts Paul, say that it looks exactly like it contradicts Paul and do not hedge.

**4. The turn. One move.**
One. Not three. There is a single hinge that dissolves the difficulty, and the whole episode is built to deliver it at this moment. Everything before it exists to make it necessary and everything after it exists to test it. If you have two candidate turns you have two episodes.

**5. The pressure. Three tests, escalating.**
Not three new reveals. Three attempts to break the turn, each one costing more than the last. This is what makes an argument feel like an argument instead of a tour. The tests should get harder, and the last one should be the one that would genuinely hurt if it failed.

**6. The landing. It costs the listener something.**
The father always turns the passage on the hearer at the end, and it is never comfortable. Not a summary. Not a recap of the beats. The one sentence that makes the argument the listener's problem rather than a fact he now owns. If your ending could be replaced by "and that is why James and Paul agree," you have not landed, you have stopped.

## Rules that follow from this

- **One thesis per episode.** If the material has two, cut one and make it the next episode. Two braided theses is what produced the circus feeling.
- **Fathers get used in the turn or the pressure, never in the caveat.** Zizioulas appearing only in a hedge at the end is the symptom of him not being in the argument. If a father is in the tags he must be doing structural work.
- **Greek words serve the turn or they get cut.** A word study that is merely interesting is a trick. `Prosopolempsia` earned its place in Part 1 because the passage turned on it. Most do not.
- **Target 1,200 to 1,400 words.** At your delivery pace that is twelve to fifteen minutes. The old scripts were 2,700 to 2,900 words, which is where the thirty minutes came from. The fix is fewer moves developed further, not more moves compressed.
- **The open loop is one sentence.** Not a section.
