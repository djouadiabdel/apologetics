# Filming Doc Spec — Building the Rnote Board

This file is instructions for Claude. When Abdel asks for a filming doc, read this first, then build to it.

The output is a PDF he imports into Rnote and annotates live while the camera runs. It is not a script. It is not a summary of the research. It is a board.

---

## 1. The two-document contract

Every episode has exactly two documents and they do different jobs.

**The research doc** teaches him the topic. Prose, cited, long, dense. He reads it cold to get confused, then organises. It exists so that by filming day he knows more than he will say. Lives in `<Series>/Research/`.

**The filming doc** is the board. Short, spatial, almost no prose. It exists so that he can talk smoothly without recalling. Lives in `<Series>/Filming/`.

The contract runs both ways. If the research doc is thin, the filming doc will be padded with prose to compensate, and he will end up reading aloud. If the research doc is good, the filming doc can be almost empty and still carry him.

**What the research doc owes the filming doc:**

- The thesis in one sentence, stated somewhere explicitly
- Every number, date, Greek or Hebrew word, and citation he might say out loud
- At least one concrete personal or historical anecdote per major beat
- The strongest form of the objection, written out as an opponent would say it
- The one image or diagram that carries the argument

If any of those are missing, say so before building the board rather than inventing them.

---

## 2. What to steal from the whiteboard format

Studied from Charlie Morgan's long whiteboard videos. Six mechanics, all of them portable, none of them requiring his persona.

**Write two to five words, never a sentence.** "Pick a market, not a model." "Find a problem." "Machine inputs." The written words are beat markers, not content. The content is spoken.

**One diagram per beat, and it must do argumentative work.** Not decoration. His hundred-metre race line makes the advantage argument visually. His day 1 to day 20 timeline is the entire case for not stopping outreach. If a diagram only illustrates, cut it.

**A number in almost every beat.** 200 outreaches, 60% show rate, 15% close, 60,000 to a million. Numbers make an argument feel like engineering instead of motivation. For scripture work the equivalent is verse distance, word counts, dates, Greek forms. James 2:8 and 2:9 quoting Leviticus 19:18 and 19:15, three lines apart, is exactly this move.

**One concrete anecdote per beat.** The Vauxhall Corsa in the car park. The Tesco meal deal. Specific to the point of feeling checkable. Vague examples kill the beat.

**Voice the objection before the viewer raises it.** "Now some people are like, well, my hourly rate." This is already move three of the spine, the difficulty. On a board it gets its own space, written in the viewer's words, not his.

**End each beat slightly open.** "But the jump from 250 to a million is a little different, and I will endeavour to explain." Small, not a cliffhanger. It buys the transition.

---

## 3. Why this produces smoothness

The thing that makes someone fluent on camera is not having words available. It is having the next physical action queued.

Charlie is always about to draw something. The drawing buys him thinking time, gives the viewer motion, and marks the beat boundary so he never loses his place. The doc should therefore be a **queue of actions**, not a body of information.

Practical consequence: every page must answer "what do I do with my hand next?" If a page has no action on it, it will be read aloud, and reading aloud is where he stiffens.

---

## 4. Page layout rules

Rnote annotation over an imported PDF. Build the PDF to be drawn on.

- **One beat per page.** Page turn equals beat change. Camera framing stays stable, and he never hunts.
- **Top third: the beat words.** Large, 36pt or more, two to five words. This is what he underlines or circles as he says it.
- **Middle: deliberate blank space.** This is where the diagram gets drawn live. Do not pre-draw it. Leave it empty and describe it in a small grey cue line at the edge so he knows what he is building.
- **Bottom strip: cues only.** The number, the anecdote in three words, the citation. Small type. These are glance targets, never sentences.
- **Nothing else on the page.** No paragraphs. If prose appears on a filming page, the spec has been violated.

The pre-written words exist so he does not burn camera time writing slowly. He annotates what is already there. The blank middle exists so there is something genuinely being made on camera.

---

## 5. Mapping the six moves onto pages

The spine in [[Homily Format - The Video Spine]] stays authoritative for scripture videos. The board serves it, it does not replace it. Typical page allocation:

| Move | Pages | Board treatment |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The address | 0 | Camera only, no board. Do not put the hook on a page. |
| 2. The text, read whole | 1 | Passage pre-printed, generous line spacing. He marks it live as he reads. |
| 3. The difficulty | 1 to 2 | Objection written in the viewer's own words. Blank space beneath for the tension diagram. |
| 4. The turn | 1 | The single move. Usually the page with the most blank space, because this is the drawing that matters. |
| 5. The pressure | 3 | One page per test, escalating. Each gets its number or citation in the bottom strip. |
| 6. The landing | 1 | Words only, no diagram. The hand stops moving. That stillness is the signal it has ended. |

Eight to nine pages for a standard episode. If it exceeds twelve, the episode is two episodes.

---

## 6. Hard rules

**Leave the thinking to him.** This is the governing constraint. The board cues, it never concludes. Write the objection, not the answer to it. Write the verse pair, not the connection between them. If a page states the insight, he has nothing to discover on camera and the delivery goes flat. This is the single easiest way to ruin a filming doc.

**No prose on filming pages.** If a full sentence appears outside a scripture quotation, it is wrong.

**Do not pre-draw the diagrams.** Describe them in a cue line. The drawing is the performance.

**Scripture is NIV.** Always.

**No em-dashes, no ellipses, no dramatic colons.** Applies to every word that will appear on screen or be spoken.

**Numbers and Greek go in the bottom strip, never from memory.** He should never have to recall a citation mid-sentence.

---

## 7. Template

```
PAGE n — <BEAT NAME>

    [top third, 36pt+]
    TWO TO FIVE WORDS

    [middle, blank]
    cue (small, grey, edge): "draw the balance, ring left, man right"

    [bottom strip, small]
    2:8 → Lev 19:18   |   2:9 → Lev 19:15   |   three lines apart
    anecdote: anulus aureus, Roman legal ring
```

## 8. Worked example, James 2 part 1

Beats one and two of the filmed episode, which is thesis plus Leviticus 19.

```
PAGE 1 — THE TEXT
    top:    JAMES 2:1-5
    body:   passage printed, double spaced, wide right margin
    cue:    "mark 2:1 and 2:5 as you read, nothing else"
    strip:  NIV

PAGE 2 — THE DIFFICULTY
    top:    DO NOT SHOW FAVORITISM
    middle: blank
    cue:    "write 2:5 underneath it, then draw the arrow between"
    strip:  four verses apart   |   "God chose the poor"

PAGE 3 — THE OBJECTION, IN THEIR WORDS
    top:    THAT IS FAVORITISM
    middle: blank
    cue:    "leave empty, this page is for the pause"
    strip:  (nothing)

PAGE 4 — WHERE JAMES IS QUOTING FROM
    top:    LEVITICUS 19
    middle: blank
    cue:    "draw the chapter as a vertical line, mark v15 and v18"
    strip:  19:15 no partiality to poor OR great   |   19:18 love neighbour
            James quotes both, 2:8 and 2:9
```

Note what is absent. Page 3 does not say why the objection fails. Page 4 does not say that the contradiction is already inside the Torah. He says those things. The board only puts the two verses in front of him at the right distance apart.
