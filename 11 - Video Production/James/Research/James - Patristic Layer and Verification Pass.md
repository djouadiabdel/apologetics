## 1. Bede, now actually read

This was the item you said you owed yourself, and it is the one that closed hardest. The ACCS volume carries **49 separate Bede extracts across James**, all from *Concerning the Epistle of St. James*, cited to PL 93. That is not the whole commentary, but it is the commentary's voice on every passage in your series, and it is enough to stop calling him unread.

### 1a. The one that is your refrain, in the eighth century

On 2:19, the demons verse, the verse your whole board turns on:

> "You can believe what God says, you can believe that God exists, and you can believe in him, which means that you love him so much that you want to do what he tells you. There are many evil people around who can manage the first two of these. **Faith with love is Christian, but faith without love is demonic.**"
> Bede, *Concerning the Epistle of St. James*, ACCS NT XI on Jas 2:19, PL 93

Read that last sentence next to your chosen refrain. **Perfect belief, zero communion.** Bede got there first and he got there in the same shape, a two-part contrast with the second half naming the demons. This is not a source that decorates the episode. It is a source that says your reading is the old one.

Note what it also does. Bede splits belief into three, believe what God says, believe that God exists, believe in him. Your board has two columns. **Bede has three steps and the third one is defined as love that acts.** That is a column you do not currently have and it is free.

### 1b. Bede on the Paul problem, which is your whole difficulty page

> "Although the apostle Paul preached that we are justified by faith without works, those who understand by this that it does not matter whether they live evil lives or do wicked and terrible things, as long as they believe in Christ, because salvation is through faith, have made a great mistake. **James here expounds how Paul's words ought to be understood.**"
> Bede, on Jas 2:20-22

Two things worth having. First, Bede states the abuse of Paul in the abusers' own voice, which is the move your spec calls voicing the objection in their idiom. Second, "James expounds how Paul's words ought to be understood" is a much stronger claim than harmonisation. **Bede does not have James and Paul agreeing at a distance. He has James as the correct reading of Paul.** Decide on camera whether you go that far. It is available to you with a patristic name on it.

### 1c. Bede on Rahab, which strengthens your weakest column

> "Rahab was justified by her faith because she performed works of mercy and showed hospitality to God's people."
> Bede, on Jas 2:25

And the framing before it, that some people would have argued Abraham was a special case since nobody now is asked to do what he was asked. **Bede anticipates the objection to his own example and answers it with the second witness.** That is exactly the two-witness structure your board already builds. He also has the inspire-and-warn pairing, that we know more than Abraham and Rahab did and can therefore do more, and will be judged more severely if we do less.

### 1d. Bede's honest limit, and you should quote it against yourself

> "The works mentioned here are works of faith. No one can have perfect works unless he has faith, but **many have perfect faith without works, since they do not always have time to do them.**"
> Bede, on Jas 2:22, with the ACCS editorial footnote explaining the case is martyrdom, someone who dies before having time to act

This one cuts against the harder version of your landing, and that is why it belongs on the board. Bede is a patristic-era source conceding that faith can be real and unexpressed when circumstance forecloses expression. **It is the same qualifier your scrupulosity paragraph in §9b arrives at from the clinical side, reached seven hundred years earlier from the pastoral side.** Putting Bede next to the psychiatry note is the single best defence you have against the charge that the qualifier is modern softness.

### 1e. Bede on chapter 3, for Episode A

Four that are ready to use.

> "The tongue is a fire which can destroy a whole forest of good works just by saying things which are evil. **This fire is the exact opposite of that saving fire which is also like a tongue**, and which consumes all the dross and chaff of our vices. The saints are inflamed by it, they burn with love because of it, and by their preaching they set others ablaze like tongues of fire."
> Bede, on Jas 3:6

That is Pentecost read against James 3, two fires and two tongues, and it is an adjacency you did not have. **Acts 2 and James 3 as the same image inverted.** Put them in two columns and say nothing.

> "Not only is it impossible for sweet and bitter water to come out of one and the same fountain, it is also true that **if the two get mixed, it is the bitter which will affect the sweet, not the other way round.** Put blessing and cursing together, and cursing will win out every time."
> Bede, on Jas 3:11-12

James says the spring cannot do both. Bede says that when it does, the direction of contamination is fixed. That is a claim about contagion with a direction, and it sits very close to what you want to say about mimetic transmission in §7 of the main document, without needing Girard's name.

> "These great ships stand for the minds of people in this life. The strong winds which threaten them are the desires of these minds, by which they are naturally forced to act."
> Bede, on Jas 3:4

Bede reads the rudder image as being about **desire**, which is your bridge from Episode A into Episode B before the chapter break arrives.

> "It is clear from this that a heart which is not right with God cannot bring forth the words or the works of righteousness."
> Bede, on Jas 3:12

### 1f. What Bede does not give you

He is a compiler and a moralist more than an exegete of the Greek. **He does not discuss `dikaioō`'s range, he does not notice the `argos` pun, and he does not treat `eplērōthē` as prophecy language.** The four best philological moves in your Part One are yours and not his. That is worth knowing so you do not go looking for a patristic warrant that is not there, and it is worth saying on camera if anyone accuses you of ventriloquising the Fathers.

## 2. Augustine, *On Faith and Works*

**Narrowed, not closed.** The full text is not on this machine and is not in Downloads. But the ACCS volume quotes it directly on James 2:17:

> "I do not understand why the Lord said, 'If you want to enter into eternal life, keep the commandments,' and then mentioned the commandments relating to good behavior, if one is able to enter into eternal life without observing them."
> Augustine, *On Faith and Works* 15.25, ACCS NT XI on Jas 2:17

So you have one excerpt from the book, at the exact verse you needed it, arguing from Matthew 19:17 rather than from James. The ACCS source list confirms the Latin title *De fide et operibus*.

Also present, and closer to your thesis:

> "In order to help them, God has put fear in the hearts of believers, **lest they think that they might be saved by faith alone, even if they continue to practice these evils.**"
> Augustine, *On Continence* 14.13, on Jas 2:14

**This does not yet change Part One materially, and the main document said it would only if you could get the whole book.** One paragraph is not the book. Keep the item open, but you can now say honestly on camera that Augustine wrote a treatise on this question and quote a line from it, which you could not do before.

## 3. Girard on the Aqedah

**Closed. Two locations, both on this machine, and they say different things.**

> "Human victims were probably offered long before animal victims were substituted for humans. **(Abraham's near sacrifice of Isaac in Genesis 22 is probably an allusion to this.)**"
> Girard, *I See Satan Fall Like Lightning*, chapter on the founding murder

> "In the sacrifice of Isaac **the necessity of sacrifice threatens the most precious being, only to be satisfied, at the last moment, with a substituted victim, the ram sent by God.**"
> Girard, *Things Hidden Since the Foundation of the World*, part II, on the biblical treatment of the founding mechanism, around p. 143 in the Stanford edition

Note the temperature difference and use it. In *I See Satan* the Aqedah is a parenthesis and hedged twice, "probably" and "allusion." In *Things Hidden* it is stated flatly as substitution. **Your main document's paraphrase, that Girard reads the Aqedah as belonging to the movement away from human sacrifice with the ram substituting for the child, is accurate to both.** You can attribute it now.

One caution the files put in front of you. *Things Hidden* attaches this to a footnote quoting **Gregory Nazianzus**, who asks why the blood of the Son would please a Father "who did not wish to accept Isaac offered as a burnt offering by Abraham, but replaced that human sacrifice by that of a ram." *Oration* 45.22, PG 36:654. **A fourth-century bishop making the anti-sacrificial argument Girard is often accused of importing from outside.** That is a better card than the Girard citation itself, and it is the one to play if a commenter says the reading is modern.

## 4. Chesterton

**Split verdict. Verify by book, not in bulk.**

**Verified, because *Orthodoxy* is on this machine in two editions.** Both references your Episode B section makes are real and correctly located.

- Chapter II is titled "The Maniac." The line is: **"The madman is not the man who has lost his reason. The madman is the man who has lost everything except his reason."** Your paraphrase, reason perfectly intact inside a closed system, is faithful. Nearby and also usable: "The madman's explanation of a thing is always complete, and often in a purely rational sense satisfactory," and "If the madman could for an instant become careless, he would become sane."
- Chapter VII is titled "The Eternal Revolution," as your document says.

**Still unverified, and nothing on this machine can fix it.** *The Everlasting Man*, *What's Wrong with the World*, and *Heretics* are not in Downloads. A metadata sweep of all 1,166 PDFs turned up no copy under any filename. **So the stream line, the assent-and-attempt line, and the hope-as-a-virtue line from *Heretics* stay off camera until you get real editions.** The main document was right that the stream line is widely quoted and widely mangled, and that is exactly the kind of line that gets a channel like yours caught.

Practical note. All three are public domain and on Project Gutenberg. This is a twenty-minute fix, not a research problem.

## 7. `Ho dikaios` at 5:6, and this one got harder

The main document told you not to resolve 5:6 for the sake of a better ending. **The NOAB annotation leans against the reading that would have given you the better ending.**

> "**Condemned and murdered the righteous one**, possibly a reference to Jesus's crucifixion but **more likely to the general mistreatment of the poor by the rich** as elsewhere in the letter (2.6b-7; 5.4; cf. Sir 34.26-27)."
> New Oxford Annotated Bible, NRSV, note on Jas 5.6

Sit with that, because it is inconvenient. The Girardian finale in §on Episode C wants `ho dikaios` to be singular, articular, and pointing at the Christ. **A mainstream critical annotation says the generic reading is the likelier one**, and it reaches that by an argument from the letter's own pattern, which is the kind of argument you respect everywhere else.

**Two honest ways to film this and you should pick one before you record, not on camera.**

The first is to argue the minority reading and flag it as minority. The article plus the singular plus "who was not opposing you" is a real argument and you are allowed to make it.

The second is stronger and more your channel. **Take the generic reading and notice that it does not weaken the Girardian point, it generalises it.** If `ho dikaios` is the poor man in the assembly rather than Christ on the cross, then James is saying the profile of the innocent non-resisting victim is being enacted in ordinary economic life, repeatedly, by people who go to church. **That is a worse indictment than the Christological reading, not a lesser one.** And it keeps you honest with the commentary rather than against it.

Do not take door one because it ends better. The document warned you about exactly that and now there is a named source making the warning concrete.
