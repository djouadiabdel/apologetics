Read first (short, and bullseye)

Gregory of Nyssa, On "Not Three Gods" (To Ablabius) — 19 min. This is the single most on-target text in the whole list. Its entire job is answering "distinct persons means three gods." It builds the being/person (ousia/hypostasis) distinction and the one-operation argument. You already have it marked "Read." Start here.

Tertullian, Against Praxeas — 1h22. Praxeas was a modalist, so this treatise defends the exact middle your friend keeps denying: not "the same thing" (modalism) and not "two gods," but three persons in one substance. It is the Latin origin of "one substance, three persons." This kills his "either identical or two gods" dilemma at the root.

Gregory of Nyssa, To Eustathius (9 min) and On the Faith, to Simplicius (5 min). Two quick reinforcements of one-essence, three-persons. High value per minute.

Then (the "begotten is not lesser" core)

Gregory of Nazianzus, Select Orations — the list says 13h, but you only need the Five Theological Orations inside it, and within those, Orations 29 and 30 (On the Son). This is the source of "greater as cause, equal in nature," and the argument that making unbegottenness the definition of God defines the Most High by a negation. That is the precise answer to his "being the source is the whole point."

Gregory of Nyssa, Against Eunomius — 12h, so targeted reading only, mainly Book 1. Read it because Eunomius argued that God's very essence is "being unbegotten," so a begotten Son cannot be God. That is your friend's completeness/source argument, verbatim, from the fourth century, and this is the definitive refutation of it. If any one text is aimed at his strongest move, it is this.

Foundations (the creed itself)

First Council of Nicaea (325) and First Council of Constantinople (381) — skim for the creed and the anti-Arian anathemas, where "Light from Light, true God from true God, begotten not made, of one substance" comes from. Most of the listed hours are surrounding acts; the creed is short.

Basil, On the Holy Spirit — 2h15. The classic on order-of-persons without inequality, and the inseparable-operation argument. Reinforces "an order of origin is not an order of rank."

Islam-facing and systematic

John of Damascus, An Exact Exposition of the Orthodox Faith — long, but Book 1 is the target, and it is already in your vault plan. Being/person, cause-and-caused with no difference of nature, time, or dignity, written by a man arguing inside the Islamic world.

Optional deep dives

Hilary of Poitiers, On the Trinity — 10h. The major Latin anti-Arian work, and a good stand-in since Augustine's De Trinitate is not on this site.

Anselm, Monologion — treats the Word and the Spirit as God's own self-knowledge and self-love by reason alone, which answers Ibn Taymiyya's "why three, and why would an attribute be a person" from the rational side. Proslogion (God as "that than which nothing greater can be conceived") speaks to the completeness argument.

Skip for this purpose

Clement's Stromata, Cyril's Catechetical Lectures, Dionysius's Divine Names, Athenagoras, Justin's Sole Government, Basil's Letters, and the minor fragments are either too broad or aimed elsewhere. One caution: Origen, On First Principles gave us "eternal generation" (useful), but it also carries subordinationist tendencies the Arians later weaponized, so take the clean version from the Cappadocians above instead.

Shortest path if you only do three: On "Not Three Gods," then Against Praxeas, then Gregory of Nazianzus Orations 29 and 30. That is under four hours and it covers his whole attack.

# Trinity Reading Plan — Being vs Person, Begottenness, and Ibn Taymiyya

## What this file is for

This is not a general "Trinity books" list. It is aimed at the exact walls a sharp Muslim interlocutor keeps hitting. Read each book for the specific answer named next to it.

The objections this plan is built to dissolve:

1. **The "lack" objection.** The Son does not have "being the source / being unbegotten." So he lacks something the Father has, so he is lesser, so he is not God.
2. **The being vs person objection.** If Father and Son are distinct in any way, they are two gods, and distinction in one God is just hidden polytheism.
3. **The completeness objection.** God is complete, and two different things cannot both be complete, so the divine nature can hold only one person.
4. **The "is he more than a sifa" objection.** An attribute has no will and does not subsist on its own, so comparing the Son to an attribute proves nothing.

The whole solution turns on one distinction: **nature is the "what," person is the "who."** How many gods is a what question, and the answer is one. The three is entirely at the who level. Every book below is here to make that distinction unbreakable and to show that "from the Father" is a relation, not a lack.

## Tier 1 — Start here (the what/who wall)

### Frank Sheed, *Theology and Sanity*

Read first. Sheed's central claim is that people cannot think about the Trinity because they never separated **nature (what) from person (who)**. That is your friend's exact block.

- Read the whole **section on God and the Blessed Trinity in Part One**, especially the chapters where he builds the **nature and person** distinction ("a nature answers the question *what*, a person answers the question *who*") and then the chapters titled around **"The Three Persons"** and **"Some Further Precisions."**
- Solves objection **2**. Gives you the plain-language engine for the "one what, three whos" move.

### Gilles Emery, O.P., *The Trinity: An Introduction to Catholic Doctrine on the Triune God*

The best single book for the *mechanics* your friend attacks. Emery is the leading Thomist on the Trinity, and this is the accessible entry to the full system.

- Read the **chapter on the biblical revelation of Father, Son, and Spirit** for grounding, then go hard on the three core chapters: **the notion of "person,"** **the trinitarian relations (relations of origin),** and **the divine processions (the generation of the Son and the spiration of the Spirit).**
- Also read the chapter on **perichoresis** (the mutual indwelling of the persons) for the "not a part, not a tenant, they are in one another" point.
- Solves objections **1, 2, 3, 4**. This is where "the persons are subsistent relations, and a relation adds nothing absolute" is laid out. That single idea answers the "lack" and the "completeness" objections at once.

---

## Tier 2 — The mechanics, deeper

### Thomas Joseph White, O.P., *The Trinity: On the Nature and Mystery of the One God* (2022)

The best modern comprehensive treatment. Read after Emery if you want the complete map.

- Focus on the **chapters on divine simplicity and the Trinity** (how one simple, complete God is compatible with real internal distinction) and the **chapters on the processions, relations, and persons.**
- Solves objection **3** directly. This is where the completeness/simplicity worry ("two complete things cannot both exist") is answered with care: one completeness, wholly the Father's and wholly the Son's, distinguished only by relation.

### Khaled Anatolios, *Retrieving Nicaea: The Development and Meaning of Trinitarian Doctrine* (2011)

Best on *how* the Church actually defeated the "from the Father, therefore lesser" argument, which is Arianism, which is your friend's argument in ancient dress.

- Read the **chapter on Athanasius** and then **Part Two, the systematic synthesis,** for the logic of why derivation of origin does not entail inferiority of being.
- Solves objection **1** historically. Shows the "lack / lesser" move was made, pressed hard, and answered, seventeen centuries ago.

## Tier 3 — The Fathers who killed "begotten means lesser" (read the sources)

### Gregory of Nazianzus, *The Five Theological Orations* (Orations 27 to 31)

Short, and directly on your friend's atom/completeness fight. Use the Popular Patristics edition (*On God and Christ*).

- **Oration 29 (On the Son, part 1):** dismantles the Arian dilemmas about begetting ("was he begotten when he already existed, or when he did not"). This is the answer to "being sourced is a defect."
- **Oration 30 (On the Son, part 2):** handles the subordination proof texts, including "the Father is greater than I" (John 14:28). This is the origin of **"greater as cause, equal in nature,"** and of the warning that making *unbegottenness* the definition of God defines the Most High by a negation.
- Solves objection **1**. Pair it with [[I Am Going to the Father]] and [[Only the Father Knows the Hour]] in your vault.

### Athanasius, *Orations Against the Arians* (Contra Arianos), Discourse 1

- Read **Discourse 1** for eternal generation as the radiance of light: the ray is from the light, never after it, never dimmer, and the light was never without its ray. Read *On the Incarnation* alongside it, but note that book is about the incarnation and salvation, while the anti-Arian generation material is in the *Orations*.
- Solves objection **1**.

### Augustine, *The Trinity* (De Trinitate), Books 5 to 7

- **Book 5** is the metaphysical core: whatever is said of God with respect to himself is common and equal to all three; only what is said *relatively* (Father, Son) distinguishes them. Being unbegotten or begotten is a **relation, not a difference of substance.**
- **Book 7** on why we say "three persons, one substance."
- Solves objections **1 and 2**. This is the root of "the difference is relation, and relation is not a quantity of Godhood." Books 8 to 15 (the mind, knowledge, love analogies) are optional for intuition.

### John of Damascus, *An Exact Exposition of the Orthodox Faith*, Book 1

The Greek fathers systematized, by a saint who lived under Muslim rule and wrote with the Islamic objection in view. This is the patron of your whole strategy.

- **Book 1, chapters 8 to 14,** especially **chapter 8** ("Concerning the Holy Trinity"): one essence, three hypostases, distinguished only by the properties of fatherhood, sonship, and procession. The Father alone is **cause**, the Son and Spirit are **caused**, but cause and caused carry **no difference of nature, time, or dignity.**
- Solves objections **1, 2, 4**. Cross-link [[THE LOGOS ARGUMENT — Complete Map]].

## Tier 4 — Facing Islam directly

How Arabic-speaking Christians defended the Trinity to Muslims using the **divine-attributes argument**, the exact sifat and Logos move you have been running.

## Ibn Taymiyya — what he did, what he conceded, and why it matters

### Who and what

Taqi al-Din Ibn Taymiyya (1263 to 1328) is the intellectual father of the Salafi movement. Around the turn of the fourteenth century he wrote **Al-Jawab al-Sahih li-man baddala din al-Masih** ("The Correct Answer to those who altered the religion of Christ"), a multi-volume refutation of Christianity.

It was written in reply to a specific Christian text, the **Letter from the People of Cyprus** (Risala ahl Qubrus), which was itself a reworking of **Paul of Antioch**, the Melkite bishop of Sidon, and his *Letter to a Muslim Friend*. Paul had argued that the Quran, read carefully, does not condemn true Christians and even supports Christian claims. Ibn Taymiyya set out to bury that argument.

The single most useful fact: the sharpest anti-Christian mind in Sunni history did not wave the Trinity away in a paragraph. He wrote **volumes**. That alone tells your friend the argument is not trivially stupid, whatever else is true.

### What he engaged (not a strawman)

Ibn Taymiyya took on the real Christian argument, the one you are using: that the three hypostases (aqanim) line up with God's own attributes, the **Son with the Word or Knowledge (kalima), the Spirit with the Life (hayat)**. He understood that the Arabic Christians were building the Trinity out of the very attributes Islam affirms. He did not pretend they were worshipping three separate gods from outside.

### What he conceded (the shared ground)

These are premises he grants, which are the raw material of your case:

1. **God has real, distinct, eternal attributes.** As an Athari, Ibn Taymiyya affirms the sifat robustly, more literally than the Ash'aris do. Knowledge, Life, Power, Speech, and the rest are real, eternal, uncreated, and subsist in God. They are "not Him and not other than Him."
2. **God genuinely has a Word and a Life.** He does not deny the reality the Christian points at. The disagreement is about what to make of it, not whether it is there.
3. **The argument deserves a serious answer.** By engaging it at book length, he concedes it is not a joke. He narrows the fight rather than dismissing it.

### The door he closes for his own followers

Ibn Taymiyya **rejected the Ash'ari doctrine of kalam nafsi**, the idea that God's eternal speech is a single silent inner meaning and the Arabic words are a created expression of it. He held that God speaks with real words, by His will. Effect for the debate: **a Salafi who follows Ibn Taymiyya cannot escape the Logos argument by saying "the uncreated Word is just abstract inner meaning."** He is committed to God's Word being God's actual, uncreated speech. That tightens the "the Word was cast into Mary and became Jesus" trap, it does not loosen it. See [[Word of God solved]].

### What he did NOT concede (his actual line of defense)

His refutation does not rest on "it is polytheism" or "distinction in God is impossible." He already believes in real distinction inside the one God. His three real objections are narrower, and they are exactly where your friend has landed:

1. **Why three?** If each divine attribute were a hypostasis, God has many attributes (power, will, hearing, sight, and more), so you would get many persons, not three. The selection of exactly three looks arbitrary.
2. **An attribute is not a person.** Knowledge does not know. Life does not act or will on its own. An attribute inheres in the essence, it does not subsist as an independent agent. So turning Word and Life into distinct subsisting persons who can act and become incarnate is, for him, a category error. This is your friend's "is Jesus more than a sifa" objection, made 700 years earlier.
3. **The Incarnation is incoherent.** The infinite, eternal Creator uniting to a created body, the unlimited being localized, he presses as a contradiction.

### Why this matters for your fight

Notice where Ibn Taymiyya's real objection sits. **It is not at the what level. It is at the who level.** He grants distinction inside the one God (that is his own creed). He disputes the **number** of distinctions, the **personalization** of them, and the **incarnation.** In other words, the sharpest Muslim critic already conceded the ground your friend keeps fighting on ("two distinct things cannot both be God," "distinction is polytheism") and moved to the harder, narrower ground: **can an attribute be a person, and can that person incarnate.**

So the "why three" and the "attributes are not persons" objections are the ones to prepare for, because they are the ones the tradition's best mind actually stood on. Your answers:

- **Why three, not more:** the persons are not derived by counting attributes. They are the three subsistent **relations of origin** in God's one act of self-knowing and self-loving: the Source, the Word the Source speaks of himself, and the Love breathed between them. Relations of origin come in that structure. It is not a tally of qualities. This is the material in Emery (relations and processions) and Augustine (Book 5) and Richard of St. Victor below.
- **Attributes are not persons, agreed:** so the Son is not a mere attribute. He is a **person**, a subsistent "who," not a quality. Worship is the marker of it. But "more than an attribute" does not mean "a separate god," it means a distinct relation of the one being. The distinction he wants (attribute vs person) is the distinction you have been making all along.
- **Incarnation:** a separate track, handled by the two-natures material in [[I Am Going to the Father]] and [[Only the Father Knows the Hour]].

### Bonus for the "why relationship inside God" angle

**Richard of St. Victor, *On the Trinity*.** Argues that God being perfect love *requires* more than one person, because love must have a beloved. This meets Ibn Taymiyya's "why more than one" from the other side and ties into the samad point: a solitary God would need a creation to have anything to love, while the triune God needs nothing outside himself. Read it after Emery.

## Debate crosswalk (objection to answer)

- "The Son lacks being-begotten, so he is lesser." → Gregory, Orations 29 and 30. Augustine, Book 5. Emery on relations. [[I Am Going to the Father]].
- "Distinction means two gods / polytheism." → Sheed (what/who). Augustine, Book 5. [[The Trinity Is Shirk (Polytheism)]].
- "Two complete things cannot both be God." → White on simplicity. Emery on person and relation.
- "Is he more than a sifa? An attribute is not a person." → John of Damascus, Book 1. Emery on the notion of person. Ibn Taymiyya section above (this is his real objection).
- "Why three?" → Emery on processions and relations. Richard of St. Victor. Augustine.
- "The one God cannot have more than one holder." → the whole plan. The word "holder" hides the what/who ambiguity. Every proof reaches one *being*, never one *person*.

## Reading order in one line

Sheed, then Emery, then White for the system. Gregory and Augustine Book 5 for "begotten not lesser." John of Damascus for the Islam-facing version. Griffith and Michel's Ibn Taymiyya for the fight itself.
