[[That they may know that you alone, whose name is Yahweh]]

## THE AXIOM — Same Lock as Before

**Psalm 83:18:**

> _"That they may know that you alone, whose name is **Yahweh**, are the Most High over all the earth."_

Get the same agreement:

- One Most High
    
- His name is Yahweh
    

Now the only question is: **does the Tanakh itself describe the Messiah as Yahweh?** Not the New Testament. Not Paul. Not any Christian source. The Torah and the prophets alone.

## EXHIBIT 1 — Psalm 110:1: Two Lords, One Psalm

**Psalm 110:1:**

> _"**Yahweh** said to my **Lord** (אֲדֹנִי — Adoni): 'Sit at my right hand, until I make your enemies your footstool.'"_

This is **David writing.** David says: Yahweh spoke to _my Lord._

Two distinct persons:

- **Yahweh** — the one speaking
    
- **David's Lord (Adoni)** — the one being addressed
    

David calls this second person **his Lord** — meaning someone David himself submits to. David was the King of Israel. Who is _above_ David? Not an angel — David didn't call angels his Lord. Not another human king — there is none above him in Israel.

Now add verse 4:

**Psalm 110:4:**

> _"**Yahweh** has sworn and will not change his mind, 'You are a priest forever after the order of **Melchizedek**.'"_

Yahweh calls David's Lord a **priest-king forever** — eternal priesthood. Human priests die. This one doesn't.

The Messiah David describes is:

1. Above David himself
    
2. Seated at Yahweh's right hand
    
3. An eternal priest-king
    

This is inside the Torah. Before Jesus was born. David embedded it.


## EXHIBIT 2 — Proverbs 30:4: The Torah Asks for the Son's Name

**Proverbs 30:4:**

> _"Who has ascended to heaven and come down? Who has gathered the wind in his fists? Who has wrapped up the waters in a garment? Who has established all the ends of the earth? **What is his name, and what is his son's name? Surely you know!**"_

The writer describes Yahweh — the creator, the one who controls wind and water and established the earth. Then immediately asks two questions:

1. What is **his** name?
    
2. What is **his son's** name?
    

The Torah is embedding a **Son** into the identity of Yahweh the creator. This is not the New Testament. This is not Paul. This is Proverbs — Hebrew wisdom literature — asking about Yahweh's Son as if the Son's name is a known mystery hiding in plain sight. _"Surely you know"_ is sarcasm aimed at people who claim to know God but don't know his Son.


## EXHIBIT 3 — Isaiah 9:6: The Child is Called Mighty God

**Isaiah 9:6:**

> _"For to us a child is born, to us a son is given; and the government shall be upon his shoulder, and his name shall be called **Wonderful Counselor, Mighty God (אֵל גִּבּוֹר — El Gibbor), Everlasting Father, Prince of Peace.**"_

**אֵל גִּבּוֹר** — El Gibbor — is not a title given to angels, kings, or prophets anywhere in the Tanakh. Look at where else this exact title appears:

**Isaiah 10:21:**

> _"A remnant will return, the remnant of Jacob, to the **Mighty God (El Gibbor)**."_

In Isaiah 10:21, El Gibbor is **Yahweh himself** — the one the remnant of Israel returns to. The identical title is given in Isaiah 9:6 to the child who is born.

Isaiah calls the coming child by the name that Isaiah himself uses for Yahweh. This is not metaphor — **Hebrew kings were never called El Gibbor.** Isaiah never calls any human ruler El Gibbor. He reserves it for Yahweh — and then applies it to the child.

## EXHIBIT 4 — Micah 5:2: His Origins Are From Eternity

**Micah 5:2:**

> _"But you, O Bethlehem Ephrathah, who are too little to be among the clans of Judah, from you shall come forth for me one who is to be ruler in Israel, **whose coming forth is from of old, from ancient days (מִימֵי עוֹלָם — from the days of eternity).**"_

The ruler who will come from Bethlehem has **eternal origins.** The Hebrew _מִימֵי עוֹלָם_ means from the days of eternity — the same language used for Yahweh's own eternal existence. No human being has origins from eternity. No angel born into history in Bethlehem has origins from eternity.

The Torah is describing a being who exists from eternity entering history through a specific city.

## EXHIBIT 5 — Zechariah 12:10: Yahweh Says "They Pierced Me"

This is the most direct and explosive verse in the entire argument.

**Zechariah 12:10:**

> _"And I will pour out on the house of David and the inhabitants of Jerusalem a spirit of grace and pleas for mercy, so that, when they look on **me**, on **him whom they have pierced**, they shall mourn for him, as one mourns for an only child, and weep bitterly over him, as one weeps over a firstborn."_

**Yahweh is speaking in first person throughout Zechariah 12.** The entire passage is: _"I will do this... I will do that... the inhabitants of Jerusalem..."_

Then Yahweh says: _"they will look on **me whom they have pierced.**"_

**Yahweh says he will be pierced.** Not a servant. Not a proxy. Yahweh himself — **me.** And Israel will mourn for him like a **firstborn son** who died.

There is no escape route here:

- If Yahweh cannot be pierced, then this prophecy is impossible — which contradicts the Torah's own promise
    
- If Yahweh can take on a form that is pierced, then the Incarnation is theologically consistent with the Torah
    
- If this is about someone else — then Yahweh lied, because he said _"me"_
    

Ask your opponent: **who else in all of history was pierced and mourned over by Israel as a firstborn son, in fulfillment of a Zechariah prophecy?**

## EXHIBIT 6 — Genesis 19:24: Two Yahwehs in the Torah Itself

**Genesis 19:24:**

> _"Then **Yahweh** rained on Sodom and Gomorrah sulfur and fire from **Yahweh** out of heaven."_

**Yahweh on earth** rained down fire from **Yahweh in heaven.** One verse. One event. Two persons each called **Yahweh.**

This is the first book of the Torah. The author of Genesis — who is meticulous about not calling anyone Yahweh who is not Yahweh — uses the divine name **twice in the same sentence** for two distinct persons acting simultaneously.

If you say _"the second Yahweh is just an angel"_ — show me anywhere in the Torah where an angel is called Yahweh and exercises Yahweh's sovereign judgment of a city. You cannot. Angels are called _malak Yahweh_ (messenger of Yahweh) — never Yahweh himself. Genesis 19:24 gives both persons the full divine name, no qualification.

## EXHIBIT 7 — Zechariah 2:10-11: Yahweh Sent by Yahweh

**Zechariah 2:10-11:**

> _"Sing and rejoice, O daughter of Zion, for behold, **I come** and I will dwell in your midst, declares **Yahweh**. And many nations shall join themselves to Yahweh in that day, and shall be my people. And **I will dwell in your midst**, and you shall know that **Yahweh of hosts has sent me to you.**"_

Parse this carefully:

- **"I come"** = Yahweh speaking, saying he will personally come and dwell among his people
    
- Then: **"Yahweh of hosts has sent me"**
    

**Yahweh says Yahweh sent him.**

The one speaking is Yahweh. The one who sent him is Yahweh. Two distinct persons, both Yahweh, one sending the other to come and dwell among Israel. This is not the New Testament reading Yahweh into Yahweh — this is **Zechariah's own grammar doing it.**

## EXHIBIT 8 — Daniel 7:13-14: The Son of Man Receives Worship

**Daniel 7:13-14:**

> _"I saw in the night visions, and behold, with the clouds of heaven there came one like **a son of man**, and he came to the Ancient of Days and was presented before him. And to him was given **dominion and glory and a kingdom**, that all peoples, nations, and languages should **serve/worship him (יִפְלְחוּן — pelach)**; his dominion is an everlasting dominion, which shall not pass away, and his kingdom one that shall not be destroyed."_

The Hebrew/Aramaic verb **פְּלַח (pelach)** used here for "serve/worship" is the same word used throughout Daniel exclusively for **divine worship.** In Daniel 3:12, the Babylonians accuse the three Hebrews of refusing to _pelach_ the king's gods — meaning: refusing religious worship. In Daniel 6:16, Daniel's accusers say he worships (_pelach_) his God.

The Son of Man in Daniel 7 receives _pelach_ — divine worship — from **all peoples, nations, and languages** with an **everlasting kingdom.**

The Torah gives divine worship only to Yahweh. The Shema (Deuteronomy 6:4) establishes this absolutely. Yet Daniel shows the Son of Man receiving universal divine worship on the same level as the Ancient of Days.

There is only one coherent explanation: the Son of Man shares in Yahweh's divine identity. The Torah does not allow _pelach_ to be given to anyone else.
