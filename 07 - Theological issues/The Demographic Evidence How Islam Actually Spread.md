## Objection 2 — "10 Days of Food Per Year Is Nothing, People Can Save It"

**Why this is idiotic on four levels:**

**Level 1 — It assumes subsistence farmers have savings.**

"They can save it" presupposes disposable income. The majority of the population in 7th–11th century Egypt, North Africa, and the Levant were agricultural laborers and craftsmen operating at or near subsistence. Subsistence means you eat what you produce. There is no surplus to save. Ten days of family food is not an inconvenience for these people — it is the margin between feeding your children and not feeding them. The objection transplants a modern middle-class assumption onto a medieval agrarian economy.[[reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/AskHistorians/comments/3u2bts/how_much_of_a_financial_burden_was_the_jizya_on/)]​

**Level 2 — It was not per household. It was per adult male.**

The jizya was assessed **per individual adult male**, not per family. A household with a father and three adult sons paid four times the stated rate. So the "10 days of food" becomes 40 days of food for a family with multiple adult males — nearly six weeks of subsistence wiped out annually. For a poor family, that is catastrophic.[[en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jizya)]​

**Level 3 — "Saving it" ignores the collection method.**

The jizya was not a self-filing annual tax you budget for. It was collected in-person, on demand, with the dhimmi in a physically submissive position. If you couldn't pay when the collector arrived, you were imprisoned until someone paid for you, or your property was seized, or — as documented in Coptic sources — your children were taken. There was no installment plan. There was no appeals process. The system was designed to make non-payment immediately catastrophic, not merely inconvenient.jewishrefugees+1

**Level 4 — The Cairo Geniza destroys this objection with actual data.**

The Cairo Geniza isn't theory — it's real community financial records. S.D. Goitein's analysis found the Jewish community in Fatimid Cairo was **collectively fundraising among wealthy members specifically to pay jizya for the poor**. Communities had formal institutions — called _korobka_ equivalents in some regions — dedicated to covering jizya obligations for members who couldn't pay. If 10 days of food per year was nothing, wealthy community members would not have needed to organize charity drives to prevent poor members from being imprisoned or converting. The existence of that institution is direct evidence that the objection is false.[[reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/AskHistorians/comments/3u2bts/how_much_of_a_financial_burden_was_the_jizya_on/)]​
