# MARK 13:32 — "Only the Father Knows the Hour"
## The Verse That Destroys Itself When Muslims Use It

---

## THE VERSE

**Mark 13:32:**

> _"But concerning that day or that hour, no one knows, not even the angels in heaven, **nor the Son**, but only the Father."_

This is one of the most frequently used verses by Muslims and anti-Trinitarians to argue that Jesus cannot be God. The argument runs:

> "God is omniscient — he knows everything. Jesus says he doesn't know the hour. Therefore Jesus is not God."

This document shows why this argument:

1. **Destroys Islam before it touches Christianity**
2. **Fails on its own terms** even before consulting any other scripture
3. **Is answered by Mark himself** within the same Gospel
4. **Is resolved by the two-nature doctrine** rooted in Philippians 2
5. **Backfires completely** via the Quran analogy

---

# STEP 1 — THE VERSE DESTROYS ISLAM FIRST

Before defending Christianity, stop and point this out to the Muslim:

**If Jesus said these words and they are true — Islam is false.**
**If Jesus said these words and they are false — Islam is false.**

There is no third option.

### Why?

Islam accepts Jesus as a true prophet whose words are reliable. The Quran says:

- Jesus is Al-Masih, born of a virgin, who performed miracles (3:45-49)
- The Gospel (Injeel) was given to Jesus by Allah (5:46)
- Muslims are commanded to believe in what was sent down to Jesus (2:136)

Now look at what Jesus says in Mark 13:32:

> _"**Nor the Son**, but only the Father."_

Jesus explicitly distinguishes himself from the Father as a **separate person** who is called **"the Son."**

This is not a generic "son of God" the way all believers are called children of God. This is **the Son** — set apart in a hierarchy above all humans and all angels, in a category entirely by himself, directly below the Father.

**If this is true:** Jesus is describing a relationship with the Father that is unique, eternal, and above all creation — which is exactly the Christian claim. Islam is false.

**If this is false:** Jesus invented a unique divine identity for himself — making himself "the Son" in a class above angels — which means he was a liar and a blasphemer, not a true prophet. Islam is false.

> **Ask the Muslim:** "Before you use this verse against me — do you realize you are quoting a verse where Jesus calls himself 'the Son' in a unique hierarchy above all angels? If that's true, he's not a Muslim prophet. If it's false, he's a false prophet. Either way, you just quoted yourself out of Islam."

# STEP 2 — THE HIERARCHY IN THE VERSE ITSELF

Even ignoring every other scripture, the verse already contains a **ladder of knowledge:**

> _"No one knows... not even the angels in heaven... nor the Son... but only the Father."_

The order is:

| Level | Being | Knowledge of the Hour |
|---|---|---|
| Bottom | All human beings | Do not know |
| Middle | Angels in heaven | Do not know |
| Above angels | **The Son** | Does not know in this role |
| Top | The Father | Knows |

**The Son is placed above all of creation — above every human, above every angel — yet below the Father.**

No Muslim prophet is ever placed above the entire angelic realm in a cosmic hierarchy. No prophet is placed in a two-person relationship with "the Father" as his Son. This verse is not describing a prophet. It is describing a being in a category entirely above creation, in a filial relationship with God himself.

# STEP 3 — THE TWO NATURES: WHY THIS IS NOT A CONTRADICTION

The Christian claim is not that Jesus is simply God. The Christian claim is that Jesus is **the God-Man** — fully divine and fully human in one person. This is called the **hypostatic union.**

**Philippians 2:6-8:**

> _"Who, though he was in the **form of God** (μορφῇ θεοῦ), did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped, but **emptied himself**, taking the **form of a servant** (μορφὴν δούλου), being born in the likeness of men."_

The Greek word **μορφή (morphē)** means the essential, outward expression of an inner nature — not a costume or a performance. Paul says:

- Jesus was in the **morphē of God** = his essential nature is divine
- He took the **morphē of a servant** = he genuinely took on human nature

**"Emptied himself" (ἐκένωσεν — ekenōsen)** does not mean he stopped being God. It means he **voluntarily set aside the independent use** of his divine prerogatives — not the attributes themselves, but their autonomous exercise — while taking on human limitations genuinely.

This means:

- In his **divine nature**: Jesus is omniscient. He knows all things.
- In his **human nature and servant role**: Jesus experiences genuine human limitations — including not retaining in his human consciousness knowledge the Father had not chosen to give him at that moment.

A genuine human being cannot be omniscient. If Jesus was truly human — not pretending to be human but actually human — then in his humanity he must experience real knowledge limitations. To say "Jesus must have known everything in his human mind or he's not God" is to say Jesus was never truly human at all — which destroys the incarnation and the atonement.

# STEP 4 — THE SERVANT ROLE IN MARK ITSELF

You do not need to leave the Gospel of Mark to prove this.

**Mark 10:45:**

> _"For even the **Son of Man** did not come to be served but **to serve**, and to give his life as a ransom for many."_

Jesus explicitly defines his earthly role: **servant.** He came as the servant of the Father.

Now go to **John 15:15:**

> _"I no longer call you servants, because a **servant does not know his master's business.** Instead, I have called you friends, for everything that I learned from my Father I have made known to you."_

Jesus himself establishes the principle: **a servant only knows what the master discloses to him.**

Connect the two:

1. Jesus assumes the role of **servant** (Mark 10:45)
2. A servant only knows what the **master chooses to reveal** (John 15:15)
3. Therefore in his role as servant, Jesus' human consciousness **only retains what the Father gives him to know**
4. The Father had not disclosed the hour to the Son in his human servant-consciousness
5. Mark 13:32 describes **not an ontological limitation** (Jesus cannot know) but a **relational/functional one** (the Father has not disclosed this to the Son's human consciousness in his servant role)

This is not forced. This is what the text itself requires when read in its own context.

# STEP 5 — MARK HIMSELF PROVES JESUS IS OMNISCIENT

Muslims say: "Stay in Mark. Don't go to Paul or John." Fine. Stay in Mark.

**Mark 2:5-12 — Three Things Only God Can Do, Done by Jesus Simultaneously:**

**Scene:** A paralyzed man is brought to Jesus.

**First — Jesus forgives sins (v.5):**

> _"Son, your sins are forgiven."_

The scribes immediately respond (v.7):

> _"Why does this fellow talk like that? He's blaspheming! **Who can forgive sins but God alone?**"_

Note: Quran 3:135 says the same thing — only Allah can forgive sins.

Jesus does not correct them and say "I'm not forgiving sins, I'm just announcing God's forgiveness." He proceeds to confirm that he has the authority to forgive sins directly.

**Second — Jesus knows what they are thinking in their hearts (v.8):**

> _"Immediately Jesus knew **in his spirit** what they were thinking in their hearts and said to them, 'Why are you thinking these things?'"_

**1 Kings 8:39:**
> _"...you alone know every human heart."_

The Tanakh says only God knows the hearts of men. Mark says Jesus **immediately** knew what was in their hearts. Mark is identifying Jesus with the God of 1 Kings 8:39.

**Third — Jesus heals the paralysis to prove the first two (v.11-12):**

> _"Get up, take your mat and go home." He got up, took his mat and walked out in full view of them all._

**Psalm 103:2-4:**
> _"Praise the LORD, O my soul... who **forgives all your sins** and **heals all your diseases**, who **redeems your life** from the pit."_

In Mark 2, Jesus:
- **Forgives sins** — Psalm 103 says Yahweh does this
- **Heals disease** — Psalm 103 says Yahweh does this
- **Knows hearts** — 1 Kings 8:39 says only God does this

All three in one scene. All three attributed to Yahweh in the Tanakh. All three performed by Jesus in Mark 2.

> **Ask the Muslim:** "Could Mark be any clearer that Jesus is omniscient and divine? Mark himself — before chapter 13 — has already shown you Jesus knowing hearts, forgiving sins, and healing diseases. Why would Mark contradict himself in chapter 13? The answer is: he doesn't. Chapter 13 is about the servant role. Chapter 2 is about the divine nature. Both are true simultaneously."

# STEP 6 — ACTS 1:7 — "IT IS NOT FOR YOU TO KNOW"

After the resurrection, the disciples ask the same question again:

**Acts 1:6-7:**

> _"Lord, will you at this time restore the kingdom to Israel? He said to them, **'It is not for you to know** the times or seasons that the Father has fixed by his own authority.'"_

Notice the shift:

- **Mark 13:32** (before resurrection): "The Son does not know"
- **Acts 1:7** (after resurrection): "It is **not for you to know**"

Post-resurrection Jesus does not say "I still don't know." He says "**you** are not to know." He places himself outside the group that doesn't know. He is no longer saying "nor the Son" — because in his glorified, resurrected state, the servant-role limitation of his human consciousness has been transformed. He is still not disclosing the time — but the reason is now the Father's sovereign will for the disciples, not his own human limitation.

This transition from "I don't know" to "it's not for you to know" is evidence that Mark 13:32 was always about the **functional servant-role limitation**, not a permanent ontological ignorance. After the resurrection, that limitation is lifted in a new way.

# STEP 7 — THE DISCIPLES AND PETER CONFIRM OMNISCIENCE

Jesus does not correct them. He does not say "no, I don't know all things — there are some things I don't know." He says:

**John 21:17** — After the resurrection, Peter says:

> _"Lord, **you know all things**; you know that I love you."_

Again Jesus does not correct him. Before the resurrection and after the resurrection — the testimony of his own disciples is that he knows all things. **1 John 3:20** confirms:

> _"God... knows everything."_

If Jesus knows everything, and God knows everything, and knowing everything is an attribute of God alone — the conclusion is forced.

**Now apply the same logic:**
