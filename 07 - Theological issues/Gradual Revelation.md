# ADDITION 1 — THE THEOLOGICAL FRAMEWORK: Gradual Revelation

## The Objection This Answers

> _"The Trinity is a Christian invention. The Old Testament Jews knew nothing about it. You are reading New Testament ideas back into the Hebrew scriptures."_

## The Framework

Before any individual text is examined, understand the **structure of how God reveals himself** throughout the Tanakh.

God did not reveal everything about his nature at once. He revealed it **gradually, in stages**, for a specific reason:

Before Israel existed, **the entire world had collapsed into polytheism.** Every nation worshipped multiple gods. Into this world, God first had to establish one unshakeable foundation:

> _There is one God. His name is Yahweh. He alone is Most High._

Only after that foundation was **firmly, absolutely established** in Israel's mind and culture could God begin revealing the **distinctions within that one divine being** — because any hint of plurality before monotheism was secured would have been misread as polytheism.

This is why:

- **Genesis and Exodus** emphasize unity — one God, one LORD, one Creator
- **The Psalms** begin to show a Son distinct from the Father (Psalm 2, 45, 110)
- **The Prophets** show Yahweh being sent by Yahweh, Yahweh being pierced, Wisdom proceeding from God
- **The New Testament** names what was always there — Father, Son, and Spirit — and shows their full distinction in history

St. Thomas Aquinas states:

> _"Just as before Christ the mystery of Christ was believed explicitly by the learned but implicitly and under a veil so to speak by the simple, so too was it with the mystery of the Trinity."_

**The New Testament does not contradict the Old Testament. It opens what the Old Testament sealed.**

Every text we examine was already in the Tanakh. The New Testament identifies the names of the persons the Tanakh was always describing.

## How to Use This in Conversation

State it before any individual verse is discussed:

> "I am not reading Christian ideas into the Old Testament. I am reading the Old Testament the way the Old Testament was always meant to be read — gradually. God revealed monotheism first, then slowly showed that within that one God there are personal distinctions. Let me show you what was already in your scripture before Jesus was born."

This neutralizes the "novelty" objection before it is even made. The argument is not that the Tanakh says the same thing as the New Testament in the same way. The argument is that the Tanakh planted seeds that the New Testament identifies by name.

# ADDITION 2 — THE WISDOM TEXTS

## The Setup

Before the New Testament ever used the word "Word" (Logos) for Jesus, **Jewish wisdom literature** had already developed a concept of a divine being who:

- Existed with God before creation
- Was the agent through whom God created
- Proceeds from God as his "pure emanation"
- Is called the "image" and "reflection" of God
- Is distinct from God yet sharing God's nature

This is not Christian theology. This is **pre-Christian Jewish theology** found in Proverbs, Sirach, and the Wisdom of Solomon — books in the Hebrew canon or deuterocanon that existed centuries before Jesus.

---

## TEXT 1 — Proverbs 8:22-31

**The full passage:**

> _"Yahweh possessed me at the beginning of his work, the first of his acts of old. Ages ago I was set up, at the first, before the beginning of the earth. When there were no depths I was brought forth, when there were no springs abounding with water. Before the mountains had been shaped, before the hills, I was brought forth... When he established the heavens, **I was there**; when he drew a circle on the face of the deep... when he marked out the foundations of the earth, then **I was beside him, like a master workman**, and I was daily his delight, rejoicing before him always, rejoicing in his inhabited world and delighting in the children of man."_

### What This Establishes

**Wisdom is:**

1. **Pre-existent** — before creation, before the earth, before the depths. Not created as part of the world.
2. **Present at creation** — "I was there" when the heavens were established
3. **The agent of creation** — "like a master workman" beside Yahweh during creation
4. **In personal relationship with the Father** — "daily his delight, rejoicing before him always"
5. **Distinct from Yahweh** — "beside him," not identical with him

**The question this creates:**

> _"What is this Wisdom that existed before creation, was beside Yahweh as his craftsman, and delighted in his presence? If it is just a concept — like wisdom as an idea — why does the Torah give it a voice, a presence, a relationship with God, and a role in creation? Concepts don't rejoice. Concepts don't stand beside someone. This is a person."_

### Escape Attempt

_"Wisdom here is personified as a literary device — Hebrew poetry uses personification. It's not a real being."_

### Closure

- The Torah uses personification for things like mountains clapping and trees singing — but those are clearly poetic imagery about non-personal things.
- Proverbs 8 Wisdom speaks in **first person across 21 verses**, describes her pre-existence before the earth existed, claims to have been **beside Yahweh as a craftsman during creation**, and says she was Yahweh's **daily delight.** This is not the same category as mountains clapping.
- Most decisively: **Proverbs 8:30 says Yahweh delighted in Wisdom daily.** Yahweh does not take daily delight in a literary device. He takes daily delight in a person.
- Even within Jewish scholarship, Wisdom in Proverbs 8 has been recognized as describing a **hypostasis** — a real personal subsistence within God — not a mere metaphor. Philo of Alexandria (a pre-Christian Jewish philosopher) identified this Wisdom/Word as the divine Logos — a real divine person distinct from the Father.

## TEXT 2 — Wisdom of Solomon 7:21-27

**The passage:**

> _"Wisdom, the fashioner of all things, taught me... for in her there is a spirit that is intelligent, holy, unique... For wisdom is more mobile than any motion; because of her pureness she pervades and penetrates all things. **For she is a breath of the power of God and a pure emanation of the glory of the Almighty**... For she is a reflection of eternal light, a spotless mirror of the working of God and **an image of his goodness.**"_

### What This Establishes

The language used here is extraordinary:

| Phrase | What It Means |
|---|---|
| "Breath of the power of God" | Proceeds from God as breath proceeds from a person — internally, not externally created |
| "Pure emanation of the glory of the Almighty" | Not a creature — creatures cannot be a pure emanation of divine glory |
| "Reflection of eternal light" | Shares in God's eternal light — not a created reflection but a divine one |
| "Spotless mirror of the working of God" | Perfectly images God's own actions |
| "Image of his goodness" | The same word (image — εἰκών) used in Colossians 1:15 for Jesus: "the image of the invisible God" |

**"Emanation" is decisive.** A creature is made from nothing — created externally. An emanation proceeds from within the being itself — like light emanating from the sun. The Wisdom of Solomon uses this word deliberately to distinguish Wisdom from any created being. She does not come from outside God. She comes **from within** God.

### The Connection to John 1

John 1:1-3:
> _"In the beginning was the Word (Logos), and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. He was in the beginning with God. All things were made through him, and without him was not any thing made that was made."_

Compare:

| Proverbs 8 / Wisdom of Solomon | John 1 |
|---|---|
| "At the beginning of his work" | "In the beginning" |
| "I was beside him" | "Was with God" |
| "Like a master workman" creating all things | "All things were made through him" |
| "Pure emanation of God" / "Image of his goodness" | "The Word was God" |

John 1 is not inventing a Greek philosophical concept and imposing it on the Hebrew God. John 1 is **identifying the Logos with the Wisdom that Proverbs 8 and Wisdom of Solomon already described** — and saying that this pre-existent divine person became flesh in Jesus of Nazareth.

### How to Use This in Conversation with a Jew

> "Before the New Testament existed, your own Proverbs described a being who existed before creation, stood beside Yahweh as his craftsman during creation, and was Yahweh's daily delight. The Wisdom of Solomon called this being a 'pure emanation of God' and 'an image of his goodness.' This is not a creature. This is not an angel. This is a distinct divine person proceeding from within God. John 1 did not create this theology. He identified the name: Jesus is the one Proverbs 8 was pointing to. The only question is: are you willing to read your own scripture?"

### How to Use This in Conversation with a Muslim

> "The Quran says Jesus is 'a Word from Allah' (3:45) and 'his Word which he cast to Mary' (4:171). Muslims have never been able to explain what it means for Jesus to be Allah's Word in a way that's different from every other prophet. The Hebrew wisdom literature that the Quran endorses explains it: the Word/Wisdom is the pre-existent divine person who proceeds from God, was with God before creation, and is the image of God's goodness. The Quran accidentally preserved the title while discarding the meaning."

# ADDITION 3 — THE TRIPLE HOLY: Isaiah 6

## The Text

**Isaiah 6:1-3:**

> _"In the year that King Uzziah died I saw the Lord sitting upon a throne, high and lifted up... Above him stood the seraphim... And one called to another and said:_
>
> _**'Holy, holy, holy is Yahweh of hosts; the whole earth is full of his glory!'**"_

## The Hebrew Pattern of Repetition

In Hebrew, intensity is expressed by **doubling:**

- "Vanity of vanities" = absolute vanity
- "King of kings" = supreme king
- "Song of songs" = the greatest song

**Doubling is the Hebrew superlative.** When you want to say "maximally X," you say "X X."

So "Holy Holy" would already mean "supremely holy."

**Why does Isaiah 6 use THREE?**

There is no other example in the entire Tanakh where an attribute is tripled for emphasis. The tripling is unique. The tripling is deliberate. And the tripling points to a **distinction within the divine being being praised** — three persons, each fully holy, the holiness proclaimed once to each.

## The New Testament Identifies All Three Persons from Isaiah 6

This is the most powerful part of the argument. Three different New Testament authors quote Isaiah 6 and each attributes it to a **different divine person:**

**John 12:37-41** — attributes Isaiah 6 to the **Son:**
> _"Isaiah said these things **because he saw his (Jesus') glory** and spoke of him."_

**Acts 28:25-26** — attributes Isaiah 6 to the **Holy Spirit:**
> _"The Holy Spirit was right in saying to your fathers through Isaiah the prophet: 'Go to this people and say...' "_ (quoting Isaiah 6:9-10)

**Isaiah 6 itself** — the vision is of the **Father** on the throne.

**One vision. One throne room. One triple holy. Three persons.**

The only explanation that makes all three attributions simultaneously true is that the three-fold holiness of Isaiah 6 is addressed to the three persons of the Trinity — who together constitute the one Yahweh of hosts.

### Escape Attempt

_"John and Paul are just applying the Isaiah passage loosely — it doesn't mean Isaiah 6 literally describes three persons."_

### Closure

- John 12:41 does not say "this is like what Isaiah saw" or "Isaiah was inspired by Christ." It says Isaiah saw **Jesus' glory** — the glory of the pre-existent Son — and spoke of him. John is making a direct identification, not a loose application.
- Paul in Acts 28:25 says "the Holy Spirit was **right** in saying through Isaiah" — meaning the Spirit is the **speaker** of Isaiah 6:9-10, not just the inspirer. The Spirit speaks through Isaiah because the Spirit is the third person of the same Yahweh Isaiah saw.
- If the three attributions are independent and accurate — John was not wrong about the Son, Paul was not wrong about the Spirit, and Isaiah himself saw the Father — then the triple holy is addressed to all three persons simultaneously. That is the only way to honor all three attributions without contradiction.

### How to Use This in Conversation

> "One vision in Isaiah 6. Three different authors of the New Testament quote it and each attributes it to a different divine person — the Father on the throne, the Son whose glory Isaiah saw, and the Holy Spirit who spoke through Isaiah. This is not one author imposing the Trinity on one text. This is three independent witnesses identifying three distinct divine persons in the same scene. And the scene's centerpiece is a three-fold holiness — a structure that already hints at three distinct beings being praised, not one being praised with maximum intensity."

# ADDITION 4 — THE TRIPLE BENEDICTION: Numbers 6

## The Text

**Numbers 6:24-26** — The Aaronic Blessing, the prayer God commanded the priests to pronounce over Israel:

> _"**Yahweh** bless you and keep you;_
> _**Yahweh** make his face to shine upon you and be gracious to you;_
> _**Yahweh** lift up his countenance upon you and give you peace."_

## What Is Happening

God gives Moses a specific, three-line liturgical blessing for the priests to speak over Israel. Each line:

- Names **Yahweh** individually
- Describes a distinct divine action:
  - Line 1: **Blessing and keeping** — provision and protection
  - Line 2: **Shining face and grace** — presence and favor
  - Line 3: **Lifted countenance and peace** — intimacy and wholeness (Shalom)

The divine name appears **three times.** God could have said "May Yahweh bless you, keep you, shine on you, be gracious to you, lift his countenance and give you peace" — one invocation, many blessings. Instead he structures it as **three separate invocations of the same name.**

## How to Use This

This text is not a standalone proof. But after the stronger exhibits are established, it becomes a **powerful confirmation:**

> "Even the daily liturgical blessing that God commanded for Israel — the most repeated prayer in Jewish life — invokes Yahweh's name three times in three distinct actions. After you understand that Yahweh is a composite divine unity (Echad) with personal distinctions shown in Psalm 110, Proverbs 8, Isaiah 48, and Zechariah 2, the structure of Numbers 6 stops being coincidental. God was shaping the liturgy of Israel around the triune structure of his own being — one Yahweh, three invocations, three distinct relational acts. Israel was praying Trinitarian prayers before the Trinity was fully named."

## The Three Actions Map to the Three Persons

This is a traditional reading going back to the early Church fathers:

| Line | Action | Person |
|---|---|---|
| "Bless you and keep you" | Creation, providence, protection | The Father — source of all blessing |
| "Make his face shine and be gracious" | Presence, revelation, grace | The Son — the face of God revealed in the incarnation |
| "Lift up his countenance and give peace" | Intimacy, completion, Shalom | The Spirit — the one who brings the peace of God into the heart |

Present this not as a proof but as a reflection:

> "Once you accept the Trinity from the stronger texts, you look back at Numbers 6 and realize: the shape of Trinitarian theology was already embedded in the liturgy God gave Israel. It was not imposed later. It was always there, waiting to be understood."
