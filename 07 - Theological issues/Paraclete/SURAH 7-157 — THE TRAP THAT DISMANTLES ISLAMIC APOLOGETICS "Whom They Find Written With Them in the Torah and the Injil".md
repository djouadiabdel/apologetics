## THE VERSE

**Surah Al-A'raf 7:157:**

> _"Those who follow the Messenger, the prophet who can neither read nor write, **whom they find written with them in the Torah and the Injil**..."_

This single verse is the most self-destroying verse in the Quran for the Muslim corruption argument. Here is why — completely and in full.

---

# LAYER 1 — THE GRAMMAR LOCKS THE BIBLE IN PLACE

The Arabic phrase is:

> **الَّذِي يَجِدُونَهُ مَكْتُوبًا عِندَهُمْ فِي التَّوْرَاةِ وَالْإِنجِيلِ**
> _"whom they find written **with them (ʿindahum — عِندَهُمْ)** in the Torah and the Injil"_

The word **عِندَهُمْ (ʿindahum)** means:

- "with them"
- "in their possession"
- "at their location"

This is not historical language. This is not "what the prophets once had." This is **present-tense possession.**

Allah is speaking about Muhammad's contemporaries — the Jews and Christians alive at the time of Muhammad in 7th century Arabia. He says Muhammad's description is written **in a scripture that they currently possess and have with them.**

**The question this creates — and never gets answered:**

> "What Torah and Injil did the Jews and Christians have with them in 7th century Arabia?"

Historically, without any dispute, the answer is:
- The Jews had the **Hebrew Bible / Old Testament**
- The Christians had the **New Testament**

There was no other document. No separate "pure Injil." No uncorrupted Torah distinct from the Hebrew Bible. The manuscripts, the church fathers, the entire historical record — there is only one set of documents the People of the Book possessed.

Allah is describing those documents. He is saying Muhammad is described in those documents. He is not saying "Muhammad is described in a document they lost centuries ago."

# LAYER 3 — THE PARACLETE ARGUMENT DESTROYS ITSELF

Muslim scholars claim John 14:16 contains a prophecy about Muhammad. Let us examine this claim fully — because it backfires catastrophically in four different ways.

## The Muslim Claim

Jesus says in John 14:16:

> _"And I will ask the Father, and he will give you **another Paraclete (παράκλητος — Paraklētos)**, to be with you forever."_

Muslims claim:
- The original Greek word was not **Paraklētos (παράκλητος)** meaning "Comforter/Advocate"
- But **Periklutos (περίκλυτος)** meaning "praised one / glorified one"
- And "praised one" in Arabic = **Ahmad** = one of Muhammad's names
- Therefore Jesus was prophesying Muhammad

## WALL 1 — Zero Manuscript Evidence

There is **not a single Greek manuscript in existence** — not one, out of thousands — that reads **Periklutos** in John 14:16. [web:1200][web:1206]

Every Greek manuscript of the Gospel of John — from the earliest papyri in the 3rd century to every subsequent manuscript in the entire transmission history — reads **Paraklētos.**

Scholars Guthrie and Bishop confirmed: [web:1206]

> _"Early translators knew nothing about the surmised reading of periklutos for parakletos, and its possible rendering as Ahmad... Periklutos does not come into the picture as far as Ibn Ishaq and Ibn Hisham are concerned."_

Even the earliest Muslim biographers of Muhammad — **Ibn Ishaq and Ibn Hisham** — who had every reason and motivation to claim this word change — did not make this argument. They quoted the Paraclete verses from John without the Periklutos substitution. The argument was invented centuries later.

> **Ask:** "You are claiming Christians changed one specific Greek word in every single manuscript in existence, across every language, in every country, without leaving a single original anywhere. The Dead Sea Scrolls preserved Old Testament manuscripts from 250 BC. No Periklutos manuscript has ever been found for John 14. Where is your evidence?"

## WALL 2 — John 14:16 Already Identifies the Paraclete as the Holy Spirit

Jesus himself identifies who the Paraclete is — two verses later, in the same speech:

**John 14:26:**

> _"But the **Paraclete, the Holy Spirit**, whom the Father will send in my name, he will teach you all things and bring to your remembrance all that I have said to you."_

**John 15:26:**

> _"But when the **Paraclete** comes, whom I will send to you from the Father, the **Spirit of truth**, who proceeds from the Father, he will bear witness about me."_

**John 16:13:**

> _"When the **Spirit of truth** comes, he will guide you into all the truth."_

Jesus does not leave the Paraclete's identity ambiguous. He defines it explicitly:

- The Paraclete = the Holy Spirit (14:26)
- The Paraclete = the Spirit of truth (15:26, 16:13)
- The Paraclete proceeds from the Father (15:26)
- The Paraclete will be with the disciples forever (14:16)
- The Paraclete will be **in** them (14:17)
- The Paraclete comes **after** Jesus' departure by a few days, not 570 years later (16:7 → fulfilled in Acts 2)

Muhammad was not a spirit. Muhammad did not live inside the disciples. Muhammad did not come a few days after Jesus' ascension. Muhammad did not proceed from the Father. Muhammad is dead and is not with anyone "forever."

**Every single description Jesus gives of the Paraclete eliminates Muhammad and matches the Holy Spirit perfectly.** [web:1196][web:1199]

## WALL 3 — If Periklutos = Ahmad, Jesus Just Called Himself God

This is the argument that ends the conversation. [web:1197]

The full sentence of John 14:16 reads:

> _"And I will ask **the Father**, and he will give you **another Paraclete**..."_

The word "**another**" in Greek is **ἄλλον (allon)** — meaning another of the **same kind.**

Jesus says: the Father will send **another** Paraclete — meaning there is already one Paraclete present. Who is the first Paraclete? **Jesus himself.** (1 John 2:1 confirms this: "we have a Paraclete with the Father — Jesus Christ the righteous.")

So the structure is:
- Jesus = first Paraclete
- The coming one = another Paraclete of the same kind as Jesus

Now apply the Muslim logic: if the coming Paraclete = Ahmad/Muhammad, and the coming Paraclete is of the **same kind** as Jesus the first Paraclete, then:

**Jesus = the same kind of being as Muhammad.**

But Muslims insist Jesus and Muhammad are the same kind of being — both human prophets. So no problem there.

**But now apply it in reverse:** if Jesus is the first Paraclete and Muhammad is the second Paraclete of the same kind, then whatever Jesus is, Muhammad is. And whatever Muhammad is, Jesus is.

If Jesus claimed to be divine — which he did throughout John — then the "another Paraclete of the same kind" is also divine. That is the Trinity.

> **Say this:** "If Muhammad is the 'another Paraclete of the same kind as Jesus,' then Jesus just claimed to be divine — because the Father sends a divine being like himself. You've just proven the Trinity using your own argument about John 14:16. Congratulations."

## WALL 4 — The Quran's Own Description of Ahmad Doesn't Match John 14

**Surah As-Saff 61:6:**

> _"And when Jesus son of Mary said: O Children of Israel! Lo! I am the messenger of Allah unto you, confirming that which was revealed before me in the Torah, and bringing good tidings of a messenger who cometh after me, **whose name is the Praised One (Ahmad).**"_

The Quran claims Jesus prophesied a messenger named Ahmad.

Now compare the Quran's version with what Jesus actually says in John 14-16:

| The Quran's version (61:6) | John 14-16 |
|---|---|
| Jesus announces Ahmad by name | No name "Ahmad" anywhere |
| Ahmad is a messenger (rasul) | The Paraclete is a Spirit, not a messenger |
| Ahmad comes after Jesus as a new prophet | The Paraclete comes days after Jesus' ascension |
| Ahmad brings a new message | The Paraclete reminds disciples of what Jesus already said |
| Ahmad is human | The Paraclete lives inside the disciples ("in you") |

The Quran says Jesus prophesied Ahmad by name and described him as a human messenger. John 14-16 has none of this. **The Quran's description of the prophecy does not match John 14-16 at all** — not even if you swap Parakletos for Periklutos.

> **Ask:** "If Quran 61:6 is the prophecy Jesus made about Muhammad, and John 14:16 is the same prophecy in the Christian scriptures — why do they describe completely different things? The Quran's Jesus names Ahmad explicitly as a future human messenger. John's Jesus describes an eternal divine Spirit who will live inside the disciples. These cannot be the same prophecy. Either the Quran invented a prophecy Jesus never made, or John 14 is about something entirely different from Muhammad. Either way, your argument collapses."

# LAYER 4 — DEUTERONOMY 18:15 DESTROYS THE ARGUMENT FROM THE TORAH SIDE

Muslim scholars point to Deuteronomy 18:15 as the Torah prophecy about Muhammad written "with them."

**The verse:**

> _"The LORD your God will raise up for you a Prophet like me **from among you, from your brothers** — it is to him you shall listen."_

## The Six Walls Against Muhammad Here

**WALL 1 — "From among you, from your brothers" = Israelite**
Deuteronomy 17:15 uses the same phrase "from among your brothers" to mean "not a foreigner." Muhammad was Arab — not an Israelite. The same author, same book, two chapters earlier, defines the phrase to exclude non-Israelites.

**WALL 2 — Must speak only in the name of YHWH**
Deuteronomy 18:19-20: the prophet must speak "in my name" (YHWH's name). Muhammad replaced YHWH with Allah and never once prophesied in the name of YHWH. [web:1204]

**WALL 3 — Must perform Moses-level miracles**
The Torah's test for a true prophet includes public miracles. Muhammad explicitly denied performing miracles in the Quran (17:59, 6:37). Moses parted the Red Sea. Jesus healed the blind and raised the dead. Muhammad's only claimed miracle was the Quran — a text, not a public sign. [web:1204]

**WALL 4 — Must know God face to face**
Deuteronomy 34:10: "There has not arisen a prophet since in Israel like Moses, **whom the LORD knew face to face.**" Muhammad never claimed to see God directly. Islamic theology holds God is unknowable and unseen. Jesus said "Anyone who has seen me has seen the Father" (John 14:9).

**WALL 5 — The New Testament explicitly applies this to Jesus**
Acts 3:22-23 — Peter in Jerusalem: "Moses said, 'The Lord God will raise up for you a prophet like me from your brothers.'" Applied directly to Jesus.
Acts 7:37 — Stephen: "This is the Moses who said to the Israelites, 'God will raise up for you a prophet like me from your brothers.'" Applied directly to Jesus.
These identifications were made in Jerusalem, before hostile Jewish audiences, within 20 years of Jesus' death. Nobody contested that Deuteronomy 18 was about Jesus. Nobody said "this is about an Arab prophet 600 years from now." [web:1201][web:1204]

**WALL 6 — Muhammad is a false prophet by Deuteronomy's own test**
Deuteronomy 18:20 gives two tests for a false prophet:
1. Speaks in the name of other gods → Muhammad replaced YHWH with Allah ✓
2. Prophesies something that does not come true → Muhammad predicted the Roman-Persian war outcome incorrectly in early versions of Surah 30, requiring later revision ✓

By Deuteronomy's own criteria — the very chapter Muslims use to promote Muhammad — Muhammad qualifies as a false prophet. [web:1201]
