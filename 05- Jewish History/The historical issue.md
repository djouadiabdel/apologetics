You're presenting the classic Christian apologetic case using the Talmud, Josephus, and the New Testament to argue that:

- Blood sacrifice was always about **substitutionary death** (Leviticus 17:11 — the soul/life is in the blood; Ezekiel 18:4,20 — the soul that sins shall die; Hebrews 9:22 — without shedding of blood there is no forgiveness).
- God gave multiple supernatural signs **40 years before 70 CE** (starting around the time of Jesus’ death) that He no longer accepted the Temple sacrifices (Talmud Yoma 39b: the lot didn’t come up right, the crimson thread didn’t turn white, the western lamp went out).
- The Temple veil was torn at Jesus’ death (Matthew 27:50-54, Mark 15:37-39, Luke 23:44-47) as a sign that God had left the Temple.
- The destruction in 70 CE was judgment for **baseless hatred** (Talmud Yoma 9b — sinat chinam, equivalent to idolatry, sexual sin, and murder), which Christians link to the rejection and crucifixion of Jesus (John 15:23-25, Matthew 23:32-39, Luke 19:41-44).
- Josephus records multiple omens (star like a sword, comet, light in the Temple, eastern gate opening by itself, voice saying “Let us remove hence,” the mad prophet Jesus son of Ananus crying “Woe to Jerusalem” for 7½ years, chariots in the clouds, etc.).
- Therefore, the old system ended, and Jesus is the true Messiah and fulfillment.

This is a coherent and powerful Christian reading. It ties together the Talmudic signs, Josephus’ historical omens, the tearing of the veil, and the destruction as divine judgment for rejecting the Messiah.

### Where the Jewish Reading Differs

You’re right that this creates real tension with the earlier prophetic emphasis on the Temple’s importance. The Jewish response (which has its own logical pressure points) is:

- The prophets criticized **empty ritual without ethics**, but they never said the Temple itself was unimportant or that blood sacrifice could simply be replaced by prayer. The Temple remained central in their vision of the future (Isaiah 2, Ezekiel 40–48, etc.).
- The Talmudic signs in Yoma 39b and the destruction for “baseless hatred” (Yoma 9b) are interpreted as judgment on **that generation’s sins** (internal Jewish hatred, corruption, etc.), not specifically rejection of Jesus.
- The post-70 shift to prayer/repentance as primary is a **rabbinic adaptation** after the fact (as you correctly noted — no prophet spoke in 70 CE authorizing it). Jews see it as God forcing Israel to live by what the prophets had already said was most important (a broken heart, justice, mercy).
- The expectation of a future Third Temple is still maintained in traditional Judaism precisely because the current system is viewed as **temporary and incomplete**, not the final ideal the prophets described.

You’re correct that this creates an irony: If the spiritual substitutes were always sufficient, why was the elaborate Temple system commanded for over a thousand years? And if the Temple was that important, why does the current system work without it while still hoping for a future one?
