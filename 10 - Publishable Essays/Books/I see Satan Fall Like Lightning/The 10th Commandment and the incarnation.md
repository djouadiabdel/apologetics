The tenth commandment leaves a problematic visual vacuum. It tells us not to look at our neighbor's possessions (Exodus 20:17) but gives our eyes nowhere else to go. That is a big problem in human psychology because human beings are wired to imitate, forbidding us to look at another without providing a new model is like telling someone not to think of a pink elephant.

 ### Christ’s Own Words: The Mimesis of "Receiving"

To break this cycle, the new model must have a desire that is completely empty of _chamad_ (grasping). Jesus explicitly states that his entire psychological makeup is based on a different kind of imitation. He is not an autonomous, self-made man; he is the perfect imitator of the Father.

Use Christ's own words in **John 5:19** to anchor your essay:

> _"Very truly I tell you, the Son can do nothing by himself; he can do only what he sees his Father doing, because whatever the Father does the Son also does."_

Jesus invites us to imitate his own imitation. His desire is completely non acquisitive. Because the Father gives without measuring or hoarding, there is no scarcity, and therefore no rivalry. Jesus replaces the horizontal gaze (looking at the neighbor) with a vertical gaze (looking at the Father).

###  Why it Had to be Flesh?


If Jesus just shouted John 5:19 from the heavens, it would not work. Why? Because human beings had become entirely bound by their physical scenses. Humanity had turned away from contemplating the invisible God and had fallen to the level of sensible things, inventing idols and worshipping creation.

Athanasius argues that the invisible God could not cure a sense-bound creature from a distance. To save us, the Word had to meet our senses "half way".

> _"He became Himself an object for the senses, so that those who were seeking God in sensible things might apprehend the Father through the works which He, the Word of God, did in the body."_

Because we could not physically _see_ the Father to imitate Him, the Word became flesh so we could watch a human being actually live out John 5:19. The Incarnation is the Father’s non-rivalistic desire translated into a visible human body that we can actually look at, touch, and copy without triggering a mimetic war.

### But Why Not a Prophet? — Flesh Was Not Enough

Here the argument has to survive one more objection, and it is the objection that actually matters. The sense-bound argument proves the model must be **flesh** visible, standing where our eyes can reach. But Israel already had flesh-and-blood models sent from God, and had them for centuries: the **prophets**. A prophet is precisely a voice given a body, God's word relocated out of the sky and into a human mouth. If a visible human relay were enough to close the tenth commandment, the prophets would already have closed it. They did not. The vacuum stayed open. Why?

Two reasons, and together they force the argument the rest of the way from "flesh" to "God."

**First, a prophet can only point; he cannot embody.** The entire grammar of a prophet is _"Thus says the LORD"_  he stands _between_ you and God and relays a message. But you do not imitate a message; you imitate a life. Recall the exact demand of John 5:19: the model must let you _watch the Father doing_, so you can copy the doing. A prophet cannot show you that, because he is not the Father. He can _describe_ the Father's non-grasping desire; he cannot _be_ it in front of you. He remains, at bottom, a shout from afar  only now coming out of a nearer mouth. The signpost has moved closer, but a signpost is still not the country. That is why every prophet's word points past himself and runs out; none of them could ever say _"whoever has seen me has seen the Father"_ (John 14:9). A prophet quotes God. He cannot let you look at God.

**Second, a prophet is himself a coveter.** Return to the Hebrew logic: _chamad_ is need in motion, and every prophet is finite, mortal, and needy _by nature_. So a prophet's own desire is never empty of grasping  he is himself inside the disease he was sent to name. Put a prophet forward as _the_ model and you have not escaped mimesis; you have merely chosen a new neighbor to imitate, and a neighbor, however holy, is a rival-in-waiting. The record makes the point without mercy: Moses struck the rock and was barred from the land; Elijah collapsed and begged to die; and **David**  the anointed king, the man after God's own heart  coveted Bathsheba and killed to take her, breaking the tenth commandment at the very summit of the covenant. The best mediator Israel ever produced was himself the case file. Crooked cannot straighten crooked. To hand a sense-bound patient a prophet as his cure is to hand him another patient.

So the two arguments stack.

The sense-bound argument gets you to _"the model must be flesh."_ The _chamad_ argument gets you to _"the model must be a flesh that lacks nothing."_ A prophet satisfies the first and fails the second  which is the whole reason the prophets, for all their number, never filled the hole the tenth commandment left. The model had to be flesh that was _also the Father's own life made watchable_: not a man _pointing_ to the Father, but the Father's non-grasping desire actually _living inside a human being you could stand next to_.

And that is the one thing Christ claims that no prophet ever claimed. A prophet says _"Thus says the LORD."_ Jesus says _"the Son can do nothing of his own accord, but only what he sees the Father doing"_ (John 5:19) not relaying the Father's words but reproducing the Father's motions, in a body, where you can see them. The prophet transmits a message from a God he cannot show you. The Son performs the Father in the flesh and then says, _"imitate this."_ That is not a taller prophet. It is a different kind of being. It had to be God.

## V. The Sharper Claim: It Cannot Be a Human — It Has to Be God

The argument is a lock with two teeth. The model that fills the vacuum has to satisfy two conditions at once, and only one kind of being can satisfy both.

**Tooth one** — the model must be human, because you imitate only what you can watch. The Father is the right model and the unreachable one.

> _"No one has ever seen God; the only God, who is at the Father’s side, he has made him known."_ — **John 1:18**

> _"You cannot see my face, for man shall not see me and live."_ — **Exodus 33:20**

**Tooth two**  the model must desire without lack, and only God lacks nothing. This is the whole of the "it has to be God" claim. Return to the Hebrew. _Chamad_ is not neutral wanting; it is grasping born of **need** — the reach of a creature toward something it does not have. Coveting is need in motion. Which means a model whose desire is _empty_ of _chamad_ cannot merely be a well-behaved person restraining his hunger. He must be a being with **no hunger to restrain**  one who lacks nothing, and therefore has nothing to grasp for. There is exactly one such being.

> _"If I were hungry, I would not tell you, for the world and its fullness are mine."_ — **Psalm 50:12** _(the one who owns everything covets nothing)_

> _"Nor is he served by human hands, as though he needed anything, since he himself gives to all mankind life and breath and everything."_ — **Acts 17:25** _(a being that needs nothing is structurally incapable of covetousness)_

> _"Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, coming down from the Father of lights, with whom there is no variation or shadow due to change."_ — **James 1:17**

This is why **no human could ever be the model**, no matter how holy. Athanasius says it flatly  _you cannot put straight in others what is crooked in yourself_  but the mimetic reason is sharper than moral failure: every human is finite, mortal, and needy _by nature_, and need is the raw material of _chamad_. A saint may covet less; he cannot covet nothing, because he still lacks. Crooked cannot straighten crooked. The cure cannot come from inside the disease.

> _"For where jealousy and selfish ambition exist, there will be disorder and every vile practice."_ — **James 3:16** _(the default state of the neighbor you might have copied)_

So the two teeth close on a single key. The model must be **human** (to be watchable) and **God** (to desire without lack). Not God _or_ man. God _as_ man. The Incarnation is not one available answer to the tenth commandment’s question — it is the **only geometry** that answers it. Chalcedon falls out of Girard: two natures are not a metaphysical puzzle here, they are the two teeth of the one lock.

### The words of Christ: the psychology of a God who only receives

This is the emphasis the essay has to carry — not verses _about_ Jesus but the sentences _from his own mouth_ describing his interior life, and every one of them says the same impossible thing: **I grasp at nothing; I only receive, and I only give.** No human being has ever been able to describe himself this way and be telling the truth.

> _"The Son can do nothing of his own accord, but only what he sees the Father doing."_ — **John 5:19**

> _"I can do nothing on my own... I seek not my own will but the will of him who sent me."_ — **John 5:30**

> _"As the Father has life in himself, so he has granted the Son also to have life in himself."_ — **John 5:26** _(even his own life is received, not seized — deity itself in the posture of receiving)_

> _"My teaching is not mine, but his who sent me... The one who seeks the glory of him who sent him is true, and in him there is no falsehood."_ — **John 7:16, 18**

> _"I do not seek my own glory."_ — **John 8:50**; _"It is my Father who glorifies me."_ — **John 8:54**

> _"I have not spoken on my own authority, but the Father who sent me has himself given me a commandment."_ — **John 12:49**

> _"The words that I say to you I do not speak on my own authority, but the Father who dwells in me does his works."_ — **John 14:10**

> _"I have come down from heaven, not to do my own will but the will of him who sent me."_ — **John 6:38**

Notice what this is: a man claiming full divinity (_"before Abraham was, I am,"_ John 8:58) who in the same breath insists he **originates nothing** and **hoards nothing**. That combination is the exact fingerprint of the non-rivalrous model — infinite enough to have nothing to gain, and self-emptying enough to reach for nothing. Only God could speak the first half. No grasping creature could mean the second.

And then the anti-scarcity move that shatters the mimetic mechanism at its root — the one thing rivals kill for, **glory**, Jesus treats as infinitely _distributable_:

> _"The glory that you have given me I have given to them, that they may be one even as we are one."_ — **John 17:22**

You cannot both own the ox. But glory, in the Father’s economy, is _given_ and _re-given_ and multiplies as it is shared. That is a desire that cannot collide with itself.

### The words of Christ answering the disciples’ rivalry to his face

The clearest proof that this is aimed straight at mimetic rivalry: the disciples stage the rivalry _in front of him_ who is the greatest and he answers not with a rule but with **himself as the reversed model.**

> _"Whoever would be great among you must be your servant... even as the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many."_ — **Mark 10:43–45** (cf. Matthew 20:25–28)

> _"I have given you an example, that you also should do just as I have done to you."_ — **John 13:15** _(spoken with a towel, on his knees, washing feet — the anti-grasp performed)_

And here is the hinge that ties the whole thing back to the Decalogue. The tenth commandment was the last word of the Law, and it was a **prohibition with a hole in it** — _do not desire through your neighbor_, and then silence about whom to desire through. Jesus closes the Law’s open sentence with a **new commandment that supplies the missing model**:

> _"A new commandment I give to you, that you love one another: just as I have loved you, you also are to love one another."_ — **John 13:34**

_Just as I have loved you._ The tenth commandment ended in a vacuum; the new commandment fills the vacuum with a Person. Where the Law could only say _stop_, the incarnate God says _copy me._

### Eden reversed  why the direction of motion demands deity

The final reason it had to be God and not a hero is the **direction**. The first sin was a human hand reaching **upward**, grasping at divinity it did not have:

> _"You will be like God, knowing good and evil."_ — **Genesis 3:5** (and the reach itself: the fruit was _chamad_ — "to be desired to make one wise," **Genesis 3:6**)

A perfect human being climbing toward God would move in the _same direction as the Fall_  up, toward a likeness grasped. That cannot be the cure; it is the disease done more skillfully. The only motion that reverses Eden is the opposite vector: divinity coming **downward, ungrasped.**

> _"Who, though he was in the form of God, did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped, but emptied himself, taking the form of a servant."_ — **Philippians 2:6–7**

Genesis 3 and Philippians 2 are mirror images. In the garden, a man **seizes** at being God. On the cross, God **refuses to seize** at being God, though it was his by right. Only the One who is genuinely grasped-at in Eden can perform the un-grasping that heals it — because you cannot renounce a throne you never owned. **A human can only climb. Only God can descend. So it had to be God.**

### Old Testament: the desire already turning vertical

The vacuum was not left entirely unmarked before Christ. All through the Psalms you can hear desire being pried off the neighbor and turned toward God — the cure previewed, sketched, longed for, but never yet _embodied_ in a watchable life:

> _"Whom have I in heaven but you? And there is nothing on earth that I desire besides you."_ — **Psalm 73:25** _(the horizontal gaze abolished, the vertical gaze alone)_

> _"One thing have I asked of the LORD... to gaze upon the beauty of the LORD."_ — **Psalm 27:4**

> _"As a deer pants for flowing streams, so pants my soul for you, O God."_ — **Psalm 42:1**

> _"Delight yourself in the LORD, and he will give you the desires of your heart."_ — **Psalm 37:4**

> _"You open your hand; you satisfy the desire of every living thing."_ — **Psalm 145:16**

And the diagnosis of the vacuum itself, in Jeremiah — the human problem stated as _desire aimed at the wrong source_:

> _"My people have committed two evils: they have forsaken me, the fountain of living waters, and hewed out cisterns for themselves, broken cisterns that can hold no water."_ — **Jeremiah 2:13**

The Psalms knew _where_ desire had to point. What they could not do was give it a face to watch. That is the exact gap the Incarnation steps into: the vertical object of Psalm 73:25, made visible enough to imitate.

This borrowing occurs quite often without either the loaner or the borrower being aware of it. It is not only desire that one borrows from those whom one takes for models; it is a mass of behaviors, attitudes, things learned, prejudices, preferences, etc. And at the heart of these things the loan that places us most deeply into debt~the. other's desire~occurs often unawares.

Jesus would not be astonished that his teaching is not recognized. He has no illusion about the way in which his message will be received. To the glory that comes from God, invisible in this world, the majority prefer the glory that comes from humankind, a glory that multiplies scandal as it makes its way. It consists in gaining victory in mimetic rivalries often organized by the powers of this world! rivalries that are political, econo111ic, athletic, sexual) artistic, intellectual ... and even religious.

The phrase "Scandal must come" (see Matt. 18:7) has nothing to do with either ancient fatalism or scientific determinism. Taken individually, human beings are not necessarily given over to mimetic rivalries, but by virtue of the great number of individuals they contain, human communities cannot escape them. When the first scandal occurs, it gives birth to others, and the result is mimetic crises, which spread without ceasing and become worse and worse.
