# Where the Knowing Sits

> [!abstract] The shape of this document, before any content
> A test was built to find conversion in novels. It failed on two of its four
> predictions. The failure was informative, because it turned out the test was
> looking in the wrong place, and the wrong place is instructive. **The
> question a novel answers is not whether somebody understands. It is who is
> allowed to, and when.**
>
> Sections 1 to 3 build the instrument and report what it said. Section 4 is
> the failure. Sections 5 and 6 are the finding and its application to *The
> Bell Jar*. Section 7 hints at where else it goes, including my own book.

---

## 1. The words, defined before they are used

**Mediated desire.** Wanting something because somebody else wants it, or
because somebody else has it. The want is not manufactured by the person
having it. Vegeta does not want strength. He has been strong for decades. He
wants the thing Goku has that cannot be trained for, which is ease.

**Mediator.** The person supplying the want. They do not have to be liked or
even acknowledged. Vegeta calls Goku an obstacle for thirty years and
organises every decision of his life around him.

**Metaphysical disappointment.** Getting the object and watching its value
evaporate on contact, because the value was never inside the object. Walter
White standing in front of a storage unit of money he cannot count, spend, or
show to the one person it was for.

**Conversion.** Girard's claim, from the last chapter of *Deceit, Desire and
the Novel*, is that his novels all end with the same event, and the event has
three parts:

1. **Deception becomes truth.** The person can name where the wanting came from.
2. **Agitation becomes repose.** The motion stops and nothing new starts.
3. **Hatred becomes love.** The rival gets looked at with no measuring.

Zuko does all three and the show marks each one. He says out loud what he
actually wanted from his father. He stops moving. He stands in a room with
Azula and does not measure himself against her.

**The skandalon.** Naming the mechanism perfectly and being unable to act on
it. Gollum knows exactly what the Ring has done to him over centuries, says
so, calls it precious in the same breath, and dies with it at Mount Doom.
Advice is the wrong instrument for a person in this condition, and this
matters later.

---

## 2. The instrument

Three bits per novel. One point each. Scored on **the last 1,500 words and
nothing else**, a window fixed in advance so it could not be adjusted to
help a text I liked.

One rule did the real work. **A bit scores 1 only if I can quote the line
that earns it.** No quote, no point. That is what keeps "the motion stops"
from being a feeling I had while reading.

The predictions were written and committed to git before any book was
downloaded, which is the only part of this a reader who distrusts me can
check.

---

## 3. What it said

| | names the source | stops | rival without measuring | |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *Don Quixote* | 1 | 1 | 1 | **3** |
| *The Charterhouse of Parma* | 1 | 1 | 0 | **2** |
| *The Red and the Black* | 0 | 1 | 0 | **1** |
| *Madame Bovary* | 0 | 0 | 0 | **0** |
| *The Bell Jar* | 0 | 0 | 0 | **0** |

Quixote takes all three and does it in dialogue. He gives his real name back,
*"I was Don Quixote of La Mancha, I am now Alonso Quixano the Good."* Then
Sancho offers him a fresh project, the shepherd's life, and he refuses it
with *"in last year's nests there are no birds this year."* A man declining a
new want on his deathbed is the second bit, written by Cervantes four hundred
years before anybody had a word for it.

> [!important] FLAG
> **Girard's own canon spread across every available score.** If conversion
> were one event with three faces, his novels should have piled up at 3. Four
> books, four different numbers.

---

## 4. The failure, which is the useful part

I predicted *The Bell Jar* would score 1 on the first bit. It scored 0.

Esther does name the mechanism, and the line is better than most theory:
*"they all wanted to adopt me in some way, and, for the price of their care
and influence, have me resemble them."* It sits in chapter XVIII. The window
sees the last 1,500 words. The naming is nine thousand words upstream.

I left the window alone. Widening it to catch a book I wanted to pass is the
move that makes the whole exercise worthless.

Stendhal failed the same way. Julien's prison conversion sits just outside
the window too.

> [!warning] FLAG
> **Two of five books put their naming somewhere other than their ending.**
> That is not noise in the instrument. That is a fact about novels, and the
> instrument only found it by being too rigid to accommodate it.

---

## 5. The Flaubert result, and the definition it forces

*Madame Bovary* scored zero, and Girard files that novel in his highest
category.

**Romantic** and **novelistic** are his terms and they are moral rather than
stylistic. A romantic work shows borrowed desire while flattering it as
original. A novelistic work exposes the borrowing. Most origin stories where
the hero's dream arrives from nowhere are romantic. *Fight Club* is
novelistic, because it spends two hours making the rebellion look
self-generated and then puts the model inside the narrator's own skull.

So a novel Girard calls novelistic scores the same as one he would dismiss.
Look at why, in the text.

Charles refuses to name any mechanism at all. His verdict on the whole
catastrophe is *"It is the fault of fatality!"*, and Flaubert marks it as
*"a fine phrase, the only one he ever made."* He is still inside the triangle
on his last afternoon, looking at his wife's lover and thinking *"He would
have liked to have been this man."* The final sentence of the novel is Homais
receiving the Legion of Honour, which is a triangle paying out on schedule.

**Nobody in that book ever understands anything.**

> [!important] THE DEFINITION THIS FORCES
> The three bits measure **a character**. Novelistic measures **where the
> author is standing**. Those are different objects, and a test built for the
> first cannot see the second.

Gatsby is the cleanest version of this and it is not a novel Girard uses.
Gatsby dies without one moment of sight. He never learns that the green light
was a function of Tom Buchanan's house. The knowing in that book belongs
entirely to Nick, who says it plainly and says it about somebody else.

---

## 6. Plath, read with that

Here the finding stops being about Flaubert.

*The Bell Jar* is first person. And the narrator is not the girl in the
story. She is recovered, years later, and she has a child. Plath plants this
on page three and most readers walk past it.

So the voice and the character are one person at two times.

> [!important] THE FINDING
> **Esther at the ending scores zero. Esther the narrator has been scoring on
> every page for two hundred pages.** *"For the price of their care and
> influence, have me resemble them"* is not the girl in the board room
> talking. That is the woman at the desk who already got out.

This is Flaubert's structure with the irony removed. In Flaubert the gap
between the knowing voice and the blind character is a gap between two
people. In Plath it is a gap between one person and her own past, and the
distance is time.

Which means the last line reads without irony, and it always has.

*"The eyes and the faces all turned themselves towards me, and guiding
myself by them, as by a magical thread, I stepped into the room."*

The girl walking into that room knows nothing. The woman telling you about
her knows everything, including the part she says three pages earlier, that
the bell jar is revocable and might come down again anywhere. The book does
not need the character to arrive, because the arrival is the voice that has
been narrating from the start.

> [!warning] The honest limit
> Plath was writing this in England in 1961, married, with a child,
> which is exactly the narrator's position. She published it in January 1963
> under a false name and died four weeks later. The novel never promised a
> guarantee. *"The bell jar hung, suspended."* Suspended, not lifted. The
> biography does not refute the ending. It is the thing the ending said was
> possible.

## 7. Where else this goes

**Novels that put the knowing in the voice and never in the character.**
*Gatsby*, above. *Lolita* is the hard case and the one worth chasing, because
Humbert narrates retrospectively with total lucidity and converts to nothing
at all. He is Gollum with a better vocabulary. A retrospective narrator does
not guarantee an arrival, which is the objection this whole finding has to
survive.

**Novels that put the knowing in the character at the last possible second.**
*Fight Club* again. The detonation is the structure. Everything the piece was
built on is reinterpreted in one line, and the character receives the
information at the same moment the reader does.

**My own book does the second one.**

*The Crowning* scores 1 on naming, and it lands on the last page.
*"It was warm because I did."* The whole descent runs on a syllogism stated
in chapter I and never turned around, and it turns around in the last
paragraphs. That is the *Fight Club* placement rather than the Plath
placement, and the consequence is structural. Plath's book has already spent
its charge and the ending has to run on something else. Mine spends
everything at the end.

It scores 0 on stopping, for the same reason *The Bell Jar* does. The
cessation is there, *"I stopped walking"*, and then the last sentence is
*"I am going to open my mouth now."* Forward motion toward a want. Both books
end one beat before the asking.

It scores **not applicable** on the third bit, because no rival appears inside
the window.

> [!warning] CORRECTION, entered 12 Aug 2026
> I first wrote that this meant the novella runs mediation without rivalry and
> that the rivalry half of its sourcebook was decoration. **That was a
> generalisation from a 1,500-word window to a whole book, and it is wrong.**
>
> A first reader, given only chapter I, reported that the descent is a loop
> and that the footsteps climbing toward the narrator are his own. She is
> right, and the chapter says so in his own measurement:
>
> - He knows the stone by body rather than by sight. *"The first stair has a
>   broken lip near the wall and I knew where it was without looking."*
> - He claims a fact he cannot have observed, *"I told you the right was
>   shorter"*, immediately after *"There was no way to see where either one
>   ended."*
> - **The returning wear.** *"The stair beneath us had been worn lower at its
>   centre by feet travelling down. The returning wear was nearer the wall."*
>   Wear made by feet climbing out of a place nobody leaves. And then *"I had
>   placed them automatically in the returning wear."*
> - He runs the experiment and reports the result. *"I placed my foot against
>   the wall to compare. The ascending step and mine landed together."*
> - Then the identity gives way. *"I could no longer remember which one of us
>   had first begun moving again."*
>
> So there is a **double** in the book, which is Girard's concept 43, and it
> is the same object as Tyler Durden. The rival is the narrator on a previous
> descent. Chapter VIII confirms it in plain statement: *"Twelve is the number
> of counts I told you about, not the number of descents,"* and *"This was the
> first descent on which I had found you already coming down."*

> [!important] AND IT PUTS THE BOOK BACK BESIDE PLATH
> Joan is *"the beaming double of my old best self, specially designed to
> follow and torment me."* The doubles are doing the same job in both books.
> Plath puts hers in the next bed. Mine is on the stairs, and the difference
> is that Esther recognises hers by name in chapter XVII while my narrator
> measures his and explains it away.
>
> That explaining-away is the real find. He states the objection accurately,
> *"The explanation required sound to arrive before we made it"*, and then
> licenses himself past it with *"time was unreliable here and therefore the
> objection proved less than it would elsewhere."* **The unreliability of time
> is not a property of the place. It is his alibi**, and the first reader
> attributed it to the world, which is the sentence working as designed.

**And a note about how it got written.**

The method is a cold dump, then a forensic reread. My first version of this
paragraph said the late placement of the naming was not a decision. The
chapter I evidence above kills that too. The returning wear, the matched
footstep and the broken lip are planted, and the syllogism that detonates in
X is stated in the opening pages on purpose. **The dump produces the voice.
The placement is built afterwards, in the reread.**

The rest still holds. The naming lands on the last page because the draft is
produced in his voice rather than about him, and a man revising a sentence
while he says it does not get to understand early. Plath had a different
instrument available. She wrote in the voice of someone who already knew, and
that let her distribute the knowing across two hundred pages instead of
detonating it once.

That is a real technique and it is learnable. It requires deciding, before
the first sentence, that the person telling the story is not the person in
it.

---

## 8. What would kill this

- If *Lolita* or another retrospective narration turns out to be the norm
  rather than the exception, then "the knowing lives in the voice" describes
  first-person fiction generally and says nothing about Plath.
- If the instrument gets a control set and the three bits turn out to be
  correlated after all, then conversion is one event, the spread in section 3
  was small-sample noise, and section 5's whole argument loses its footing.
- The scoring was done by me, unblinded, on five books I already knew. That is
  the weakest joint in the entire chain and no amount of quoting fixes it.
- Section 7 already had to be corrected once, and the correction came from a
  first reader working with one chapter and no theory. **A window score
  generalised to a whole book is the error this document made, and it is the
  error the instrument invites.** Assume it is present elsewhere.
