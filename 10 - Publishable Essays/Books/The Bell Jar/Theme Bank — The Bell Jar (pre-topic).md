# 🫙 Theme Bank — everything we have on *The Bell Jar*, sorted before choosing

All evidence below is already collected and quoted in the mimetic reading note. Nothing here needs new reading. What this file does is cut the same material along a different axis: **not chapter order, but candidate essays**, each with its claim, its quotes, its live threat, and what it would cost to write.

Two things to decide at the end, not before: which theme, and **whether the piece is about *The Bell Jar* or about Plath.** Everything we have is the novel. No poems, no journals, no *Ariel*, no letters. An essay that says "Plath believed" instead of "the novel does" opens a research bill we have not paid.

---

## §0. Constraint set, 12 Aug 2026

**Decided:** the piece stays inside the novel, allows hints about what Plath is *doing*, and runs Girardian throughout.

Three consequences, and they reorganise this whole file.

**1. The authorial claims have a licence, and it has a date on it.**
Talking about what Plath is doing without journals or biography is only legitimate because the frame test ran and resolved. §4 of the reading predicted on 6 Aug that the electrocution motif would pay off if the scapegoat frame were authorial. It paid off twice, ch. XII and ch. XVII, in Esther's own vocabulary. So the permitted register is **construction**, never belief. What she puts where, in what order, next to what.

Sentences that are inside the licence:
- She puts Buddy and Marco four chapters apart.
- She gives Joan a doctor who supplies a vocabulary and Esther a doctor who supplies none.
- She puts the affirmation of life in the church, over the grave of the double.
- She makes the biology sufficient on its own, and then builds the Nolan structure anyway.

Sentences that are outside it: anything beginning "Plath believed", "Plath felt", "Plath knew from her own hospitalisation".

**2. The candidates stop being independent.**
Under the Girard filter these are not eleven essays. They are stations on one continuous argument, and the argument already has an order:

> desire is borrowed and she knows it → knowing buys nothing → but there is no rivalry, so the frame is doing half the work at most → Joan arrives and supplies the missing half, causally → the scapegoat structure assembles completely → and the novel refuses to operate it → the exit turns out to be a model who does not charge resemblance → Valerie shows what the false version of that exit looks like → the last line says mediation was never the disease

Everything in section A is a stop on that line. **The decision is no longer which theme. It is where to cut the line and how much of it one piece can carry.**

**3. The interesting version argues with Girard rather than illustrating him.**
Illustrating mimetic desire on a canonical novel is a genre with a lot of entries. The novel earns a piece because it **breaks the theory in four specific places**, and all four are already documented:

| Break | Where | The problem it makes for Girard |
|---|---|---|
| Lucidity buys nothing | §2, ch. VII | The seeing *is* the conversion in *Mensonge romantique*. Here it changes nothing. |
| The body has no model | §8 | The only autonomous agent in the book is the organism, and it overrules the desiring consciousness and is right to. |
| The machine is built and refused | §16 | Full surrogate-victim structure, and the most trusted voice in the book forbids the reading out loud. |
| Repose without conversion | §14, Valerie | Desire ends by surgery and looks from outside exactly like the real thing. |

Plus the fifth, at the end: the last line makes mediation the way out, which declines the idea that a self should be free of mediation at all.

**That is the essay.** *The Bell Jar* as a counter-case to *Deceit, Desire and the Novel* rather than an example of it. It is the same discipline as the myth detector, run on a novel: name what would kill the reading, then report that some of it survived and some did not.

## A. The eleven candidates

Ranked by how much of the work is already done, not by how good they are.

---

### A1. Recovery is the last scholarship

> The fig tree offered positions and she could not choose. The asylum offers **one ladder with an ordinal ranking** and the paralysis stops.

Wymark → Caplan → Belsize → out. The illness was never indecision about what to want. It was being handed alternatives instead of a scale. Give her a scale and she is excellent, because she always was.

**Quotes in hand**
- *"the one thing I was good at was winning scholarships and prizes, and that era was coming to an end."* (ch. II)
- *"Joan had walk privileges, Joan had shopping privileges, Joan had town privileges. I gathered all my news of Joan into a little, bitter heap, though I received it with surface gladness."* (ch. XVIII)
- *"Even so, I felt it unfair of Joan to beat me through the gates."* (ch. XIX)
- *"Either I got better, or I fell, down, down, like a burning, then burnt-out star, from Belsize, to Caplan, to Wymark and finally to the state place next-door."*

**Why this one is worth something:** it is the only reading in the file I have not seen elsewhere. The fig tree is the most anthologised page in the novel and everybody stops at "she can't choose." Nobody says the hospital fixes it by removing the choice.

**Live threat:** it makes recovery sound like a trick, which flirts with saying she is not really well. The clinical account (§18 of the reading) blocks that, so it has to be conceded inside the essay rather than dodged.

**Form:** straight literary essay, 2,000-2,500. No Girard vocabulary needed at all. Works at a general venue.

---

### A2. The price of care is resemblance

> *"they all wanted to adopt me in some way, and, for the price of their care and influence, have me resemble them."* (ch. XVIII)

The whole apparatus in one sentence, and Esther says it herself. Then the structural answer: **Nolan is not on the list.** Her function is entirely negative. No doctrine, no vocabulary, no verdict, no exchange.

**Quotes in hand**
- The list itself, above. The famous poet, Philomena Guinea, Jay Cee, the Christian Scientist lady.
- *"Joan chattered on about Egos and Ids. I never talked about Egos and Ids with Doctor Nolan. I didn't know just what I talked about, really."*
- **"Propaganda!"** — Nolan destroys the *Defence of Chastity* article and installs nothing in its place.
- *"'I hate her,' I said, and waited for the blow to fall. But Doctor Nolan only smiled at me as if something had pleased her very, very much, and said, 'I suppose you do.'"*
- *"I'll be there when you wake up, and I'll bring you back again."*
- The control group: Doctor Quinn is *"abstract"*, supplies the vocabulary, and Joan announces **"I'm going to be a psychiatrist"** inside one conversation. A new fig on the old tree. Joan changed lender, not habit.

**Why this one:** it is the load-bearing claim of the whole reading, and it is the only theme here with a light at the end of the tunnel, which is Heart of Flesh's stated filter.

**Live threat, and it is real:** §13.6. Insulin, twenty pounds, five shock treatments, a reaction that arrives at night without anybody's help. Plath makes the biology sufficient on its own. If the clinical account carries it, Nolan is a good doctor and not a door, and the essay is decoration. The defence is not more Nolan quotation. It is the Joan/Quinn pairing: **one doctor hands over a vocabulary and a new identity and that patient dies, one hands over neither and that patient leaves.** If Plath meant nothing by that, it is a convenient coincidence, and convenient coincidences do happen. Concede it in the piece.

---

### A3. I read about you, and I ran away

> Joan's suicide attempt is **caused** by reading the newspaper account of Esther's. Documented chain of custody.

She reads, pools her money, takes the first plane because *"I thought it would be easier to kill myself in New York,"* and puts her fists through her room-mate's window. Then she hands Esther the clippings back and says *"you ought to stick them in a scrapbook."*

**Also in hand**
- *"Joan was the beaming double of my old best self, specially designed to follow and torment me."* (ch. XVII, Plath's own word)
- *"Her thoughts were not my thoughts, nor her feelings my feelings, but we were close enough so that her thoughts and feelings seemed a wry, black image of my own."*
- *"Sometimes I wondered if I had made Joan up. Other times I wondered if she would continue to pop in at every crisis of my life to remind me of what I had been."*
- The pale blue envelopes: two identical letters from Buddy on the same morning, then *"I didn't like him much, anyway. No, it was his family I liked."* Same mediator, and neither of them wants the man.

**Why this one:** it is the single piece of evidence that mimetic transmission is *in* the book rather than laid over it, and it is one line long. Contagion runs as fashion for two hundred pages and then runs as death.

**Live threat:** it is 1963 and the essay will be read as a Werther-effect piece. That is a different argument with its own literature, and it will eat the essay if not named early and set aside.

**Form:** this is the one that survives contact with a Girard-literate venue and gets nothing extra from a general one.

---

### A4. Breakdown as inventory

> Both the decline **and** the recovery are given as quantities and dates. No scene is ever supplied for the turn.

Seven nights without sleep, three weeks unwashed, reading gone, writing gone, then the handwriting itself. On the way back up: injections three times a day, sugary fruit juice, twenty pounds, a reaction that happens while she is asleep and is reported to her afterwards by a nurse, five shock treatments, town privileges, a discharge interview.

The one line that reads like a turning point, and it is five words: *"The bell jar hung, suspended, a few feet above my head."* Suspended. Not lifted, not broken.

**Why this one:** it is a craft essay rather than a reading, and it is the thing almost no other novelist attempts. Illness in fiction gets a cause and a crisis. Here there are only counters. It also feeds straight back into his own fiction, where it is already logged in the seedbank as a move to steal.

**Live threat:** none structurally, this is just true. The risk is that it stays observation and never becomes an argument. It needs a second novel to compare against or it is a long note.

**Form:** essay on technique, 1,500-2,000. Best fit for a nonfiction slot. Also the safest first credit.

---

### A5. Valerie, or repose by subtraction

> *"I said good-bye to Valerie's calm, snow-maiden face behind which so little, bad or good, could happen."*

The coldest sentence in the book. Valerie has had metaphysical desire removed surgically, and she has exactly the repose that every conversion account treats as the mark of the real thing.

- *"'I've had a lobotomy.' I looked at Valerie in awe, appreciating for the first time her perpetual marble calm. 'How do you feel?' 'Fine. I'm not angry any more. Before, I was always angry.'"*
- *"'What will you do when you get out?' 'Oh, I'm not leaving,' Valerie laughed. 'I like it here.'"*
- Two scars on the forehead *"as if at some time she had started to sprout horns, but cut them off."*

**The claim:** the end of desire has a false version, it is indistinguishable from the real one from outside, and the only difference is whether anything can still happen. Agitation becoming rest is not sufficient evidence of anything.

**Why this one:** shortest, sharpest, and it cuts against a piety rather than confirming one. It also belongs in the Girard concept file as a counter-case whatever else happens.

**Live threat:** it is three pages of the novel. Not enough text to hold 2,000 words alone. This is a section of something else, or a very short piece paired with the Nolan material.

---

### A6. The victim goes looking for the crime

> Under Doctor Gordon's machine, unanaesthetised: *"I wondered what terrible thing it was that I had done."*

The novel opens on the Rosenbergs and closes the loop two hundred pages later, on her own body. And the inversion is exact:

| | Direction | Line |
|---|---|---|
| Marco, ch. IX | verdict precedes the evidence | *"Sluts, all sluts. Yes or no, it is all the same."* |
| Esther, ch. XII | punishment precedes the crime, so she supplies one | *"I wondered what terrible thing it was that I had done."* |

**Second payoff, ch. XVII, and it is more explicit than the first**
- The maid reading the list on the kitchen wall: *"'Greenwood, Greenwood, Greenwood.' The maid's warty index finger slid down the list of names of the patients in Belsize tacked up on the kitchen wall. 'Greenwood, no breakfast today.'"* Designated by name, from a posted list, in front of a witness who then avoids her eyes.
- Ch. XV, the nurse writing *"E. Greenwood, E. Greenwood, E. Greenwood, E. Greenwood"* on adhesive labels. The victim is marked in handwriting, repeatedly, before anything happens.
- Esther supplies the word herself: *"like a person coolly resigned to execution,"* and *"they would bear me, howling and hitting, past the smiling audience now gathered in the lounge."* A procession, two escorts, a named crowd, a smiling audience.

**Why this one:** it was a prediction on 6 Aug and it resolved twice. The frame is authorial, not imposed. That is a finding with a date on it, which is unusual for a literary essay and worth saying so in the piece.

**Live threat:** the essay has to survive the sentence "the Rosenberg frame is just period setting." The kitchen list and the adhesive labels are the answer and they should go in early rather than last.

---

### A7. The machine built and refused

> Two doubles, one ladder, one death, and the survivor's recovery dated from it. Then the book's most trusted voice forbids the reading out loud.

- The structure is fully assembled: Joan hangs herself at the end of ch. XIX, ch. XX opens with fresh snow and the discharge interview, and the affirmation of life is spoken **over the grave of the double**. *"I took a deep breath and listened to the old brag of my heart. I am, I am, I am."*
- Buddy asks the persecutor's question: *"Do you think there's something in me that drives women crazy?"* Two women's collapses become an occasion for a man to locate his own position.
- And Nolan, in the only line where she is angry: *"'Of course you didn't do it! Nobody did it! She did it!'"* Three sentences, three refusals. No persecutor, no guilty community, no economy where one death buys a life.
- Esther, at the service: *"all during the simple funeral service I wondered what I thought I was burying."* She never answers.

**Why this one is the most honest and the hardest to sell:** the finding is that the text supports neither resolution. The novel builds the mechanism and declines to operate it, and the refusal is placed in a doctor's mouth rather than argued.

**This is also the theme that fits the machine.** Claim, defend, trapdoor, don't move. The essay loses its own argument in the last section and keeps the position. Do not let it convert.

---

### A8. The last line, and the problem it creates

> *"The eyes and the faces all turned themselves towards me, and guiding myself by them, as by a magical thread, I stepped into the room."*

Fourteen chapters treat being constituted by other people's regard as the illness. The novel ends by making it the way out, and presents it without irony.

**Two readings, and the piece has to choose deliberately**
1. **Ironic.** She is walking back into the same apparatus. Supported by *"How did I know that someday the bell jar wouldn't descend again?"* and *"Those girls, too, sat under bell jars of a sort."*
2. **Not ironic, and this is the harder one.** Being constituted by other people is the condition and not the disease. What changed is **which** people and **on what terms**. Nolan's eyes are in that room. Jay Cee's are not. The mediating gaze stopped charging resemblance as the price.

Reading 2 sits better with A2 and makes the novel less Girardian than it looks, because it refuses the idea that a self should be free of mediation at all.

**Pairs with the refusal of erasure.** The mother offers the other exit, *"We'll act as if all this were a bad dream,"* and Esther gives the flattest sentence in the book: *"I remembered everything. But they were part of me. They were my landscape."* Nothing gets scrubbed. That is the same operation as keeping the trade in the sentence that calls Rahab righteous, which is already sitting in the James material.

**Live threat:** every undergraduate essay on this novel is about the last line. Reading 2 is the only reason to enter that room.

---

### A9. Every object is an argument

> When a paragraph is all objects and no reasoning, that is where the action is.

- Gordon's beige windowless waiting room, and *"I wondered why the room felt so safe."*
- The father's stone in pink marble *"like tinned salmon."*
- Graves *"like an oblong bathtub full of dirt."*
- The black raincoat bought with the last money in the account, from a salesgirl who explains it is only showerproofed. Buying your shroud and being upsold.
- Weeding the dead flowers out of the vases, then the dying ones, into a basin *"cold as a tomb,"* before she can enter the room.
- Cooking the hotdog correctly by watching everybody else, then burying it in the sand when nobody is looking.
- Putting the chair back in the exact spot before going down to the cellar.

**Why this one:** it is the unlock that changed how he reads, it generalises past Plath, and it is the only theme here that is also a teaching piece. Possible video rather than essay.

**Live threat:** it is method, not argument. Needs a thesis about what Plath specifically does that Dostoevsky does not, or it is a good reading habit written up.

---

### A10. The men run one operation

The §3 table, intact. Buddy needs her pure while he is not. Mr Willard needs no weakness to exist at all. Marco needs one untouchable woman and every other woman a slut. Eric will not enter what he loves. Constantin wants nothing and so she gets nothing from him.

**The formula:** every man needs a woman in a **fixed** position so he can occupy a **mobile** one. Mrs Willard says it and does not hear herself: *"what a man is is an arrow into the future and what a woman is is the place the arrow shoots off from."*

**Why it is ranked low:** this essay exists a hundred times. The only new thing we have is that **Buddy is the polite form of Marco's operation and the book puts them four chapters apart on purpose**, with the verdict-precedes-evidence line as the hinge. Without that hinge, skip it.

---

### A11. Lucidity buys nothing

> Girard's heroes are freed when they see the triangle. Esther sees it in ch. VII and the seeing changes nothing.

This is the thesis line of the whole reading and it is the theme I would write **last**, because it needs all the evidence in section B below and it argues with a theory the reader may not have. It puts her nearer the underground man than Emma Bovary: conscious, articulate, and paralysed **by** the consciousness.

Note that A2 partly dissolves it. The novel does have an exit, it just is not insight.

## B. The evidence pool, indexed by what it proves

Same quotes, sorted so a draft can grab them without rereading the long note.

**Desire is borrowed, and she says so**
- *"I wished with all my heart I could crawl into her and spend the rest of my life barking out one idiom after another."* (the UN interpreter, wanting a **being** and not a thing)
- *"Sleeping with Buddy wouldn't count, though, because he would still be one person ahead of me."*
- *"there would be a pleasant irony in sleeping with a man Mrs Willard had introduced me to."*
- Jay Cee answers for her: *"She wants to be everything."* Only afterwards does Esther say she wants to be a poet.
- *"I wished I had a mother like Jay Cee. Then I'd know what to do."*
- *"I would catch sight of some flawless man off in the distance, but as soon as he moved closer I immediately saw he wouldn't do at all."*
- Every fig is a position, never an activity. Poet, professor, editor, champion. Never write, study, row.
- Even her prose is borrowed: she is proud of one image and immediately *"I had the dim impression I'd probably read it somewhere else a long time ago."* Her heroine is Elaine, six letters, because Esther has six.

**Mediation runs all the way to the exit**
- *"If you were going to kill yourself, how would you do it?"* She shops for the method like a fig, and is disappointed that a gun is not available to her.
- *"I had bought a few paperbacks on abnormal psychology at the drug store and compared my symptoms with the symptoms in the books, and sure enough, my symptoms tallied with the most hopeless cases."*
- *"The only thing I could read, beside the scandal sheets, were these abnormal psychology books."* Production dies first, then reception narrows to models.
- Ch. XIII opens mid-argument about Ibsen's *Ghosts*, a mother deciding whether to kill her son, and then proceeds entirely through the mother's possessions. The play is the script being handed over before the scene.

**And it runs to the end, after she is well**
- *"Ever since I'd learned about the corruption of Buddy Willard my virginity weighed like a millstone around my neck."* Same accounting as ch. VI, opposite sign.
- Irwin selected by specification: *"a kind of impersonal, priestlike official, as in the tales of tribal rites."* She names the rite herself.
- *"I lay, rapt and naked, on Irwin's rough blanket, waiting for the miraculous change to make itself felt. But all I felt was a sharp, startlingly bad pain."*
- *"It occurred to me that the blood was my answer. I couldn't possibly be a virgin any more. I felt part of a great tradition."* Membership, and the tradition named is the one about staging evidence for other people.
- *"I was my own woman. The next step was to find the proper sort of man."* Freedom declared and the search reopened in the next breath.

**The body, which is the one thing with no model**
- *"Then I saw that my body had all sorts of little tricks, such as making my hands go limp at the crucial second, which would save it, time and again, whereas if I had the whole say, I would be dead in a flash."*
- The water spits her up *"like a cork."*
- The heartbeat, three times in the novel: *I am, I am, I am.*
- Callback: ch. X, the hand that will not lift the receiver, *"as if it had collided with a pane of glass."* The body is consistent three chapters before she names it.

**This pool scores for the clinical reading, not the Girardian one.** Keep it. Any essay that ignores it is cheating.

## C. What is not a theme, and should not be forced into one

These are engines for fiction, already logged in the seedbank. Do not spend them on an essay.

- The body as an adversary that saves you against your will.
- The black raincoat, showerproofed.
- The flowers sorted dead from dying before she can enter the room. Already has an Algerian relocation in the seedbank.
- The chair put back in the exact spot before the cellar.
- Breakdown told as counters with no scene. Already listed as a move to steal.
- Hard cuts with no transitions, ch. XIII. Beach, hospital, graveyard, cellar, no time markers.

## D. Choosing, after the §0 constraint

The novel-only and Girard-throughout decisions are made, so questions one and two are closed. What is left is where to cut the line.

### D1. What the filter did to the eleven

| | Status under §0 |
|---|---|
| **A11** lucidity buys nothing | **Promoted to the opening move.** It was ranked last because it argues with a theory the reader may not have. Under §0 the reader arrives with the theory, so this becomes the first paragraph rather than the hard sell. |
| **A3** the double and the contagion | **Load-bearing.** *"I read about you, and I ran away"* is the sentence that proves the transmission is in the book. Without it the whole reading is imposed. |
| **A7** the machine built and refused | **The centre.** This is where the essay stops illustrating and starts arguing. |
| **A2** the price of care is resemblance | **The resolution.** Also the only place the piece has anywhere to land. |
| **A5** Valerie | **Absorbed, as the control.** Three pages cannot hold a piece alone, and it is devastating placed right after A2. |
| **A6** the victim goes looking for the crime | **Absorbed, as the evidence that the frame is authorial.** Under §0 this is what buys the right to say "what Plath is doing" at all, so it moves early. |
| **A8** the last line | **The ending, and the quarrel.** Reading 2 is the piece's most Girard-adverse claim and it should close the essay. |
| **A1** recovery as the last scholarship | **Survives, and gets a Girardian engine it did not have.** Rivalry needs a shared object. The fig tree offers alternatives, which is why nobody competes for two hundred pages. The ladder supplies a single object with an ordinal ranking, and rivalry appears immediately. So the paralysis was a structural absence of a common object. That is a real Girardian claim and it is stronger than the version I ranked first yesterday. |
| **A4** breakdown as inventory | **Demoted to the falsifier section, and it must stay in.** Nothing in mimetic theory predicts insulin. This is the permanent limit on the reading and the essay is dishonest without it. |
| **A10** the men run one operation | **Keep only the hinge.** Marco's *"Sluts, all sluts. Yes or no, it is all the same"* as verdict-precedes-evidence, and Buddy as the polite form of the same operation four chapters away. Drop the rest. |
| **A9** every object is an argument | **Out.** Craft, no Girard load. Save it for the channel. |

### D2. So the real question is length

Three cuts of the same line, and they are genuinely different pieces.

1. **The full counter-case, 3,000 to 4,000.** All five breaks, falsifiers included. This is the thing worth writing and it is too long for Ekstasis, which caps essays at 3,000.
2. **The centre only, 1,800 to 2,500.** A3 into A7 into A2. Joan, the assembled machine, Nolan's refusal. Fits Ekstasis, and it is the version where the trapdoor works, because the argument builds the scapegoat reading for eight paragraphs and then has Nolan take the floor out. Claim, defend, trapdoor, don't move.
3. **The single break, 1,500.** Valerie and repose by subtraction, run against the conversion accounts. Smallest, sharpest, and the only one that could be drafted in a sitting.

### D3. Venue, unchanged

Nonfiction is open year-round at two places. Dappled Things fiction does not reopen until Feb 2027, and the 20 Sept Heart of Flesh window is fiction only. **No essay here competes with the September deadline.** This is a parallel track and the story is still its own problem.

### D4. My call

**Cut 2.** The centre carries the argument, it is the length both open venues take, and it is shaped like the machine he already writes well. Cut 1 is the better piece and it can be built later by growing cut 2 outward, since the falsifier material and Valerie are already written.

One warning for the draft. Section §16 of the reading says do not resolve the contradiction, and cut 2 is built entirely out of §16. The temptation will be to let Nolan win or let the parallel win in the last paragraph. **Lose the argument and keep the position.** The finding is that the novel assembles the mechanism and declines to run it, and that a doctor forbids it rather than an argument.
