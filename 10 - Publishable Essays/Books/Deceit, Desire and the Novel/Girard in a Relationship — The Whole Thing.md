# Girard in a Relationship — The Whole Thing

**What this is.** Girard's account of two people who love each other, in plain language, start to finish. Every stage has a simple explanation, a character you already know, a real quote, and a scenario.

**Sources, all verified in my own files.**

## 0. The whole thing in one paragraph

You do not want things directly. You want what somebody you admire wants, and their wanting is what makes the thing look valuable. That person is the model. If the model is far away, nothing bad happens, you just imitate them. If the model is close enough to reach for the same thing you are reaching for, they stop being a model and become an obstacle, and the closer they are the worse it gets. In a couple the model is as close as a human being can be, and the thing you are both reaching for is the other one's regard, which only one person can be holding at a time. Everything after this is detail.

---

## 1. Stage one: desire is borrowed

**Simple version.** There is no straight line from you to what you want. There is always a third point, a model, and the model's wanting is what lit the object up.

**Character.** Don Quixote does not decide to be a knight. Amadis of Gaul decides for him. Girard's line on it:

> « Don Quichotte a renoncé, en faveur d'Amadis, à la prérogative fondamentale de l'individu : il ne choisit plus les objets de son désir, c'est Amadis qui doit choisir pour lui. »
> *Don Quixote has given up, in favour of Amadis, the individual's most basic prerogative. He no longer chooses the objects of his desire, Amadis has to choose for him.* (ch. I)

**In a couple.** Notice how much of what you want with a person you did not invent. The idea of what closeness should look like, how often people talk, what counts as being loved. All of it came from somewhere. You inherited a spec sheet and you are grading a real person against it.

**Scenario.** You feel a drop because she took nine hours to reply. Ask where the number came from. Not from her, and not from you either. It came from watching other people, or from one earlier relationship where the rhythm was different. The nine hours is being measured against a model you never chose deliberately.

## 2. Stage two: the only variable that matters is distance

**Simple version.** Girard splits every case in the world by one question. Can you and your model collide over the same object?

> « Nous parlerons de médiation externe lorsque la distance est suffisante pour que les deux sphères de possibles dont le médiateur et le sujet occupent chacun le centre ne soient pas en contact. Nous parlerons de médiation interne lorsque cette même distance est assez réduite pour que les deux sphères pénètrent plus ou moins profondément l'une dans l'autre. »
> *External mediation is when the gap is wide enough that the two spheres of the possible, one centred on the model and one on the subject, never touch. Internal mediation is when the gap is small enough that the two spheres go into each other.* (ch. I, English pp. 9–12)

And note this, because people always get it wrong:

> « Ce n'est évidemment pas de l'espace physique que mesure l'écart entre le médiateur et le sujet désirant. »
> *The gap being measured is obviously not physical space.*

**Character, external.** Sancho and Don Quixote sit on the same road all day and never fight, because Sancho wants food and a purse and an island handed to him by his master. Girard:

> « Aucune rivalité avec le médiateur n'est possible. L'harmonie n'est jamais sérieusement troublée entre les deux compagnons. »
> *No rivalry with the model is possible. The harmony between the two companions is never seriously disturbed.*

**Character, internal.** Naruto and Sasuke. Same age, same village, same ladder, same teacher. They cannot admire each other without competing, and by the end the object is gone and only the other one is left.

**Why a couple is the extreme case.** External mediation is safe because the model cannot want what you want. In a couple the model is the object. She is the person whose valuation makes you real, and she is the thing you are reaching for. Distance zero. And Girard's law:

> « Ici comme ailleurs, c'est le rapprochement qui engendre le conflit. »
> *Here as everywhere, it is closeness that generates conflict.* (ch. IV)

That sentence is why "we just need to get closer" is not automatically the fix.

## 3. Stage three: double mediation, the closed circuit

**Simple version.** Normally: A wants what B wants. Double mediation: A copies B's desire and B copies A's desire at the same time. Now there is no outside object at all. The thing circulating is the wanting itself.

> « La double médiation est une figure fermée sur elle-même ; le désir y circule et se nourrit de sa propre substance. La double médiation constitue donc une véritable "génératrice" du désir, la plus simple possible. »
> *Double mediation is a figure closed on itself. Desire circulates inside it and feeds on its own substance. It is a real generator of desire, the simplest one possible.* (ch. IV, English pp. 99–104)

**Read that word "generator" carefully.** It does not need fuel from outside. That is why these fights do not run out. Nothing external is feeding them, so nothing external can starve them.

**Character.** Every escalation in *Kaguya-sama: Love Is War*. Each new scheme exists only because the other one schemed. Remove either party and the entire apparatus vanishes in one episode.

**The killer line, and this is the one to memorise:**

> « Si les amants ne sont jamais d'accord ce n'est pas parce qu'ils sont trop "différents", comme l'affirment le sens commun et les romans sentimentaux, c'est parce qu'ils sont trop semblables l'un à l'autre, c'est parce qu'ils sont tous des copies les uns des autres. Mais plus ils se ressemblent, plus ils se rêvent différents. »
> *If lovers never agree it is not because they are too different, as common sense and sentimental novels claim. It is because they are too similar, because they are all copies of each other. And the more alike they become, the more they dream themselves different.*

**Scenario.** A fight where both of you are saying "you never understand me," in the same tone, with the same wound, twenty minutes apart. That is not two different people failing to meet. That is two identical positions, and the claim of difference is the last thing either of you has left.

## 4. Stage four, symptom one: coquetry

**Simple version.** If being wanted is what proves you are worth something, then being wanted has to keep happening, and giving in would end it. So the person keeps the wanting alive and keeps refusing it. Not as a plan. As a condition.

> « La coquette ne veut pas livrer sa précieuse personne aux désirs qu'elle provoque mais elle ne serait pas si précieuse si elle ne les provoquait pas. La préférence que s'accorde la coquette se fonde exclusivement sur la préférence que lui accordent les Autres. C'est pourquoi la coquette recherche avidement les preuves de cette préférence ; elle entretient et attise les désirs de son amant, non pas pour s'y abandonner mais pour mieux se refuser. »
> *She does not want to hand over her precious person to the desires she provokes, but she would not be so precious if she did not provoke them. The value she grants herself rests entirely on the preference Others grant her. That is why she hunts so greedily for proof of that preference. She keeps her lover's desire alive and fans it, not in order to give in to it but in order to refuse better.* (ch. IV, English pp. 105–106)

**What it looks like, concretely.** Warm voice note, then a day of nothing. A kiss at the end of a message and a flat answer to the actual question. Tells you something intimate, then mentions someone else. Nobody writes this down in advance.

**And here is the part almost everyone misses.** The coldness is not fake:

> « L'indifférence de la coquette envers les souffrances de son amant n'est pas simulée mais elle n'a rien à voir avec l'indifférence ordinaire. Elle n'est pas absence de désir ; elle est l'envers d'un désir de soi-même. »
> *Her indifference to her lover's suffering is not simulated, but it has nothing to do with ordinary indifference. It is not an absence of desire. It is the back side of a desire for herself.*

The lover then makes a specific mistake, and this is the engine:

> « Il croit même reconnaître dans l'indifférence de sa maîtresse cette autonomie divine dont il se sent lui-même privé et qu'il brûle de conquérir. »
> *He thinks he recognises in her indifference that divine self-sufficiency he feels deprived of and is burning to conquer.*

You do not want her. You want whatever it is she seems to have that lets her not need you. That is the actual object.

**Character.** Asuka. She needs Shinji to want her so she can be revolted by him wanting her, and when he finally offers nothing at all she comes apart, because the refusing was holding her up.

**THE TEST, and run it before you ever apply this word to a real person.** Does she cool down *right after you get closer*? Or does she cool down when she is tired, working, or dealing with her family? If the cooling tracks her own life, it is her life and this section does not apply. Coquetry is defined by the withdrawal being *caused by* your approach.

Girard adds a footnote that matters here:

> « La coquetterie est d'ailleurs une médiation très instable, à fleur de peau, et qui exige d'être constamment renouvelée par de nouveaux désirs. [...] Lorsque le médiateur se rapproche du sujet désirant, la coquetterie disparaît. »
> *Coquetry is a very unstable mediation, skin deep, needing constant renewal by new desires. When the model comes close to the desiring subject, coquetry disappears.*

Translation: it does not survive real closeness. If it is still there after real closeness, it was never coquetry.

## 5. Stage four, symptom two: master and slave

This is the chapter title itself, `CHAPITRE IV - Le maître et l'esclave`, English pp. 96–112.

**Simple version.** Whoever shows what they feel loses. Whoever hides it gains. So both people end up managing the display instead of living in it.

> « Le secret de la réussite, en affaires comme en amour est la dissimulation. Il faut dissimuler le désir qu'on éprouve, il faut simuler le désir qu'on n'éprouve pas. Il faut mentir. »
> *The secret of success, in business as in love, is concealment. You have to hide the desire you feel and fake the desire you do not feel. You have to lie.*

Stendhal's rule, which Girard repeats: to show a vain woman that you want her is to reveal yourself as the inferior one.

**Character, and this is the best entry in the whole system.** Iroh and Zuko. Iroh never withholds, never bargains with his approval, never uses Zuko's need against him. So for two and a half seasons Zuko treats him like garbage, because under this rule a person who cannot withhold has no power. Iroh's line at the Western Air Temple, that he was never angry, only sad, is a refusal to play at all. That refusal is what makes the reunion land.

**The fork, and this is your actual question answered.**

Choosing the slave seat on purpose is not an exit. Girard gives masochism and sadism a whole chapter (`CHAPITRE VIII`, English pp. 176–192) precisely because they are two seats at one table, not opposites. The masochist has not left the structure, he has stopped resisting it, and the tell is that the other person becomes more godlike the less they give.

But total openness *can* be the exit. Same behaviour, different thing underneath. The difference is whether there is a bill attached.

| | Open with a bill | Open with no bill |
|---|---|---|
| Why you said it | to produce a response | because it was true |
| After you send it | you check what it did | you go do something else |
| When nothing comes back | it lands as a wound | it lands as nothing |
| Next disclosure | bigger, because the last one failed | same size |
| What it is | masochism, ch. VIII | conversion, ch. XII |

**The test.** After you tell her something, do you check what it did? Read receipt, tone, speed, whether your name was in it. If you check, it was a payment plan.

**Do not "fix" this by going cold.** That is just taking the master seat, which is the same table. The fix is the bill, not the openness.

## 6. Stage four, symptom three: Flaubert's law

> « Lorsque la médiation double envahit le domaine de l'amour, tout espoir de réciprocité s'évanouit. Flaubert formule dans ses notes ce principe absolu que "jamais deux êtres n'aiment en même temps". »
> *Once double mediation invades love, every hope of reciprocity vanishes. Flaubert states the absolute principle in his notes, that two beings never love at the same time.* (English p. 108)

**Simple version.** The wanting cannot sync, because each person's warmth is being produced by the other's coolness. You get close, she pulls back. You give up, she warms. Not because either of you is cruel. Because that is what a closed circuit does.

Girard then says the hard part:

> « La passion romantique est donc exactement l'inverse de ce qu'elle prétend être. Elle n'est pas abandon à l'Autre mais guerre implacable que se font deux vanités rivales. »
> *Romantic passion is exactly the opposite of what it claims to be. It is not surrender to the Other, it is a merciless war between two rival vanities.*

And he brings in Denis de Rougemont on Tristan and Iseult, who "s'entr'aiment, mais chacun n'aime l'autre qu'à partir de soi, non de l'autre," a false reciprocity that is a mask over a double narcissism.

**The one useful consequence.** The asymmetry is not a verdict on you and not a verdict on her. It is geometry. Stop reading it as a message.

## 7. Stage five: the person who is not in the room

This is the part you asked about and it is the most practically useful thing in the book.

**Simple version.** Triangles chain. Your model has their own model. Girard calls these « triangles en chaîne ».

> « Oreste est l'esclave d'Hermione ; Hermione est l'esclave de Pyrrhus ; Pyrrhus est l'esclave d'Andromaque qui est elle-même fidèle au souvenir d'un mort. Tous ces personnages ont les yeux fixés sur leur médiateur et sont, envers leurs esclaves, d'une indifférence absolue. »
> *Orestes is Hermione's slave. Hermione is Pyrrhus's slave. Pyrrhus is Andromache's slave, and she is faithful to the memory of a dead man. Every one of them has their eyes fixed on their model and is absolutely indifferent to the person enslaved to them.* (ch. IV)

Read the last link again. **The chain terminates in someone who is not alive and not present.** Everyone in the play is arranged around an absence.

Girard's other example is Proust: Saint-Loup is Rachel's slave, Rachel is the polo player's slave, the polo player is André's slave.

**What this means for fights.** A large share of couple conflict is not two people fighting each other. It is two people arranged around third parties nobody has named. The usual candidates:

- an ex, whether hers or yours
- a family whose approval sets what is allowed
- a friend she talks to more easily
- an imagined future rival who does not exist yet
- a past version of the person's own attention, which someone else once received

**The diagnostic.** If you resolve the surface issue and a new surface issue appears within a few days, the surface was never it. Something in the chain is unnamed.

**Scenario.** The fight is about reply speed. You win the argument, she agrees to reply faster, she does, and four days later you are upset about the length of the replies instead. The reply speed was a proxy. What is actually being measured is whether the absent party still holds more ground than you do.

**Note on absent rivals.** They are worse than present ones, not better, because they cannot be beaten. A present rival can lose. Andromache's dead husband cannot.

## 8. Stage six: the fake exits

Girard spends most of chapters VII and VIII on the ways out that are not ways out. All of these feel like escape.

**Fake exit 1, strategic withdrawal.** Reply slower, be less available, have your own life so you look like you need her less. Girard's whole point is that this works, and that is the objection. You have played the master seat correctly and your behaviour is now fully determined by hers.

**Fake exit 2, askesis for possession.** Denying yourself now in order to have more later. `CHAPITRE VII - L'ascèse du héros`. Girard is clear that the hero's self-discipline in internal mediation is a longer road to the same object, not a departure from it.

**Fake exit 3, deciding not to want anything.** Chapter XI, on Valéry's Monsieur Teste and on Nietzsche. Girard's read is that engineering your own indifference is the most advanced form of pride available, not freedom from pride.

**Fake exit 4, and this is the funniest one, curing someone else of it.**

> « Nous ressemblons à ces amis de Don Quichotte qui s'acharnent à guérir le malheureux de sa folie parce qu'ils en sont eux-mêmes les victimes, sous une forme aggravée. »
> *We are like those friends of Don Quixote who work so hard to cure the poor man of his madness, because they are victims of it themselves in an aggravated form.*

If you catch yourself explaining mimetic desire to her during a fight, you are here.

## 9. Stage seven: the real exit

**Girard's fork, stated flatly:**

> « Dès que le sujet désirant perçoit le rôle de l'imitation dans son propre désir il doit renoncer au désir ou renoncer à son orgueil. La lucidité moderne déplace et élargit le problème de l'ascèse. Il ne s'agit plus de renoncer momentanément à l'objet pour mieux le posséder, mais de renoncer au désir lui-même. Il faut choisir entre l'orgueil et le désir puisque le désir fait de nous des esclaves. »
> *The moment the desiring subject sees the role of imitation in his own desire, he has to give up either the desire or his pride. Modern lucidity shifts and widens the problem of askesis. It is no longer about giving up the object for a while in order to possess it better, it is about giving up the desire itself. You have to choose between pride and desire, since desire is what makes us slaves.*

**What is actually being renounced.** Not her. Not wanting. The demand that another person supply your worth. Girard calls the thing being dropped « le désir métaphysique », and its structure is always the same, a person treated as the source of a being you think you lack.

**What conversion looks like from inside.** This is the passage, chapter XII:

> « En renonçant à la divinité le héros renonce à l'esclavage. Tous les plans de l'existence s'invertissent, tous les effets du désir métaphysique sont remplacés par des effets contraires. Le mensonge fait place à la vérité, l'angoisse au souvenir, l'agitation au repos, la haine à l'amour, l'humiliation à l'humilité, le désir selon l'Autre au désir selon Soi, la transcendance déviée à la transcendance verticale. »
> *In giving up divinity the hero gives up slavery. Every plane of existence inverts. Lying gives way to truth, anguish to memory, agitation to rest, hatred to love, humiliation to humility, desire according to the Other to desire according to the Self, deviated transcendence to vertical transcendence.*

Use that as a checklist, not as poetry. Six pairs, and you can check yourself against any of them on a given day. The most testable is the third, agitation to rest.

**When it happens, and this is the discouraging part.**

> « Le héros triomphe dans la défaite ; il triomphe parce qu'il est à bout de ressources ; il lui faut, pour la première fois, regarder en face son désespoir et son néant. Mais ce regard si redouté, ce regard qui est la mort de l'orgueil est un regard sauveur. »
> *The hero triumphs in defeat. He triumphs because he is out of resources. For the first time he has to look his despair and his nothingness in the face. And that dreaded look, the look that is the death of pride, is a saving look.*

Never on a good day. Always at the end of the rope. Girard's image for it:

> « Toutes les conclusions romanesques font songer au conte oriental dont le héros est agrippé par les doigts au bord d'une falaise ; épuisé, ce héros finit par se laisser choir dans l'abîme. Il s'attend à s'écraser sur le sol mais les airs le soutiennent ; la pesanteur est abolie. »
> *All the novel-endings recall the eastern tale where the hero is hanging by his fingers off a cliff. Exhausted, he finally lets himself drop into the abyss. He expects to hit the ground, but the air holds him up. Gravity is abolished.*

**Character.** Don Quixote on the deathbed, disowning Amadis, which Girard quotes twice as the single sentence the whole book was heading toward. Julien in the cell. Raskolnikov. Every one of them at the bottom.

## 10. Stage eight: rebuilding external mediation on purpose

If internal mediation is the disease and distance is the only variable, the constructive move is to have a model that cannot become a rival.

**Girard's own answer, from *Things Hidden*.** The problem with every human model is that it sends two orders at once:

> "taken as model, imitate me; and as rival, do not imitate me."

That is the double bind, and there is no clever way to obey both. His solution is not less imitation. It is a model built so the second order never fires:

> "he offers not the slightest hold to any form of rivalry or mimetic interference. There is no acquisitive desire in him. As a consequence, any will that is really turned toward Jesus will not meet with the slightest of obstacles."

> "They do not claim that humans must get rid of imitation; they recommend imitating the sole model who never runs the danger of being transformed into a fascinating rival."

And from *I See Satan*, the precise content of it:

> "What Jesus invites us to imitate is his own desire, the spirit that directs him toward the goal on which his intention is fixed: to resemble God the Father as much as possible."

> "Far from being a paradox, this invitation is more reasonable than that of our modern gurus, who ask their disciples to imitate them as the great man or woman who imitates no one."

**Now the trap, and it is a real one.** If the model above the pair is a faith that one of you has and the other is only curious about, then you are not two people looking up at the same thing. You are the model. That is chapter II's deviated transcendence with you standing in the slot, and it will look like the exit for about a month.

**Rule.** The shared thing has to be something you can be outpaced in. Concretely:

1. Do not teach. Answering her questions with an apparatus makes you the authority in four messages.
2. Same text, same week, both report. She says what she noticed, you say what you noticed, and part of your job is to be honestly stuck in front of her.
3. She sets the pace. If she stops for a month, that is not data about anything.
4. It has to survive you being a disappointment. A desire built on your prestige collapses when your prestige does, which is the entire thesis of this book.

**The general form.** Two people jointly turned toward something neither of them owns and that does not run out. Rivalry requires scarcity. Give the imitation somewhere non-scarce to point.

## 11. Scenario bank

Read the scenario, guess the diagnosis, then check.

**A.** She sends a long warm message. You feel great for an hour. By evening you need another one.
→ Metaphysical desire. The object was never the message, it was proof of being. Proof does not accumulate, which is why the dose repeats.

**B.** You send something honest and vulnerable. She answers warmly but does not match it. You feel a specific sting.
→ The bill. Section 5. Openness was real, the invoice was attached, and the sting is the invoice going unpaid.

**C.** You catch yourself feeling more strongly about her right after she has been distant.
→ The mechanism in section 4. The distance looked like self-sufficiency and self-sufficiency is what you are actually after.

**D.** A fight about one concrete thing gets resolved. A different concrete thing appears four days later.
→ Chain triangle, section 7. Somebody is not in the room and has not been named.

**E.** You consider replying slower to see what happens.
→ Fake exit 1. It will work and that is the problem.

**F.** You feel calm about her for a whole week and it worries you, like something has been lost.
→ Worth sitting with. Under this system that is the third inversion, agitation to rest, and it is supposed to feel like loss at first, because the agitation was doing a job.

**G.** You explain a Girard concept to her mid-conversation.
→ Fake exit 4. Section 8, the friends of Don Quixote.

**H.** You count something. Seconds, messages, who started it, how many times.
→ Counting is the rivalry in the only form available when the rival is absent. The count is not measuring her, it is asking whether the absent party still holds more ground.

## 12. What this system is not for

Guardrails, because a diagram this good will explain everything you point it at, including things that are not true.

- **Not every withdrawal is coquetry.** Run the test in section 4. Tiredness, work, family obligations, and a person's own stated reasons are not the structure.
- **Not every obstacle is a message about feelings.** Family rules, geography, and schedules apply to everyone and mean nothing about you.
- **A plainly stated no is the opposite of coquetry.** Coquetry needs ambiguity to run on. Someone who tells you straight what they can and cannot do right now is not playing.
- **The theory is about your own desire first.** Every one of Girard's heroes uses the insight on other people for eleven chapters and only turns it on himself at the end. That order is the disease.
