---
title: The Cross-Examination
---
There are two men in my head and they have never once agreed on anything.

The first one I call Voss. He has read everything, and he has an answer for everything, and the answer is always clean, and it is always, underneath, the same answer. Men wrote this. Men remembered it wrong. Nobody is home. He wins every round. What eats him alive is that the rest of us will not just say so.

The other one I call Ames. Ames has almost nothing. One thread and two tired hands. He loses every round, and he knows he loses, and he will not get up from the table.

I set a verse down in front of the two of them and let them go. Eight of them, the smallest seed to the empty grave. I did not step in. I would not know where to start.

> It is like a mustard seed, which is the smallest of all seeds on earth.
> Mark 4:31

**Voss.** This is where it started it all for me, so this is where we gets under my skin. And I need you to see how small the crack was, because the smallness is the whole thing. It is the whole horror of it.

Smallest of all seeds, he says. It is not. Poppy is smaller. Orchid is smaller. A child could beat him with a magnifying glass before lunch. And yes, it is a parable, yes it is farmer's talk, I know, I taught it, do not hand me back the thing I taught. It does not matter.

Because the girl who is only sick when Mark tells it and already dead when Matthew tells it. The day of the week the man died, which John and Mark flatly cannot agree on. And this is maddening to me, genuinely maddening, that I can sit here and hold all of it in my head at one time, the seed and the girl and the day and the two temple cleansing, ALL of it, and hand you the cause of every last piece, and people still look at me like I am the one who lost something.

That I can do that. That a mind can do that. Every one of them has one cause now and the cause is men, writing, remembering it each their own way, and the second the cause is men writing there is nothing left in the whole pile that needs a God behind it.

And tell me, how is God the one orchestrating all of it when the thing cannot even survive being read closely by me, by ME, a mortal, a man who dies, who could do nothing at all in front of a real God if there were one. A real God does not get caught out by a graduate student with a Greek text. It is finished. I shut it on the smallest of all seeds and it has never once creaked.

**Ames.** I know you shut it. I watched you shut it.

But look at your hands while you swear to me it is finished, because you are pulling one thing down to bring down a coat you never took off. You still go white when something cruel happens in a room. You still walk toward the person everyone else is edging away from. You will do it a dozen times before we are through, and you will call it something else.

And you did the thing you always do. You read down to the size of the seed and you stopped reading. Go four sentences on, same breath, same chapter. It tells you why the man is talking in seeds at all. To utter, it says, the things hidden since the foundation of the world.

That is what the seed was buried over. Not its size. The thing underneath it. And the thing underneath it, hidden since the foundation, is a body. The first killing and every killing that has copied it since, the crowd and the one they threw out, the blood Genesis says is already screaming up out of the dirt.

You were standing in a field planted over the oldest grave in the human race. And you got down on your knees in it and counted the seeds.

> Let any one of you who is without sin be the first to throw a stone at her.
> John 8:7

**Voss.** Fine. Take the one you love, and watch, because I can shut this one too. I can shut every last one of them. That is the thing I keep trying to make you feel in your teeth.

The woman, the stones, he stoops and writes in the dirt, let the one without sin throw first, and the mob just drains away into the street, oldest to youngest. The most human thing in the whole book. And it is not in the oldest manuscripts. It is not in the earliest John. A scribe put it there three hundred years later.

I do not think this. I KNOW it, the way I know my own name, because catching that, the exact place a later hand slipped in, is the thing I did with my one life. So the most beautiful thing in the Gospels is a fake. A graft.

And here is what I want you to really look at. Not the manuscript. Me. You hand me the loveliest thing you own and I have the drawer open already, I do not even reach for it, later insertion, in it goes, and it shuts, and I feel nothing. Nothing. And that, that I can feel nothing while I do it, is the thing I would stand up and defend in front of God and everybody and call honesty.

**Ames.** You are right about the manuscript. I am not going to arm wrestle you over the text, you would snap my wrist, you know it.

So stay on the last thing you said. Not the scholarship. The thing you confessed underneath it and did not hear yourself confess. You have a drawer for everything, and it always shuts, and you feel nothing when it shuts. Voss. That is not a free man standing in a field. That is a man in a very small, very clean, very well lit room who has stopped trying the door, because the door is painted on the wall, and he is the one who painted it.

And here is the thing your drawer could not swallow. The earliest copies did not have this scene, and the church could not keep it out. It kept crawling back into the book for a thousand years, against the manuscripts, because it was too true to lose.

And what it is true about is you. Watch a stoning actually happen. Nobody throws first. Everybody stands there with the rock going heavy in his hand, waiting for the first arm to go up, because the first stone is the one that turns a street full of scared people into a mob, and the moment it is a mob nobody is guilty because everybody is.

And one man kneels down and will not give them the first stone. He hands every one of them his own face back instead. And the thing that needed all of them moving as one just falls apart on the cobbles, the old men leaving first, because the old men have the most faces to answer for.

You have a drawer for the verse. You have never once had one for why your own hand will not close around the rock. Somebody emptied that hand a long time before you got here. You could at least ask who.

> Jesus reached out his hand and touched the man. "I am willing," he said. "Be clean!"
> Mark 1:41

**Voss.** He touches a leper. Puts his bare hand on the one body in the country that not the priest, not the man's own mother, will go near. Lovely.

And I will tell you what it is, exactly, all the way down, because I have the name for it and the name closes. Compassion. Evolved. It is in the chimps. It is a herd animal keeping its herd alive, and yes it feels like heaven from the inside, I know it feels like heaven from the inside, I felt it on my knees for years, but from the outside it is a hormone and three hundred thousand years of apes who lived because they stuck together.

And here is the part that should just end it. I am kinder now, off my knees, than I ever was on them. With no God at all. There is a cause for the kindness and the cause is an animal, and once you are holding the animal you can put the miracle down. I do not miss it. The animal does every single thing the miracle was supposed to do, and it never once asks me to believe a thing I cannot see.

**Ames.** Some of it is the animal. Not this one, and you know it, so turn your own Darwin on it.

The animal does not walk toward the sick one. The animal drives the sick one out. The herd lives by shoving whatever is weak or marked or catching to the edge and leaving it out there to die, so the rest stay clean. That is not my Bible, Voss. That is your biology, page after page.

And the human animal is worse, because it does it with a reason attached. Your beloved Greeks and Romans set the wrong-shaped baby out on the hill at night and came home and slept. Every people on the map had its lepers past the gate, its untouchable, its one poor devil it could throw out so the rest could feel whole again.

Walking at that man is not the old wiring firing. It is the old wiring thrown into reverse. It was not obvious. By every rule that came before it, it was lunacy, until enough people watched one man do it on purpose, touch the leper, eat with the traitor, sit the whore down at his table, and it slowly, across centuries, rebuilt the floor of what a person is even allowed to think is decent.

You did not evolve the love of the leper. You caught it, a long way downriver, off the very hand you keep swearing is not there, and then you filed it under kin selection. But kin selection is the love of your own blood. This is the love of the one who is not your blood and will never, ever pay you back. That is the one love the animal was built, from the floor up, to refuse.

> I saw Satan fall like lightning from heaven.
> Luke 10:18

**Voss.** I saw Satan fall like lightning from heaven. And I know what that is, precisely, and it costs me nothing. Watch.

It is a man in the first century with no germ theory, no plate tectonics, no notion what a virus is, reaching for spirits to explain his bad luck, the same reflex that stuck a god behind the thunder and a demon behind the fever. Prescientific. We outgrew it.

There is no accuser in the clouds getting knocked out of the sky. You will not catch me so much as blinking at the word Satan, or demon, or any of it. I have a drawer for the devil, and it is a big one, and it is nearly full, and it has never once cost me an hour of sleep.

**Ames.** No devil in the clouds, agreed. Put the horns away. But the word, Voss, the actual Greek, and you above all people care about the actual Greek. Ho satanas. The accuser. The prosecutor.

Satan is not first a thing with a tail. He is a job. He is the voice that goes through a crowd and says there, him, that one, name him, and the whole room goes quiet and level and finally, finally united the instant the name lands, because now they have somebody to be against together, and being against together feels like peace.

That is what this man says he watched drop out of the sky. So now say your own life out loud, plain, the way you make every believer say his to your face. What have you been for thirty years. The debater with the sledgehammer. Your words. You are proud of them. The man who runs the prosecution. Who finds the guilty sentence and holds it up over his head. Who wins by proving the other one wrong and feels the whole room lean in behind him when he does it.

You told yourself you walked out of the church into clean cold reason. There are two reasons in that one coat and you only ever wore the one. There is the reason that gets to the truth by loving it, that would honestly rather be corrected than win. And there is the reason that gets there by clubbing a man to the ground with it and standing over him while the crowd nods along.

The first one is rare, and quiet, and almost nobody has it. The second one is the oldest altar there is. It is the accuser. It is the exact thing this verse says is falling out of the sky. You did not leave him when you left the church. You went and got the law degree, and you have been billing his hours ever since, and calling it the truth.

> Father, if you are willing, take this cup from me; yet not my will, but yours be done.
> Luke 22:42

**Voss.** The garden. Take this cup from me. Even he does not want it.

He is face down in the dirt sweating it, begging his way out of it, and I have the cause and it is small and it is human and I understand it from the inside better than any believer who ever drew breath. It is a man who does not want to die. That is all it is.

There is no plan being nobly and calmly carried out. There is a body pumping adrenaline into the dark and a scared young man who can see exactly what the morning is bringing him. And I know that fear, I have stood on the lip of it, we all have. It is the most honest second in the whole book, and it is also the most ordinary second in the whole book, and by now, if you have been listening at all, you can see those were never two things.

**Ames.** Yes. He did not want it. Now hold it, do not walk over it in the dark the way you just did, because it is the hinge the whole thing turns on.

Listen to what every other version of me is, all day, awake and asleep. A hand closing on something. Grabbing. Trying to get solid, trying to feel real, wanting the thing the other man has, the win, the name, the seat at the front, so I can feel like I am somebody instead of a speck on a speck. And you are the one who handed me that. You said it in your own lecture, out of your own mouth. We are self-centered because we are tiny, and being looked at takes the sting out of being tiny.

Alright. Then look at him. The one life in the whole story that could have kept its grip, that could have reached up and pulled down the exact self-sufficiency you keep telling me is a fairy tale, the throne, the untouchable body, all of it there for the taking, and with all of it on the table he opens the hand. Not my will.

He grabs at nothing. He takes his being off of no one. He is the only man in that garden, maybe the only man in the whole book, who is not trying to become somebody at some other body's expense, and that, that alone, is why he is the one free man there while everyone around him is still clutching in his sleep.

You filed the wanting under human wiring, a bug we are stuck with. He is the one place the wiring lets go. You walked right up to the only door out and called it adrenaline.

> He saved others, but he can't save himself! Let him come down now from the cross, and we will believe in him.
> Matthew 27:42

**Voss.** On the cross they are screaming come down and we will believe you. He saved others, they yell, he cannot save himself. And he does not come down. And I have the cause and it is clean as glass.

He does not come down because he cannot, because there is nobody up there to reach in and lift him off it. And the silence that lands when the crowd finally, out loud, in front of everyone, asks for the proof, that silence is not a mystery. It is the answer. It is THE answer. It is the same flat silence I have gotten back every single time I have needed anything more than weather out of that sky.

There is nothing left to explain on that hill. He stayed on the wood because of gravity and iron and the weight of a grown man, and that is the whole of it, and it is the most complete thing I own.

**Ames.** Then hear what the crowd is actually ordering him to do, word for word, because the words are the whole thing. Save yourself. Be untouchable. Get down off it and show us nothing can touch you and THEN we will bow.

And that, Voss, that is the god you already threw out, and you were right to throw him out. The strongman on the throne, needing nothing, feeling nothing, untouched and untouchable, the god who proves he is a god by never once being hurt. If he had come down he would have turned into that exact god in front of the whole crowd and proved you right forever.

He would not do it. He turned down the one move that wins the argument on the spot, because taking it meant agreeing with the mob about what a god even is.

And you know this shape from the inside. You live inside it. It is the whole frame of you. Nothing can touch me. I have reasoned my way clear of every fear, hell, death, the void, all of it, calm, one at a time. I am immune.

But the immune man is always, every single time, the one an hour from the fall, and you know it, because you told me the story yourself and did not hear yourself telling it. Peter said I will never fall, not me, never, and the word was not cold before the rooster. You said you had reasoned your way clear of all of it, and then a mudslide in Colombia took thirty thousand souls in one night and you were on the floor of it.

Refusing to come down is not the weak spot in the story. It is the one being in the whole thing who would not put on the immunity you are standing here right now wearing like a coat you forgot you had on.

> My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?
> Matthew 27:46

**Voss.** And then the end. My God, my God, why have you forsaken me. God himself, if you have to have the word, nailed up and screaming into the sky, and the sky hands him back nothing.

And I do not even need the drawer for this one, and I want you to notice that. This is the one place I stop filing and just agree. This one I believe. It is the truest line anyone has ever written down, in any book, the only sentence in the whole of Scripture that fits, without a seam, the world I actually open my eyes onto every morning. A good man screaming why into an empty room and getting the weather back.

And that is not one man on one hill. That is everybody's real prayer, the one underneath all the polite ones, and that prayer, the true one, the only fully honest one anyone ever prayed, went unanswered. That is not even my argument against God anymore. I am past arguing. That is just the transcript of the room.

**Ames.** I have no answer that fills that silence, and I am not going to hand you a warm one, because you would be right to throw it back in my face, and I would deserve it. So two things, only two, and then I will shut up and stand in it here beside you.

First. You called it forsaken. You called it wrong. An innocent man handed nothing, a scandal, a thing that must not be. And you got that ruler off this cross, off this exact wood, the innocent one must not be left, and you are swinging it, this second, at the man who nailed it into you. You cannot even file the scene without the ruler the scene gave you.

Second. He was quoting. That scream is not a loose howl. It is the first line of a psalm, the twenty-second, and any Jew on that hill who caught the first line was already hearing it run to the end. And it does not stay in the dark. It goes down, all the way down, through the bones out of joint and the tongue stuck to the roof of the mouth and the men splitting his clothes by lot, which they were doing that very minute under his feet, and then, past all reason, past the place any honest man would let it turn, it turns. It ends on the strangest words in it, that the afflicted one cried and was heard, and that a people not yet born would be told what was done here.

A scream that sounds exactly like the empty sky, with an address on the back of the envelope.

It does not feed one starving child tonight. I keep saying it because it is true and I will not lie to you to win an inch. It is a thin thread. He asked why too. He asked your question, in your words. I am holding it in the dark and I am tired, and it is still, on the worst nights, the only thing that has ever kept me from setting the whole cup down for good.

> Put your finger here; see my hands. Reach out your hand and put it into my side. Stop doubting and believe.
> John 20:27

**Voss.** And the resurrection, which you are about to put down in front of me like it is the last card, the one that takes the whole hand. Except the four of them cannot even agree on it.

Matthew has the risen man meeting them up in Galilee, seventy miles north. Luke bolts them to Jerusalem and will not let them leave the city. Same morning. A hundred miles apart. Four witnesses to the most important sunrise in the history of the species and they cannot keep the room straight, cannot keep the CITY straight, cannot keep the order of who saw what straight.

And I have the cause and it is the same cause as the seed and the stones and all of it. Men, telling a story, and the story drifting the way every story that was ever told drifts, picking up a detail here, dropping a whole city there. There is nothing in this a rumor cannot carry the whole way on its own legs.

I have accounted for the empty tomb exactly the way I accounted for the smallest of all seeds. Nothing is left over. The room is complete. And I want you to see how complete it is, because the completeness is the thing I am proudest of in this world.

**Ames.** Then let me stand right on the thing you are proudest of, because that is the exact place you went wrong, and you, only you, have the training to see it.

You spent your whole life on forgeries. So you know, better than any believer alive, what a coordinated lie looks like on the page. It looks clean. It agrees with itself down to the chairs in the room, because getting the story straight is the whole entire point of telling a lie together. The forger's work has no seam.

Four accounts that cannot agree on the city, that trip over each other about who reached the tomb first and who saw what, are not four men in a back room fixing a story. They are the opposite of that, and you know it.

And here is the part you have to take, in your own discipline, in your own voice, because it is the whole of what I have got. Your clean, complete, airtight version, the room with nothing left over, the seamless account of every last thing, is not the shape of the truth, Voss. It is the shape of the forgery. You built, around your own life, with your own hands, the one thing you spent your whole career catching other men in, and you cannot see the seam because for once you are standing on the inside of it.

And now look, one last time, at who the risen man walks toward, in the one scene I have never once been able to put down. Not the believers. Not the ones who got it right. Thomas. The doubter. The one who crossed his arms and said unless I put my own finger into the actual holes in him I will not believe a word out of any of you.

And the man does not throw him out. He does not win the argument. He does not even really scold him. He holds his own broken hands out into the open air and says here. Put your finger here.

The doubter is not the villain at the end of the book. He is the one it crosses the whole room to reach. That is where you have been standing this whole time. And I am not going to press a proof into your hand, because I do not have one. I have never had one. I have only got the thing the story will not stop doing, in every account, no matter which city they set it in. It keeps walking toward the one who cannot believe. And it keeps showing him the wounds instead of the throne.

That is the two of them. I did not rig it.

Voss does not lose on the ground. He never loses on the ground. He has a drawer for every card, and half of what is in those drawers is just plainly true, and I will not insult either of us by pretending it is not.

The only thing Ames has on him, after all eight, is the shape of him. Everything explained. Nothing left over. Every thread tucked, every card filed, every drawer shut with that clean little click, and the door painted on the wall so well he stopped trying the handle years ago and started calling the room the world.

I stopped trusting that shape a long time ago. And here is the part I cannot get around. I first learned to distrust it in a believer who was certain of everything, who had an answer for my every doubt and a verse for my every wound. And I hear the exact same airlessness now in the man who is certain of nothing. It is the same sealed room with the paint switched.

I am certain of almost nothing. I carried one thread through eight verses and it did not snap. He asked why too. He opened his hand in the garden. He walked at the doubter with the wounds held out.

That is not a proof, and I will not dress it as one. It is a direction. I am turned toward it in the dark and I am not letting go, and some nights, I will tell you as plain as I know how, that is the whole of the faith. All of it. One tired man who will not set the cup down.
