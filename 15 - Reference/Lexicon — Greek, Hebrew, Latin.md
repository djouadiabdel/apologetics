# Lexicon — the words that carry weight

> Glosses are in English because this serves the whole vault: the theology track, Girard, James, and `08 - Islam Debates`. The **French** column is what you will actually meet in Lossky, Damascène and Augustin.
>
> The column that matters is **THE TRAP**. Anyone can learn a definition. Knowing where a word betrays people is the difference.

**Learn these twelve first.** They carry more than all the rest combined:
*ousia · hypostasis · physis · prosopon · homoousios · ekporeusis · perichoresis · theotokos · echad · skandalon · paradosis · principaliter*

---

## 1. Counting God

| Word | Lit. | FR | Means | THE TRAP |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **οὐσία** *ousia* | being | *essence, ousie* | the **what**. What a thing is. | Aristotle used it for both the individual *and* the kind. The Fathers had to pick. They picked "the kind." |
| **ὑπόστασις** *hypostasis* | that which stands under | *hypostase* | the **who**. A concrete subsistent. | Literally the same meaning as Latin *substantia*. So Greeks saying "three hypostases" sounded to Latin ears like "three substances," i.e. three gods. **Centuries of the quarrel are this one translation.** |
| **φύσις** *physis* | growth, nature | *nature* | the **what**, again | Cyril uses *physis* where Chalcedon uses *hypostasis*. Whether that is one dispute or two vocabularies is **your Module 2**. |
| **πρόσωπον** *prosopon* | face, mask | *personne* | the **who**, from the outside | "Mask" is the problem. Nestorius' *prosopic union* sounded to Cyril like a union of appearances, not of being. |
| **ὁμοούσιος** *homoousios* | same-being | *consubstantiel* | same essence as the Father, Nicaea 325 | One iota from **ὁμοιούσιος** *homoi-ousios*, "of *like* essence." The whole 4th century turns on that letter. |
| **ἁπλότης** *haplotēs* | simplicity | *simplicité* | no parts, no composition | Not "easy to understand." It means nothing in God is added to anything else, which is **why the relation cannot be an add-on**. |

## 2. Where the Persons come from

| Word | Lit. | FR | Means | THE TRAP |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **ἐκπόρευσις** *ekporeusis* | going out from | *procession* | origin **from the ultimate source** | Reserved to the Father alone in Greek. |
| **προϊέναι** *proienai* | to come forth | *procéder* | coming forth, broadly | Greeks accept this **through the Son**. Latin has only *procedere* for both words, so each side denies something the other never said. **Module 4.** |
| **γέννησις** *gennēsis* | begetting | *génération* | how the Son is from the Father | Not creation. *Genētos* (created) vs *gennētos* (begotten) differ by one letter and are opposites. |
| **ἀγέννητος** *agennētos* | unbegotten | *inengendré* | the Father's property | Nazianzen: tell me what unbegottenness *is* and I will explain procession, and we shall both go mad. |
| **περιχώρησις** *perichōrēsis* | going around | *périchorèse* | mutual indwelling of the three | Latin renders it *circumincessio*. The three are **in** one another; this is what stops distinction becoming division. |
| **μοναρχία** *monarchia* | single origin | *monarchie* | the Father as sole source | Not political. It is the thing the East will not trade for the Filioque. |

## 3. The incarnation

| Word | Lit. | FR | Means | THE TRAP |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **θεοτόκος** *theotokos* | God-bearer | *Théotokos, Mère de Dieu* | Mary bears God, not merely a man | Nestorius preferred *Christotokos*. Ephesus 431 is fought over this word, and it is a claim about **Christ**, not about Mary. |
| **λόγος** *logos* | word, reason, account | *Verbe* | John 1:1 | French says *Verbe*, not *Mot*. If you translate it "word" in your head you lose the "reason/rational principle" half. |
| **σάρξ** *sarx* | flesh | *chair* | "the Word became *sarx*" | Not "body" (*sōma*). *Sarx* is the weak, mortal, human condition. Choosing it was deliberate. |
| **κένωσις** *kenōsis* | emptying | *kénose* | Phil 2:7, he emptied himself | Emptied of **what**? Every Christology answers differently. Do not let it pass as a pious word. |
| **μονογενής** *monogenēs* | only-begotten / one-of-a-kind | *Fils unique* | John 1:14, 3:16 | *mono+gennaō* (only-begotten) or *mono+genos* (unique in kind)? Modern scholarship leans to the second. Real, live, and it touches Arius. |
| **ἀσυγχύτως, ἀτρέπτως, ἀδιαιρέτως, ἀχωρίστως** | | *sans confusion, sans changement, sans division, sans séparation* | Chalcedon's four adverbs | **All four are negative.** Chalcedon never says what the union *is*, only four things it is not. Notice that before you decide the council settled anything. |

## 4. Worship

| Word | Lit. | FR | Means | THE TRAP |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **προσκυνέω** *proskyneō* | to bow toward | *se prosterner* | obeisance, reverence | Given to kings and angels in scripture without blasphemy. |
| **λατρεύω** *latreuō* | to serve | *rendre un culte* | the service due to God alone | The distinction Catholics and Orthodox use for icons and saints. See your `Worship — Proskyneo and Latreuo`. **Test it, don't inherit it.** |

## 5. Hebrew: the names and the oneness

| Word | Lit. | Means | THE TRAP |
|---|---|---|---|
| **יהוה** *YHWH* | (he causes to be?) | the Name | Never vocalised in reading; *Adonai* is said instead. "Jehovah" is the vowels of *Adonai* on the consonants of YHWH. |
| **אֱלֹהִים** *elohim* | gods (plural form) | God | It takes **singular verbs**. The plural does **not** prove the Trinity, and using it that way is how you lose an argument to someone who knows Hebrew. |
| **אֶחָד** *echad* | one | Deut 6:4, *YHWH echad* | *Echad* can be a **composite** one: Gen 2:24, two become *one* flesh. |
| **יָחִיד** *yachid* | solitary, only | absolute singularity | Maimonides' creed swapped *echad* for *yachid*. **The Shema does not use yachid.** That swap is the whole argument, and it is not in the text. |
| **רוּחַ** *ruach* | breath, wind | spirit | Grammatically **feminine**. Do not build doctrine on the gender of a noun, in either direction. |
| **דָּבָר** *davar* | word / thing / event | speech that acts | Hebrew makes no split between saying and doing. Background to *Logos*. |
| **כָּבוֹד** *kavod* | weight | glory | Glory is heaviness, not brightness. |
| **חֶסֶד** *hesed* | — | covenant loyalty | Nearly untranslatable. Not "mercy," not "love": loyalty owed inside a bond. |
| **צֶלֶם / דְּמוּת** *tselem / demut* | image / likeness | Gen 1:26 | The Fathers split them; the Hebrew parallelism probably does not. |
| **מָשִׁיחַ** *mashiach* | anointed | messiah | Applied to kings, priests, **and Cyrus the Persian** (Isa 45:1). |

## 6. Girard's words — these are yours

| Word | Lit. | Means | THE TRAP |
|---|---|---|---|
| **σκάνδαλον** *skandalon* | the trigger of a trap | stumbling-block | Not "offence" in the modern sense. It is the obstacle you **cannot stop returning to**. Girard's whole account of desire is in this word. |
| **φαρμακός** *pharmakos* | the one expelled | the human scapegoat of the Greek city | Not *phármakon* (drug). Same root: the poison that cures. **The victim is both.** |
| **עֲזָאזֵל** *azazel* | (goat that goes? / a name?) | Lev 16, the goat sent into the wilderness | Two goats: one killed, one **sent away alive**. Girard's mechanism is in the ritual before it is in any myth. |
| **שָׂטָן** *satan* | accuser | the accuser | In Job it takes the definite article: *ha-satan*, **the** accuser. A role in a court, not yet a proper name. |
| **διάβολος** *diabolos* | one who throws across | slanderer | The Greek for the same office. Accusation is the job description. |

## 7. The Church

| Word | Lit. | FR | Means | THE TRAP |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **ἐκκλησία** *ekklēsia* | the called-out assembly | *Église* | church | A civic word first: the citizen assembly. LXX uses it for *qahal*. |
| **παράδοσις** *paradosis* | that which is handed over | *tradition* | tradition | Same root as *paradidōmi*, "to betray/hand over." Judas hands Jesus over with this verb. |
| **ἐπίσκοπος / πρεσβύτερος** *episkopos / presbyteros* | overseer / elder | *évêque / prêtre* | bishop / priest | **In the New Testament they are used of the same men** (Acts 20:17 with 20:28; Titus 1:5 with 1:7). When they separate, and on what argument, is **Module 5**. |
| **διαδοχή** *diadochē* | succession | *succession* | the line of bishops | Irenaeus uses it to identify **true teaching**, not to confer jurisdiction. |
| **ἀνάθεμα** *anathema* | set apart, devoted | *anathème* | cursed, cut off | Originally something devoted to God. The word for consecration became the word for exclusion. |

## 8. Latin operators

| Word | Means | THE TRAP |
|---|---|---|
| ***filioque*** | "and from the Son" | Three words, added to a creed that a council forbade adding to. The **procedure** is a separate grievance from the **doctrine**. Keep them apart. |
| ***principaliter*** | principally, as from the origin | Augustine's own qualifier: the Spirit proceeds **principaliter** from the Father. FR: *en premier lieu*. **This may be the whole of Module 4.** |
| ***persona / substantia / subsistentia*** | person / substance / subsistence | *Substantia* literally translates *hypostasis* but was used to translate *ousia*. The confusion is baked into Latin. |
| ***communicatio idiomatum*** | exchange of properties | Lets you say "God died." It licenses the **speech**, not a mixing of the natures. |
| ***lex orandi, lex credendi*** | the law of prayer is the law of belief | How you pray is what you believe. **Module 6 in four words.** |
| ***a fortiori*** | from the stronger | "if that, then this all the more." Ibn Taymiyya's *tawātur* argument is an *a fortiori*. |

## How to actually learn these

Not by rereading this file. **Add the word to the fiche whenever you meet it in a text**, in the GARANT column, with the sentence it appeared in.

A word learned where it was doing work stays. A word learned from a table does not.

And the twelve at the top are the ones to know cold, because you will meet them in every module.
