The DSS are exactly what show the diversity of messianic expectations _before_ Christianity and Islam. Add these (start after you finish the Genesis blocks):
    - _The Complete Dead Sea Scrolls in English_ – Geza Vermes (read the messianic/apocalyptic sections first: 4Q521, 1QSb, 4Q246 "Son of God" text, War Scroll, etc.)
    - James VanderKam – _The Dead Sea Scrolls Today_ (short, excellent overview)
    - Richard Hess – _Israel’s Messiah in the Bible and the Dead Sea Scrolls_
- **Historical-critical / scholarly layer** BibleProject is great for literary/theological reading but stays mostly at the "what does this mean in the big story?" level. To reach expert depth you'll eventually need:
    - Scholarly commentaries (e.g., on Isaiah, Genesis, or messianic texts)
    - Books on Second Temple Judaism and messianism

## The Orthodox reading list that runs in parallel

This is the other half. Pair by season, not by day — whatever BibleProject course you're on, you're also slowly working through the corresponding Orthodox text. Finish when you finish; don't rush to sync.

**Foundation layer (alongside Bible Reading Skills → early Genesis):**

- Fr. Thomas Hopko, _The Orthodox Faith_, Volume 1: _Doctrine and Scripture_ — free at oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith
- St. Athanasius, _On the Incarnation_ — free at newadvent.org, short (~100 pages), foundational. Read it twice.

**Middle layer (Genesis sequence → Exodus):**

- Hopko, Volume 2: _Worship_ — free, oca.org
- Fr. Alexander Schmemann, _For the Life of the World_ — not free, but ~$15 and worth breaking the "no paying" rule for this one alone. It reframes everything you'll read in Exodus through Orthodox sacramental theology. If you genuinely can't spend anything, his lectures covering the same material are on YouTube (search "Schmemann Sacraments lectures").

**Upper layer (Jonah, Ezekiel, into the Gospels):**

- Hopko, Volume 3: _Bible and Church History_ — free
- Vladimir Lossky, _The Mystical Theology of the Eastern Church_ — available free as PDF through archive.org (legitimate, out-of-copyright-style hosting). This one is hard. Go slowly. It will shape how you read Matthew.

**Capstone (Matthew sequence → Paul):**

- Hopko, Volume 4: _Spirituality_ — free
- _The Philokalia_, Volume 1 — available free at archive.org. Read slowly, in small doses, ideally alongside your prayer rule rather than as "study."
- Fr. John Behr's lectures on YouTube throughout — he's the current dean of Orthodox patristic scholarship in English, and his lectures on Irenaeus, Athanasius, and the Gospel of John are extraordinary and completely free.

That's the full four-year reading list. Everything free except Schmemann (and even that has a workaround).
