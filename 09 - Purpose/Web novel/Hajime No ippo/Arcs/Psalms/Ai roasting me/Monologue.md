**The monologue problem — how to learn when to shut up:**

You said your Kamogawa dialogue "sucks ass." Here's the principle you're looking for.

**If the body already said it, the mouth doesn't get to say it again.**

Ippo screamed "no" when Kamogawa tried to remove the wraps. That scream contains _everything_ — the shame, the fear, the refusal to face what's underneath. The scream is the scene. After that scream, anything Ippo says out loud ("it was my fault coach, I should have trained more") is _weaker_ than what his hands and his voice already delivered. He's translating a body-truth into word-language, and word-language is always a downgrade.

So the practice is: after you write a body moment that feels like the peak — the scream, the flinch, the hand going flat on the canvas — stop. Ask yourself: does anything that comes out of this character's mouth after this moment ADD something the body hasn't already said? If the answer is no, the mouth stays closed. Let the silence after the scream be the scene. Let other people fill the silence. Let the room react. But the character who just screamed? He's done. His body finished the sentence.

**And no** — the consistency thing isn't about your spelling. Your spelling is fine, that's editing, you're right. Consistency means: your best line in this draft ("the pressure of those hands full of wrinkles leave subtle but a known mark") is operating at a completely different level than your worst line ("Coach surprised but then says nothing as he looks with a seriousy face at ippo who is talking about the fact that..."). One is alive. The other is a plot summary wearing a sentence costume. Closing the gap between those two is the real 20%.
