### **Chapter 29 — Camera: Miyata**

**What happens:** Miyata on the train home after leaving the spar. He saw Ippo get handled by Imai. He didn't stay to watch. The chapter opens in motion — the train, the window, the lights passing. Miyata isn't processing with words. He's processing with his body. His hands in his lap. The way he keeps shifting his weight like he's trying to find a stance on a moving floor.

What he saw at the spar wasn't weakness. That's what disturbs him. Weakness he could understand — Ippo having a bad day, Imai being that good. What he saw was something else. Something in the way Ippo absorbed punches — not the iron chin that used to swallow shots, but something looser. Like a door that used to be bolted and now just swings. He doesn't have medical language for it. He has a fighter's language: the timing was off by a quarter-beat.

He thinks about the promised fight. Not with excitement. With something that sits in his chest like a stone he swallowed. If Ippo's timing is off by a quarter-beat, then the fight they've been building toward — two fighters at their peak — might be built on an assumption that's no longer true. And that thought is so terrifying that he does what Miyata always does with terrifying thoughts: he files it away behind his expression and stares out the window.

**Movement:** Miyata enters the chapter thinking "I went to see where Ippo is." He exits thinking "I saw something I didn't expect and I don't know what it means but I can't forget it."

**Seeds planted:**

- His hands. Write his hands specifically — the knuckles, the tape marks, the way they rest. These hands are the ones that are supposed to meet Ippo's face in the promised fight. They're waiting for something that might not arrive.
- He passes a poster for a boxing event on the train platform. He doesn't look at it. This is Miyata not wanting to think about the sport for one second, which is new.
- He thinks about calling Ippo. He doesn't. He'll call later about the mosquito, and that call will be the excuse he built from this train ride.

**Other arcs moved:** Ippo's arc moves backward — we learn through Miyata's eyes that the spar was worse than we thought. The promised fight arc takes a hit. The reader's understanding of the spar deepens retroactively.

**What changes:** Miyata didn't come to the spar on his own. **Takamura invited him.** This reshapes the entire emotional texture of the chapter.

There's pride in that invitation — Takamura thinks Ippo will look good against Imai, or at least thinks the spar will be entertaining.
