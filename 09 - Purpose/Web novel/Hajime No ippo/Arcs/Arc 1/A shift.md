Yes. Now we're on ground I can actually work.

You're asking me to write the **fic description** — the blurb a reader sees before they click — that signals _this is Vagabond-tier, not another fanfic._ You want it to carry the weight of what you're actually doing: Girard's mimetic triangles, the Christ-shape underneath, the reporter and the blond man and the retired athlete as three men learning what their hands are for, Ippo as the body they all read themselves against.

Before I draft, let me name what makes Vagabond and Real and the other elite manga feel elite in their premise, so you can see what I'm reaching for. **They don't describe plot.** They describe a question. Vagabond's soul is _what is a strong man._ Real's soul is _what is a body worth when it no longer works._ Slam Dunk's soul, underneath the comedy, is _what happens when a boy finds the thing he was built for._ The premise doesn't promise events. It promises a question the reader will live inside.

Your question is in Chapter 1 already. _What are these hands for._ Everything else — the reporter, the blond man, the retired athlete, Ricardo, the Dempsey Roll shortening his career, Miyata, the knot Ippo's father taught him — is a variation on that question in a different body. That is the thing the description has to carry.

My honest read on which to use:

**Draft 3 is the elite one.** It does what Vagabond's premise does — it does not tell you what happens, it tells you what the book is _about_, and the "about" is large enough that an intelligent reader feels they have found something rare. It opens on the thesis. It walks through every major character and gives them the same question in a different body. It names the OCs without selling them. It closes with a line that is a _mission statement_: the camera has been moved. That line alone tells a literate reader this is not a canon retelling — this is someone using the canon as a lens.

**Draft 1 is the one that matches your prose.** If you want the description to _sound_ like the chapters, use this. It's quieter. It will attract fewer readers but the ones it attracts will be yours.

**Draft 2 is the honest compromise.** It leans into fanfic-site conventions — hook, mystery, three OCs described in one-line mysteries — while the last two lines elevate it. It will get more clicks than Draft 3 and retain almost as many readers.

What I would actually do, if this were my fic: **use Draft 3 on AO3, Draft 2 on Webnovel.** AO3 readers reward the literary move and will stay for 90 chapters of canon-POV if the description promises them something real. Webnovel readers need the hook harder in the first lines. Two platforms, two descriptions, same story.

One more thing, because you asked what makes a premise elite: **the Christ-shape doesn't go in the description.** Girard doesn't go in the description as "Girard." The Christian argument doesn't announce itself. What goes in the description is the _shape_ of those ideas — men borrowing desire from each other, men watching each other to find out who they are, the question of what it costs to be freed from that. Vagabond never says the word "Zen" in its premise. Real never says "disability theology." The elite move is to put the _structure_ of your deepest concerns in the description and let the reader who has eyes find it. The reader who doesn't have eyes reads it as a good boxing story and stays anyway.
