
Light heel sound clicks on the side of the street. The sound waves hit Itagaki's body and pass through him into the gym. His heart drops to the ground, and as he tries to hold onto his breath with his raging chest, it gets locked in place by the darkness he is living inside of. His hands try to hold onto his chest but the moment his fingertips touch it he aches harder, and the waves passing into the gym make it all mundane — the same way it was for him in high school, searching for a goal to hunt and never reaching it.

The pain is sharp enough that his fingers almost give in and let the headband fall to the ground. The kind of wetness that wrinkles your fingertips, the kind where touching another fabric is uncomfortable in your soul. He tries to hold the band with his other hand and wipes the wet one across his pants, his jacket — but a wet spot blooms on the cloth, and that wetness puts him in the middle of a pool, a dark one, unable to swim to safety.

The wind smacks his wet parts, especially his head. Without the band the cold cuts straight through skin into brain. He almost lifts the band back into place, but it is still wet, and putting it on would make him sicker. He pulls his hand to the back of his head, reaching for the hood the jacket should have, only to find none — and his head answers with a sharp pain at the front and the sides.

With all of these problems shaping his reality, he searches outside for a way to forget the pain.

The wind that keeps creeping onto him. The city, dark and blind to his struggle.

But there is one thing that, even as an echo passing through him, calls to him. His hand clenches the headband and shreds of water drop on the ground. His sweat falls slowly, bit by bit, on the dry sidewalk, making no sound at all. The sweat is now all over his hand, and trying to clean it he swings his arm left and right, swiftly, to drop every bit of it onto the ground.

A word creeps out of his mouth, cutting straight through his tongue. The sharp residue spreads across the inside of his mouth, the cold weather gets in, and the pain sharpens until he tries to put his hand over his lips — and now the wet sweaty band is on his mouth, tasting like toilet water.

A weird noise comes again from his mouth as he spits. The bitterness, the unexplainable mix on his tongue, makes his head turn in distress. The warmth from the gym calls his name and he takes a step toward it — but he freezes. He stands in the freeze.

He turns to the mop that swept through the echo of the ghost clicks. His legs start to shake in every direction. His knees bend inward, a small squat, struggling to keep himself standing as the residue of the mop's splash hits his tingling body, leaving it feeling like a punch is on its way.

His eyes lock onto the ghost as it turns back. Smooth movement, not a bit of extra energy spent — like the world champion who will face Takamura. Itagaki flinches and glances at the gym, but he can't do anything about the punch already on its way to him, and he waits, muttering words he should not have said.

Itagaki speaks first. As the four words leave his mouth, each one leaves a scar on his gum by the doing of his tongue. The burning gets deeper with every word, pressing back into the seconds before, where the cold and the bitterness colored his taste buds — but this time he endures to the end.

His voice was steadier and calmer than his hand's grip — the grip that was tightly squeezing every drop of sweat from the band, the fuzziness on his palm making him squeeze harder, trying to take relief in the act, only for the look on the ghost's face to ruin it and bring back the sensation of a punch on its way.

Itagaki said _what did you think_ to this echo of the window. His abdominal muscles contracted hard, drawing the squared shape of them under the fabric. The cold air pushed downward into his lungs, against the contraction, and he held it the way a body holds itself before a punch.

The cramp passed through the entire wall of his abdomen and hit him in the neck — especially the throat, where the words had left a mark unseen before. He tried to fix it by muttering another word, but the pain was sharp and his legs would not let him. He stayed there looking at the ghost.

It was as if two powers had collided in front of each other — the silence and the world of the echo. As he closed his eyes he fell onto one side, and the second he blinked open he fell to the other, the same kind of falling he felt between the warmth of the gym and the cold dark outside, where the empty window was still chasing him.

Then the ghost's hands moved.

Both of them, slow, sliding into the pockets of the jacket. No weight in the motion. The fabric barely shifted. Itagaki's eyes followed the hands down and stayed on the pockets after the hands were gone, as if the cloth was still moving without them.

The mouth opened.

_Nothing in particular._

Three words. They landed on Itagaki's chest before his ears were done hearing them. Something hot started behind his sternum — small at first, the size of the wet spot on his jacket — and then it spread. His neck warmed. His ears warmed. The cold wind was still on his face but it was hitting somewhere else now, somewhere he could not feel, and the heat under his skin kept climbing without asking him.

His hand was still on the headband. He did not remember it being there.

He tried to put a thought together — any thought, the word _why_, the word _but_, the name — and nothing came. His head was too hot for thoughts. So he locked onto the ghost instead, the way he locked onto a target in the ring when his coach's voice went too far away to follow.

Miyata was already turning.

Itagaki looked at him from behind. The shoulders. The back of the neck. The hands still in the pockets. His own mouth opened, the cold rushed in over the burning gum, and he got halfway to a word before it died on his tongue. The mouth closed. Opened again. Closed.

The person walking away from him was heavy. Heavier than the wet band in his hand. Heavier than the gym door behind him. Heavier than the mop, the bench, the cold — heavier than anything Itagaki had had to carry tonight.

He stayed on the sidewalk with his mouth half-open, watching the heavy thing get smaller.
