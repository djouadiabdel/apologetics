# Chapter 30

The gym moved around him like a body healing around a wound — slowly, from the edges inward, the furthest people resuming first.

The heavy bag received a punch from someone who had stopped watching. A towel dropped from a hook near the showers. Voices returned, cautious at first, the murmur of men deciding it was safe to speak again after the scream had silenced them. The conversations picked up the way rain picks up after a pause — one voice, then two, then enough that the silence was covered and the gym could pretend it was a gym again.

Fujii moved first among the press. He crossed the floor with his notebook already open, his pen moving before he arrived anywhere, writing observations the way a man breathes — automatically, without deciding to. The young man beside him followed. The one with the notebook. The one whose mouth lifted only on one side. The one who had been furniture since he first appeared at the edge of Fujii's shadow, writing things that no one asked to read, watching things that no one asked him to watch.

Itagaki passed through them.

He moved fast — not running but not walking, the kind of movement a body makes when the brain has already left the building and the legs are catching up. His shoulder clipped Fujii's elbow. His eyes were pointed at the door. The gym parted around him without noticing it had parted, the way a crowd adjusts for a man carrying something fragile without looking at what he's carrying. He was through the door before the door had time to be an obstacle.

Fujii noticed. His pen stopped for half a second — the instinct of a reporter registering an exit — and then resumed. The young man beside him noticed too. His pen did not stop. It moved faster.

---

Aoki was on his feet. Kimura was beside him. They were heading toward Fujii with the synchronized aggression of men who had been waiting for permission to be angry and had decided to grant it to themselves. Their faces were set. Their shoulders were forward. They had words prepared — words about the spar being unfair, about Ippo not being warmed up, about Imai coming in with an advantage that had nothing to do with skill — and the words were on their way to Fujii's notebook when Takamura hit them both.

Not punched. Hit. Open palm, one on each head, simultaneous — the casual violence of a man who could end most people in the room and had chosen instead to use them as percussion instruments. Aoki hit the floor. Kimura hit the floor beside him. The sound was louder than it needed to be because Takamura wanted it to be louder — he wanted the room to hear the impact, wanted the heads to turn from Ippo's corner to his corner, wanted the gravity of the gym to shift from the man on the canvas to the man who was standing with his arms spread and his chin up and his voice filling the space the way a brass section fills a concert hall.

"The great Takamura!" His voice found every corner. "The man who will kill the world champion Eagle and take his throne! Write that down!" He pointed at the young reporter — not at Fujii, at the young one, the furniture — and his finger was a command. "You. Notebook. Write this: Takamura Mamoru will destroy David Eagle in his own country and bring the belt back to Japan and hang it on his wall next to the other belt which he also won by destroying someone!"

Aoki, from the floor, tried to speak. Takamura's hand found the back of his neck and squeezed — not enough to hurt, just enough to remind Aoki that the floor was where Takamura had put him and the floor was where he would stay until Takamura decided otherwise.

"Ask me anything!" Takamura spread his arms wider. "Any question! As long as you write it down! I am generous today!"

The young reporter's pen was still. His notebook was open to a blank page. His mouth — the one that lifted only on one side — was not lifted. He was looking at Takamura the way he looked at everything: with the quiet attention of someone who was reading the room's body language and finding the chapter everyone else had skipped.

He asked a question.

It was not about Takamura. It was not about Eagle. It was not about belts or thrones or destruction or any of the words Takamura had thrown into the air like confetti to draw every eye in the room to himself and away from the man sitting against the ropes with his wrapped hand pressed to his chest.

The question was about Ippo.

Not about the spar. Not about the knockdown. Not about whether Ippo was okay or when he would fight again or what this meant for his ranking. The young man's mouth opened and what came out was a sentence shaped differently from every other sentence that had been spoken in this gym since Ippo's body hit the canvas.

"When his guard dropped in the second exchange — his eyes moved first. Before the hands. Was anyone else watching where they went?"

Takamura held his chin. The gesture was theatrical — the pose of a man pretending to think about something he hadn't been thinking about at all. "That raises a good point," he said. He looked down at Aoki, who was still under his hand. "Don't you think, Aoki?"

Aoki agreed. Aoki would have agreed that the sky was green if Takamura's hand was on his neck. He tried to add something — his mouth opening, the words forming — but Takamura's grip tightened and the words became a wheeze.

"Hmm." Takamura's chin was still in his hand. "I think Ippo is indeed doing something clumsy, but—"

"No."

The voice came from across the ring.

---

Ippo was sitting where they had left him. His back against the ropes. His wrapped hand against his chest. His other hand — the one they'd managed to remove the glove from — flat on the canvas beside his thigh. His legs were straight in front of him. His jaw was swelling on the left side, the skin darkening into the first stage of a bruise that would take a week to finish arriving.

His eyes were on the young reporter.

The reporter had asked the question to the room — to Takamura, to Aoki, to anyone who would answer. But the question had crossed the ring like a stone across water and landed on Ippo's chest and Ippo's body had caught it before his mind could decide whether to let it pass.

_His eyes moved first. Before the hands._

Ippo's fingers — the ones on the flat hand, the unwrapped hand — pressed into the canvas. The pressure was small. A contraction. The kind of movement a body makes when it has been touched on a bruise it didn't know it had.

"Nothing is special." His voice was steady. His chest was empty. The words came from the place where words come from when the body has already decided what to say and the mind is just providing the grammar. "I just could have trained more."

The sentence sat in the air. Kamogawa, beside him, did not move. Aoki, on the floor, did not move. Takamura's hand was still on his chin. Kimura was watching from the floor with his weight on one elbow.

The young reporter looked at Ippo.

Ippo looked back.

The reporter's pen was still. His notebook was open. His face had the expression of a man who had thrown a stone into a pool and was watching the ripples tell him how deep the water was. He had asked a question about the body — about the eyes moving before the hands, about the guard dropping as a consequence rather than a cause — and the answer he received was about training volume. The answer was a wall. A clean, well-constructed wall with no cracks in it, the kind of wall a man builds when the thing behind it is something he cannot afford to let anyone see.

The reporter's mouth lifted on one side. Not a smile. Not agreement. Just the small movement of a man filing something away — not the answer, but the distance between the answer and the question.

He closed his notebook.

---

Ippo's hand — the wrapped one — tightened against his chest. His fingers curled inside the tape, the knuckles pressing outward against the fabric, the compression of the wraps answering the compression of his grip. His jaw hurt. The bruise was building. The emptiness in his chest was still there — wide, hollow, the shape of a thing that had left and taken something with it on the way out.

But his hands were wrapped. His hands were still wrapped.

He held onto that.

Across the gym, the young reporter slipped his notebook into his jacket pocket. He said something to Fujii — quiet, brief, the kind of sentence that disappears into the noise of a room. Fujii frowned at him. The young man's shoulders moved in what might have been a shrug.

He walked toward the door. The same door Itagaki had passed through minutes earlier. The same door Imai had passed through before that. The gym was losing people the way a body loses heat — slowly, from the extremities first, the edges going cold while the center still burned.

The young man paused at the threshold. He didn't look back. His hand was in his jacket pocket, resting on the closed notebook, and the notebook held a question that had no answer yet and a wall that had been built too quickly and the distance between the two, which was the only thing worth measuring.

He stepped through the door.

Behind him, the gym continued. Takamura was still talking. Aoki was still on the floor. Kamogawa was still beside Ippo, his old hands resting on his knees, his eyes on his fighter's wrapped fists.

And Ippo sat against the ropes with his hands against his chest, holding the last thing he had left, staring at the canvas where his outline was already drying into nothing.
