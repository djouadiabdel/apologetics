**Chapter 27 — Imai's POV**

**The question this chapter answers:** Why does winning feel like losing?

**The movement:** Satisfaction → confusion → hollowness. Imai arrived expecting a wall and hit air. By the chapter's close, his body knows something his mind won't say yet — that the thing he came here to measure himself against wasn't in the ring.

---

**Beat 1 — Press record five seconds earlier.** Imai is standing over Ippo. Not moving. The gym is loud around him — towels, voices, Aoki's yelling — but the sound hasn't reached him yet. His hands are still up. Not because he's guarding. Because his body hasn't received the signal that the fight is over. He's waiting for Ippo to get up. That's the beat: Imai standing in his stance, watching the canvas, expecting the next thing to happen. The next thing doesn't happen.

**Beat 2 — The hands come down.** Someone calls the spar. Coach, the ref, whoever's managing this — it doesn't matter. What matters is the moment Imai lowers his guard. The fists open. Fingers flex. And the feeling that should be there — the heat, the satisfaction, the proof — isn't. His body is looking for it. Hands opening and closing like they're trying to grip something that should have been deposited there by the act of winning. Nothing. The gloves feel heavier now than they did during the spar.

**Beat 3 — Anchor object: the hand wraps.** Imai goes to his corner (or the edge of the ring, wherever he ends up post-spar). He starts unwrapping his hands. This is the anchor. The wraps come off slowly — not because he's savoring anything, but because his hands are doing the thing they always do after a fight and the rest of him hasn't caught up. The unwrapping is mechanical, the same loop-pull-loop he's done a thousand times after sparring sessions and Inter-High bouts. But each layer that comes off reveals more of his bare hand, and the bare hand has no mark on it. No swelling. No redness from a hard exchange. Ippo didn't hit him hard enough to leave a trace. The wraps should be a trophy. They're a receipt for something he didn't buy.

**Beat 4 — The spot where Ippo fell.** While unwrapping, Imai's eyes go to the canvas. The spot. Maybe there's a mark — sweat, a smudge, the faint depression where a body landed. He doesn't stare. He glances, then looks away, then glances again. The way you look at a thing that bothers you but you can't name why. In his Inter-High fights, when he put someone down, the canvas had meaning — it was the end of a sentence he'd been building for three rounds. This canvas says nothing. It's just a spot where a man fell because the man wasn't fighting him.

**Beat 5 — The gym's reaction tells him.** Imai reads the room. Not consciously — he's not the type to scan for social cues. But even he can feel it: nobody is celebrating his win. Nobody is congratulating him. The gym people are moving toward Ippo, toward the ring, toward the problem. Imai is standing in the ring and the room is flowing around him like water around a rock that isn't in anyone's way. He won and the room doesn't care. Not because they disrespect him — because his victory isn't the story. He's a footnote in someone else's chapter.

**Beat 6 — One physical detail that betrays him.** He doesn't say anything about how he feels. No inner monologue about hollowness. Instead: one small thing his body does that it shouldn't. Maybe he rolls his shoulder — the post-fight check, the instinct to test whether anything got damaged — and nothing hurts. A fighter who just sparred a champion and feels nothing in his body afterwards. That absence is the loudest thing in the chapter. Or maybe he touches his jaw, checking for the impact that should have been there from Ippo's counter. Clean. He walked through a fight with the Japanese champion and came out unmarked. That should be pride. It isn't.

**Beat 7 — The door.** Imai ducks under the ropes. Steps out of the ring. The gym is still focused on Ippo behind him. He doesn't look back — or he looks back once, briefly, and what he sees is Kamogawa's back, the old man moving toward his fighter, and Imai's view of Ippo is blocked by the people who belong to Ippo. He walks toward his bag, or toward the door, or toward whatever comes next. The last image: Imai's bare hands, unmarked, wraps balled up and held loosely at his side. The seed is planted. Not a thought — a sensation. The feeling of having beaten something that was already beaten. The hunger that winning was supposed to kill is still alive, and it's worse now, because the meal was empty.
