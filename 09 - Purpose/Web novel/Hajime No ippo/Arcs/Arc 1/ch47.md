My eyes speak back to me. They are filled with a pressure I have felt multiple times across the years, a pressure that wants to be released and dropped, and it arrives now as I look at this picture of the Featherweight division champion with his face smashed to pieces.

And that is the champion? Can't even imagine the face of the other guy.

I wipe my eyes before someone sees them but it is too late. My hands are too wet for a few tears. This feels like how I was that night at the bookstore, the same kind that sneaks up on you when you think you are just reading.

In the mirror across the aisle my eyes are red. Not the red of a man who hasn't slept. A red that is millions of thin lines striking a white canvas and no amount of blinking can change that view. I am holding a magazine. I am standing in a bookstore. I am crying over a man I have never met whose face is broken open on glossy paper.

At least I want to know what happened in the fight. Maybe the writers can produce something that makes watching it unimportant.

Ryūhei Sawamura vs Ippo Makunouchi.

---

My chest fills with a dull leaden weight as I read the description of this fight and it is making me think of times where I used to be rough in defense, tackling people, rendering them facing the ground. The way a body drops when you hit it right. The way the grass stains on their jersey are the last thing you see before the whistle. I had forgotten that I knew what it felt like to put someone down. This magazine is reminding me.

The writer speaks of how Ippo Makunouchi defended his title and caused the other fighter to retire and spend most of his time in the hospital.

I drop the magazine to my side to see if the feeling in my forearms would stop. It does not. The sensation has nothing to do with holding anything. It has to do with reading.

But nothing changed at all. From my eyes being in tears to my skin acting strange, all that I was doing was reading this magazine and thinking of her.

---

My eyes widen at the description of how this fight ended. The Dempsey Roll. It looks like some sort of special movement only he can do, as per the author. From what I got from him, getting hit by it is like your ribs are suddenly made of glass. A coldness in the center of the chest that comes and goes as you are presented with his face in front of you, like you have swallowed a small piece of dry ice and your body is trying to deal with it. It does not hurt like a stab. It is a much more different feeling.

I am on the last page of a long article on this fight and more about Ippo Makunouchi.

My eyes were only focused on the magazine. There was nothing in the world that took my attention but the magazine. Not the people that walked by, not the eyes they gave me as I am a new face across the halls of this store. I just stood there trying to tunnel even harder to avoid all those needles of theirs, just like how I did with everything when me and her were not on the right page together.

It is already too late and I had not felt much of that time passing by.

I guess I get this fight and its circumstances, although I am not sure what is going on with this day. This black haired kid looks Japanese and a pro but still no picture of him at all. Every article talks about him like everyone already knows his face. I am the only one searching.

Maybe I will try to get the DVD of the Ippo guy fight to watch it.

---

Walking across the place with the DVD in my hand, I can not help it but want to turn around more in this store. The halls stretch and the shelves repeat and the feeling behind my eyes is not tears at all but a kind of pressure that tickles, as I look at the rows of books with the idea of her walking beside me as I search for her.

It was always our little game.

I move my head away from a couple coming toward me and my vision takes a millisecond to catch up. A floaty tilt in my inner ear as if the floor has gone slightly soft under my feet and I am now facing this scary fighter's poster.

I recognise him. It is the Takamura guy. He is fighting a blond haired European looking guy. Or was it American?

I see that Takamura is Japanese too and it is clear now unlike that weird picture they had of him before in the small magazine I picked up. His face is sharp and obvious and real on this poster in a way that the magazine never allowed.

But wait. I think I glimpsed something like him before. Or is it that everyone still looks the same to me after five years?

Nah nah. I am sure. I recognize people.

I feel shivers pass down through my spine and I blame the weather while I am still trying to confirm where I have seen that Takamura before. But I stop myself from thinking as the DVD in my hand calls upon me.

This Takamura guy? What is with him?

I will just try to watch this DVD and forget about all of this.
