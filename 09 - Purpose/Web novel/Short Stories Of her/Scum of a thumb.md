The thumb keeps going up. There's always more.

_A man leads. A man decides. A woman shouldn't have to carry the weight of choosing._

She said it like a wish, not a weapon. She wanted to be carried somewhere. She wanted a hand on the small of her back steering her through a door she didn't have to find herself.

And God help me, I wanted to be the one steering. Not for her. For me.

Because a man who decides is a man who is sure, and I was sure of nothing, and I thought if I could just hold the wheel hard enough my hands would stop shaking. I led so I wouldn't have to admit I was lost too. I picked the restaurant, the street, the plan, and called it taking care of her, when the truth is I couldn't bear for either of us to see me standing in the road not knowing the way.

So when she leaned her weight on the decision, the weight I'd begged to carry, it broke me — and I blamed her for being heavy. I blamed her for needing a leader, when I was the one who'd auditioned for the part so no one would notice I was a boy in a man's coat, lost in the same dark she was, pretending the coat was a map.

That's the dross off this one. Not her wanting to be led. My need to lead so I'd never have to say _I don't know where we're going either. Hold my hand and let's both be lost._

The role told me a man is never lost. So I was lost alone, beside her, holding a wheel that wasn't connected to anything.
