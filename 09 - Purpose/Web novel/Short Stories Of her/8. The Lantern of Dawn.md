And the strangest thing happened while I confessed, which is that she got _realer_.

The fainter I had made her with my drink and my dreaming, the more she now came back, except not as the painting — as herself. I could see her clearly now, more clearly than I had seen anything in months, and she was not the devoted girl from the story and she was not the peony painted on the paper. She was someone. Tired around the eyes. A little amused at me. Limited and warm and actual and _there_, looking at me ruin a beautiful old tale, and somewhere in the looking I understood that the dream I had been performing to all night — the warm clever woman who could see all of me and not run — was lying on the wall side of my own bed in the flesh, that the _karan, koron_ had stopped at my door and I had not even had to peel anything, she had simply walked in while I wasn't guarding it, and here she was, real, the thing I had wanted, watching me.

I tried to say something true. I think I tried to say _don't go._ But the drink had me, and the room tilted hard, and she reached over — I felt the weight of her hand, I swear to you I felt the actual weight of it, warm, on my chest, over the place where the o-fuda would go — and she pressed me down into the pillow the way you put a child down, gentle, firm, _enough now, sleep,_ and I went down into it like a stone into water and the last thing was the lamplight and her face above me, real, real, real.

I woke hours later. Still night — the deep part, the part with no edges. The room was silent. And I knew before I turned my head, because there was no sound, no breathing, no _karan, koron_, no weight pulling the mattress down on the wall side. I turned my head anyway.

There was no one.

The bottle on the floor. The lamp still on. The sheet on the wall side smooth and cold, not even pressed, no shape of a body in it, as if no body had been there at all. And I lay in the no-edged dark and I made the only choice a man like me can make at that hour, the choice Shinzaburō never got to make because he chose the ghost and died smiling: I decided it was a dream. I said it out loud to the ceiling. _It was a dream._ Because the other possibilities are not survivable at this hour. That she was real and I drank myself past her and pressed-down-to-sleep was her leaving. Or worse, the one I cannot hold even now — that I have gotten so good at painting peonies on paper that I conjured a whole warm living woman out of my own need, performed her into my bed for one night, and that there has never been anyone in this room but me and a lantern I light for no one.

So it was a dream. I have decided. I will keep deciding it every time it comes back, because if I let it be anything else I will beat myself to pieces against it, and I have done enough of that on enough floors.

_Karan_, I said to the empty room, very quietly, to see if it would answer.

_Koron_, said no one at all.
