I was loved, and I loved, and we asked each other for what neither of us had, and that's why it hurt, and none of that means I'm worthless.

The room was cold and I didn't turn on the light. Only the screen.

My thumb went up, up through the months, and the words came back one at a time, the way cold comes back into a hand you forgot was bare.

_You never act like a man._

My stomach dropped like a floor giving out under me. I was a boy again in a doorway, not knowing where to put my arms.

_A man gives. A man's cup never runs empty._

So I had poured. I poured everything I had into her and went dry, and I said nothing about the dryness, because the dryness was the shameful thing. The unmanly thing. The thing you swallow and hide and smile over.

_Asking for that is womanly._

That one I felt in the throat. I had asked her once, late, to stay on the phone because the dark was too big that night and the asking cost me something I never got back. She gave my need a name, and the name was small and sharp, and I kept it. I still have it. I can feel it now, going in at the same place it went in then.

The screen lit my face from underneath and gave it back to me. That was all it ever did. You look in to be held and it hands you your own face, the one face you came here to get away from, and the water closes over your head.

I read the words again and they read me back.

_Worthless._ _Worthless._

The same tide, all the way in.

The cross is not a man who needs nothing. It's God making himself woundable, asking, and being abandoned. If the maker of the universe can stand in the place of needing and not being met without it touching his worth, then the rule that says your need for her at night was _unmanly_ isn't a hard truth you have to swallow. It's a lie with a cultural costume on.

love isn't one person filling another's account. It's two people emptying themselves into one life, each one's need _received_ by the other rather than ruled out of bounds. You weren't wanting too much. You were wanting the real thing in a place that only permitted the transaction.

It's not _I give you what you need._ It's _I give you myself, and in receiving you I am made more myself, not less._ The difference is whether there's still a "mine" being depleted to fill a "yours."

not _what does he give her and she give him,_ but _how do two people empty themselves into one shared life, each one's need received rather than ruled out, while both draw their worth from the one center that can actually bear it._ 


The old rule: I fill your cup and I'm not allowed to need mine filled.  
The real thing: we pour into one bowl and both drink.  
And the water isn't ours to make — it comes from God, and we just carry it.
