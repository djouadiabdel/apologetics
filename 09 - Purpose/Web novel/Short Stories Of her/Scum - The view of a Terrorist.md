The thumb keeps going up. There's always more.

_You never plan anything. You never take me anywhere._

Other men's tables. Other men's flowers, held up to a camera, sent around. A calendar she wanted full and I left empty, because I thought the love lived somewhere you couldn't book a table for.

I was wrong about her. I wasn't wrong about that.

_A real man buys. A real man leads. A real man's woman wants for nothing._

I had my hand on the back of her neck the night she shook and couldn't stop. I stayed awake when she slept. I got up before the sun to become someone she could lean her whole weight on and not feel him buckle. None of it had a receipt. None of it could be lifted into the light and counted.

So it counted as nothing.

I never wanted to be a man who buys. I wanted to be welded to her. One thing. No seam. Nothing to keep a ledger of.

I said it to her once, badly, the only way I knew — that a marriage didn't have to be a shop or a bloodline, that somewhere out there was a child who belonged to no one, and we could be the place a child like that came home to.

She looked at me like I'd wandered off the map. Like the map was the gifts and the table and the name carried forward, and I'd walked out past the edge of it into something that wasn't even on her list.

And here's the thing the fire teaches you, that I learned too late and in the cold:

what floats to the top of the melt is the skim. The light worthless film. The dross.

The dates. The flowers. The performance.

That was the gold she'd been looking for the whole time.

And the part of me that sank and held the part that stayed, the part that doesn't burn she called that the failure.

She wanted the scum.

And the metal, she named nothing.
