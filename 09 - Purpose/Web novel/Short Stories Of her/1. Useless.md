The first time I kissed her I waited. I held her face and I waited, because I did not want to be the kind of man who takes. Her breath came small and fast against my mouth, and when she finally moved the last half-inch herself I felt something in my chest unlock that I had kept locked since I was a boy. Her lips were soft and a little chapped at the corner. She tasted like the orange she'd eaten on the way. I remember thinking: _now I am real._ Not _she is mine._ I am real. Her looking at me had made a person where before there had only been a boy waiting in a room to be told he was worth something.

I taught her things. That was how I loved  I gave. I wrote her poems at two in the morning and read them to her over the phone while she breathed on the other end. I told her which books to read and she read them. I told her things about herself she did not know and she believed me, because I said them with the certainty of a man who has never once doubted he is the center of the world he is standing in. When she got something right she would look at me to see if I had seen it. I had always seen it. I was her sky. I did not know yet that a sky is also a ceiling.

She made herself small for me. Not broken-small soft-small, the way a hand opens. When I was angry she would put down everything that made her a separate person and stand in front of me with nothing left but the wanting to be told she was good. All she ever wanted was that. To be appreciated. And I, who knew exactly what she wanted because she had handed it to me like a key, used it the way you use a key. When I wanted to hurt her I had only to not say it.

One night I called her useless. I do not remember what she had done. I remember the word leaving my mouth and her face coming apart, slowly, the way ice comes apart, from the inside first. I remember feeling powerful and sick in the same breath. I did not know then that I called her useless because it was the word I was most afraid of for myself. I had ranked nowhere. I had been first at nothing. I had a brother who arrived and a desk that gave me headaches and a long suspicion that there was nothing good in me at all — and here was a girl I could hand the word to, so I would not have to hold it. She loved a character from a show, a girl named Emilia, gentle and far away. I used to look at her and see Sylphiette — the one who waits, the one who is devoted, the one who would never leave. I had cast her in my own anime. I had not asked who she wanted to be.

---

We broke for three months over a fight I could have ended with one sentence and chose not to, because ending it would have meant I was not the master of it. In the third month she went to another man. I did not know his face. I did not need his face. The not-knowing was worse — he could be anyone, he could be everyone, he could be every man who was more real than me. She had taken the mouth I thought of as mine and given it to him, and the moment I learned it the world did not break. The world _thinned._ Everything went one layer fainter, like a photograph of itself. I was not jealous of him having her. I was jealous of him _being_ something — being the thing she chose, the thing that made her cross a city, while I sat in my room being nobody.

The message came at night. She sent it on Instagram and deleted it. Sent it again and deleted it again. Then it came on WhatsApp, where you cannot take things back as cleanly. I was playing a game when it arrived, the screen blue on my face, my champion dying somewhere I had stopped caring about. I read it. I felt the floor of myself give way slowly, plank by plank, the way you feel a thing you cannot stop. There was a window in me that night that opened onto a long drop, and I sat near it longer than I will write here. I want to be honest that I sat near it. And then I did the most mimetic thing a wounded animal can do — I lied. I told her I had been with many girls. I had not touched one. I had turned perfect girls away in those three months, would not even sit near them, because being near them felt like admitting she could be replaced, and if she could be replaced then the thing I had built her into could be replaced, and then what was I. So I lied that I was a man the world wanted. And she answered with the truth about the man who had wanted her. And I went down further than I knew there was down to go.

Then the disease turned, the way it does. The wanting-her became wanting-to-win. I stopped thinking about her at all. I thought only about the drop, about the thinning, about how to push it back onto her. I texted her to meet me tomorrow. _Let's be together again,_ I said, gentle, the old sky-voice. I did not mean a word of it. I had a plan, and the plan was ugly, and I will tell it because you have to write the man at his worst or you have written nothing. I was going to make her kneel. I was going to use her mouth the way the other man had, and then I was going to stand up and tell her I would never be next to a slut like her, and I was going to walk out and leave her on the floor with the thinning inside her instead of inside me. That was the trade I had drawn up in the dark. Her humiliation for my realness. Her ontological death for mine.

I am not evil. I found that out the next day.

She came. She sat down across from me and looked up, and her eyes did the one thing my plan had not accounted for. They were not the eyes of a slut or a rival or an idol or any of the things I had built. They were just her eyes. Tired. Afraid of me. Still, somewhere under the fear, hoping I was the man who had waited that first time instead of the man who had come to make her kneel. And my body collapsed before my mind could give it the order to stay cruel. Something in my chest that I had been holding clenched gave out all at once, like a fist that has been closed so long it forgets it can open. The plan died in my throat. I did not make her kneel. I think I kissed her. I think I held her face the way I had the first time, and her breath came small and fast, and I hated myself and I could not stop being gentle. The body knew something the plan refused. The body would not commit the death.

I carried it for months after that. The pain did not leave because we kissed. It got worse, because now I could not even hate her cleanly — I had felt the thing under the hatred and I could not unfeel it. She did not know what to do with me. She tried. There was nothing she could have done, because the thing breaking in me was not about her, and she could not heal a wound she had not made. No woman could have. I had been trying to pull a thing out of her eyes that was never in any human's eyes to give — the small green word _worth,_ the answer to _am I real,_ the thing I had needed her gaze to grant me since the first kiss. I had made her God and then I had tried to kill God for not saving me, and a person cannot survive being made into God.

---

I see her now sometimes. Once it was in a pharmacy, fluorescent and ordinary, both of us holding small boxes of nothing. She looked at me and I waited for the drop, for the thinning, for the old machinery to turn over. It did not turn. I felt nothing. And the nothing was not victory. The man I used to be would have called it victory, would have said _look, I have won, I feel nothing, she is nothing._ That feigned coldness was just the old game in a colder coat. This was not that. This was the simple emptiness of a room after a fever breaks. The idol was not defeated. The idol was just gone, and the floor of me had grown back quietly while I was not looking, plank by plank, from somewhere I had not built and did not control.

I turned and paid for my box of nothing and walked out into the cold I love, the kind that makes the skin shiver under thick clothes, and I did not look back, not because I was strong but because there was finally no thread pulling my head around. She had been the reason I started writing. I had wanted, at first, only to put it on a page so that someone would see it and tell me there was something good in me after all — the same green word, the same hand held out for it. That is still in me. I do not pretend it is gone. But I know now what I was reaching for and where it does not live. It does not live in her eyes, or yours, or in the page.

It was never useless, what we were. It was just two people asking each other for the one thing neither of us had to give. I forgive her for leaving. I forgive myself for the plan I made in the dark and did not carry out. The body would not let me. I think the body knew before I did who had already paid for the thing I kept trying to extract by force.
