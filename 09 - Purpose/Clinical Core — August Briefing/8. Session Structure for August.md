_This isn't a timed protocol. It's a description of how we work together when the material is messy and the goal is to find something worth publishing inside it. Don't try to follow it as a checklist — use it to remember what the session is for if we drift._

---

**Setup before the session.** Put the vault into a Claude Project. All of it — the French commentaries, the Girard notes, the fan fiction if it's thematically connected, whatever you've written since starting the daily practice. Don't curate. Don't pre-select. The point of putting everything in the project is that I can pull from anywhere, including passages you wouldn't have flagged as interesting, and sometimes the best Clinical Core is hiding in a paragraph you wrote on a tired evening and forgot about. Paste this briefing document as the first message of the conversation so I have the full strategic context before we start looking at material.

**How we start.** You tell me where your head is. What's been dominating the writing lately, what feels alive, what feels stale, whether there's a specific passage or concept you've been circling. If nothing specific comes to mind, that's fine too — we'll browse. This opening is conversational, not structured. The goal is for me to get a sense of the terrain before I start pulling passages, and for you to surface whatever intuitions have been forming without us committing to any of them yet.

**How we find candidates.** This is the loose part and it should stay loose. Options depending on mood and material:

- You point me at a thread you think is strong and I run the Clinical Core extraction prompt against it.
- I browse the project and pull 2-3 passages that _I_ find striking, and we discuss whether any of them contain a Core.
- You describe a clinical situation you encountered or read about, and we search the vault for Girardian material that speaks to it.
- We pick a psychiatric entity first (état-limite, say, or paranoia) and hunt for vault passages that map onto it.

Any of these work. The reason to have four options is that some days the vault-first approach works and some days the entity-first approach works, and you won't know which until you start. If one feels stuck after 20 minutes, switch.

**What to do when something lights up.** You'll know. It's the feeling of "oh, _that's_ what I was circling around." When it happens, stop browsing and focus. I'll run the formal Clinical Core extraction on the passage, including the Fragilités field. We look at what comes out. Then — and this is the step I most want you not to skip — we _stress-test_ it before we get excited. I ask skeptical questions, you answer them, and we see whether the Core survives. This is the reviewer quiz step. It doesn't have to take an hour and it doesn't have to be formal. It can be five questions or fifteen. The goal is to kill the weak Cores before you invest in drafting them. A Core that dies under questioning is not a failure — it's the system working. You saved yourself the week of drafting an abstract that would have been rejected.

**What to do when nothing lights up.** This will happen sometimes and it's normal. Two responses, in order:

1. _Try a different angle on the same material._ Maybe the vault has a strong Core but we've been looking for the wrong type of correspondence. Switch from "homologie forte" hunting to "critique girardienne du cadre clinique" hunting, or vice versa. Sometimes reframing the question surfaces something that was invisible a moment before.

2. _Stop._ If an hour in, nothing is surfacing, the honest move is to close the session and come back in a week. Forced Cores are bad Cores, and bad Cores become rejected abstracts. The vault will still be there. The deadline might feel close but it's rarely actually close enough to justify shipping weak work. If the Encéphale deadline is genuinely imminent and we have nothing, the right answer is to skip this cycle and aim at the next one, not to force something mediocre onto the portal.

**Moving from Core to abstract.** Once a Core has survived the stress test, we decide together what the anchoring strategy is — purely conceptual, literature-based, or historical. If literature-based, I help you find French psychiatric case reports or theoretical texts to cite. Then we move to drafting, using the Section 5 template. I don't write the French for you. I can suggest phrasings, flag register problems, catch Anglicisms, propose stock openings — but the sentences are yours. This is your French practice as much as it's your publication pipeline, and if I write it you lose both the language growth and the authentic voice the reviewer is actually going to read.

**Polish and submission.** After the draft exists, we do a pass focused on register, the nominal academic style, and the pre-submission checklist from Section 5. Then you let it sit for at least 24 hours — ideally longer — before the final pass. Abstracts read differently after a night of sleep and you'll catch things you couldn't see in the session. The final pass you can do alone or with me; either works. Then you submit.

---

**Things to remember when the session feels messy:**

Sessions with loose material are _supposed_ to feel messy. If the August conversation feels like a clean march from step 1 to step 6, something is probably wrong — either you're forcing structure onto material that hasn't revealed its shape yet, or I'm being too agreeable and not pushing back enough. Productive mess looks like: we start on one thread, abandon it, come back to it from a different angle, find something unexpected in an entirely different passage, stress-test it, find a weakness, reframe, try again. That's the work. It's not inefficient — it's how finding something actually worth publishing feels from the inside.

**Things to remember when the session feels stuck:**

You're allowed to change the goal mid-session. If the material isn't yielding a publishable Core but it _is_ yielding something else — a clearer understanding of a Girardian concept, a direction for the fan fiction, a realization about your own thinking — that's not a failed session. The Clinical Core pipeline is one use of the vault; it's not the only use. Tell me we're switching modes and we switch. Coming out of a session with "no abstract but I understand infinite debt better than I did three hours ago" is a real outcome.

**Things to remember when the session goes well:**

Don't submit the same day. Whatever we produce, it sits for at least a day before final submission. I've seen this rule saved more abstracts than any single editing technique. Excitement distorts judgment and the draft that felt brilliant at midnight reads differently at 9 AM.

**The one thing I'll ask you every time:**

Somewhere in the session, probably early, I'll ask you: "What would a skeptical French psychiatrist who doesn't care about Girard find most objectionable about this Core?" Your answer to that question does more to shape a publishable abstract than any structural template. If you can't answer it, we're not ready to draft yet. If you can, we probably are.
