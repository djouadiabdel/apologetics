# Books to Own Physically

Cross-references [[Plan Till EVC PSYCHIATRY]], [[Chemin de 365 — Comprendre la foi en profondeur]], [[Bibliothèque JesusMarie — par thème]], and [[Letters of the New Testament - Commentaries and Connections]].

## The rule this list follows

Almost everything you have queued is free. All of JesusMarie is free PDF. Every early Father is free at ccel.org and newadvent.org. The CNUP référentiel is a free PDF. So buying a physical copy is never about access, it is about one of three things:

1. You will reread it for the rest of your life, not once.
2. You need to annotate it deeply and hold it, and a screen ruins that.
3. It is slow, meditative reading that a screen actively fights.

Everything else stays a free PDF that you mark up in Rnote.

There is a second reason to keep something as a PDF rather than paper, and it is specific to you. Your video workflow is annotate-the-PDF-in-Rnote and show it on screen. So for anything that is going to become video fodder, the PDF is the better tool, not the paper. Paper is for the formation books you read for yourself and return to for years, the ones that are not about making a video next week.

So the split is:

- **Physical** = lifelong formation, deep rereading, slow prayerful texts.
- **PDF plus Rnote** = reference works, video material, anything you look things up in.

## Which language for each

Two forces decide this, and they point opposite ways.

**Default to French for the soul.** Faith formation, prayer, the Fathers, the desert, the East. French is your mother tongue for God, you pray in it, and France has the richest Catholic and patristic publishing in the world (Sources Chrétiennes, Bellefontaine, Solesmes, Points Sagesses). You do not read Greek or Latin, so the "original" is a translation either way, and the French one will sit closer to your heart.

**Switch to English where English is the real text.** Three cases. First, the psychiatry books, because descriptive psychopathology and philosophy of psychiatry are Anglophone fields and the key books were never translated well or at all (Sims, Owen, Sass). Second, Bede on the letters, because the only accessible complete translation is English. Third, anything you will quote on your English channel, which for now is your NIV.

**One caution about paying twice.** All the French Fathers are already free on JesusMarie as PDF. So when you pay for paper in French, pay for an edition the free site does not match, a Sources Chrétiennes or a Bellefontaine with real apparatus, not a reprint of what you can download. When you pay for English, you are buying something the free French library does not give you at all, so it is never a duplicate.

Per book:

- **Bible.** Both. **NIV in English** for the channel and the James videos. A **Bible de Jérusalem in French** as your Catholic formation and Fathers Bible, because it carries the deuterocanon they quote and its notes key to French Catholic study.
- **Catechism.** **French**, the _Catéchisme de l'Église Catholique_. Your whole Chemin de 365 is French and you cross-reference by paragraph.
- **Augustine, Confessions.** **French.** This is the most personal reread you own, so read it in your tongue. The Folio classique (the classical d'Andilly French) is beautiful, or Frédéric Boyer's modern _Les Aveux_ if you want it to feel alive and present. Keep an English Chadwick only if you later want it for precise study.
- **Athanasius, On the Incarnation.** **English**, the St Vladimir's Popular Patristics edition, because it is cheap and carries the C.S. Lewis introduction that the French editions do not. The French _Sur l'Incarnation du Verbe_ (Sources Chrétiennes) is finer but pricier and you can read it free on JesusMarie.
- **Basil, On the Holy Spirit.** Read it first in the cheap **English** Popular Patristics so it is in your hands now. If it grips you, and on your central question it will, the keepsake is the **French** Sources Chrétiennes bilingual _Sur le Saint-Esprit_. The plain French text is already free on JesusMarie, so only pay for the SC apparatus, not the words alone.
- **Sayings of the Desert Fathers.** **French.** This is bedside dipping in your mother tongue. Dom Lucien Regnault's collections from Solesmes (_Les Sentences des Pères du désert_) are the warm, accessible way in. Buy Ward in English only if you end up wanting the standard scholarly alphabetical collection too.
- **Athanasius, Life of Antony.** **French**, _Vie d'Antoine_ (Sources Chrétiennes). Formation reading, and it pairs with the French desert sayings.
- **Palladius, Lausiac History.** **French**, _Histoire lausiaque_. Same reason, it belongs on the desert shelf with the two above.
- **Philokalia.** Start **French** with the _Petite Philocalie de la prière du cœur_ (Points Sagesses), the classic cheap francophone gateway. Only if you go deep for years do you buy the full **English** Palmer/Sherrard/Ware set from Faber, which is the great complete edition but a real commitment.
- **The Way of a Pilgrim.** **French**, _Récits d'un pèlerin russe_ (Points Sagesses). It is a French devotional classic in its own right, read it in French.
- **Imitation of Christ.** **French**, _L'Imitation de Jésus-Christ_. It was a French devotional staple for centuries and reads best there.
- **John Climacus, The Ladder.** **French**, _L'Échelle sainte_ (Bellefontaine, Spiritualité orientale series), the standard French edition.
- **Bede, Seven Catholic Epistles.** **English**, Cistercian Studies. There is no accessible French edition, and this is the practical reason it is English, not preference.
- **Sims' Symptoms in the Mind.** **English.** There is no French equivalent, it is a distinctly Anglophone classic. See the bridge note below.
- **Owen, Psychiatry and Human Nature.** **English** only, never translated.
- **Sass, Madness and Modernism.** **English** only.
- **CNUP Référentiel de Psychiatrie et Addictologie.** **French**, obviously, it is the French exam book. Do not buy yet.

**The EVC bilingual bridge, do not skip this.** You will learn the psychiatric concepts in English from Sims and Owen, but the EVC is written and marked in French. So you must convert every concept into its French clinical label, and that conversion lives in the CNUP référentiel. Keep a running French-English glossary as you read Sims (thought insertion / _pensée imposée_, thought block / _barrage_, flight of ideas / _fuite des idées_, and so on). The English gives you the understanding, the French gives you the exam word. Owning both languages here is not a luxury, it is the whole method.

## Tier 1 — buy these, it is genuinely bad not to own them

### Faith and the Fathers

- **A Bible you can write in.** Non-negotiable, one physical copy you mark up for life. You quote NIV, keep that for the James videos. But since you are working the Fathers and the Catholic arc of the Chemin, the natural physical study Bible is an **RSV-2CE (the Ignatius Bible)**, because it has the deuterocanonical books the Fathers quote and it is the translation most Catholic and patristic study material keys to. Owning both is fine, they do different jobs.
- **Catechism of the Catholic Church.** This is the backbone of the entire [[Chemin de 365 — Comprendre la foi en profondeur]]. You look things up in it constantly and cross-reference by paragraph number. Cheap. Bad not to have it on the desk.
- **Augustine, _Confessions_.** The center of gravity of the whole interior-life arc (Arc IX), and it appears in JesusMarie too. You will reread this every few years for life and annotate it to death. Get a real translation, not a free public-domain one: **Henry Chadwick (Oxford)** for precision, or **Maria Boulding (New City Press)** for warmth. This is the single most "bad not to own" book on the faith side.
- **Athanasius, _On the Incarnation_.** Short, foundational, the great text on why God became man (your Arc III). The **St Vladimir's Popular Patristics** edition is cheap and carries the famous C.S. Lewis introduction on why to read old books at all. Own it.
- **Basil of Caesarea, _On the Holy Spirit_.** This is your stated priority, the top line of your JesusMarie list and the heart of Arc IV. It is short. The Popular Patristics edition is a few euros. Given that the Holy Spirit is your actual central question, it would be strange not to have this one in your hand.

### Stories of the earliest fathers (the thing you asked for by name)

These are the desert and the first monks, the orthodox earliest fathers as living people, not as doctrine. They are short, episodic, made for dipping into daily, and they are far better on paper by the bed than on a screen.

- **_The Sayings of the Desert Fathers_ (the Alphabetical Collection), tr. Benedicta Ward.** This is the one. The _Apophthegmata_, the actual sayings and small stories of Antony, Macarius, Poemen, Arsenius, Sisoes and the rest. Cistercian Publications. If you buy one book of desert stories, this is it.
- **Athanasius, _The Life of Antony_.** The founding story of Christian monasticism, the earliest of them all, written by the same Athanasius as _On the Incarnation_. Popular Patristics or Classics of Western Spirituality. Own it right after the Sayings.
- **Palladius, _The Lausiac History_.** The traveler's eyewitness collection of the desert monks and mothers, more narrative than the Sayings. The natural third book once the first two have hooked you.

### The Christian East (your "deux poumons" arc)

- **_The Philokalia_, vol. 1, tr. Palmer, Sherrard and Ware (Faber).** The treasury of Eastern prayer named in Arc XII. This is exactly the slow, prayerful reading a screen fights. It runs to several volumes and you will spend years in it, so start with volume 1 in paper. Do not try to read this as a PDF, it will not happen.
- **_The Way of a Pilgrim_ (Récits d'un pèlerin russe).** Short, cheap, and the living companion to the Philokalia and the Jesus Prayer. Own it.

### Psychiatry (the EVC spine, the two worth buying now)

- **Femi Oyebode, _Sims' Symptoms in the Mind_, 7th ed.** Your Spine 2, descriptive psychopathology. You annotate this constantly across the whole plan and you will use it now, not in 2028. Physical, marked up. Bad not to own.
- **Gareth Owen, _Psychiatry and Human Nature_.** The book that holds your whole approach together, the bridge between the clinical and the philosophical. It threads through almost every Part of the plan. Physical is worth it.

## Tier 2 — worth owning, second wave, no rush

- **Bede, _Commentary on the Seven Catholic Epistles_** (Cistercian Studies). Not free online, and you will read it verse by verse as you go through James and the rest. Buy it when you start working the letters seriously.
- **John Climacus, _The Ladder of Divine Ascent_.** The great ascetical classic of the East (Arc IX), a lifelong reread. Popular Patristics.
- **_The Imitation of Christ_.** A lifelong bedside reread (Arc VI and IX). Cheap. Paper suits it even though it is also free.
- **Louis Sass, _Madness and Modernism_.** Central to the phenomenology of psychosis, but this is more of a read-deeply-once book than a daily reference, so paper is a preference, not a must. Buy it when you reach the psychosis stage.
- **Irenaeus, _On the Apostolic Preaching_** (the short one, Popular Patristics), if you want Irenaeus in hand. The full _Against Heresies_ stays a PDF, it is long and referential.

## Tier 3 — do NOT buy, keep them free

These are reference works or one-pass reads. Buying them wastes money and shelf space, and for several of them the PDF is the better tool because you will search them or draw on them in Rnote.

- **The whole _Dictionnaire de Théologie Catholique_ (DTC).** A massive multivolume reference. You look things up in it by letter. Never buy, always look up free.
- **Aquinas, _Somme Théologique_ (the full Summa).** Free, enormous, you dip by question. If you want Aquinas in paper, buy a one-volume selection like Peter Kreeft's _A Shorter Summa_ instead of the set.
- **Chrysostom's homilies on Paul.** Free and complete in the NPNF at ccel. Huge. Keep as PDF, this is exactly the material you will annotate in Rnote for videos on the letters.
- **The CNUP _Référentiel de Psychiatrie et Addictologie_ (2024).** It is a free PDF and it will likely be updated before your sitting, so do not buy it now. When you hit the roughly eighteen-months-out clinical flip in the plan, buy the current edition then to annotate for the exam, not before.
- **All the JesusMarie apologetics pages** (Islam, Protestant, Orthodox polemics). Situational, free, keep as PDF and pull what you need.
- **The _Golden Legend_ / _Légende dorée_.** Lovely but big and dip-in. The desert fathers books above serve your "earliest fathers" wish far better. Read the Legend free.

## If you only buy five things this year

In order:

1. Augustine, _Confessions_ (Chadwick or Boulding)
2. _The Sayings of the Desert Fathers_ (Ward)
3. Basil, _On the Holy Spirit_ (Popular Patristics)
4. Oyebode, _Sims' Symptoms in the Mind_
5. The Catechism

Two of the five are cheap and small (Basil, the Catechism), two are your two lifelong faith books (Augustine, the desert), and one is the psychiatry spine you will use from now until the exam. That is the whole shape of your project on one short shelf.
