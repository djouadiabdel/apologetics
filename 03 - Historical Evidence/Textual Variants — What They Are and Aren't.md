## The claim to stop making

You will hear that the Great Isaiah Scroll is word for word identical with the Masoretic text. It is not.

Kipp Davis has worked on the scroll itself, inside the vault at the Shrine of the Book. The published editors counted around **2,600 textual variants in 1QIsaᵃ** against the Masoretic text.

Anyone can look this up. Drop the claim. It is the kind of thing that gets you disqualified in one sentence by someone who has read a single article.

## What a variant actually is

Most of them are nothing.

Spelling. Hebrew has a fuller and a shorter way to write the same word and scribes moved between them. Word order. A synonym in place of a synonym. A conjunction added or dropped.

Some are real. A word missing. A phrase that reads differently. A line that is there in one copy and not in another.

None of the 2,600 turn Isaiah into a different book.

## The claim that survives, and it is better

A thousand years separates 1QIsaᵃ from the medieval codices our Bibles are translated from. The Leningrad and Aleppo codices are tenth century. The scroll is late second century BC.

Across that gap the text comes through as recognisably the same book, with visible scribal work on the surface.

That is defensible and it is more interesting than the false version. A frozen text proves nothing except that somebody was careful with a photocopier. A text that came through a thousand years of hand copying, with the seams showing, and is still the same book, tells you something about the community carrying it.

## There was no single text yet

This is the part apologists skip and it matters more than the variant count.

At Qumran the same community held several different versions of the same books at the same time. Not one text with errors. Different editions in circulation together.

**Jeremiah is the clearest case.** The Hebrew of the Masoretic text runs about one seventh longer than the Greek of the Septuagint, and whole sections sit in a different order. Scholars had already argued the Greek was translated from an older, shorter Hebrew. Then six copies of Jeremiah turned up at Qumran. Two look like the Masoretic type. Two look like the Hebrew behind the Greek.

Both were being used, by the same people, at the same time.

The Samaritan Pentateuch is a third type and still in use today by the surviving Samaritan community on Mount Gerizim.

So the standardisation that produced "the" Hebrew text happened later than most people assume. Before that there was a spread.

## What this costs and what it does not

**It costs** the picture of a single unchanging text handed down untouched. Hold that picture and the evidence is against you.

**It does not cost** the messianic argument, and this is worth being clear about. The dating is what the prophecy case needs, and the scrolls settle the dating harder than anything else could. Isaiah exists complete, in Hebrew, around 125 BC. Whatever Isaiah 53 is, nobody wrote it afterwards.

**It helps** one specific argument. The Septuagint reads *parthenos*, virgin, at Isaiah 7:14 where the Hebrew has *almah*. The usual reply is that Jewish translators made that call one to two centuries before there were Christians. The pluriformity finding strengthens it further, because those translators were sometimes working from Hebrew that genuinely differed from what survived into the Masoretic text. They were not being loose. They may have been reading a different copy.
