1 Now I would remind you, brothers, of the gospel I preached to you, which you received, in which you stand, 2 and by which you are being saved, if you hold fast to the word I preached to you—unless you believed in vain. 3 For I delivered to you as of first importance what I also received: that Christ died for our sins in accordance with the Scriptures, 4 that he was buried, that he was raised on the third day in accordance with the Scriptures, 5 and that he appeared to Cephas, then to the twelve. 6 Then he appeared to more than five hundred brothers at one time, most of whom are still alive, though some have fallen asleep. 7 Then he appeared to James, then to all the apostles. 8 Last of all, as to one untimely born, he appeared also to me. 9 For I am the least of the apostles, unworthy to be called an apostle, because I persecuted the church of God. 10 But by the grace of God I am what I am, and his grace toward me was not in vain. On the contrary, I worked harder than any of them, though it was not I, but the grace of God that is with me. 11 Whether then it was I or they, so we preach and so you believed.

#### What Paul says in plain English

- “I **delivered** to you … what I also **received**.” These are not casual words. In first-century Jewish and Greek culture, these are **technical rabbinic terms**:
    
    - Greek: _paralambanō_ = “I received” (the standard word rabbis used when a student officially took over a sacred tradition from his teacher).
    - Greek: _paradidōmi_ = “I delivered / passed on” (the word rabbis used when they formally handed the tradition to the next generation).
    
    This is exactly how oral tradition was guarded in Judaism—like passing down the exact wording of a prayer or a legal ruling. Paul is saying: “This is not my opinion or my invention. This is the official, carefully transmitted summary I was given.”
    
- The content of the creed (the earliest Christian “statement of faith”):
    
    - Christ died for our sins (according to the Old Testament Scriptures)
    - He was buried
    - He was raised on the third day (again, according to the Scriptures)
    - He appeared to Cephas (Peter), then to the Twelve, then to 500+ people (most still alive when Paul wrote this), then to James, then to all the apostles, and finally to Paul himself.

#### The timeline – why this is “impossible to deny” early

- Paul wrote 1 Corinthians around **AD 53–55** (everyone agrees on this date).
- But he says he _received_ this creed much earlier—probably right after his conversion (around AD 33–35).
- Scholars (including skeptical ones like Bart Ehrman) date the creed itself to **within 2–5 years of the crucifixion** (so roughly AD 30–35, possibly as early as AD 32).
- That means the resurrection claim was being formally taught and passed on while the original eyewitnesses (Peter, James, the Twelve, the 500) were still alive and in the same city (Jerusalem).

**Why this matters historically** Paul did **not** invent the resurrection story. He inherited it from the very people who claimed to have seen the risen Jesus. A second-century forger could never have written this; the language and the timing lock it down to the earliest church. This is why even non-Christian historians treat 1 Corinthians 15:3-7 as one of the strongest pieces of early evidence for the resurrection belief.
