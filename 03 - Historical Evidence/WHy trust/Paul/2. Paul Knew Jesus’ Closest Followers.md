## Paul Knew Jesus’ Closest Followers and Was a Faithful Messenger

So did Paul preach a message so different than Jesus? We’ve seen that’s not the case.

There’s also the fact that Paul spent 15 days with Peter. This happened 3 years after his conversion. ([Galatians 1:18-19](https://ref.ly/Gal%201.18-19;nkjv?t=biblia)) As skeptical New Testament scholar Bart Ehrman says, “_it defies belief that Paul would have spent two weeks with Jesus’ closest companion and not learned something about him…” (Did Jesus Exist?,_ 145_)_  

What Paul would’ve learned would have to include what Jesus taught. And Paul relied much on the oral tradition that proceeded him. On many occasions, Paul speaks of passing teachings he picked up from others. ([1 Corinthians 11:2](https://ref.ly/1%20Cor%2011.2;nkjv?t=biblia), [15:3](https://ref.ly/1%20Corinthians%2015.3;nkjv?t=biblia), [1 Thessalonians 4:1-2](https://ref.ly/1%20Thess%204.1-2;nkjv?t=biblia))

Going back to Paul’s letter to the Galatians, we read that 14 years later Paul returns to meet with John, Peter, and Jesus’ own brother James. He submits his gospel to him out of concern that he was teaching a different message. ([Galatians 2:2](https://ref.ly/Gal%202.2;nkjv?t=biblia)) Rather than being rejected by these men who personally knew Jesus, they endorsed his message and mission to the Gentiles. The only thing that they asked is that he would remember the poor–which Paul was eager to do. ([Galatians 2:9-10](https://ref.ly/Gal%202.9-10;nkjv?t=biblia)) It’s interesting to note that the apostles trusted Paul to collect offerings for the poor saints affected by the famine in Jerusalem. ([Romans 15:25-26](https://ref.ly/Rom%2015.25-26;nkjv?t=biblia), [1 Corinthians 16:3](https://ref.ly/1%20Cor%2016.3;nkjv?t=biblia), 2 Corinthians 8-9)

Also, the Corinthian church is familiar with Peter and Jesus’ brothers, so they could have cross-checked Paul’s message with them. ([1 Corinthians 1:12](https://ref.ly/1%20Cor%201.12;nkjv?t=biblia), [3:22](https://ref.ly/1%20Corinthians%203.22;nkjv?t=biblia), [9:5](https://ref.ly/1%20Corinthians%209.5;nkjv?t=biblia))


### Master the verification chain (Galatians 1:18 & 2:1-10)

**Read these two passages slowly, out loud, at least twice right now.** (ESV again for precision.)

**Galatians 1:18** (the first meeting)

> Then after three years I went up to Jerusalem to visit Cephas [Peter] and remained with him fifteen days. But I saw none of the other apostles except James the Lord’s brother.

**Galatians 2:1-10** (the second meeting, the “Jerusalem Council”)

> Then after fourteen years I went up again to Jerusalem with Barnabas, taking Titus along with me… 2 I went up because of a revelation and set before them (though privately before those who seemed influential) the gospel that I proclaim among the Gentiles, in order to make sure I was not running or had not run in vain… 9 And when James and Cephas and John, who seemed to be pillars, perceived the grace that was given to me, they gave the right hand of fellowship to Barnabas and me, that we should go to the Gentiles and they to the circumcised. 10 Only they asked us to remember the poor, the very thing I was eager to do.

#### Plain-English breakdown

- **First trip (AD 36-ish)**: Three years after Paul’s conversion, he spends **15 days** with Peter and meets James (Jesus’ actual brother). This is not a quick coffee — 15 days is long enough to talk through everything.
- **Second trip (AD 46/47)**: Fourteen years later, Paul deliberately submits his entire gospel (the one he preaches to Gentiles) to the three most important leaders: James, Peter, and John.
- Their response? They give him the **“right hand of fellowship”** (ancient way of saying “we completely agree — you’re one of us”). The _only_ request is “remember the poor” (which Paul was already doing).

#### Why this is historically devastating (the “impossible to deny” part)

1. **Named, living witnesses** — Peter, James, and John were still alive and leading the Jerusalem church when Paul wrote Galatians (around AD 48–55). The Galatians themselves knew Peter personally (Galatians 2:11-14 shows Paul correcting Peter in front of the Galatian churches).
2. **Public accountability** — Paul is writing this letter to churches that could (and did) talk to Peter and the others. If Paul had lied about the meetings or what was agreed, the eyewitnesses could have sent a correction letter that would have destroyed Paul’s authority overnight. They never did.
3. **Dates are solid** — Historian N.T. Wright’s chronology (widely accepted) puts the first visit at AD 36 and the second at AD 46/47. These are not vague “some time later” claims — they fit perfectly with the external timeline we already have from Roman and Jewish records.

**Key historical principle this illustrates** This is the opposite of circular reasoning. It is **external verification by the very people who were there**. Paul is not saying “trust me.” He is saying “I checked with the eyewitnesses, here are the dates, here are the names, and they endorsed me.” That is how real historical claims work.


### Your real question: How do we trust that the external verification actually happened?

This is the best part, because it’s **not** circular. Here’s the logical chain historians use (same one we covered earlier):

- Paul is not writing a private diary or a book for strangers 200 years later. He is writing a **public letter to the churches in Galatia** (real churches that existed at the time). These churches already knew Peter personally and had contact with the Jerusalem leaders (Galatians 2:11 even shows Paul publicly confronting Peter in Antioch — the Galatians knew the players).
- When Paul writes, “I went to Jerusalem, met Peter for 15 days, then 14 years later the three pillars (James, Peter, John) gave me the right hand of fellowship,” he is making a **falsifiable public claim** to people who could check it **immediately**.
- If the story were false, the easiest way to destroy Paul would be for Peter, James, or John (or anyone who knew them) to write a short correction letter: “Paul is lying — we never met him / we never endorsed him.” That letter would have circulated and ended Paul’s influence overnight. **No such correction ever appears.** Instead, the early church universally treats Paul’s letters as authoritative alongside the others.
- The letters themselves survived and were copied **millions of times** in the early centuries. If there had been any contemporary contradiction from the eyewitness circle, it would have been preserved and used against Paul. It wasn’t.
- Even skeptical scholars (Bart Ehrman, etc.) accept that these two meetings happened basically as Paul describes. Why? Because the letter is early (written ~AD 48–55), the named people are real historical figures, and there is zero counter-evidence from the Jerusalem side.
