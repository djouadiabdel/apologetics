2 Corinthians 11:23-28 and Philippians 3:4-8.

He rises from the scroll like a storm that refuses to die.

“I was the golden child of my people,” he begins, voice low at first, then rising. “Circumcised the eighth day, of the stock of Israel, of the tribe of Benjamin, a Hebrew of the Hebrews; as touching the law, a Pharisee; concerning zeal, persecuting the church; touching the righteousness which is in the law, blameless.”

He pauses. The pen hovers. Then the hammer falls:

“But what things were gain to me, those I counted loss for Christ. Yea doubtless, and I count all things but loss for the excellency of the knowledge of Christ Jesus my Lord: for whom I have suffered the loss of all things, and do count them but **dung** — skybala, the Greek word is cruder, almost a curse — that I may win Christ.”

And now the storm breaks wide open.

“Are they ministers of Christ? (I speak as a fool) I am more:

in labours more abundant, in stripes above measure, in prisons more frequent, in deaths oft.

Of the Jews five times received I forty stripes save one. Thrice was I beaten with rods, once was I stoned, thrice I suffered shipwreck, a night and a day I have been in the deep;

in journeyings often, in perils of waters, in perils of robbers, in perils by mine own countrymen, in perils by the heathen, in perils in the city, in perils in the wilderness, in perils in the sea, in perils among false brethren;

in weariness and painfulness, in watchings often, in hunger and thirst, in fastings often, in cold and nakedness.

Beside those things that are without, that which cometh upon me daily, the care of all the churches.”

He stops. The list is not a boast. It is a receipt. Every lash, every scar, every night adrift on black water is signed in his own blood and dated to the day he met the risen Jesus on the Damascus road.

He could have walked away at any moment. He had the papers, the status, the connections. One quiet retirement to Tarsus and the beatings would stop. Instead he kept showing up at the synagogues — **knowing** the forty-minus-one lashes were waiting — because he refused to stop preaching the same gospel the eyewitnesses had already signed off on.

That is the cost argument in its rawest form. A man who had everything the ancient world valued — pedigree, power, respect — traded it all for a life that looked like failure to everyone watching. And he never recanted. Not when the rods came down. Not when the stones flew. Not when the ship split beneath him and the waves swallowed him for a day and a night.

He kept writing. He kept preaching. He kept counting his former glory as dung.

Because the man who appeared to him on that road was worth it.
