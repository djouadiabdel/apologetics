## The Full Hebrew Name

In Hebrew, Isaiah 9:6 gives the child **one compound name** — not four separate titles. It is:[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pele-joez-el-gibbor-abi-ad-sar-shalom)​

> **פֶּלֶא יוֹעֵץ אֵל גִּבּוֹר אֲבִי-עַד שַׂר-שָׁלוֹם**  
> _Pele-Yoetz — El-Gibbor — Avi-Ad — Sar-Shalom_

Break each component down:

---

**פֶּלֶא יוֹעֵץ — Pele Yoetz**  
"Wonderful Counselor"

_Pele_ (פֶּלֶא) does not mean "nice" or "impressive." In Hebrew, _Pele_ is the word used exclusively for **divine miracles** — things only God does.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pele-joez-el-gibbor-abi-ad-sar-shalom)​

Examples:

- Exodus 15:11: _"Who is like you, O LORD... doing **wonders** (Pele)?"_
    
- Psalm 77:14: _"You are the God who **works wonders** (Pele)."_
    
- Judges 13:18: The Angel of the LORD says his name is _Peli_ (from Pele) — "too wonderful to understand."
    

So the child's counsel is not merely excellent — it is of the category of divine miracle-working. No human counselor is ever called _Pele_ in the Old Testament.

---

**אֵל גִּבּוֹר — El Gibbor**  
"Mighty God"

This is the most decisive part.

_El_ (אֵל) is one of the primary Hebrew words for **God.** Not "mighty one," not "strong ruler" — it is the divine name.

Muslims and some Jews argue: _El_ can mean "strong" or "mighty" in a general sense — so this could just mean "mighty hero/warrior."

**Destroy it with one verse:**

Isaiah uses the **exact same phrase El Gibbor** in Isaiah 10:21:

> _"A remnant will return, the remnant of Jacob, to the **Mighty God** (El Gibbor)."_

Who is the remnant returning to? **God himself** — YHWH.[](https://answersingenesis.org/jesus/is-isaiah-96-about-divine-messiah/)​

The same author, in the same book, two chapters later, uses _El Gibbor_ to refer to **YHWH directly.** There is no way to read _El Gibbor_ in Isaiah 9:6 as merely "mighty warrior" when Isaiah himself uses it for God two chapters later.

Furthermore, Isaiah 10:21 is a **return to God** after judgment. So Isaiah is consciously connecting the child of 9:6 with the God of 10:21. They are the same El Gibbor.[](https://answersingenesis.org/jesus/is-isaiah-96-about-divine-messiah/)​

---

**אֲבִי עַד — Avi Ad**  
"Everlasting Father / Father of Eternity"

_Avi_ (אֲבִי) = "my father" or "father of"  
_Ad_ (עַד) = eternity, perpetuity, forever[](https://www.gotquestions.org/Everlasting-Father.html)​

The Hebrew phrase means **"Father of Eternity"** — not a human father, but the one who **owns and authors** eternity itself.[](https://www.gotquestions.org/Everlasting-Father.html)​

This is confirmed immediately in the next verse — Isaiah 9:7:

> _"Of the increase of his government and of peace **there will be no end.**"_

The child's kingdom is eternal because the child himself is eternal. _Avi Ad_ = the one from whom eternity comes. This is not a human attribute. No prophet in the entire Old Testament is called the father of eternity.[](https://www.gotquestions.org/Everlasting-Father.html)​

---

**שַׂר שָׁלוֹם — Sar Shalom**  
"Prince of Peace"

_Sar_ = prince, ruler, official  
_Shalom_ = not just "peace" but complete wholeness, restoration, nothing broken, nothing missing

This is not political peace between nations. _Shalom_ in Hebrew is the restoration of everything that was broken since the Fall — the complete healing of the relationship between God and humanity.[](https://www.gotquestions.org/Everlasting-Father.html)​

The Sar Shalom brings Shalom permanently — which connects directly to the Priest office: the atonement that permanently restores the broken relationship.

## The Structure of the Verse Itself

Here is the critical grammatical point that ends every "this is just Hezekiah" or "this is just a human king" argument:

The verse says:

> **"To us a child is born, to us a son is given, and the government will be on his shoulders."**

Two parallel statements:

- "A child is born" — human nature, born in time, from a woman
    
- "A son is given" — the son is not born but **given** — he pre-exists and is given from above
    

In Hebrew poetry, parallel lines say the same thing in different ways to deepen meaning. "Born" emphasizes his humanity. "Given" emphasizes that he comes from somewhere else — he is given to humanity from God.[](https://www.gotquestions.org/Everlasting-Father.html)​

This is why John 3:16 echoes this exact language: _"For God so loved the world that he **gave** his only Son."_

Isaiah 9:6 is the prophecy. John 3:16 is the fulfillment announcement.

## OFFICE 2 — THE PRIEST

In Israel the priest had one central job: **deal with the problem of sin** between a holy God and an unholy people.

Specifically:

- Offer sacrifices that cover sin
    
- Intercede between God and the people
    
- Cleanse what is unclean
    

But the entire Levitical priesthood had a fatal flaw that every honest Jew knew: **the sacrifices had to be repeated forever.** You could not sacrifice a lamb once and be done. Every year, every day — the blood kept flowing because the problem was never permanently solved.

Psalm 110:4 introduces something radical:

> **"The LORD has sworn and will not change his mind, 'You are a priest forever after the order of Melchizedek.'"**

Melchizedek appears in Genesis 14 — before Aaron, before Levi, before the Law. He is a priest-king with no recorded beginning or end. God swears that the coming Messianic figure will be a priest in THIS order — eternal, not temporary, not requiring repetition.

This means the Messianic Priest:

- Offers one sacrifice that is permanent and complete
    
- His priesthood never ends and never needs repeating
    
- He is simultaneously King and Priest — something the Levitical system never allowed

## OFFICE 3 — THE KING

The Davidic king was God's representative on earth. But Israel's kings kept failing — Saul, Solomon, the kings of Judah and Israel — all disappointing, all dying.

God promised David in 2 Samuel 7:12-16:

> **"I will raise up your offspring after you... I will establish his kingdom. He shall build a house for my name, and I will establish the throne of his kingdom forever... I will be to him a father, and he shall be to me a son."**

And Isaiah 9:6-7 describes this king:

> **"For to us a child is born, to us a son is given; and the government shall be upon his shoulder, and his name shall be called Wonderful Counselor, Mighty God, Everlasting Father, Prince of Peace. Of the increase of his government and of peace there will be no end, on the throne of David and over his kingdom."**

This means the Messianic King:

- Rules forever — not temporarily
    
- Is called "Mighty God" and "Everlasting Father" — divine titles
    
- Brings permanent peace — not just political stability but cosmic restoration
    
- Rules over all nations — not just Israel
