[[The Titles of Jesus A Convergence Argument from the Tanakh]]
INTRODUCTION
────────────
Jesus was not just a man with one role. Every title he carried was loaded with centuries
of Old Testament prophecy. Each title is a claim. Each claim has a corresponding
prophetic fingerprint written before he was born. Together they form a web so
interlocked that you cannot accept one title and reject the others — they all point to
the same person doing the same thing.

This document examines seven core titles, their Old Testament roots, how Jesus fulfilled
them, and how to present each one to a Muslim or a Jew.

TITLE 1 — MESSIAH (המשיח / المسيح)
"The Anointed One"
────────────────────────────────────

WHAT IT MEANS:
"Messiah" (Hebrew: Mashiach; Arabic: Al-Masih) means "the Anointed One" — a person
set apart and consecrated by God for a specific mission. In Israel, kings, priests, and
prophets were anointed with oil. The Messiah was expected to be all three simultaneously.

OLD TESTAMENT ROOTS:
• Daniel 9:25-26 — Names "Mashiach the Prince" specifically, gives the timeline of his
  coming (see Daniel 9 document), and says he will be "cut off" (killed).
• Isaiah 61:1 — "The Spirit of the Lord GOD is upon me, because the LORD has anointed
  me to proclaim good news to the poor..."
• Psalm 2:2 — "The kings of the earth set themselves against the LORD and against his
  Anointed (Mashiach)."
• Psalm 45:7 — "God, your God, has anointed you with the oil of gladness beyond your
  companions."

HOW JESUS FULFILLED IT:
• Jesus begins his public ministry in Luke 4:18-21 by reading Isaiah 61:1 in the
  synagogue and saying: "Today this Scripture is fulfilled in your hearing."
• He accepts the title directly when the Samaritan woman says "I know the Messiah is
  coming," and he replies: "I who speak to you am he." (John 4:26)
• At his trial he is asked directly: "Are you the Messiah?" He answers: "I am." (Mark 14:62)

FOR THE MUSLIM:
Both the Quran and Islamic tradition accept Jesus as "Al-Masih" (The Messiah). The word
appears 11 times in the Quran (e.g., 3:45, 4:157, 4:171, 5:17). Yet Islam never
explains what "Masih" actually means or what mission it implies. In biblical Hebrew and
in all Jewish context, Mashiach is a royal, priestly, saving figure — not merely a
prophet. By calling him Al-Masih, the Quran is using a title it does not explain, from
a tradition it does not fully engage with. The natural question is: "Masih of what?
Anointed for what purpose?"

FOR THE JEW:
Daniel 9 gives a mathematical timeline for the Mashiach's arrival and says he will be
"cut off" — killed — and then the temple and city will be destroyed. The timeline points
to the period when Jesus lived. After 70 AD, the Mashiach cannot come because the
temple no longer exists for him to be anointed in. The rabbinical tradition has never
offered an alternative candidate that fits Daniel's timeline.

TITLE 2 — SON OF MAN (בן אדם / ابن الإنسان)
"The One Who Comes on the Clouds"
─────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT IT MEANS:
This is the title Jesus used about himself MORE than any other — over 80 times in the
Gospels. On the surface it sounds humble ("son of a human being"). But in the Jewish
world of the first century, every scholar who knew Daniel 7 understood this title as a
claim to divine, cosmic kingship.

OLD TESTAMENT ROOT — DANIEL 7:13-14:
"I saw in the night visions, and behold, with the clouds of heaven there came ONE LIKE
A SON OF MAN, and he came to the Ancient of Days and was presented before him. And to
him was given dominion and glory and a kingdom, that all peoples, nations, and languages
should serve him; his dominion is an everlasting dominion which shall not pass away,
and his kingdom one that shall not be destroyed."

KEY FACTS ABOUT THIS PASSAGE:
• He comes "with the clouds of heaven" — a phrase used in the Old Testament exclusively
  for God himself (Psalm 104:3, Isaiah 19:1, Exodus 13:21).
• He receives worship from all nations — in the Old Testament, only God receives this.
• His kingdom is eternal — something no human king's kingdom is.
• He approaches the "Ancient of Days" (God) as a distinct person — showing two divine
  persons in relationship.

HOW JESUS USED IT:
• The decisive moment is at his trial before the High Priest (Mark 14:61-64):
  The High Priest asks: "Are you the Messiah, the Son of the Blessed?"
  Jesus answers: "I AM, and you will see the SON OF MAN seated at the right hand
  of Power and COMING WITH THE CLOUDS OF HEAVEN."
  Response: The High Priest tears his robes and says "BLASPHEMY."

  This is the smoking gun. The High Priest did not tear his robes because Jesus said
  "I am a prophet." He tore his robes because Jesus quoted Daniel 7 and applied it to
  himself — claiming to be the cosmic figure who would be given God's throne and receive
  the worship of all nations. That is a claim to deity.

FOR THE MUSLIM:
Muslims accept that Jesus spoke words that are recorded in the Gospel (Ibn Taymiyya
himself admitted that the teaching portions of the Gospel are what Allah commanded
Christians to follow). Jesus' most repeated self-description is Son of Man = Daniel 7
figure. If Jesus' teachings are reliable, then his most frequent claim about himself
must be taken seriously. And that claim, in its full Jewish context, is a claim to share
the throne of God.

FOR THE JEW:
Daniel 7:13-14 is in your own scripture. The figure described receives the worship of
all nations and an eternal kingdom — attributes that belong to God alone. The rabbinical
tradition either interprets this figure as Israel collectively or avoids the passage.
But Jesus quotes it about himself at the most serious moment of his life — his trial —
and the High Priest immediately recognizes it as a claim to divine status (hence
"blasphemy"). The question is not whether Jesus made the claim. It is whether the claim
was true.

TITLE 3 — SON OF GOD (בן אלוהים / ابن الله)
"The One in Unique Relationship with the Father"
─────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT IT MEANS:
This is the most contested title — especially with Muslims. But it is critical to
understand that in the Old Testament, "Son of God" is not a biological claim. It is a
royal, covenantal title meaning: the one uniquely appointed and loved by God to carry
out his purposes on earth as king.

OLD TESTAMENT ROOTS:
• 2 Samuel 7:14 — God to David about the coming Messiah: "I will be to him a FATHER,
  and he shall be to me a SON."
• Psalm 2:7 — "The LORD said to me, 'You are my Son; today I have begotten you.'"
  (This psalm is about the Messianic King — this is why it is quoted in Acts 13:33,
  Hebrews 1:5, and Hebrews 5:5 as fulfilled in Jesus.)
• Psalm 89:26-27 — About the Davidic king/Messiah: "He shall cry to me, 'You are my
  Father... And I will make him the firstborn, the highest of the kings of the earth.'"

HOW JESUS FULFILLED AND DEEPENED IT:
• Jesus does not just accept the royal/covenantal meaning — he deepens it into a
  unique, eternal relationship with the Father.
• John 5:18: "He was calling God his own Father, making himself equal with God."
• John 10:30: "I and the Father are one." — The Jews immediately pick up stones.
• John 17:5: "Father, glorify me in your own presence with the glory that I had with
  you BEFORE THE WORLD EXISTED." — A claim to pre-existence before creation.

THE CRITICAL DISTINCTION FOR MUSLIMS:
Muslims reject "Son of God" because they interpret it as God physically fathering a
child — which is indeed blasphemous and not what Christianity claims. The Quran attacks
this biological meaning (6:101, 112:3). Christianity agrees it is blasphemous in that
sense. The Christian claim is not biological. It is ontological — Jesus shares the
nature of God in a unique, eternal way, as established in prophecy and confirmed by his
own words. 

Ask the Muslim: "Did you know that 'Son of God' is a Messianic title used in Psalm 2
and 2 Samuel 7 — written 1,000 years before Jesus? If the Old Testament uses it for
the coming king, why is it blasphemous when Jesus claims it?"

FOR THE JEW:
Psalm 2 and 2 Samuel 7 are Messianic texts in your tradition. They both describe the
coming king as uniquely related to God as a Son to a Father. Jesus claims to fulfill
exactly these texts. The question is not whether the title exists in the Old Testament
— it does. The question is whether Jesus fills it correctly.

TITLE 4 — KING OF ISRAEL (מלך ישראל / ملك إسرائيل)
"The Davidic King Who Rules Forever"
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT IT MEANS:
Not a political title in the ordinary sense. In the Old Testament, Israel's king was
God's representative on earth — the human embodiment of divine rule. The prophets
consistently promised a coming king from David's line who would rule not just Israel
but all nations, forever.

OLD TESTAMENT ROOTS:
• 2 Samuel 7:12-16 — God promises David: "Your throne shall be established forever."
• Isaiah 9:6-7 — "For to us a child is born, to us a son is given; and the government
  shall be upon his shoulder, and his name shall be called Wonderful Counselor, Mighty
  God, Everlasting Father, Prince of Peace. Of the increase of his government and of
  peace there will be no end, on the throne of David..."
  (Note: "Mighty God" and "Everlasting Father" are divine titles given to this child.)
• Jeremiah 23:5-6 — "I will raise up for David a righteous Branch, and he shall reign
  as king... And this is the name by which he will be called: THE LORD OUR RIGHTEOUSNESS."
  (His name = YHWH — the divine name itself.)
• Micah 5:2 — "But you, O Bethlehem Ephrathah... from you shall come forth for me one
  who is to be ruler in Israel, whose coming forth is FROM OF OLD, FROM EVERLASTING."

HOW JESUS FULFILLED IT:
• Born in Bethlehem (Micah 5:2) — confirmed by Matthew 2:1 and Luke 2:4.
• Descended from David through both Mary (Luke 3) and legally through Joseph (Matthew 1).
• Enters Jerusalem on a donkey (Matthew 21:1-9) — fulfilling Zechariah 9:9:
  "Behold, your king is coming to you, humble and mounted on a donkey."
• The crowd shouts "Hosanna to the Son of David!" — accepting him as Davidic king.
• The title on his cross reads: "Jesus of Nazareth, King of the Jews" (John 19:19) —
  written in Hebrew, Latin, and Greek — in the three great languages of civilization.
• Nathanael calls him "King of Israel" in John 1:49 in the same breath as "Son of God."

CONNECTION TO OTHER TITLES:
Son of God + King of Israel are the TWIN TITLES of the Messiah. Psalm 2 gives both:
the king on Zion's throne (v.6) is called God's Son (v.7). Every time Jesus accepts one,
he is implicitly accepting both.

TITLE 5 — RABBI (רַבִּי / معلم)
"The Teacher Whose Authority Was Like No Other"
────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT IT MEANS:
Rabbi means "my master" or "my teacher." In Jewish tradition, a rabbi teaches the Torah
and interprets it. But the way Jesus taught was categorically different from every
other rabbi — and that difference is itself a theological claim.

HOW EVERY OTHER RABBI TAUGHT:
"Rabbi X said... Rabbi Y said... and therefore we conclude..."
Their authority was always derived from the chain of tradition.

HOW JESUS TAUGHT:
"YOU HAVE HEARD it said... but I SAY TO YOU..." (Matthew 5:21-48)
He does not quote a chain of rabbinical authority. He places his own word above
the Torah as it was previously understood. He speaks as the one who gave the Torah.

Matthew 7:28-29: "The crowds were astonished at his teaching, for he was teaching them
AS ONE WHO HAD AUTHORITY, and not as their scribes."

THE DEEPER CLAIM IN THE TITLE:
When he forgives sins directly (Mark 2:5-7), the scribes immediately ask:
"Who can forgive sins but God alone?" — They understood that he was doing something
only God could do, not merely a rabbi.

When he says "Before Abraham was, I AM" (John 8:58) — "I AM" (Ego Eimi) is the Greek
translation of YHWH — the divine name God gave himself in Exodus 3:14. The Jews
immediately pick up stones to kill him for blasphemy.

FOR THE MUSLIM:
Muslims accept Jesus as a teacher (mu'allim). But a teacher who says "I am before
Abraham," "I and the Father are one," and "Your sins are forgiven" is not teaching
about God — he is claiming to BE God. You cannot take the teaching and leave the
teacher's identity claims. They are the same person speaking.

TITLE 6 — JESUS OF NAZARETH (יֵשׁוּעַ הַנָּצְרִי / يسوع الناصري)
"The Name That Fulfills Isaiah and Roots Him in History"
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT IT MEANS:
"Jesus" is the Greek form of the Hebrew "Yeshua" (ישוע) which means "YHWH saves" or
"God is salvation." This is not coincidence — the angel commands both Joseph and Mary to
name him this specifically because "he will SAVE his people from their sins." (Matthew 1:21)

"Of Nazareth" roots him geographically in history. But there is a prophetic dimension:

Matthew 2:23 — "He shall be called a NAZARENE." (Connected to Isaiah 11:1 — "A shoot
from the stump of Jesse... a BRANCH (Hebrew: Netzer) shall grow from his roots.")
The word "Nazarene" is linguistically connected to "Netzer" — the Branch.

OLD TESTAMENT "BRANCH" PROPHECIES:
• Isaiah 11:1 — "A shoot from the stump of Jesse (David's father), and a Branch (Netzer)
  from his roots shall bear fruit. The Spirit of the LORD shall rest upon him."
• Jeremiah 23:5 — "I will raise up for David a righteous BRANCH."
• Zechariah 3:8 — "I will bring my servant the BRANCH."
• Zechariah 6:12 — "Behold, the man whose name is the BRANCH: for he shall branch out
  from his place, and he shall build the temple of the LORD."

His very name and hometown together fulfill a strand of prophecy stretching across
four different Old Testament books.

HIS NAME AS A TITLE:
At his trial, Pilate's sign on the cross reads: "Jesus of Nazareth, King of the Jews."
When the demons are confronted by him they cry: "Jesus of Nazareth — have you come to
destroy us? I know who you are — the Holy One of God!" (Mark 1:24)
Even his enemies and demons identify him precisely by this combination.

TITLE 7 — LAMB OF GOD (שה האלהים / حمل الله)
"The One the Entire Sacrificial System Pointed To"
───────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT IT MEANS:
When John the Baptist sees Jesus and says "Behold, the LAMB OF GOD, who takes away
the sin of the world!" (John 1:29), every Jew present would have immediately connected
this to two of the most central events in the entire Old Testament.

CONNECTION 1 — THE PASSOVER LAMB (Exodus 12):
• A lamb without blemish is killed, its blood placed on the doorposts.
• The angel of death "passes over" every home covered by that blood.
• Israel is delivered from slavery through the lamb's death.
• Paul explicitly says: "Christ, our Passover LAMB, has been sacrificed." (1 Cor 5:7)
• Jesus is crucified at Passover. At the exact hour the Passover lambs are being killed
  in the temple, Jesus dies on the cross.

CONNECTION 2 — ISAIAH 53 (written 700 years before Jesus):
"He was oppressed and afflicted, yet he opened not his mouth; like a LAMB that is led
to the slaughter, and like a sheep that before its shearers is silent, so he opened
not his mouth... He was cut off out of the land of the living, stricken for the
transgression of my people."

ISAIAH 53 — THE FULL PROPHETIC FINGERPRINT:
• Born of no special outward appearance (v.2) ✓
• Despised and rejected (v.3) ✓
• Bore our griefs and carried our sorrows (v.4) ✓
• Wounded for our transgressions, crushed for our iniquities (v.5) ✓
• The LORD laid on him the iniquity of us all (v.6) ✓
• He was led like a lamb to slaughter and did not open his mouth (v.7) ✓
• Cut off from the land of the living (v.8) ✓
• Assigned a grave with the wicked (crucified between criminals) yet with a rich man
  in his death (buried in Joseph of Arimathea's tomb) (v.9) ✓
• He shall see his offspring and prolong his days (v.10) — resurrection ✓

FOR THE MUSLIM:
The Quran commands Christians to judge by what Allah sent down in the Gospel (5:47)
and tells Muhammad to consult those who read the previous scripture (10:94). Isaiah 53
is in the previous scripture. It describes a servant who bears the sin of others, is
killed, and then sees life again after death — 700 years before Jesus. There is no
other candidate in history who fits every detail of this passage. The question is not
whether the prophecy exists — it is in your endorsed scripture. The question is:
who else fits it?

FOR THE JEW:
Traditional rabbinical interpretation says Isaiah 53 refers to Israel as a nation.
But the text consistently uses the singular "he" and "him" throughout. And Israel as a
nation cannot be "stricken for the transgression of MY people" — because Israel IS the
people. The servant in Isaiah 53 suffers for someone else's sins. That is not a
description of a nation. It is a description of an individual substitute.

THE WEB: HOW ALL SEVEN TITLES CONNECT
────────────────────────────────────────

None of these titles stands alone. They form a web. Every thread connects to the others:

MESSIAH → The Anointed One who is both King and Priest → leads to KING OF ISRAEL
KING OF ISRAEL → The Davidic king is called God's Son → leads to SON OF GOD
SON OF GOD → The divine Son who comes on clouds → leads to SON OF MAN
SON OF MAN → Daniel 7 figure who receives eternal dominion → leads to KING OF ISRAEL
RABBI → The teacher who speaks with divine authority, forgives sins → leads to SON OF GOD
JESUS OF NAZARETH → The Branch, Yeshua = YHWH saves → leads to LAMB OF GOD
LAMB OF GOD → The one whose death saves → fulfills what MESSIAH was anointed to do

They are not seven separate claims. They are seven facets of ONE claim:
That the God of Israel became a human being, lived among his people, fulfilled every
thread of prophecy woven across 1,500 years of scripture, died as the substitutionary
sacrifice, and rose from the dead — establishing an eternal kingdom.

FINAL ARGUMENT — THE PROBABILITY QUESTION
───────────────────────────────────────────

For any skeptic — Muslim or Jew — the last question is mathematical:

• Messiah arriving on Daniel's timeline: not random
• Born in Bethlehem as Micah predicted: not random
• From the line of David: not random
• Entering Jerusalem on a donkey: not random
• Dying as Isaiah's suffering servant: not random
• Died at Passover as the lambs were being slaughtered: not random
• Tomb empty three days later: not random

Each of these is a specific, verifiable claim made centuries before Jesus was born.
The probability of all of them being fulfilled by coincidence in one person is
astronomically small by any reasonable statistical standard.

The question is not: "Can you explain each one individually?"
The question is: "What single explanation best covers all of them simultaneously?"

There is only one.
