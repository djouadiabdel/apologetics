## The dating is finished

1QIsaᵃ contains all sixty-six chapters and dates to around 125 BC. The chapter existed, complete, in Hebrew, roughly a century and a half before Jesus.

So the corruption argument is dead. Nobody wrote this afterwards to fit.

Note this is not an answer to critical scholarship, which never claimed otherwise. It is an answer to *tahrif*, the claim that the scriptures were tampered with, and that is the objection you will actually meet.

Note also that multiple authorship changes nothing here. Even on the critical dating, chapter 53 sits in the exilic material, sixth century BC. Five hundred years is still five hundred years. See [[Dead Sea Scrolls]].

## The real objection

It is not about the date. It is about who the servant is.

The standard Jewish reading is that the servant is Israel, corporately. The nation suffers among the nations, is despised, and its suffering has meaning for the world. On that reading the chapter is about a people, not a person, and there is nothing messianic in it.

**Their best verse is Isaiah 49:3.**

> "He said to me, 'You are my servant, Israel, in whom I will display my splendor.'"
> — Isaiah 49:3 (NIV)

The text names the servant. That is as direct as it gets and you should quote it yourself rather than wait for it.

## The answer

Two verses later the same servant has a mission *to* Israel.

> "And now the Lord says, he who formed me in the womb to be his servant to bring
> Jacob back to him and gather Israel to himself... 'It is too small a thing for you
> to be my servant to restore the tribes of Jacob and bring back those of Israel I
> have kept. I will also make you a light for the Gentiles, that my salvation may
> reach to the ends of the earth.'"
> — Isaiah 49:5-6 (NIV)

A servant who simply is Israel cannot be sent to bring Israel back. The sentence stops working.

**And 53:8 does the same thing.**

> "For he was cut off from the land of the living; for the transgression of my people he was punished."
> — Isaiah 53:8 (NIV)

My people. The servant is being distinguished from the people he suffers for, inside the chapter under dispute.

## The honest position

The corporate reading works in some passages and breaks in others. The individual reading does the same in reverse. The servant material moves between the nation and a figure within the nation, and it appears to do so deliberately.

That is a stronger thing to say than "they are wrong." It is also true, and it is what the text looks like when you read all four servant songs in order rather than lifting 53 out on its own.

The four are 42:1-4, 49:1-6, 50:4-9 and 52:13 to 53:12. Read them together before arguing about any one of them.

## What I would say

The date is settled and I will not spend time on it. The chapter is in a cave by 125 BC.

The argument is about who the servant is, and your reading has real support. Isaiah 49:3 names him Israel. But two verses later he is sent to restore Israel, and in 53:8 he is struck for the transgression of *my people*. So the text is doing something more complicated than either of us wants, and I think it is doing it on purpose.
