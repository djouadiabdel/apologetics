## The Old Testament Structure: Prophecy Aims at the Messiah and Ends There

The Old Testament's prophetic line is not an open-ended sequence with the Messiah as one stage among others. It is a teleological structure that finds its completion in Him. Several texts establish this.

Genesis 49:10 — Jacob's blessing over Judah: "The scepter shall not depart from Judah, nor the ruler's staff from between his feet, until *Shiloh* comes (עַד כִּי־יָבֹא שִׁילֹה — *ad ki-yavo Shiloh*), and to him shall be the obedience of the peoples." The rabbinic tradition (Targum Onkelos, Targum Jonathan, Genesis Rabbah 98:8, Sanhedrin 98b) identifies *Shiloh* as the Messiah. The verse marks a terminus — rulership remains in Judah *until* Shiloh, and then "to him shall be the obedience of the peoples." This is endpoint language.

Daniel 9:24-27 — the seventy weeks prophecy. Verse 24: "Seventy weeks are decreed about your people and your holy city, to finish the transgression, to put an end to sin, to atone for iniquity, to bring in everlasting righteousness, to seal both vision and prophet (וְלַחְתֹּם חָזוֹן וְנָבִיא — *velachtom chazon venavi*), and to anoint a most holy place." The Hebrew *velachtom* — "to seal up" — means to close, to complete, to finalize. Vision and prophecy are *sealed* by the events of the seventy weeks, which culminate in Messiah being cut off (v. 26). This is one of the most direct statements in the Hebrew Bible that prophecy has a terminus, and that terminus is the Messiah.

In Arabic, *velachtom* corresponds to خَتْم — the same root as خاتم (*khatim*, "seal"). Muslims call Muhammad خاتم النبيين (*khatim al-nabiyyin*) — the seal of the prophets — citing Surah 33:40. But Daniel 9:24 says the seal of vision and prophet is placed *at the Messiah's cutting off* — centuries before Muhammad. The "seal of prophecy" was already placed. Muhammad cannot be the seal because the seal had already closed the canon of prophecy in the first century.

Malachi — the last book of the Hebrew prophets in Christian ordering and the last prophetic book chronologically. Malachi 3:1: "Behold, I send my messenger, and he will prepare the way before me. And the Lord whom you seek will suddenly come to his temple; and the messenger of the covenant in whom you delight, behold, he is coming." Malachi 4:5-6: "Behold, I will send you Elijah the prophet before the great and awesome day of the Lord comes." The last words of the Hebrew prophetic canon point to: (a) a forerunner (identified by Jesus as John the Baptist in Matthew 11:14, 17:11-13), and (b) the Lord coming to His temple. After Malachi, Jewish tradition itself holds that prophecy ceased — this is recorded in 1 Maccabees 4:46, 9:27, 14:41 and in the Babylonian Talmud (Yoma 9b, Sotah 48b, Sanhedrin 11a): "When the latter prophets — Haggai, Zechariah, and Malachi — died, the Holy Spirit departed from Israel."

So by Israel's own testimony, prophecy ended around 400 BC and would only resume with the messianic age. The next prophetic figure was John the Baptist, identified as the forerunner of the Messiah. The Messiah Himself then completes the prophetic line.

## The New Testament Structure: Christ as the Telos, Not a Stage

John 1:18 — "No one has ever seen God; the only God, who is at the Father's side, he has made him known (ἐκεῖνος ἐξηγήσατο — *ekeinos exēgēsato*)." The verb *exēgēsato* is where we get "exegesis." Christ has *exegeted* God — fully expounded Him. This isn't partial revelation that needs supplementing.

Colossians 2:9 — "For in him the whole fullness of deity dwells bodily (ἐν αὐτῷ κατοικεῖ πᾶν τὸ πλήρωμα τῆς θεότητος σωματικῶς — *en autō katoikei pan to plērōma tēs theotētos sōmatikōs*)." All the *plērōma* — fullness — of deity dwells in Christ bodily. Not part. All. There is nothing further to add.

John 19:30 — Christ's last word on the cross: Τετέλεσται — *Tetelestai* — "It is finished." This is a perfect passive — "it has been completed and stands completed." The salvific work and the revelatory work culminate here. In Arabic Bibles: قد أُكْمِل — "it has been completed/perfected." Compare this with Surah 5:3: الْيَوْمَ أَكْمَلْتُ لَكُمْ دِينَكُمْ — "Today I have perfected for you your religion" — which Muslims understand as the completion of Islam in Muhammad. As we have said before, so now I say again: If anyone is preaching to you a gospel contrary to the one you received, let him be accursed."

This is critical. Paul anticipates the exact scenario Islam claims — an angel from heaven bringing a different gospel — and pronounces *anathema* on it in advance. Galatians 1:8 was written approximately AD 48-55, roughly 575 years before Muhammad's first revelation. It is a pre-emptive disqualification.
