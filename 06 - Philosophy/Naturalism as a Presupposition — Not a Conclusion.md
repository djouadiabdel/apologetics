## The structure

Two people agree on every fact. Same sources, same dates, same list of what the witnesses said. One concludes a man rose from the dead. The other concludes something else happened.

The disagreement is not about the evidence. It arrived before the evidence did.

This is worth stating carefully because it is the ceiling on everything in folder 04. Once the facts are fixed, the inference is dominated by the prior on whether miracles happen at all, and no amount of source checking touches a prior.

## Hume, and why the usual reply is not enough

Hume's argument in the *Enquiry*, section X, is that a miracle is a violation of a law of nature, that the laws are established by uniform experience, and that no testimony is strong enough to overturn uniform experience.

The standard reply is that this is circular. If you define uniform experience as including the event in question not happening, you have assumed the conclusion.

That reply is correct as far as it goes and it does not get you where people think it does. Hume can drop the word "violation" and restate it as a probability claim. Rare events need proportionally stronger evidence, and testimony is not a strong instrument. That version is not circular and it is harder to answer.

## Earman, and the one thing you get back

John Earman, *Hume's Abject Failure: The Argument Against Miracles* (2000). Earman is a philosopher of science with no apologetic stake, and his conclusion is that Hume's argument is a bad argument whether or not its conclusion happens to be true.

The technical point is that Hume had no probability apparatus adequate to what he wanted to prove. On a proper Bayesian treatment, multiple independent testimonies can raise a low prior hypothesis without any fixed ceiling. There is no threshold of improbability below which testimony stops being able to move a prior at all. Hume needed such a threshold and never had one.

Keep this narrow, because it is easy to steal more from it than it gives. **Earman does not argue that the resurrection happened, and citing him does not either.** What he removes is the in principle blocker, the claim that no quantity of testimony could ever count for a thing like this. Once that is gone, the other person has to make a specific empirical case about these particular witnesses instead of a general case about testimony as such. That is a real gain, and it is the only one available on this side of the argument. It is also the reason to run this argument early rather than late, since it is about what kind of conversation is possible rather than about who wins it.

And the load bearing assumption has to be named against myself. Testimonies multiply only if they are independent. Markan dependence across the gospels is the standard objection and it is a good one. Whoever says "multiple independent sources" owes an argument for the independence and usually does not have one. This is the same defect as G4 in [[Counting the Contradictions]], and I flagged it against my own project before I ever pointed it at anyone else.

## The honest concession

**You cannot argue somebody out of a prior with evidence.**

The prior is what determines how the evidence gets weighed. Feed the same testimony to two people with different priors and it moves both of them a little, in the same direction, and they still end up on opposite sides.

So the apologist who thinks he is going to accumulate enough facts to force a conclusion is wrong about what he is doing. He is not going to get there. Anyone who says the case is overwhelming has not understood the shape of the problem.

## What you can actually do

Three things, and they are enough.

**Show the prior is doing the work.** Not as an accusation. As a description. If someone says the resurrection is the least likely explanation, ask what would have to be true for it to be the most likely one. Usually the answer is nothing, at any evidence level, which means the position is not being held on evidential grounds. That is fine and it is worth both people knowing.

**Show the prior is not itself a finding.** Methodological naturalism is a working rule for doing science. It says look for natural causes. It does not say there are only natural causes, and it cannot, because no experiment tests that. Sliding from the working rule to the metaphysical claim happens quietly and it is worth naming.

**Take the same medicine.** My own prior is doing work in the other direction and I have to say so. This is the same discipline as the Ehrman guard in [[Counting the Contradictions]]. The rule blocks the apologist's invented harmony and it blocks me dissolving an item because it feels fine. Symmetric or worthless.

## Alex O'Connor is the useful case

*Tier K throughout this section. Everything attributed to him here is from video and needs a timestamped citation before it goes anywhere public.*

He argues, well, for a sustaining first cause. Not a god that started things and left, but one holding everything in place right now, because a hierarchical causal chain has no causal power at all unless something at the bottom is supplying it.

Then he stops. He says explicitly that this is not to say personal, not to say that he cares about human beings.

The gap between those two positions is not an evidence gap. Nothing in the metaphysics tells you whether that cause acts in history, and nothing ever will, because that is a historical question and not a metaphysical one. What fills the gap in either direction is a prior.

And he says what his is built from. The problem of suffering. Billions of years of predation, ninety nine point nine percent of species extinguished, and this is the mechanism the good God is said to have chosen.

That is the honest reason most people hold the prior they hold. Not an argument about testimony. A judgement about what a good God would plausibly do.

## Where that leaves it

The best available outcome of an evidential case is that the case is honestly built. Never that it happened.

Say that out loud, early, to whoever you are talking to. It costs nothing you actually had, and it is the only thing that makes the rest of what you say worth listening to.
