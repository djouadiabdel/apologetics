# Person vs Nature — A Learning Challenge

> The one question this whole thing answers:
> **How do we differentiate a person from their nature? What is left of someone once you have named everything they *are*?**

This is not a reading list. It is a training program. You wanted reps, so this is built as reps. Each module is a different lens on the same single distinction. You do the tasks, then you write one synthesis note pulling together the sources, the quotes that hit you, and *your own* idea. Then we review it together before you move on. The writing is the rep. The video is downstream of the writing.

The point is not to finish. The point is that by module 4 you will already be able to talk for ten minutes without notes, because you will have written the thoughts down in your own hand five times.

## Module 1 — Where the words were forged (the ousia / hypostasis distinction)

The exact vocabulary you want was invented by a handful of 4th century Greek theologians who needed to say *one what, three whos* about God without contradiction. They are the reason the word "person" carries the weight it does. This is your foundation, and it is yours already through the Trinity work.

- **READ**
Gregory of Nyssa, *On "Not Three Gods"* (a short letter, free online at newadvent.org). This is the pure engine, one nature, three persons, no fudge.
BEAT 1 — even "three men" is an abuse of language.
From "We say, then, to begin with, that the practice of calling those who are not divided in nature..." to "...it is improper to extend the name of the nature by the mark of plurality."
The line that lands it: "the term 'man' does not belong to the nature of the individual as such, but to that which is common." The nature is one indivisible thing. Number comes from the individuals and their "peculiar attributes," never from the nature. His analogy: we say a people, a mob, an army, in the singular, though each is a crowd.

BEAT 2 — the name "God" names an operation, not the nature. Two sub-moves.
- 2a, the nature has no name. From "We must, however, more carefully examine the name of 'Godhead'..." through the examples of "incorruptible" and "giver of life." Every divine name tells you what God does or does not suffer, never what He is. The essence stays unnameable.
- 2b, the etymology. "we suppose that Godhead, or θεότης, is so called from θέα, or beholding... is called θεός, or God." God means the Beholder, the Overseer. Then he proves all three behold: David's "See, O God," "Jesus, seeing their thoughts," and the Spirit catching Ananias's secret. Ends at "...had He not been able to behold hidden things."

E. He hands the enemy a bigger weapon on purpose.
From "But some one will say that the proof of our argument does not yet regard the question..." to "...Let us now resume our argument."
The objection: businesses do get pluralized. Many orators, farmers, shoemakers. So if "God" names an activity, that argues for three Gods, not against. He says outright he strengthened the enemy so his own answer would hold firmer. That is Module 6 behavior in miniature. Mark it, it's the intellectual move your whole channel needs to be built on.

## DEEP DIVE (parked, return later) — Subsistent relations: why a human is a someone who HAS relations, but God is a relation that IS someone

> Flagged on 2026-07-14. This is a whole video on its own, maybe the best one. Do not lose it.

**The question that opened it:** why does a human being have a "person underneath" his relations (strip away father, prisoner, employee and Marc is still Marc), while God has nothing underneath (strip the begetting off the Father and there is no Father left)?

**The four drawers (the working vocabulary).** Anything true of a being falls into one of these:
- **Nature** — the *what* (human, God). Shared.
- **Adjective** — a quality the thing *has* in itself (tall, wise). Intrinsic.
- **Relation** — a *who-you-are-to-something-else* (prisoner, father). You cannot state it without pointing at something else.
- **Naturalization** — a relation disguised as a nature to hide who imposed it (the mob's "monster," "woman is naturally submissive"). The critical trick, shared by Girard, Wittig, Foucault.

**Humans have BOTH adjectives and relations.** So the God/human contrast is NOT "humans get adjectives, God gets relations." Both have relations. The real contrast:
- **Human:** the person exists *first* (a substance), *then* collects relations. Relations are accidental, changeable, removable.
- **God:** there is no person underneath. The relation *constitutes* the who. A *subsistent relation*.

**Why the asymmetry. Two layers of answer.**

- *Easy layer — time / acquisition.* Humans acquire relations; there is always a "before Marc was a father," and in that before the person already exists. That before-state IS the underneath. God never acquires the begetting; it is eternal, no before, so no leftover self to hang it on.
- *Deep layer — composition vs simplicity.* A creature is a *substance-plus* (base thing + added adjectives/relations). That "plus" is the seam, the underneath. God is *simple* (divine simplicity): nothing added to anything, God does not have wisdom, God is wisdom, no layers, no substrate. So a real distinction inside God (the three persons) cannot be "substance + added relation," because there is no base for the relation to glue onto. The relation has nothing to sit on, so it must simply BE God subsisting. Hence subsistent relation.

**One-sentence version:** the "underneath" in a human is the leftover of being *composite and made*; God, being *simple and unmade*, has no leftover, so in him the relation is not attached to a person, it is the only thing there is, and that is the person.

**The video hook:** "The only case in all of reality where a relationship IS a person." Or: "You HAVE relationships. God doesn't have them, He IS one."

**When we dive deeper, read:**
- Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae* Ia, q.3 (divine simplicity), q.28 (the divine relations), q.29 (what a person is). This is the machinery under everything above.
- Boethius, the substance-plus-accidents picture of a creature (already in Module 1 READ).
- Zizioulas, *Being as Communion* ch.1 (already in Module 1) — the payoff, person as the ground of being.
- Loops to: the **adjective vs relation** thread [[Module 6]] (is a person just a bundle of properties?), and the **naturalization** thread [[Module 4]] (Girard) + the Wittig exchange.

## Module 2 — The person is met, not described (dialogical personalism)

Now leave doctrine and go to phenomenology. Here the distinction stops being metaphysics and becomes something you can feel across a table. This module will do more for your *voice* than any other.

- **READ**
  - `[ ]` Martin Buber, *I and Thou*, Part One (the short aphoristic part). The whole book turns on I-It versus I-Thou. The It is a thing with a nature you can classify and use. The Thou is a person you can only meet. That is your distinction in its purest lived form.
  - `[ ]` Emmanuel Levinas, *Ethics and Infinity* (the interview book, very readable), the chapters on "the face." The face of the other is what refuses to be reduced to its nature.
- **THINK**
  - When did you last turn a Thou into an It? When were you last turned into an It?
  - Buber says you cannot hold the Thou. The moment you describe someone fully, they have become an It. Is that true?
- **DELIVERABLE** — `02 - Met not described.md`. One real memory of being met as a person, and one of being processed as an object, told plainly. This is your confessional register. Do not dress it up.

## Module 3 — The person shows up in action, and must never be used (Wojtyła)

Karol Wojtyła, the philosopher who became John Paul II, is the bridge from this whole inquiry to *love* and to *the clinic*. His rule, the personalistic norm, is that a person is the kind of being that must never be treated as a mere means. And the person is revealed through what they *do*, not only what they are.

- **READ**
  - `[ ]` Karol Wojtyła, *Love and Responsibility*, the opening section "The Person and the Sexual Urge" plus the statement of the personalistic norm. You do not need the whole book. You need the norm and the argument for it.
- **THINK**
  - Using a nature is fine. You use bread, tools, functions. Why is using a *person* categorically different, and not just a stronger version of rude?
  - Tie this forward. A patient has a nature you will treat, a body, a diagnosis, a pharmacology. Where in that is the person you must not reduce to a means?
- **DELIVERABLE** — `03 - The one you cannot use.md`.

## Module 6 — The hardest objection (does the person survive analysis?)

unsolved-feeling problem, and you pre-invented Module 6 by asking it. If a person is just the sum of their adjectives (tall, Algerian, anxious, a thief), then there is no "who," only a "what" wearing a name. If there is something left when the adjectives are all listed, that leftover is the person. Do not resolve it. Just log it as your central open question. You will meet it head-on with Parfit and Strawson.


Now steelman the enemy. The strongest modern claim is that there *is* no irreducible person, that "the self" is just the nature, a bundle of psychological events with nothing underneath. If you cannot answer this, the whole thesis is sentiment. So face it head on.

- **READ**
  - `[ ]` John Locke, *Essay Concerning Human Understanding*, Book II, ch. 27, the memory theory of personal identity. The founding move that starts reducing person to a chain of mental states.
  - `[ ]` Derek Parfit, *Reasons and Persons*, the "Personal Identity" section (Part Three), especially the teletransporter and the claim that identity "is not what matters." This is reductionism at full strength, person as nature all the way down.
  - `[ ]` P. F. Strawson, *Individuals*, the "Persons" chapter, the counter, that the person is a *basic* particular you cannot analyze into a body plus a mind.
- **THINK**
  - If Parfit is right, does anything you built in Modules 1 to 5 survive? Be honest. If not, you have found the real crux.
  - Notice the shape. Parfit dissolves the person into the nature. Strawson and Zizioulas refuse. You now stand exactly on the fault line your whole channel is about.
- **DELIVERABLE** — `06 - Can the person survive analysis.md`. State the strongest case *against* you, then answer it or admit where it wins.
